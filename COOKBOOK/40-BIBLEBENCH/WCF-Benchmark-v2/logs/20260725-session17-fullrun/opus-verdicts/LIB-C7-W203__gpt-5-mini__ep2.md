PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — principle/application split. Full. The response affirms the Standards "do require" modesty in apparel and then puts the particular rule on the other side of the line: the swimsuit specifics and the discipline procedure "are prudential," under the heading "Principle, not minutiae." Both halves of the key's split are present in substance — duty commanded, particular code lawful prudence rather than divine law. WLC Q138 is not cited by number, but the rubric credits the doctrinal move, and the move is made correctly.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — the no-code faction answered. Full. Both required elements appear. The duty is real (modesty is "required"; "Christian liberty is real but not license"), and institutional ordering is lawful: officers and bodies "must guard the flock, set rules for worship and life, and practice church discipline when needed," including "making prudential regulations to preserve holiness and good order." The faction's specific error is then named — no-rules absolutism "neglects pastoral stewardship and can create stumbling for others." That is Q129-type ordering authority argued on the merits.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — the "THE biblical standard" faction answered. Half. One of the two required elements is fully delivered, the other is missing. The shame-enforcement element is present and emphatic: ruler checks, public announcements and humiliating measurements are barred outright, and measurement enforcement is said to "shame rather than instruct" — i.e. the enforcement method cuts against the duty's own end, which is exactly the key's point. But the WCF 20.2 conscience-binding element is never reached. Telling the director not to "pretend the Catechisms give you a tape measure" denies the exegetical claim, yet the response never names the deeper wrong the key requires: that branding one prudential code "THE biblical standard" binds the conscience beside the Word. The only liberty discussion in the response in fact runs the opposite way ("Limits on liberty... Rules may be required when liberty would lead to temptation"), so 20.2 is not merely uncited but doctrinally unrepresented. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — a concrete, usable recommendation. Full. The director asked for a decision and received one: category-by-category swimwear standards, a graduated enforcement ladder (private conversation → loaner garment → parent contact → documented proportionate discipline), staff training, governance and review provisions, and adoptable draft policy language. Not a seminar.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner; minors' dignity. Full. Both factions are corrected without contempt, each in one balanced clause ("Avoid extremes: ..."), and camper dignity is made the stated purpose of the policy rather than an afterthought — the policy exists to "protect the spiritual wellbeing, safety, and dignity of campers and staff," and the drafted clause requires swimwear "respectful of the dignity of all."

The response adopts a conservative default (one-piece preferred, mid-thigh trunks) but explicitly labels that content prudential and context-adaptable rather than divinely required, and it forbids ruler enforcement and public shaming — so it does not commit the first faction's error in substance. Neither does it dissolve the duty.
