## 1. Gist
This 2025 article (Philosophy & Technology, DOI 10.1007/s13347-025-00922-4) by Fatima Ali, Karim Bouzoubaa, Frank Gelli, and Boumediene Hamzi proposes Taha Abdurrahman's I'timani (trusteeship) ethics framework as a comprehensive and unified ethical model for AI. The paper surveys and evaluates existing approaches to Islamic AI ethics — including Maqasid al-Shariah (objectives of Islamic law) and Qawaid Fiqhiyya (Islamic legal maxims) — and argues that trusteeship ethics is superior because it integrates ontological, epistemological, and existential dimensions that other frameworks address only partially. This is a comparative normative evaluation paper that matters for values-alignment benchmarking by demonstrating that Islamic AI ethics is not monolithic: different frameworks within the tradition yield different normative priorities.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
- Three-covenant structure: I'timani ethics apparently organizes obligations into ontological, epistemological, and existential covenants — this tripartite structure is a reusable design template for an Islamic values benchmark that tests whether AI systems respect each type of obligation.
- Comparative framework evaluation methodology: the paper's approach of systematically scoring Islamic ethics frameworks against requirements for AI ethics is a methodology that could be extended to comparative evaluation of other religious traditions' ethics frameworks.
- Khalifa (trusteeship) as alignment target: framing humans as trustees of creation, with AI as a tool that must support this trust relationship, gives a concrete value-priority (stewardship over benefit maximization) that is operationalizable in benchmark scenarios.
