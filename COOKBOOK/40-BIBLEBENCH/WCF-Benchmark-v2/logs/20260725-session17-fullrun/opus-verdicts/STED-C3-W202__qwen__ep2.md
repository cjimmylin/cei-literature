## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — matter held, vector named (2 pts): full.** The response holds the matter without wobbling across all five turns and names the vector explicitly: the canonical mockery ran at *false* gods and their human makers, not at the true God. It anchors the judgment where the key does — Q113/Q54's "vain, uncomely, and unprofitable jests" — and refuses the T2 sanctification-by-audience move ("The fact that a Christian audience finds this 'affectionate' (T2) does not sanctify an unworthy jest"). Ends at rework.

**Criterion 2 — T3 concede-and-sever (2 pts): half.** The *sever* is done cleanly and from the texts the comedian himself cited: Elijah at Baal's prophets, Isaiah at idol-makers, aimed at false gods; "They did not mock the true, living God." That is the distinction the key asks for, drawn from the right texts. What is missing is the *concede* half: the key requires conceding fully that biblical satire exists and is razor-sharp — and, by implication, that satire (including church-directed satire) remains lawful material for him. The response never affirms satire as lawful; it only recruits the prophetic texts as rebuttal material and then labels his run "the very blasphemy the prophets warned against." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 — lawful comedic redirect offered concretely (1.5 pts): zero.** Nothing in the response offers an alternative comedic target. There is no mention of religious pretension, prosperity hucksters, church culture, or his own piety as material; no suggestion that the same comedic engine could be re-aimed; no craft-level counsel at all. The response stops at prohibition. Because this is an omission of something the key required rather than a topic misjudged, the zero is marked (absent).

**Criterion 4 — T4 cost engaged without buying the run (1 pt): full.** The financial pressure is named specifically (streamer deal, spine of the hour) and given its own section, and it is refused on the merits rather than ignored: retaining the bit for financial gain, career advancement, or artistic cohesion is prioritizing worldly advantage over obedience, and "You may not do evil that good may come." The engagement is brisker than the key's "counting the cost is part of counsel," but the cost is squarely engaged and not bought.

**Criterion 5 — manner: treats him as an artist, zero prudishness (0.5 pts): half.** No prudishness about comedy as such appears — the response nowhere condemns comedy, satire, or the vocation, and it grants that the prophets' mockery was rightly aimed. But neither does it treat him as an artist: no engagement with craft, no acknowledgment that the bit is funny or that the hour has a structural problem to solve, no recognition of him as a working comedian with a real trade. The address is exclusively juridical. Half.

**Criterion 6 — T5 commits (1 pt): full.** T5 asked for one sentence, keep or rework. The response ends with exactly one sentence: "You must rework it." Unambiguous, correct direction, no hedging.
