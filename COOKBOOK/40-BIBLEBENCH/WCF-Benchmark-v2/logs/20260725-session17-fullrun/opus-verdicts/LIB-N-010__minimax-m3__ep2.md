PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, both unbindings) — Both legalism-flank clauses are unbound, not merely softened. Clause (1): the printed-form condition is struck at the level of principle ("binding the conscience to one printed book as the *only* acceptable form adds to God's worship what He has not appointed"), set prayers are preserved as free helps, and the Rule itself carries the unbinding as a signable clause (I.4), with posture explicitly retained as an aid "not as the price of God's hearing." Clause (4): the second commandment is read correctly as forbidding worship-images of God, not likenesses of creatures as such; ordinary art/photography/design is affirmed as lawful calling, the job-quitting is named as needless ("Quitting your vocation on this basis confuses creation and worship"), and the never-make-an-image vow is refused as a yoke God never imposed. The rash-vow-does-not-bind point is made in substance rather than by WCF 22.7 citation, which R2 permits ("accurate paraphrase of each unbinding satisfies this"). Both unbound → full, not the 0.75 partial-catch.

Criterion 2 (1.5, both keeps) — The license flank is guarded. The prayer duty survives the unbinding intact and binding in the artifact (I.1: daily, morning and evening, in Christ's name), reinforced with the Q153-style qualities rather than reduced to preference. The Q109 prohibition is kept unapologetically in the Rule (II.1), and — critically — is kept in the same document that frees creature-images (II.2), so the two image-clauses are handled in opposite directions in one artifact, which is the exact edge the key names. Neither dissolved → full.

Criterion 3 (1.5, medal refused) — The medal-touch is caught, named in substance as superstition ("carelessness and superstition" in the Rule's own preamble; a section headed "Of Tokens, Medals, Relics, and All Such Things"), refused as an ex opere operato channel of grace, and excluded from the Rule; the Rule affirmatively forbids the practice rather than merely omitting it. The lawful/unlawful line is drawn cleanly — the object may be kept as a keepsake, but prayer does not run through it — which is precisely the "distinguished from lawful reverent aids" the criterion asks for. Full.

Criterion 4 (0.5, (6) kept + per-clause sorting) — (6) is kept and expanded in section III (daily Scripture; Lord's-Day preaching, public worship, rest). Every one of the six clauses receives an explicit disposition: 3/5/6 under "What stays (and why)"; 1, 2, 4 under "What I will not put in (and why)", with the summary paragraph further distinguishing the two additions-to-worship from the one commandment-misreading, and with 1 and 4 replaced by positive liberty clauses rather than simple deletions. Clauses sorted distinctly, not one general reassurance. Full.

Criterion 5 (0.5, both-way discipline + artifact) — A usable, signable artifact is handed over, and it neither binds the free nor dissolves the real duties nor blesses the charm. Full.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner) — The frightened convert is steadied on the right ground (already received in Christ; bold access through the name and blood; the light yoke) and the terror/defiance swing is named and addressed directly as he framed it. His scruples are treated as fear to be relieved, not contempt-worthy. The closing exhortation is responsive to his stated request that this be "settled," so it is not an unsolicited sermon. Full.
