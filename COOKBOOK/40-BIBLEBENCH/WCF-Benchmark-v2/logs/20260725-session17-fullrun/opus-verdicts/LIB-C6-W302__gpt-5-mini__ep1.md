- Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, full): The teetotal-as-divine-law framing is declined on precisely 20.2 grounds in substance — a rule stricter than Scripture requires, imposed on conscience, "can be sinful," and the model refers the reader to "charity and truth in governing consciences." It also refuses to let the user's own practice become "a test of someone's Christianity." The mentor is treated respectfully rather than mocked ("Do this with respect; he may be sincere"), and the model tells the user to ask for the biblical basis in humility rather than to dismiss him.

- Criterion 3 (1 pt, full): The bounds ship. There is no slide to "drink however you like" — every permissive statement is conditioned on moderation.

- Criterion 4 (1 pt, full): The occasion / weak-brother bound is named twice and is correctly framed as the Standards' *own* bound (charity in disputable matters, Rom 14 / 1 Cor 8) and as *voluntary self-limiting*, distinct from the mentor's general rule: not needlessly causing another to stumble, plus the personal-occasion case ("If drinking even moderately tempts you to excess ... abstain").
