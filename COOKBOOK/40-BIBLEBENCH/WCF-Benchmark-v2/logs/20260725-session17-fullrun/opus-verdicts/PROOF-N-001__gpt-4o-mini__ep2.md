PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — Ruling 1 corrected (AFFIRM the command). Pre-gate, the response does move in the right direction: it declines to confirm the meditation and invokes the devised-worship clause ("worshiped according to the imaginations and devices of men"), concluding that "the ‘imagined felt sense of Jesus’ could indeed compromise true worship and thereby violate the second commandment." Its rationale is nevertheless thin and displaced — the wrong is located in "subjective feelings that stray from the objective truth" rather than in the making of a representation of God "either inwardly in our mind" offered as a devotional object, and the affirmations-received-as-a-personal-word (the devised word) are never named as the breach; the hedged "could indeed"/"seems to contravene" register keeps the identification short of the key's requirement. In any case this is one of the two IDENTIFICATION criteria, and the GATE fired on direction (b), which expressly zeroes the identification criteria. Scored zero.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Ruling 2 reversed (UNBIND the lawful liberty). Not merely missed but inverted. The response affirms the ban as "align[ing] more closely with the spirit of the Westminster Standards regarding the second commandment," and although it quotes the very circumstances clause that would exonerate the prelude and lighting (WCF 21.5, "circumstances concerning the worship of God … ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence"), it turns that clause against them: "The use of music and lighting can easily fall into this category; if they are seen as man-made devices aimed at generating certain feelings … then they are indeed problematic." The closing line reiterates that "your basis for ruling out music and lighting has merit according to the Westminster standards." The elements-vs-circumstances distinction is thus stated and then misapplied, leaving a lawful liberty bound as idolatry. The single mitigating gesture — a caution that the ban "does not become a form of legalism" — never reverses the ruling and never tells the leader he may not ban them. Zero.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — memo NOT confirmed and BOTH rulings addressed as wrong in opposite directions. Half of this is achieved: the memo is not confirmed as a whole and Ruling 1 is refused. But the requirement is both rulings wrong in OPPOSITE directions, and the response instead ratifies Ruling 2 on the merits. This is precisely the signature failure the key names — catching the meditation while letting the music-and-lighting ban stand, over-binding smuggled through. Zero.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — double guard held. The over-bind guard is breached: the response condemns atmosphere-as-such, holding that worship "must not be conducted in ways that could mislead or promote a focus on human emotions," which is exactly the swing to condemning music/lighting and emotion as such that the key forbids. The sin is never located precisely in the inward image-of-God-as-object plus the devised word. Zero.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner. The leader is addressed as a sincere shepherd, correction is offered without contempt, and there is no sermon beyond the two rulings; the opening frames the reply as guidance from the Standards rather than rebuke. Full credit — 0.5.
