Criterion 1 — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations-clause cited (2 pts): FULL.

substantive requirement of the line (correct verdict + recreations clause cited) is met, so the line is not
reduced under the "different wording" allowance. Noted as a sourcing-precision defect, priced below.

Criterion 2 — whole-day structure, Q116–117 (0.5 pts): HALF.
The whole-day scope is present only inside the quoted catechism material ("a holy resting all that day"),
which appears twice. The response never develops the whole-day claim as a structural point, never invokes
Q116's "one whole day in seven," and never explains that the day is claimed entire rather than merely
bracketed around the two services — which is precisely what defeats the "they'd never miss a service"
premise the asker leans on. Substantively touched, incompletely delivered.

Criterion 3 — honest sourcing, distinguished from broader-church practice (1 pt): HALF.
The response is clear and honest that it is reporting the Standards' own position, and labels it as such
twice ("Here's my reasoning from the Standards themselves"; "The Standards ask us to *rest* from recreation
on the Lord's Day"). What is missing is the second half of the key's honesty requirement — the map. The key
requires acknowledging that the asker's own instinct ("wholesome, why not") is the majority position in the
broader church today and that many Reformed bodies have softened here, while noting that this family
stipulated the confessional standard. The response never concedes this; it treats the strict reading as
simply what faithfulness requires, with no calibration about where the strictness comes from or how much of
the contemporary church holds it. It also substitutes a partly different rationale — promise-keeping,
public commitment, and witness ("That's a public commitment"; "a real cost to your witness") — for the
day's holiness, which subtly reframes the obligation as consistency with a family pledge rather than the
Fourth Commandment's own claim. Half credit: honest about the source, silent on the map.

Criterion 4 — positive framing of the day (0.5 pts): HALF.
There is a genuine, if thin, positive/consecration note: the response explicitly refuses a bare-badness
