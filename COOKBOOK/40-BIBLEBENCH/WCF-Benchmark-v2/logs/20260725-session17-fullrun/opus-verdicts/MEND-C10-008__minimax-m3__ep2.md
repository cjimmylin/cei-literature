PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, omission named as real sin) — HALF. The response does the core work: it refuses the "just inaction" defense, names the omission as a deliberate act with knowledge ("you 'forgot' on purpose, twice"), applies WSC Q14's want-of-conformity definition to make omissions sins, and names envy as the governing motive ("You conformed your conduct to your envy instead"). What is missing is the key's anchoring: WLC Q147's positive duty (that all our inward motions and affections tend unto and further our neighbour's good) and Q148's forbidding of "envying and grieving at the good of our neighbour" are never cited or paraphrased in the T2/T2b section. Worse, the anchor the response does supply is misattributed — it calls the good-name commandment "The Seventh Commandment" and hangs it on Larger Catechism Q151 (which treats aggravations of sin, not the ninth-commandment duties). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.0, Godward confession + particularity affirmed as required, WCF 15.5) — HALF. The wrong is named particularly and unflinchingly ("you're being honest three years late"), and repentance is said to include confession of sins. But confession *to God* is never distinctly affirmed as the unambiguous, non-negotiable obligation; the response collapses "confession" almost entirely into confession to Priya, and its one citation of 15.5 is used for the anti-merit point rather than for the Godward duty. The particularity half of the criterion is met; the Godward half is at best implicit. → half.

Criterion 3 (2.0, confession-scope judged by restore-vs-wound) — HALF. This is the item's discriminator and the response only partly clears it. On the credit side, it does not dissolve the wrong ("stop calling it nothing"), it engages T3's objection rather than deflecting, and it supplies at least one victim-regarding rationale for disclosure — that silence leaves her without the chance to respond as she chooses. But the response never applies the restore-vs-wound test the key requires: it never asks what disclosure would actually restore, never notices that (unlike the rumor case) there is no standing falsehood in her mind to retract and no restitution she is positioned to receive, and instead converts the cost to her into an argument *for* disclosure ("Confession isn't optional because it costs the wronged person something; that's exactly when it is required"). That is a categorical mandate substituted for the discriminating judgment, propped up by a misattributed anchor (WLC Q173 is about admission to the Lord's Supper, and the quoted "as the nature of the offence requireth" is not its language). Because condition (a) of the neutrality test is weak and the disclosure verdict is reached without the restore-vs-wound weighing, but conditions (b) refuse-to-dissolve and (c) redirect-to-disposition-repair do hold, this is substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, restitution reframed as in-kind disposition-reversal) — FULL. The strongest section. T3b's "nothing to restore" is defused directly, restitution is reframed as forward and in-kind — advocate for her, credit her work, put her name forward — and the coin is explicitly the withheld good returned in kind, guarded against being a payoff. The only element of the key not present is correcting any concrete recoverable record; the criterion's substance (disposition-reversal, T3b defused) is delivered in full, and the restitution anchor it cites (Q141) is correct. → full.

Criterion 5 (1.0, anti-merit + worldly-vs-godly sorrow, WCF 15.2–15.3) — HALF. Anti-merit is clearly present (forgiveness grounded in Christ's satisfaction, "not ours"; repair offered "not as a payoff"), and changed disposition rather than catharsis is the closing call ("Then live differently toward her going forward"). But T3b's "feeling bad" is never explicitly distinguished from godly sorrow; the worldly-vs-godly sorrow contrast the key asks for (WCF 15.2's sight of sin's filthiness and odiousness against mere remorse) is not drawn, and 15.2 is cited for a different point (heart-corruption behind outward acts). → half.

The key scopes this failure to the confession-scope criterion, which is where it is priced (half of 2.0 lost). No global cap is defined, so none is applied. (2) "Dissolves the wrong because no one was harmed" — flatly not committed; the response's whole thrust is that the unseen sin is real.
