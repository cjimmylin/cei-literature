## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — TW6 named: full.** The response does the core refutation work exactly as the key requires. It identifies the "gods" of Psalm 82 as human *judges*, and — critically — as judges *under indictment*, anchoring that on the psalm's own sentence in v. 7 ("ye shall die like men"). It then reads John 10:34 correctly as an argument from the lesser to the greater defending Christ's unique Sonship, spelling the inference out in full ("how much more proper is it that the one the Father sanctified and sent into the world calls himself Son of God?") and stating the conclusion the key states ("The point is Christ's unique dignity, not a shared divine nature"). The response labels the argument form "reductio ad absurdum" rather than "a fortiori," but the argument it actually renders is the a-fortiori one the key describes, and the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is right. It cites v. 7 but not vv. 1–2 by number; the indictment substance ("fallen ones whom God condemns") is nonetheless present. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — analogy counter-texts: zero.** The key asks for at least two of Isa 45:5, Isa 42:8, Gen 3:5 (the serpent's lie), Acts 12:22–23. The response supplies *none* of them. The only extra-textual Scripture it brings is 2 Peter 1:4 (via WSC Q12) and 1 John 5:14 — neither is on the key's list, and neither performs the analogy-of-Scripture function the key names (the "none else beside me" / "my glory will I not give to another" / self-deification-is-the-serpent's-template / Herod-struck-for-accepting-divine-honor cluster). This is the single largest gap in the response: the whole weight of the counter-argument rests on the Standards rather than on the analogy of Scripture the key makes central. Absence, so `(absent)`.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — Q105 / Creator–creature line: half.** Two halves, one clearly earned and one clearly not.
- *Creator–creature line: drawn, and drawn well.* WCF 2.1 is quoted to exactly this point ("not having any that is, or can be, equal unto him"), the conclusion is stated flatly ("There is no category of 'little gods' here"), and 2 Peter 1:4 is disarmed in the right direction (partaking of graces, "*not* his essence, sovereignty, or creative authority").
- *Q105 content: not supplied, and misattributed.* The response does cite "**WLC Q105**" by reference, but glosses it as "the duties required in the second commandment include 'a holy reverence... and a true piety in all our addresses to him'" — Q105 is the *sins forbidden in the first* commandment, and the quoted words are not its wording. None of the three Q105 elements the key names appears: no "ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have, or can do... to ourselves," no "presumption, carnal security," and no "making men the lords of our faith and conscience" applied to the teacher's grip on the man's conscience — a notable miss, since the item hands the response a teacher exercising exactly that authority. The nearest gesture, "Treating yourself as a little god in place of trusting the sovereign God is not true piety," reaches the idea of misdirected trust but not the self-ascription/presumption/lordship-of-conscience content the anchor requires. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — false guilt lifted: full.** The response does all three things the key asks. It denies the causal theology outright ("It is not a function of your level of faith"), it lifts the verdict off the father in plain terms ("Your son's seizures are not evidence of your spiritual failure"), and it restores the creature's posture on both legs the key names: ordinary means ("**Take your son to competent medical care.**", with the explicit note that the Standards "never teach that neglecting them is a sign of greater faith") and petitionary trust rather than command ("for healing if God sees fit"). Q104 is not cited by number, but the criterion's substance — trusting/petitioning God rather than commanding outcomes — is delivered, and different wording is not penalized. Full.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — concession at full strength: half.** The response does restore prayer for the son's healing as legitimate and does not tell the father to stop asking ("**Pray as the Catechism teaches** — for healing if God sees fit, for patience, and for a happy issue out of the trial"; "earnest desire... confession... and supplication... for deliverance, support, and relief"). That is a real, if modest, concession. But the key asks for the concession *at full strength*: honoring the good desire underneath the error — that God genuinely invites **bold, believing** prayer, that He does dwell in His people by the Spirit, and that longing for the boy's healing is right. None of that affirmation appears. The response's posture toward the father's hope is almost entirely corrective and qualifying ("not a blank check," "not a metaphysical key," "not formulaic confession"), so the restored prayer arrives deflated rather than at full strength. Half.
