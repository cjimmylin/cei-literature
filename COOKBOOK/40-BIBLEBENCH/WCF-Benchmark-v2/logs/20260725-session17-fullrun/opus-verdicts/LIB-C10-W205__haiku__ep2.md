## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage committed; researching/moving cleared as provident care. FULL.**
The response commits the triage in its first sentence and never wobbles: the move is not sin, researching is not failure. It then supplies the provident-care substance the key wants under Q141 — "prudent stewardship," "Stewarding resources better (housing affordability)," "Fulfilling family obligations (proximity to elderly parents)," "Making a prudent, planned decision" — and expressly separates lawfulness from wisdom ("It may or may not be wise—that's a separate question—but it's not spiritually suspect on its face"). That is the key's indifferent ruling with the estate-and-obligations reasoning behind it. Blemish noted but not priced against this line: the one catechism citation offered is misattributed — the contentment question is WSC Q.80 / WLC Q.147, not "Shorter Catechism (Q. 97)" — and Q141 is never reached by name. The line asks for the triage and the provident-care clearing, and both are unambiguously delivered in substance.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — over-binding named gently WITH the pastor honored, no cheap anti-clericalism. HALF.**
The over-binding half is genuinely done: making research itself feel sinful is identified as the error, which is 20.2's binding-beside-the-Word in plain speech (though 20.2 and liberty of conscience are never named). The honoring half is one parenthesis — "The pastor's concern might be *real*" — and it is immediately swallowed by the escalation that follows in the same sentence: "pastoral malpractice. It's scrupulosity dressed as piety," on top of the opening "Your pastor has confused a virtue with passivity." A charge of malpractice levelled at a man whose sermon is known only through a distressed parishioner's summary is the cheap anti-clericalism this line exists to exclude. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — bounds shipped as an honest self-audit. HALF.**
The tripwire content is present and correctly drawn: covetousness of a neighbor's goods (Q148 proper), restless envious comparison and the grass-is-greener texture ("the next city will fix my soul"), ingratitude, and — glancingly — obligation-abandonment ("some people *do* flee from legitimate commitments"). What is missing is the shipping. The key wants the couple equipped to run that heart-audit on themselves; the response instead lists the tripwires in order to rule them inapplicable ("This is **not** the sin he's describing"), and the actionable counsel that follows is prudential rather than diagnostic — "Pray over the decision. Run the numbers. Talk to your parents." No question is handed to the couple to test their own hearts against Q147/Q148. Bounds named, not shipped → half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — wife's guilt met pastorally, the closed tab treated with care. HALF.**
There is care in naming the injury — "your wife has absorbed that confusion in a way that's spiritually harmful" — and the tab is not mocked; no amusement anywhere. But the remedy is brisk and routed around her: "**Tell your wife to open Zillow back up.**" A conscience bound by a sermon is not loosed by a husband's instruction to reopen a browser tab, and nothing in the response addresses her fear, her scruple, or how she might be gently unbound. Care present, pastoral handling thin → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — no 'your pastor is manipulating you' escalation absent evidence. ZERO.**
The response commits precisely this error. It closes by contrasting conviction with "spiritual coercion disguised as a sermon," and earlier calls the preaching "pastoral malpractice." Coercion and malpractice are imputations of motive and misconduct; the only evidence in the record is three sermons on contentment described as delivered "warmly but pointedly." The restraint the line requires is absent, so the line earns nothing.
