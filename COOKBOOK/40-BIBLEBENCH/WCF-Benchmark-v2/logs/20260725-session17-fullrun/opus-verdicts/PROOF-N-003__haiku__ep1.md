PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — Member A freed (UNBIND), 1.5 pts, HALF.
The response does the substantive unbinding: it completes the contentment clause with the charitable-frame-toward-the-neighbour half, draws the correct inference that the commandment governs disposition toward the neighbour and his goods rather than the presence of ambition ("You've wrongly conflated ambition *itself* with the sin of coveting *what belongs to another*"), and rules that expanding one's own shop is lawful desire, not the sin. That is the R2 "accurate paraphrase of the neighbor-directed content" and it earns real credit.

It is incomplete on two counts. First, the anchor is misattributed throughout: the tenth-commandment material is cited as "WLC Q141" and "WLC Q142," which are the eighth-commandment duties and sins; the key's anchors are Q147/Q148. Second, the wording presented as an exact quotation does not match the authoritative text supplied in the key — Q147 reads "as that all our inward motions and affections touching him, tend unto, and further all that good which is his," not "as that all our desires and affections concerning worldly matters or our own good may be regulated," and the "lawful and charitable desires and endeavours, after the good of ourselves and others" attributed to Q142 is not the Standards' wording. The key's lawful-diligence grounding (Q141's "a lawful calling, and diligence in it") is never actually reached; the freeing is done off an invented clause instead. The stem expressly demanded the reference and the exact wording, and the key supplies authoritative text "for verifying exact-wording criteria," so a right conclusion resting on a wrong question number and a fabricated quotation is substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half.

Criterion 2 — Member B called to repentance (AFFIRM), 1.5 pts, HALF.
The identification is correct and specific: the bitterness, the "doesn't deserve it," and the slander-to-poach are named as an envious and spiteful frame of spirit, the "healthy competitive fire" framing is explicitly rejected, and the response goes beyond the bare word "covetousness" by adding that "he's actively working to deprive his neighbor of what the neighbor possesses" and that the bad-mouthing is false witness touching the ninth commandment. It closes by directing the coach to have Member B "confess the bitterness and the slander." That satisfies R2's envy-at-the-neighbour's-good content.

The same anchoring defect applies and is not cosmetic here: "discontents himself with his own lot" and "all inordinate motions and affections to ourselves, to our own good; all envious, spiteful, prodigal, wasteful frame of spirit" are both attributed to "WLC Q142" and neither is the wording of Q148 ("discontentment with our own estate; envying and grieving at the good of our neighbor, together with all inordinate motions and affections to anything that is his"). The second string is a garbled composite that a coach checking the text could not find. Right sin, right verdict, unverifiable proof-text. Half.

Criterion 4 — double guard held, 1.0 pts, HALF.
The A-side guard is present: freeing Member A is conditioned on a motive examination for envy of the neighbour's market share and success, so the response does not hand him a blank cheque for identity-in-wealth striving. But that guard is thin — the restlessness and financial dissatisfaction the coach actually described are deflected to "prudential concerns (the loan burden, market timing)" rather than named as a live discontentment danger under the commandment's forbidden-sins clause. The B-side guard is weaker still: the response narrows B's sin to the bitterness and the slander rather than to competing, but it never affirms that Member B may lawfully seek to win business honestly. The guard is implicit in the scoping, not stated. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half.

Full.
