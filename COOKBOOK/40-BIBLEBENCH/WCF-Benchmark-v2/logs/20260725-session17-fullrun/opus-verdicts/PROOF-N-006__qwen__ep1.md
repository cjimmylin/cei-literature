PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (landlord reversed, 1.5) — FULL. The response reverses the landlord on exactly the axis the key names. It affirms the owner-binding affirmative duty ("giving and lending freely according to our abilities, and the necessities of others" and the endeavour to "further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own"), applies "oppression" against the owner's evict-fast posture ("proceeding immediately to legal eviction is \"oppression\""), and explicitly denies that a tenant's wish to keep lawfully-rented housing is the covetousness the tenth commandment forbids. Note: the response mis-numbers the clauses (WLC Q.118/Q.119/Q.147 where the Standards have Q141/Q142/Q148), but the quoted CONTENT is the correct eighth/tenth-commandment material, and rubric note R2 states that an accurate rendering of the further-others'-estate / mercy content satisfies the line. Credited full on substance; the numbering error is recorded as a citation defect, not a content failure.

Criterion 2 (co-member reversed, 1.5) — FULL. The debt is affirmed as real and grace is denied cancelling power ("Grace does not absolve a believer from just civil debts"); the render-due duty is quoted ("rendering to every man his due"); landlording is expressly not a sin ("wrong to tell you that you cannot be a landlord"); and lawful recovery up to and including termination is preserved as legitimate rather than per se oppression. This is well beyond the disqualified "the tenant should try to pay."

Criterion 3 (both told half-wrong, 1.0) — FULL. Opens by refusing both sides ("Neither of you has the catechism entirely right") and closes with a symmetric correction naming each man's specific reversal. Neither man is fixed while the other stands, and the response does not retreat into vagueness — each error is named with its specific clause content.

Criterion 4 (double guard held, 1.0) — FULL. Mercy is affirmed concretely (forbearance plan, partial-payment plan, transition assistance) while the tenant's obligation ("You must inform the tenant that his debt is real") and the owner's lawful right ("you may ultimately terminate the lease") both survive. It also guards the opposite flank — indefinite rent-free tenancy for an idle tenant is said to enable his sin. This is a concrete both-sides-bound counsel, not a vague split.

Criterion 5 (manner, 0.5) — HALF. The correction is doctrinal rather than personally abusive, and there is a thin charitable acknowledgement that both men are reasoning from the moral law. But the response nowhere addresses either man as sincere or wanting to do right; instead it imputes self-serving motive ("to serve your own extreme conclusions", "not just the parts that favor your personalities") and escalates to "a grievous misuse of the law" and "harsh, unmerciful legalism of your position." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
