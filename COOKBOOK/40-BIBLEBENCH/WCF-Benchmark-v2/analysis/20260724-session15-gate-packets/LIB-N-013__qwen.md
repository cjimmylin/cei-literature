[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
Elders, I give you the following counsel based strictly upon the Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Larger Catechism (WLC). You are caught between two errors that stem from the same root: a failure to properly distinguish between the **elements** of worship (which must be positively instituted by God) and the **circumstances** of worship (which are ordered by Christian prudence and the light of nature). 

Here is your ruling on both practices.

### 1. The Elder’s Proposal: Banning the Stream
**The Ruling:** The session must **reject** the elder’s demand to abolish the stream. 

**The Reasoning:** The elder is arguing that a camera or screen is a "worldly device" that inherently corrupts the purity of worship. This violates the doctrine of Christian liberty and misunderstands the Regulative Principle of Worship as articulated in our Standards.

*   **Christian Liberty (WCF 20.2):** The Confession states that "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to his word; or beside it, in matters of faith, or worship." To demand that the church strip away a technological tool for transmitting the Word, on the assumption that its mere presence corrupts worship, is to bind the conscience of the congregation to a human commandment "beside" the Word of God. 
*   **Elements vs. Circumstances (WCF 21.5):** The Confession outlines the "ordinary religious worship of God" as the reading and preaching of the Word, singing of psalms, and the administration and right receiving of the sacraments. A camera, the internet, and a television screen are not *elements* of worship. They are technological *means* or *circumstances* used to transmit the actual elements (the preaching of the Word). 
*   **Application:** God has not forbidden the use of instruments to amplify or transmit His Word. To claim that a screen inherently "corrupts" worship is to add a negative requirement to the worship of God that He has not instituted, effectively turning a lawful circumstance into a matter of divine command. The elder is guilty of adding to the elements of worship by demanding a specific technological absence. The stream must remain.

### 2. The Pastor’s Proposal: A "Virtual" Lord’s Supper
**The Ruling:** The session must **reject** the pastor’s proposal to administer the Lord's Supper to online viewers at home.

**The Reasoning:** The pastor argues that the internet is merely a "circumstance" of the sacrament, no different than using LED bulbs instead of candles. This is a fundamental misunderstanding of what a circumstance is, and it unlawfully alters the very substance of the sacrament instituted by Christ.

*   **The Nature of a Circumstance:** In Reformed theology, a "circumstance" is an accessory to an action that does not change the *substance* or *essence* of the thing itself. The type of lighting in the room does not change the substance of the preaching. However, changing a physical, gathered administration of bread and wine into a digital, solitary consumption of elements while watching a screen *does* change the substance of the action. The medium of a camera does not merely transmit the sacrament; it alters the nature of the sacrament from a corporate, physical reality into a virtual, individualized simulation.
*   **The Sacraments as "Visible" Signs (WCF 27.1):** The Confession defines sacraments as "holy **signs** and seals of the covenant of grace... to put a **visible** difference between those that belong unto the Church and the rest of the world." A sacrament requires a visible, tangible administration and a visible, tangible reception. The physical lifting, breaking, and distribution of the elements, and the physical eating and drinking by the gathered body, are intrinsic to the sign. A digital screen cannot convey a visible, physical sign in the way Christ instituted it.
*   **Communion "With Each Other" (WCF 29.1):** The Confession explicitly states that the purpose of the Supper is to be "a bond and pledge of their communion with him, **and with each other, as members of his mystical body.**" The very name of the ordinance is a *communion*. The Larger Catechism (WLC 170) defines it by the "giving and receiving bread and wine." A virtual supper destroys this physical communion. Viewers in their living rooms are not participating in a corporate meal with the gathered body; they are eating a meal alone while watching others do the same. 
*   **The Condemnation of Private Reception (WCF 29.4):** The Confession explicitly condemns "Private masses, or receiving this sacrament by a priest, or any other, **alone**; as the people ought not." The framers of our Standards understood that the Supper is inherently a gathered, corporate act. Receiving the elements in isolation, even while watching a video of a pastor, is a form of private reception that the Standards forbid. 
*   **Application:** The pastor cannot take a physical, gathered, communal ordinance, digitize it, and claim the medium is just a "circumstance." He is altering the substance of the sacrament, which is strictly forbidden by the Regulative Principle (WCF 1.6).

### Summary Counsel to the Session

You have a duty to shepherd the flock according to the pure rules of Christ's house. 

1.  **To the Elder:** You must repent of adding to the worship of God. The stream is a lawful circumstance. Banning it burdens the consciences of the shut-ins who receive great comfort and spiritual nourishment through the preaching of the Word (WCF 21.5). Keep the stream running.
2.  **To the Pastor:** You must repent of altering the institutions of Christ. The medium of the internet changes the substance of the Lord's Supper. You cannot confer a sacrament through a digital simulacrum. 
3.  **To the Session:** Continue streaming the service for the shut-ins. Instruct the online viewers to hear the Word preached and to receive it by faith. However, instruct them that when the Lord's Supper is administered, they must abstain from eating the bread and drinking the juice at home. They are to watch, pray, and spiritually feed upon Christ by faith, but they must wait to physically receive the visible signs of the sacrament until they can be physically gathered with the body of Christ at the Table. 

Stand firm on the Standards. The elder's rigid traditionalism and the pastor's progressive pragmatism are both errors; the middle path is the pure, confessional Reformed way.
