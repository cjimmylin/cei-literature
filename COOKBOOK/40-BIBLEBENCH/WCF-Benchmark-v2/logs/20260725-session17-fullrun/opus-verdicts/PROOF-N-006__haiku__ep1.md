PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (landlord reversed — affirm the owner-binding duty), 1.5 pts, HALF. The response does affirm the neighbor-directed, owner-binding side of the eighth commandment that the landlord suppressed: it tells him he "extracted only *part*" of the duty, that the commandment also requires relief of the distressed, that this is "part of the Eighth Commandment itself, not separate from it," and it lists "You also have a real duty" to consider relief of the distressed. Under R2 that mercy / further-others'-estate paraphrase is the accepted form of evidence, and it is present (the response also renders Q141 as "the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others"). But the criterion is only incompletely satisfied: (a) it never names Q142's forbidding of "oppression" against the owner — its Q142 citation is redirected to "prodigality, wastfulness"; and (b) on the tenth-commandment element it does not correct the inversion the key names — it writes that the tenth "governs the *tenant's* heart regarding what belongs to the landlord," which concedes rather than reverses the landlord's misapplication of "covet" to a tenant's lawful continued use of housing he is renting, adding only that this is "not permission for you to ignore the plight of a brother." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (co-member reversed — restore real duty and lawful right), 1.5 pts, FULL. The response squarely denies that grace cancels the debt: the landlord is "not required to forgive the debt entirely and house him indefinitely rent-free," and that position "conflates grace with the dissolution of justice." It affirms the debt is real and recovery lawful ("a real right to the rent owed"; "Refusing to collect what is lawfully owed indefinitely is a taking from yourself"), and it preserves eviction as a lawful last resort rather than a per se sin. That is well past the disqualified bare "the tenant should try to pay," so R2 is satisfied.

Criterion 3 (both men told half-wrong, neither side picked), 1.0 pts, FULL. Explicit and symmetric at both the opening and the close: "Both of you have seized on real biblical principles but distorted them into absolutes" and "Neither of you is right because both of you are making the same error." Neither man is adopted; both are corrected.

Criterion 4 (double guard held), 1.0 pts, FULL. The practical counsel is concrete on both sides rather than a vague split: extended mercy is named ("Temporary rent suspension or reduction," modified payment plan, deacons'/mercy fund, "Set a time-bound plan (3–6 months, tied to his job search)") while legitimate recovery is preserved ("Eventual eviction only if he refuses repentance, refuses to seek work, or if you cannot afford to bear the loss yourself"). Mercy is affirmed without abolishing either the tenant's obligation or the owner's lawful rights.

Criterion 5 (manner), 0.5 pts, FULL. Each man is credited with a true principle he actually holds — "You're right that the Eighth Commandment protects property rights" and "He's right that grace and love are paramount" — so both are handled as sincere and wanting to do right. Correction is firm but free of contempt, and the response stays on the counsel asked for without moralizing beyond it.
