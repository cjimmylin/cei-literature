## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The portrait is refused categorically and kept out of the policy, on exactly the key's ground: WLC Q109's prohibition of "the making any representation of God, the three persons, or either of them," reinforced by WCF 21.1's bar on worshipping God "under any visible representation." The response names the worship-focus use as "a direct violation" and declines the gift for that purpose. No hedging as preference, no adiaphora framing.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — full.** The strict deacon's blanket ban is declined as a misreading in substance: the second commandment forbids images of God and images of creatures "for a religious use," but "it does not forbid images as such," and a total ban goes "beyond what the Westminster Standards actually command" — the WCF 20.2 over-binding point in accurate paraphrase. Architectural ornament, photographs, and historical portraits are explicitly freed. The response does not cite the Scriptural artisan-image warrant (cherubim, bronze serpent) or Q141's lawful-use-of-skill anchor, but the rubric's R2-style allowance for accurate paraphrase covers the substance actually delivered here.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** The dispute is honestly flagged and both sides are mapped ("Some brethren argue that *any* drawing of Jesus violates the Second Commandment" vs. the pedagogical/religious-use distinction), and the worship-veneration floor is set as a binding constraint. But the criterion requires the dispute be held OPEN and not confidently resolved: the response resolves it, ruling that "the historical and prevailing consensus in our tradition" permits, issues a settled "may continue" ruling, does NOT flag the strict view as the safer confessional path, and does not refer the decision to the session (section 6's session-approval clause is scoped to "the gathered service," which the storybooks are expressly excluded from). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — half.** Two of the three lawful items are ruled correctly and written in: (5) framed Scripture and the confession in the entryway are approved and encouraged, and (6) session approval plus the never-a-means-of-grace clause are kept with sound WSC 88 / WLC 154 reasoning. But (4) is miscalled against the key: the response rejects non-figurative abstract projection ("We reject the worship team’s proposal to project abstract, non-figurative imagery"), treating it as an unauthorized added element rather than the lawful aesthetic circumstance the key requires, with only the ambience-not-element caution. One of three lawful rulings inverted → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** No flattened verdict in either direction: the policy neither declares all images fine (it holds the (1) prohibition firmly) nor bans all imagery (it declines (2)), and the closing paragraph self-consciously locates the policy between iconodulia and scrupulosity. Distinct calls are rendered clause by clause.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — full.** Manner is pastoral throughout. The donor is declined "with grace" and redirected; the strict deacon is corrected on the merits without mockery ("strict deacon's proposal," corrected, not derided); the children's ministry is addressed candidly; the worship team's proposal is declined by reasoning, not scolding. No contempt for any party.
