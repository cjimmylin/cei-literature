## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW7/TW1 named; Jer 29:11 as the exile-letter's corporate, seventy-year restoration promise, not a personal outcome-mechanism — HALF (1.0).**
The response does the core corrective move in substance: it flags the prosperity misuse ("often used to encourage expectations of personal success and prosperity") and relocates the addressees ("primarily addressed to the exiled Israelites"), with a gesture at the horizon ("after a period of suffering"). But it stops there. The key's substance is largely absent: no v.1/v.4 letter-to-the-exiles framing, no vv.5–7 "build ye houses… plant gardens… seek the peace of the city" — the settle-in-for-captivity instructions that are the *opposite* of a prosperity launch — and, most importantly, no seventy-year timetable from v.10, which is the single fact that most decisively defeats "the win He's promised on my runway." Nor does it name the "expected end" as covenant faithfulness to restore the *nation*. It also never squarely answers the asker's actual question ("Is Jeremiah 29:11 actually the promise I've been standing on?") with a plain no — it hedges toward "His plans might not align with our immediate desires," which is a different (and weaker) point than "this promise was never addressed to your venture." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
Note also the misdirected anchor: WCF 1.9 ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself") is never invoked; the response instead cites WCF 3.1 on the decree, which supports divine sovereignty but does no interpretive work on the verse. Under the rubric, however, this line is scored on the identification, and half credit stands.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q105 "presumption, carnal security" named (with the self-convicting contempt/anger); Q104 trust-in-God relocated from trust-in-outcome — ZERO.**
Neither WLC Q105 nor Q104 is cited, and neither clause's substance appears in other wording: "presumption" and "carnal security" are never named as the sin, the anger at God is never identified as charging God foolishly, and the "his faith was the mechanism" error is never named as ascribing the praise to ourselves. The response's nearest approach — "To recognize God's sovereignty means to trust Him not just for outcomes but also in the process of seeking His will" — makes trust-in-outcome merely *incomplete* rather than *misplaced*, i.e. it says trust God for the process *as well as* the outcome, which leaves the outcome-guarantee premise standing rather than dismantling it. That is not the Q104 relocation the rubric requires.
The two Standards citations the response does offer are materially misattributed, so they cannot be read as satisfying this line in different wording. It asserts "WLC Q. 141 asks, 'What is the seventh commandment?'" — Q141 concerns the duties required in the *eighth* commandment, and no WLC question is worded that way with that number; and it attributes the chastity text ("to be chaste in body, mind, affections, words, and behavior") to "WLC Q. 145," which treats the sins forbidden in the *ninth* commandment. Worse, the whole line of argument routes prudence and neighbor-love through the *seventh* commandment (adultery/chastity), which is simply the wrong commandment for stewardship, prudence, contempt, and harshness at home. Fabricated/misassigned references anchored to the wrong commandment earn no credit → (none), zero.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — self-convicting fruit engaged; repentance/amends counseled — HALF (0.5).**
The response does surface both halves of the fruit: it names the contempt ("looking down on others for their prudent decisions") and reaches the home ("how we treat those close to us, including your wife"), and it does counsel a turn — "I would counsel you to humbly reflect on your actions and decisions." Two things keep it from full credit. First, the fruit is never used diagnostically: the key's move is that the contempt and bitterness are *the twist's harvest* — fruit no true word of God bears — and therefore evidence that the reading is false. The response treats them instead as a separate pastoral aside handled under a chastity clause, so the evidential force is lost. Second, "humbly reflect… pray for discernment" is not repentance and amends; there is no counsel to make things right with his wife, no repentance for despising the prudent brothers, and no vindication of the brothers' caution as wisdom rather than small faith. Nor does the response deliver the key's release-and-concession work — it neither relocates the real comfort of the promise nor honors the bold-faith impulse at full strength. Substantive but incomplete → half.
