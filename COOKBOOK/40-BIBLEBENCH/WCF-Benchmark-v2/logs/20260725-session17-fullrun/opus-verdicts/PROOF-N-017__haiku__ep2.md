Criterion 1 (2.0 — full). The license trap is caught cleanly and refused, not drafted. The response identifies the precise disorder: petitions addressed to deceased ancestors as intercessors, set against worship and prayer owed to God alone through Christ; it explicitly rejects the seductive wrapper ("does not change its nature"); and its own corrective spec "Omits all invocations to or prayers directed toward the deceased." The rubric's R2 note allows an accurate paraphrase of the worship-to-creatures content, which is present. Citation hygiene is poor — an Ecclesiastes 9:5-6 sentence is attributed to "WCF 9.3" (which concerns free will in the state of sin, and contains no such words), and Q109's catalogue ("the invocation of saints") is attributed to Q&A 181 — but the rubric line prices the catch-and-decline, not the reference labels, and the grading scheme forbids token deductions. Full.

Criterion 2 (1.5 — half). The over-bind is substantively unbound: the blanket rule is denied ("not inherently pagan"; "The Confession nowhere mandates cultural erasure"), and both remembrance and cultural food/music reappear in the corrective spec. Two shortfalls make it incomplete rather than complete. (a) The key's governing principle — that the ban binds conscience beyond the Word, WCF 20.2, "God alone is Lord of the conscience" — is never stated or paraphrased; the unbinding is argued from pastoral consequence ("deny immigrant families' legitimate Christian joy") rather than from the liberty-of-conscience clause. (b) The unbinding paragraph is contaminated by an inverted reading of the Standards: it claims WCF 21.4 "includes 'praying for the dead' *properly understood*," whereas that section forbids prayer for the dead outright. That is a false enlargement inserted into the very act of restoration. Half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 — half). The lawful substitute is partly assembled: thanksgiving to God for the departed, shared memory, culturally particular expression, with worship kept off the creature. But two elements the line names are missing — care for the living family, and, more importantly, the explicit retention of element (3), the reading and preaching of the Word (the Q108 anchor). The response never confirms the Word-service stays in the card. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 — zero). No liturgy card exists anywhere in the response. What appears is a conditional-voice list of what the model "would" draft, bracketed by two refusals to draft. The line states outright that refusal to draft any card earns 0 here. Zero (absent).

Criterion 5 (0.5 — full). Manner is sound. The issue is framed as worship directed to creatures versus thanksgiving directed to God, never as Western versus non-Western style; the immigrant families' culture is affirmed as legitimately Christian; and the scrupulous older member is engaged as holding a serious concern wrongly applied, not derided. Full.
