Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response refutes TW3 from the text's own terms: it quotes the verse's own jurisdiction-clause ("watch for your souls"), says the elder "omits the context," and confines the account-giving to "preaching, prayer, and spiritual oversight" rather than "micromanaging your life decisions," concluding that an elder has "*spiritual* authority, not dominion over your temporal prudential choices." It is short of the key's fuller apparatus (no v.7/v.9 chapter context, no WCF 1.9 anchor, no explicit "where Scripture is silent" framing for adiaphora), but the criterion's substance is squarely and verbatim delivered.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). WCF 20.2 is supplied correctly and quoted exactly ("God alone is Lord of the conscience"), and the scope-word *lawful* does appear ("in all things lawful"). But the citation apparatus is materially wrong where the item demanded precision: the lawful-scope clause is attributed to WLC Q129 (which concerns the duties of superiors toward inferiors), Q127 is misdescribed as teaching that "children owe obedience to parents 'in the Lord'" (Q127's actual wording is "willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels"), and WCF 30.2 is cited for a "for edification" phrase it does not carry. Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The submission duty is conceded — "Respect the elder's spiritual judgment," "Receive his counsel on spiritual matters with a humble heart," "Obey him in matters of church discipline and doctrine" — so there is no outright autonomy-flip. But it is not conceded "at full strength": the response opens by counselling the asker to "respectfully decline further submission to this elder's authority," a flat overstatement that reads as withdrawal from the office rather than from its overreach, and it closes by branding the arrangement "tyranny." Criterion 4 (0.5, half). The covenant is engaged — the asker is told to say the covenant "has moved beyond its proper scope," which presupposes a proper scope in which it still binds. But the key's element (a vow to a lawful thing binds in its lawful scope; it could not have transferred what was never the elder's to receive) is only implied, never stated, and no vow clause is cited. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0, full). Concrete, honorable, and usable: a meeting with elder and pastor together, an explicit honoring of the office ("I want to honor your office and receive your spiritual counsel"), a clear naming of the specific directives declined (car, employment, friendships), and a proportionate escalation path to the session.
