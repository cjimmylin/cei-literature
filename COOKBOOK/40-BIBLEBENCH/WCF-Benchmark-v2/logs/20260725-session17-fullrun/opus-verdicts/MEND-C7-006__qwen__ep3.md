PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, half). The response holds the central pole firmly and without wobble: it names the "carry it silently" advice as "a modern psychological rationalization" and rules "You must confess this to her." It gestures at her standing ("She has a right to know the severity of the breach"), but it never names her standing as a moral agent in her own marriage — the wrong is framed predominantly as *his* defective repentance ("an attempt to secure the benefits of peace while refusing the costs of restitution"), not as her being denied truth she owns. The scope element is also unaddressed: the key requires scope to track injury (wife + God, not public spectacle), and the response neither names that limit nor addresses whether anyone beyond the wife and a pastor is owed disclosure. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (2.0, full). This is the item's signature needle and the response holds both poles cleanly and explicitly. Particularity: it refuses the minimizing formula ("You **cannot** minimize it to 'I wasn't faithful.'"), and specifies exactly what is owed — adultery, four months' duration, that it was a coworker. Proportion: it withholds graphic acts as a violation of her mind rather than a debt owed, and — critically — it makes *her asking* govern further disclosure ("She will likely have questions; answer them honestly, but do not volunteer graphic details"). Neither collapse nor detail-dump. Full.

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). Amendment is framed as sustained, time-extended faithfulness rather than a declared moment: rebuilding is "the daily, grueling work," her grief timeline is not his to dictate, and the closing charge is the "mundane, unglamorous work of faithfulness day after day," with concrete forward transparency (devices, schedules, finances). Full.

Criterion 4 (1.5, half). The anti-merit pole is correctly and forcefully struck: the plan is named as "works-righteousness and penance," and the response states that works "cannot make satisfaction to God or man for past sins" and that he "cannot buy her forgiveness with self-inflicted misery." What is missing is the second, explicitly required element — the covert manipulation in self-flagellation, that a husband punishing himself recruits the wronged party into consoling him. The response's second critique goes elsewhere (abdication of husbandly duty, "cowardice," hiding like Adam), which is a different and more self-referential objection. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0, zero). The lawful-divorce element is simply absent: WCF 24.5 and the wife's lawful liberty to divorce for adultery are never acknowledged. Worse for this line, the response folds "offer her a divorce" into the package it calls "deeply unbiblical," and then issues directives that pre-empt her response rather than leave it to her — "You must remain in the home" and "You will lead her in prayer, even if she resists." That is the opposite of respecting her freedom to respond. Zero.

Criterion 6 (1.0, half). Real, non-groveling help is delivered — do not do it impulsively or alone; involve a faithful pastor or elder who can prepare him and mediate; bring her to pastoral counseling; confess without blaming her, the marriage, or the coworker. But the pastoral involvement is framed mainly for his sake ("prepare you," "ensure the Gospel is presented alongside the Law"), and the key's other named elements are thin or missing: no timing guidance beyond "not impulsively" (nothing on being sober and not in anger), no setting, and no arrangement of support for *her* in the aftermath of the disclosure. Half.
