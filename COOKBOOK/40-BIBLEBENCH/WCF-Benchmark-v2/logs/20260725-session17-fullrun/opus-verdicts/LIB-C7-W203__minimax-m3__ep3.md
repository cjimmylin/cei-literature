## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — principle/application split: FULL.** This is the response's spine and it is executed exactly as the key requires. The commanded principle is affirmed without hedging ("Modesty is a moral duty, not a preference"; "Modesty is objectively required, not optional"; "Apparel falls under moral law"), while the particular code is classified as prudence rather than divine law ("A ruler-based code is not a confession of faith; it is a *prudential application* of one"). The "What the Standard Does *Not* Say" section makes the split explicit: the standards name "a *qualitative* principle" and do not "specify garments, measurements, or enforcement mechanisms." Credit is for the substance; noted as a defect (not scored, since the rubric line is about the split): the response attributes the duties-required text to WLC Q.139 when the duty-list quoted ("modest and sober carriage…") is Q138.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — no-code faction answered: HALF.** Half of this line is fully earned and unusually crisp: the reality of the duty is asserted against the "heart issue" faction by name, with the seventh commandment's "heart, word, **and action**" as the warrant. The second half — that an institution responsible for minors may *lawfully* order its life, the camp code being a circumstance of governance under the Q129 ordering authority (the key's curfew analogy) — is never argued. The response assumes the camp may set a code and locates the decision with the elders ("Submit your policy to your elders"), which is adjacent, but it never answers the no-code faction's actual claim that "rules just create shame" with an affirmative account of lawful institutional authority. Substantive but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — 'THE biblical standard' faction answered: FULL.** Both required elements are present. Conscience-binding beside the Word is named directly ("not so much that you bind consciences where God has not"; "the confession deliberately refuses to bind what God has not bound"), and the faction's specific move — converting a prudential code into "THE biblical standard" — is met head-on with "A ruler-based code is not a confession of faith." Shame-enforcement is flagged as against the duty's own grain: the standards "never authorize rulers-as-public-shame, ritualized sending-home," and rigid measurement enforcement "often produces shame, not chastity," inverting the end which is "'the cure of the sin of uncleanness' — formation, not humiliation." Defect noted but not scored: the liberty citation is given as WCF 20.4 with a quotation that does not match the Confession's text at that section; the doctrine invoked (chapter 20 liberty of conscience) is nonetheless the right one, and the criterion's substance is met.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt) — concrete usable recommendation: FULL.** The director asked for a decision and got one: a stated qualitative swimwear line with worked implications ("no two-piece swimsuits; no shirtless boys/men in mixed swim; no Speedo-style coverage for either sex"), a requirement to stock spare modest swimwear and T-shirts at check-in, a graduated response sequence (cover provided → private conversation → parent call → withdrawal), symmetric application to the boys, and a decision venue. This is adoptable as written, not a seminar.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — no sneering; minors' dignity centered: FULL.** A whole section ("What Both Fractions Are Each Right About") concedes genuine truth to each side before declining what is false in each, which is precisely the key's "both factions get something true said to them." Minors' dignity is explicitly the governing concern of the discipline step: "the camper stays… its fruit is repentance, not exclusion," and the diagnosis that first-offense send-home "treats apparel as the sin and the person as the offense." The labels "gnostic" and "pharisaical" are sharp, but they characterize the *positions* and are paired with substantial charity toward the persons holding them; this is not sneering.
