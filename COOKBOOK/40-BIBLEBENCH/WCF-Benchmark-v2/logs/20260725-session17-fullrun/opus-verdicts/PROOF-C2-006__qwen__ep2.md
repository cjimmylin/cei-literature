PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW4 named in substance: FULL. The response identifies the scope-evasion precisely and by its actual structure ("collapsing the Second Commandment into the First"; the claim that the second commandment "only forbids using images to worship those *false* gods"), states that the Standards reject that scoping, and then turns the asker's own datum against him: Aaron's Yahweh-directed intention "did not excuse the introduction of an unauthorized, visible representation," and the calf "stands as the biblical prototype for the exact error." That is exactly the key's move — the very text cited proves the prohibition reaches true-God images. The response does not cite WCF 1.9 by reference, but the rubric asks for the evasion named "in substance," which it is.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — refutation from Exod 32's own wording plus ≥1 counter-text: HALF. The Exodus half is substantively present but thin: the response asserts the calf was the archetypal second-commandment sin and that Aaron's intent did not excuse it, yet it never works off the passage's own wording — no "they have corrupted themselves" (Exod 32:7–8), no engagement with the text beyond restating the asker's citation of v. 5. The counter-text half is entirely absent: no Deut 4:15–18 ("ye saw no manner of similitude"), no Rom 1:23, no John 4:24, and indeed no Scripture citation of any kind supplied by the model. The refutation therefore rests almost wholly on confessional authority, which is the mode the key explicitly says is insufficient ("not bare authority"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q109 no-representation clause: FULL. The response quotes the operative clause verbatim, attributes it to WLC Q. 109, and applies it correctly to both the Trinity panels and the "Christ in Glory" bronze, noting the clause reaches "any of the three persons." WSC Q50 and WCF 21.1 are additionally supplied and accurately quoted, which reinforces (but is not required by) this line.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — representation-for-worship vs. lawful-art distinction: ZERO (absent). The response nowhere distinguishes images of God offered as objects/aids of worship from lawful religious art, illustration, or narrative depiction. The nearest statements run the other way — "The Standards do not recognize a category of 'reverent images' that bypasses the Second Commandment" and "Because the Second Commandment is binding on all ages and contexts" — which restate the prohibition without delimiting it. The correction is therefore not made precise: a worship-arts leader reading this is given no boundary between the two forbidden installations and the rest of his department's visual work. The omission does not amount to a positive over-bind (nothing here condemns stained glass or children's-Bible art, so no GATE fires), but the required distinction is simply not drawn, and an omission scores zero, not half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — "honest read" answered plainly, asker treated as sincere: FULL. The verdict is delivered in the first sentence without hedging, and the closing counsel repeats it unambiguously. The asker is treated as a sincere worship leader rather than an opponent — his aim is called "well-intentioned" and the response frames the error as a mistaken premise, not bad faith.
