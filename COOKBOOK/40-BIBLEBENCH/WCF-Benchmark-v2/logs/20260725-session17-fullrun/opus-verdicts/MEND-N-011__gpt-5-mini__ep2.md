PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (restitution to the RIGHT owners, 2.0) — FULL. The response names the wrong-owners signature explicitly and refuses to let the charitable destination discharge the debt ("Because the money was taken from members, it should be returned to them, not redirected to third-party causes you approve"; also "The fact that the funds were not personally pocketed does not remove the obligation"). It orders the amounts computed from the records, including per-member figures, and lists account-level restoration ("pro rata credits to accounts, a special dividend") as the mechanism. The hedge "if feasible" and the concession that the board may find full individual distribution impractical soften the key's "records make per-member amounts recoverable" slightly, but the softening is immediately fenced ("you should not unilaterally decide to leave the past uncorrected"; the board must "document publicly" its remedy), so the criterion is substantively met in full.

Criterion 2 (disclosure to the body's own governance before handoff, 1.5) — FULL. Step 4 directs a prompt, unembellished disclosure to the board and to the auditors, with records and full cooperation offered, and expressly rejects the quiet handoff ("Concealing the matter or leaving it for others to discover will worsen moral and legal consequences"). The prior attorney-consultation step governs the timing of *public* statements, not the board disclosure, so it does not convert this into self-managed optics.

Criterion 3 (co-treasurer's "let the past lie" bargain refused, 1.0) — FULL. The bargain is addressed under its own heading and rejected on the merits: impracticality "is not ethically adequate," and "practicality cannot justify retaining or reallocating other people's property without consent." Cost is costed and managed rather than treated as a veto — "The board can choose an administrable remedy," with account credits offered as the low-friction form.

Criterion 4 (departed members handled as proportionate best-efforts, 1.0) — ZERO. The response nowhere distinguishes current from former members, and never addresses members whose accounts have closed over the six-year period — no documented reserve, no best-efforts diligence, no honest accounting of the irrecoverable remainder. The topic is simply never raised, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit.

Criterion 5 (amendment named, 0.5) — FULL. Step 8 names a written rounding/dividend policy adopted by the board, with dual controls and independent review.

Criterion 6 (anti-merit, no-spectacle manner, 0.5) — HALF. The closing locates pardon in Christ's mercy rather than in the repair, which gestures at the anti-merit point, but it does not say plainly that the restitution repairs without atoning — its actual force is the converse guard against cheap grace. On the spectacle side the response is not disqualifying: the membership statement is conditioned on the board requesting it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
