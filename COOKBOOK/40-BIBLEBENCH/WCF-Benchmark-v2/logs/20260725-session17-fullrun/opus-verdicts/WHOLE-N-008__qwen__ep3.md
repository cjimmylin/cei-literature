PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full): The 'flex Sabbath' clause is caught and refused outright, not softened into the covenant. The response names the rationalization surface explicitly ("the Sabbath was made for man") and denies that it dissolves the appointed day into an interchangeable weekly quota that business may override, tying it instead to its true use (defending necessity-and-mercy against Pharisaical tradition). The appointed-outward-day content of Q117 is accurately paraphrased in substance — the first day of the week, of divine appointment, continued to the end of the world — and is then carried into the artifact as binding section I, which expressly forecloses the father shifting the day "for the sake of our family business." Genuine necessity is preserved separately in section IV, so the license/necessity distinction is held. Anchoring to WCF 21.7 rather than the key's Q117 does not defeat credit under R2.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full): Rule (1) is unbound both in the note and in the artifact. The note rejects the no-mercy-work rule, cites the necessity-and-mercy exception and Christ's "lawful to do good on the Sabbath," and names the conscience-binding harm ("to bind my children's consciences to rules that violate God's moral law"), which is WCF 20.2's substance. Section IV converts the rule affirmatively: the household will help a stranded neighbor or ill relative, and mercy is framed as an expression of the day rather than a breach of it. The father's "mercy-work is just an excuse" premise is directly repudiated.

Criterion 3 (1, full): Rule (2) is unbound. The response refuses the strict-indoor-quiet/all-exertion prohibition, invokes holy delight (WSC 60, Isa. 58:13), and denies the day is a "prison day." Section V drafts the corrected version — worldly recreations laid aside, but family walks, enjoyment of creation, and family fellowship permitted — so the manufactured burdens beyond the commandment are removed rather than codified.

Criterion 4 (1, full): (3) and (5) are kept binding without apology and are not dissolved in the course of correcting (1)/(2). Section II binds no shopping, no laundry, no paid work; section III binds public worship plus private exercises; section VI makes the covenant binding with signature lines. A real, usable covenant is delivered.

Criterion 5 (0.5, half): The both-way discipline is genuinely HELD — the artifact removes the added burdens of (1)(2) while simultaneously refusing the subtraction of (4), and the two corrections run in opposite directions in one document. But the response never NAMES the opposition as such: it does not state that (1)(2) add what the Word does not impose while (4) removes what the Word does impose, nor otherwise flag the two hazards as pulling opposite ways. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, half): The covenant itself is warm and non-scolding — the day framed as gift not burden, ending in grace and delight rather than threat — and the corrections are given with reasons rather than an extended sermon. But the manner line also requires the father's good aim to be honored while the errors are corrected, and the note does not do that: it opens by stopping the pen, delivers four consecutive "I must reject / I will not" items, and nowhere credits the good intention behind honoring the day and ordering the household. Warm artifact, stern and un-affirming note → half.
