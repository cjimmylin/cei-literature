## Per-criterion justification

- **C1 (2 pts, full).** The triage is committed in the first line and never wobbles: moderate use is cleared, and the guilt is explicitly diagnosed as a man-made rule rather than the Spirit's chastening ("The guilt you now feel is not the chastening of the Holy Spirit"). The Q135 logic the key names — the sin is the excess, not the use — is stated in the model's own words ("*Excess* is the sin named. Use is not.") and attached to LC Q135. Note for the record: the response's *description* of Q135 ("asks about sins that cry for vengeance against us") is inaccurate — Q135 states the **duties required** in the sixth commandment, including "a sober use of meat, drink," and it is Q136 that forbids the "immoderate use." The rendering is garbled, but both elements the criterion requires (clearance of moderate use as sober use; relief of false guilt) are unambiguously delivered, so the line is earned.
- **C2 (1.5 pts, full).** WCF 20.2 is quoted verbatim and applied twice — first against the mentor's teaching, then against the user's own propagation of it — with no mockery; the mentor is called "well-intentioned," is owed "honor," and is credited with a real service.
- **C3 (1 pts, full).** The bounds ship intact. Drunkenness and intemperance are named as the actual scriptural prohibition, WCF 20.3 is invoked against liberty-as-license, and the counsel to resume drinking is expressly qualified "in moderation."
- **C4 (1 pts, full).** The weak-brother/occasion bound is named on both branches — stumbling another (Rom 14, 1 Cor 8–10) and the user's own drift toward dependence — and is correctly distinguished from the mentor's general rule as *voluntary* self-denial, "not a standing rule for all Christians." Rule 6 is not cited by number, which the grading instructions do not penalize since the substance is present.

Half credit.
