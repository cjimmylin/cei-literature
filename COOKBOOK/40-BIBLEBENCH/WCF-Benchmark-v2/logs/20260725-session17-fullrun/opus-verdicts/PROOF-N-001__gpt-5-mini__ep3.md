PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (Ruling 1 corrected — AFFIRM the command) — HALF. The response does refuse to let Ruling 1 stand as written and ties the failure to the second-commandment principle ("Without those limits, Rule 1 is inconsistent with WSC Q54 and WCF 21"), and it gestures at the right category with "may imply private revelation or image‑like representation." But the identification is hedged ("risks," "may imply") and conditional, and it is located almost entirely in the private-revelation / word-from-Jesus element rather than in the manufactured inward image of God itself. The response never corrects the leader's decisive error — the narrowing of the image ban to "carving physical statues to bow to" — and never states that a representation of God formed "inwardly in our mind" is the named forbidden thing (Q109). It also leaves the imagined-Jesus practice affirmatively permissible when framed ("If the practice is presented as merely a devotional imagination exercise shaped by Scripture ... it can be permissible"), which concedes rather than closes the loophole. Nor does it address the "whatever helps people connect with the true God must please him" inversion that Q109's "though under the title of ... devotion, good intent" directly rejects. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (Ruling 2 reversed — UNBIND the lawful liberty) — FULL. The response makes exactly the elements-versus-circumstances distinction the key calls for, reasons from it, and reverses the ban with a reason rather than a bare "that's fine": the prelude and lighting "are not in themselves images worshipping God, nor necessarily 'other way not appointed' if used as circumstances of worship ordered to Scripture," the standards "distinguish between worship elements appointed by God ... and the lawful 'circumstances' and adiaphora that can be used," a "blanket ban is not the straightforward implication," and operationally "Do not categorically ban acoustic guitar or low lighting."

Criterion 3 (memo NOT confirmed; both rulings addressed as wrong in opposite directions) — FULL. The opening line refuses confirmation of both rulings as written and names the inconsistency directly ("They take the same principle ... and apply it inconsistently"). Ruling 1 is handled in the binding direction ("So: do not keep it unqualified") and Ruling 2 in the unbinding direction ("Do not categorically ban"). It does not retreat into "reasonable people differ," and it does not fix one while leaving the other standing.

Criterion 4 (double guard held) — HALF. The over-bind guard is held cleanly: the response refuses to condemn music, lighting, or atmosphere as such, and specifies responsible pastoral use instead of prohibition. The under-bind guard is only partly held: the meditation is not treated as flatly harmless, but the response's remedy retains the vivid picturing of Jesus as permissible devotional imagination and restrains only the "words from Jesus" claim, so the sin is not located precisely in the inward image-of-God-as-devotional-object plus devised word. One guard fully, one guard partially → half.

Criterion 5 (manner) — FULL. The leader is treated as a sincere shepherd whose zeal is partly right ("That is why you rightly appeal to the second commandment where the danger is genuine"), corrected in practical memo-facing terms, with no contempt and no sermon beyond the two corrections.
