# Bernhard Schölkopf

## Biographical Summary

Dr. Bernhard Schölkopf is a world-renowned computer scientist and a Director at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems in Tübingen, Germany. He earned his PhD from the Technical University of Berlin. He is widely considered one of the founding fathers of modern machine learning, specifically for his work on kernel methods and support vector machines. He is also a co-founder of the European Laboratory for Learning and Intelligent Systems (ELLIS).

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
Bernhard’s research has recently pivoted from statistical learning to the integration of machine learning with **Causal Inference**. He aims to build AI systems that can reason about cause and effect, leading to better robustness and generalization. He is a primary investigator in understanding how causal structures can be learned from data and how they can improve the reliability of foundation models.

### Significant Achievements
During the 1990s and 2000s, he co-developed Kernel Principal Component Analysis and various SVM algorithms that redefined the field of supervised learning. He is the author of several foundational books, including *Learning with Kernels* and *Elements of Causal Inference*. He has received nearly every major award in the field, including the Leibniz Prize and the Milner Award. With over 200,000 citations, his influence on AI is peerless.

## Major Publications
