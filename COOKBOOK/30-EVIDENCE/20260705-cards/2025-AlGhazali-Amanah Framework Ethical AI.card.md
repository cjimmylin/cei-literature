## 1. Gist
This 2025 journal article in AI and Ethics applies Al-Ghazali's classical Islamic principle of Amanah (trust/trusteeship) as a framework for ethical AI governance in organizations. The paper integrates three Islamic ethical concepts — Amanah (trust), Adl (justice), and Maslahah (communal benefit/public interest) — into a governance model for organizational AI deployment. Al-Ghazali (1058–1111) is one of the most influential Islamic philosophers and jurists, making this paper an attempt to ground contemporary AI governance in classical Islamic intellectual heritage. It is relevant to values-alignment benchmarking as a substantive Islamic alternative to Western AI governance frameworks (accountability, transparency, fairness), with distinct normative priorities.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a normative governance paper, not an empirical benchmark. Core claims: (a) Amanah (trust as a sacred obligation) provides a more robust foundation for AI accountability than Western contractarian or utilitarian governance models; (b) the three-principle framework (Amanah + Adl + Maslahah) maps onto AI governance challenges of accountability, fairness, and societal benefit respectively; (c) organizations can implement this framework as a practical governance structure. The paper draws on Maqasid al-Shariah (objectives of Islamic law) as a meta-framework.
