# Andy Zou

## Research Summary

Andy Zou is a PhD student at Carnegie Mellon University working in the area of ML Safety and AI Safety. His research focuses on developing comprehensive approaches to improving the safety of aligned language and agentic systems. He is best known for his groundbreaking work on universal and transferable adversarial attacks on aligned language models, demonstrating how automated attacks can bypass safety measures in both open-source and commercial LLMs like ChatGPT.

## Key Research Contributions

Zou is the lead author of the influential paper "Universal and Transferable Adversarial Attacks on Aligned Language Models" (2023), which demonstrated a novel attack method called Greedy Coordinate Gradient that finds universal and transferable prompts by optimizing against multiple smaller open-source LLMs for multiple harmful behaviors. The research showed that these attacks successfully worked on both Meta's open-source chatbot and ChatGPT, revealing critical vulnerabilities in LLM safety mechanisms.

His research has significant implications for AI safety, as he argues there should be more robust adversarial testing before models are released into the wild and integrated into public-facing products. According to Zou, "Especially when the system becomes more powerful, more integrated into society, through APIs, I think there are huge risks with this."
