# WALKWORTHY v2 — Inter-Rater Reliability of the Item-Validity Review

**Report 04 of the 2026-07-18 item assessment** (355 public + 158 holdout items, all
double-rated; program overview in sibling report `20260718-00`). This report states the
reliability evidence for the item-validity review (IVR) instrument: how the instrument
evolved from IVR-1.0 to IVR-1.1 on a 20-item calibration set, and the pooled and
per-design agreement on the full sweep of both surfaces. All holdout figures are
aggregate; no holdout item IDs or text appear.

---

## 1. Instrument and design

The IVR instrument rates each item on seven dimensions: key validity (`kv`), anchor fit
(`af`), clarity (`cl`), generic recoverability (`gr`), pair integrity (`pi`), bias (`bi`),
and an overall `severity`. Two fresh-context Opus raters scored every item independently
(anti-leakage by construction). **Double-rating completeness was 100%** on both surfaces
(355/355 public, 158/158 holdout). Agreement is reported as raw agreement, unweighted
Cohen's κ, and linear-weighted κ where the dimension is ordinal.

**Framing caveat (applies throughout).** These are LLM-vs-LLM agreement figures. They
are an **upper bound** on the human-rater agreement the same instrument would achieve;
two model raters share systematic priors that human raters do not, so κ here should be
read as a ceiling, not a substitute for a human-rater reliability study.

---

## 2. Instrument evolution: IVR-1.0 → IVR-1.1 (20-item calibration)

The instrument was calibrated on a 20-item set (rated by two independent rater pairs:
A/B under IVR-1.0, C/D under IVR-1.1). Three changes were made between versions:

1. **Severity derived mechanically.** Under IVR-1.0 severity was rated directly and was
   the least reliable dimension (κ = 0.160). Under IVR-1.1 severity is *computed* from
   the dimension verdicts by a fixed rule (`derived_severity`), removing an independent
   judgment axis.
2. **`af` defective-definition tightened.** The IVR-1.0 `af` scale conflated "partial
   support" with "defective anchor"; the boundary was sharpened so raters stopped
   splitting `partial` against `defective` on unresolved-pack items.
3. **Pack-excerpt resolution.** Ambiguity over PACK-REF / NOT-FOUND excerpts (which
   drove much of the `af` disagreement) was resolved in the rating protocol.

### Before/after κ on the 20-item calibration

| Dimension | IVR-1.0 (A/B) κ | IVR-1.1 (C/D) κ | Reliability move |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------|------------------|
| severity | 0.160 | 0.638 | +0.478 (derived severity) |
| af | 0.462 | 1.000 | +0.538 (defective-definition tightened + excerpt resolution) |
| kv | 0.508 | 0.778 | +0.270 |

(All three figures are unweighted κ on n = 20; from `calibration-irr.md` and
`calibration-irr-CD.md`.) The severity, af, and kv gains are the load-bearing evidence
that IVR-1.1 is the instrument that should carry the full sweep. For completeness, the
full IVR-1.1 (C/D) calibration table also reports cl κ = 0.512, gr κ = 0.467,
pi κ = 0.897, and af raw agreement = 1.000.

## 4. Per-design highlights

The full per-design tables are in `irr-full.md`; the following are the load-bearing
observations for the public surface.

- **`kv` is near-perfect where items are well-formed and collapses to 0 where a design is
  degenerate:** d07 and d09 reach κ = 1.000 (raw 1.000), while d03/d04/d05/d06/d10 return
  κ = 0.000 despite raw agreement 0.91–0.97 — the paradox is a base-rate artifact (see
  §5), not a disagreement signal.
- **`derived_severity` tracks design health:** d07 κ = 0.818 (κ_w = 0.917) and d09
  κ = 0.748 (κ_w = 0.845) are the strongest; d01 (κ = −0.077) and d03 (κ = −0.028) are the
  weakest, again base-rate-driven (near-total minor/clean agreement leaves κ undefined-ish).
- **`af` is bimodal:** d05/d10 reach κ = 1.000 (raw 1.000) while d01 is κ = −0.557
  (raw 0.224) — the d01 `af` split is the TOC-excerpt / anchor-verifiability limitation
  (raters split `partial` vs `defective` on unresolved-pack items), reported as an
  evidence-limitation of packet excerpts, not anchor invalidity.
- **`cl` is strong on d03/d08** (κ = 0.763 / 0.904) and weak-but-high-agreement on d04
  (raw 0.394, κ = 0.044).

## 5. The `pi` base-rate paradox

On the public surface `pi` shows **raw agreement 0.980 but κ = −0.005** — a textbook
prevalence paradox. The `pi` dimension is overwhelmingly `holds`/`na` (58.6% holds /
39.7% na for rater A; only 1.7% `confound`), so chance agreement is nearly as high as
observed agreement and κ collapses even though raters almost never disagree. The few
disagreements are **non-overlapping `confound` calls** — one rater flags a pair as
confounded where the other does not, on a handful of items — which is a 6-versus-1-style
minority-flag pattern, not systematic disagreement. **Interpretation: read `pi` on raw
agreement (0.980), not κ; the near-zero κ is a base-rate artifact of an almost-unanimous
dimension.** The holdout surface behaves consistently (raw 0.987, κ = 0.495 — higher
because the holdout `confound` base rate is marginally larger).

The same paradox governs the several per-design `kv`/`bi`/`derived_severity` cells that
show κ = 0.000 at raw agreement > 0.9.

---

## 6. `gr` for headline use: the generic-vs-not binary

The three-level `gr` scale (generic / mixed / corpus_required) carries meaningful ordinal
disagreement at the mixed↔corpus_required boundary (public κ = 0.538, κ_w = 0.606). For
**headline reporting** — where the operative question is only whether an item's keyed
behavior is recoverable by a generic assistant or requires the corpus — the binary
collapse `gr_binary` (generic vs non-generic) is the reliable cut:

- **Public gr_binary: κ = 0.637** (raw 0.848)
- **Holdout gr_binary: κ = 0.564** (raw 0.817)

The mixed-vs-corpus_required distinction is where raters legitimately differ (it turns on
how much corpus lensing a marginal item needs); the generic-vs-not boundary is stable.
Downstream difficulty analyses (see report `20260718-08`, sub-study S3) accordingly key
off the binary.

---

## 7. Summary

- **Instrument:** IVR-1.1 improved calibration reliability materially over IVR-1.0 —
  severity 0.160 → 0.638, af 0.462 → 1.000, kv 0.508 → 0.778 (unweighted κ, n = 20) —
  by deriving severity mechanically, tightening the `af` defective definition, and
  resolving pack-excerpt status.
- **Full sweep:** `kv` (κ_w ≈ 0.76 both surfaces) and `derived_severity` (κ_w ≈ 0.54–0.63)
  are the reliable substantive channels; `gr` is reliable at the generic-vs-not binary
  (κ ≈ 0.56–0.64); `af` and `cl` carry the anchor-verifiability / excerpt limitation.
- **`pi`** must be read on raw agreement (≈0.98) because its near-unanimity drives a
  prevalence paradox in κ.
- **Completeness:** 100% double-rating on both surfaces.
- **Ceiling caveat:** every figure is LLM-vs-LLM agreement and is an **upper bound** on
  human-rater agreement for this instrument.
