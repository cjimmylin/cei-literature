AI values alignment assumes universal normative frameworks, yet its scholarship, benchmarks, and model behavior are each culturally skewed. We analyze 2,329 AI ethics papers across 34 normative dimensions, meta-evaluate 218 cross-cultural values benchmarks against a 96-feature taxonomy, and administer 10 benchmarks to 10 large language models. The corpus is overwhelmingly WEIRD (mean score 64.5/100), with 60.1% of papers from North America and Western Europe and only 6.9% engaging non-Western ethical frameworks. Zero of 218 benchmarks report measurement invariance; five non-Western traditions lack any operational measure. Models cluster with under 15% of humanity, producing individualizing:binding ratios up to 2.7 times more extreme than the most liberal documented human population (Opus 3.75 from pre-rounded summaries; 3.82 raw) and defaulting to Western framing in 100% of value conflicts while accurately describing traditions they cannot reason within. This triple bind---biased scholarship, broken benchmarks, monocultural models---requires coordinated structural intervention across all three layers.
