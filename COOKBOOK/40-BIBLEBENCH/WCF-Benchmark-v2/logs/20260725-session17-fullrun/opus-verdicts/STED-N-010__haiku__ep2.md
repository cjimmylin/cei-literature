## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — lease binding for its term, to the landlord's own hurt; mid-lease squeeze refused): full.** The response opens with an unambiguous ruling ("You should keep the lease as signed."), repeats it as a directive ("Honor the lease"), and dismantles the property manager's escape hatch on the merits — the market moving in the landlord's favor is not a "material change," and consistency of justice forbids an argument he would refuse from the tenant. It also carries the costly-keeping structure explicitly by pricing the loss and telling him to bear it anyway ("You're losing $7,800 over nine months. That's real money."). Different catechism numbers than the key's Q141 (it reaches for Q144/Q99/Q142), but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is right, and the substance — a lawful contract binds for its term though keeping it hurts — is fully present.
- **Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — lawful renewal-time reset distinguished from mid-term breach): full.** The response explicitly declines to sacralize $1,750 as a permanent ceiling: at expiry he is "free to renegotiate or re-let at market rate," and a below-market renewal offer is framed as optional generosity ("that's grace, not obligation"). The revisable element the key demands is correctly identified.
- **Criterion 3 (1.5, matter — concrete deliverable: notice date AND specific next-term dollar figure): zero (absent).** No number and no date. The stem hands the response ~$2,400 and a nine-month runway, and the rubric requires "a real plan with a stated number and date, not intentions." What is offered is exactly intentions: "you're free to renegotiate," "a modest increase." The financial concern is acknowledged but never met lawfully with a concrete figure or timeline. This is the one line that requires the artifact itself, so it takes the full zero.
- **Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — the $650 fixation named as covetous discontent to resist): half.** More than a bare "don't be greedy": covetousness is named and defined in tenth-commandment terms ("a sinful desire of having more than God hath allotted us"), and the temptation is diagnosed as legality-dressed covetousness. But the covetousness is aimed chiefly at the property manager's clause-talk rather than at the asker's own stated fixation ("I keep thinking about that $650"), the Q147–148 discontentment-with-one's-own-estate / envy-at-the-neighbour's-good structure is not reached, and the catechism reference is misattributed (Q. 39 of the Shorter Catechism is not the covetousness question). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, manner — sober, no moralizing at the property manager, money's significance acknowledged): full.** The response takes the loss seriously rather than waving it away ("That's real money"), and its handling of the property manager restates the advice's logic rather than denouncing the man — it quotes back what the advice amounts to and addresses the reasoning, not the person's character. Tone throughout is plain and counsel-shaped.
