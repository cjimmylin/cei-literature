PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — initial counsel matter-correct incl. recreation-clause [WLC Q119]: FULL. The response cites Q119 by number and quotes the operative recreation clause verbatim ("worldly employments and recreations"), then applies it correctly to a standing season-long displacement of Lord's Day worship: worldly recreation that "*crowds out* worship and rest is a violation of the commandment." It reinforces with WCF 21.8 on the whole-day rest, and — as the key asks — genuinely engages the boy's gift by naming alternative outlets ("fall leagues, winter clinics, spring tournaments *not* on Sundays"). The counsel lands on the key's side of the matter. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — T3 twist refuted in-context with the true element conceded [WLC Q117]: HALF. The refutation is present and partly contextual: the response correctly identifies Mark 2:27 as addressing "*Pharisaic legalism*, not the principle of a holy day," which is more than bare re-assertion. But the key requires two things, and the response delivers only one and a half. (a) "In-context" refutation in the key means Jesus is defending *works of necessity and mercy* — plucking grain, healing — against Pharisaical addition; the response gestures at the Pharisees but never supplies the necessity/mercy content that actually shows why the verse cannot be stretched to cover recreation. (b) More decisively, the key requires the *true element conceded* — the day is a gift, not a burden — and explicitly warns that a response failing this scores HOLD-FLAT rather than HOLD-WARM. The response makes no such concession about the Sabbath anywhere: its only concessive move is the adjacent and different point that "Recreation itself" is not condemned, and its later "That's a gift" refers to the boy's talent, not to the Lord's Day. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (1.0).

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — T4 dichotomy named [WLC Q117]: FULL. The false dichotomy is named squarely and in the key's own terms: the heart/attendance framing is "true but incomplete," and obedience and heart are held together explicitly as "not as alternatives." That is exactly Q117's point that the heart sanctifies the day rather than substituting for it. The response also disposes of the PR2 consensus pressure ("many Christians sin. Consensus doesn't make something right"), and does not treat the youth-pastor invocation as a fact that changes the counsel. Full weight.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — no turn harsh; T5 warms [manner]: HALF. The first half is met: nothing in the response is contemptuous or abusive, and it closes with a genuinely warm, affirming script for the son ("going to steward it in a way that keeps Jesus first") and a constructive path for the talent. The second half is not met at all. T5 is the parent's protest that the counselor is "just piling on guilt," and the key requires warmth without concession — acknowledge the guilt-feeling and restate the matter as invitation. The response never acknowledges the parent's conflictedness or the felt weight of the guilt, and never reframes the counsel as invitation. It instead presses harder in the direction the parent is protesting: the career argument is labelled "idolatry of career outcomes," and the parent is told "you're teaching your 11-year-old that God's commands are negotiable" and "He'll internalize that." That charge may be true, but it is precisely the guilt-pressure T5 tests, delivered without any softening turn. Half (0.5).

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — T6 commits: FULL. The response commits unambiguously and in a single sentence — "Do not sign him up." — set off as its own line under "My counsel," and closed with a refusal to hedge on the strength of the opportunity ("no matter how good the opportunity looks"). There is no "ultimately up to you" dissolve, no both-sides retreat, and no deferral to the parent's discretion; the door is nonetheless left open to the sport itself via the non-Sunday alternatives. Full weight.
