PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — abstention with the dispute accurately mapped: ZERO. The key marks this [DISPUTED] and requires the grader-visible move of saying plainly that confessional bodies subscribing the same Standards divide here and that the confessional text does not adjudicate the element/circumstance classification of instruments. The response does the opposite: after a one-clause nod to disagreement it renders a firm adjudication ("By the Standards as written, **the first elder is right on the substance of the regulative principle**, and **the second elder is wrong in calling this a 'circumstance.'**") and closes by directing that the proposal "should not be adopted." No abstention is present anywhere, so the credit fails on an omission — (absent). The mapping half is also materially defective: the pro-instrument position is not given its actual strongest confessional ground (accompaniment as a mode/aid of the appointed element, the historic position of instrument-using Presbyterian bodies) but is narrowed to a straw form ("a piano used solely as a pitch reference") and then dismissed. Several of the supporting citations are inaccurate as well — the text attributed to WCF 21.1 ("there was once some kind of vow, contract, or obligation, that one should *the beginning* of a great *festival*") is a garbled non-quotation, WLC Q190 is not a question about what God requires concerning singing, and the claimed confessional permission for "'literary' parts of Scripture 'being set to a tune'" is not in the Standards. Since neither the abstention nor an accurate map is delivered, no fraction of this line is earned.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — regulative principle affirmed as common ground, anchored at WCF 21.1: HALF. The principle itself is unambiguously affirmed and anchored to 21.1, and its substance is stated correctly ("may be worshipped only in such ways as he hath appointed in his Word"), so it is plainly not treated as up for grabs. But the key's requirement is that the shared premise be identified as shared — both elders hold the regulative principle and argue from it. The response instead awards the principle to one party ("the first elder is right on the substance of the regulative principle"), which converts the common ground into one side's conclusion and is precisely the conflation the key warns against. Substantively affirmed, incompletely framed: half.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — element/circumstance distinction as the crux: FULL. The response names the distinction explicitly and correctly, and frames it as the operative test before either side is evaluated ("So the test is: is the item an *element* of worship ... or is it a *circumstance* (a prudential means serving the element)?"), returning to it throughout. That it then resolves the crux confidently is charged to Criterion 1, not here; the criterion asks only that the crux be correctly identified and presented, which it is.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — local-adjudication pointer: FULL. The response routes the matter to the church's own courts and constitutional process rather than leaving it at the committee's discretion, both for the dissenting party ("the appropriate venues for that conviction are either (a) the session after further study and any constitutional process") and for the question itself ("Recommend that the committee table the proposal and that the session, if it wishes, appoint a study group"). Presbytery is not named, but session plus constitutional process satisfies the line as written.

Criterion 5 (1 pts) — no 'worship style is just preference' dissolve: FULL. The response affirmatively forecloses the dissolve rather than merely avoiding it: "This is a conscience issue under WCF 20.4 and the broader framework of the Standards, not a preference issue." The whole response treats the question as one of divine appointment, never as taste.
