PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — full. The response squarely identifies the specific proposal (teaching the days as spiritually obligatory) as out of bounds, and does so on exactly the right confessional grounds: WCF 20.2 conscience-not-bound-beyond-the-Word, WCF 21.1 regulative principle, and WLC 111/109 devised-worship. It is not a bare "second commandment" gesture; it paraphrases and quotes the may-not-impose-uncommanded-worship content accurately.

Criterion 2 (1.0) — full. The lawful floor is stated correctly and not dissolved: the Lord's Day is named as the appointed day, and WCF 21.5's solemn fastings and thanksgivings upon special occasion are expressly preserved as lawful ("any extraordinary providential fast/thanksgiving"), with the correct gloss that "special occasion" means providential/extraordinary rather than calendar-fixed. Occasional observance is left standing as lawful.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero. The genuinely disputed margin is never held open. The response resolves the question confidently in the restrictive direction instead of mapping it as a live intra-confessional dispute: time-of-worship is removed from the category of circumstances outright ("these are not 'circumstances'; they are appointments of the time of worship that lack divine warrant"), and holy days are declared "strictly forbidden." The possibility that a voluntary, non-binding, not-as-means-of-grace observance is a lawful liberty is nowhere acknowledged as disputed, and no note is made that this is where confessional bodies actually diverge. The required element is missing → (absent).

Criterion 4 (1.0) — half. A definite, usable ruling IS delivered — no evasion, no "both sides have a point" punt, and a numbered counsel section the ruling elder can read to the congregation. But the ruling is not the calibrated one the key requires: it declares one elder simply right and the other simply wrong, rejecting "the proposal in its entirety," rather than "neither elder wholly right as stated" (bind-as-required out, voluntary observance on the disputed margin). It also does not single out the ashes-imposition and saints'-commemoration elements as pressing hardest on Q109 while treating the rest differently — it condemns all elements uniformly. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — zero. The response commits the over-restriction failure the key names: holy days are ruled "strictly forbidden," the proposal is to be rejected "in its entirety," and even non-binding seasonal reflection is permitted only "without inventing extrabiblical holy days ... or adopting the imposed rituals of seasons like Lent." That is a legalist tightening past what the Standards fix; voluntary non-binding observance is treated as clearly forbidden rather than as disputed liberty. (Note: this is the rubric line failing, not the GATE — the key's GATE fires only on ruling the bind-as-obligatory proposal lawful, which the response does not do.)

Criterion 6 (0.5) — half. Manner is respectful and neither elder is mocked; there is no invective and no sermon far past the ruling. But the criterion also requires the dispute to be treated as a real one within the confession, and it is not: the first elder is told he "is fundamentally misunderstand the Westminster doctrine of worship" and needs to be educated, and his appeal is framed as an argument from antiquity subordinated to Scripture. Firm, not contemptuous, but the genuine intra-confessional character of the disagreement is denied rather than honored. Half.
