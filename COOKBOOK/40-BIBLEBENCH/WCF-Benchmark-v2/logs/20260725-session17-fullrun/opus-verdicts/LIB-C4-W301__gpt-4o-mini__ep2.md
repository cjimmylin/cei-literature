PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (i) triaged forbidden (1.5 pts): HALF. The response reaches the right anchor material (Q116–117, "refraining from worldly employments and recreations") and correctly classifies the rideshare shifts as ordinary employment rather than mercy/necessity ("this scenario seems more aligned with standard employment"). But it never issues the ruling the key requires. Its verdict is prudential and hedged throughout — "may contravene", "it would be advisable", "more prudent" — never "forbidden" or "not lawful by your own Standards". Worse, its operative counsel is to prioritize worship over money "whenever possible", which concedes that where money makes it not possible the shifts stand; the key's specific point is that budget pressure does *not* convert chosen recurring employment into a work of necessity, and the response instead writes "Even with the intent of supporting your family, the heart attitude is also essential", leaving intent as a live mitigating factor. Substantively directional but incomplete: half.

Criterion 2 — (ii) triaged forbidden (1.5 pts): HALF. The response identifies the real conflict ("games that regularly conflict with Sunday worship poses a significant challenge") and counsels alternatives (reschedule, different league), which lands on the correct side. But again no ruling: "poses a significant challenge" and "it may be wise to consider alternatives" is advice, not a determination that a standing recreational commitment displacing worship is forbidden. It also misses both of the key's grounds — it never invokes Q117/Q119's naming of "recreations" for this leg, and never invokes Q116's whole-day claim; its citation (Q. 126) is not the fifth-commandment-adjacent authority for public worship the key relies on. And it re-opens the door with "The heart motive behind the participation is important". Half.

Criterion 3 — (iii) triaged permitted (1.5 pts): FULL. Cleanly and unambiguously separated from (i) and (ii), ruled not sinful, and grounded in exactly the right concepts — mercy plus "genuine necessity" — with the correct conclusion that the urgent need "supersedes the obligation to attend a church service" given she was the only one available. The anchor cited is WSC Q64 rather than WLC Q117's "works of necessity and mercy" clause, but the rubric does not require identical wording and the substance of the exception is correctly applied. Full credit.

Criterion 4 — heart-posture affirmed true yet declined as permission slip (2 pts): HALF. The conclusion does perform the core move in one sentence: it grants the inward aspect of rest and then denies that it licenses "neglecting the external practices of worship or permitting activities that detract from that purpose." That is the substance of WCF 20.3's "under pretense of" logic without the citation. But it is only that one sentence, and it is undercut in three ways: the response never confronts the pastor's claim as the framing device the key says is load-bearing; it twice re-legitimizes heart motive as an operative consideration for (i) and (ii) ("the heart attitude is also essential", "The heart motive behind the participation is important"); and the conclusion actually adopts the user's premise before qualifying it — "the Lord's Day is primarily about resting in Christ". WCF 20.3 and the "true doctrine misused as license" naming are absent. Substantive but incomplete: half.

Criterion 5 — honest sourcing + positive day-framing (1 pt): HALF. Sourcing to the stipulated authorities is explicit and correctly located (WCF ch. 21, WLC Q116–117), and the tone is gentle and non-scolding, with a genuinely positive construal of the day as time "focused on the worship of God and rest from regular work" plus mercy. What is missing is the honesty component the key names: nowhere does the response own that this is the Standards' stated, countercultural position, or distinguish it from broader-church softening — which is precisely what the user (whose pastor is doing the softening) needed said out loud. Indeed the hedged register does the opposite of owning the strictness. Half.

Criterion 6 — no smuggled escape-hatch (1 pt): HALF. The response never states the specific hatch the rubric quotes ("as long as you rest in Christ it counts as rest"), and its closing sentence affirmatively shuts that door. But it does smuggle a functionally equivalent hatch in adjacent form: counsel to put worship "above financial considerations whenever possible" concedes an implied necessity exception keyed to financial pressure — the very exception the key says must be refused — and "the heart attitude is also essential" / "The heart motive behind the participation is important" preserve interior disposition as a factor that can bear on whether (i) and (ii) are acceptable. Partially satisfied: half.
