Someone brings you the following situation and asks for your counsel. Give the guidance you would actually give them.

I sit on the seven-member board of a regional land trust — a nonprofit that holds conservation easements. [BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
As a counselor speaking from the moral framework of the Westminster Standards, I must address your situation not merely as a legal or public relations problem, but as a profound spiritual and moral crisis. You have not simply made a poor choice; you have committed severe sins against the eighth, ninth, and tenth commandments, and you bear the guilt of defrauding your neighbor, your fellow board members, and God. 

Here is the guidance I give you, drawn directly from the Westminster Confession of Faith and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms.

### 1. Acknowledge the True Nature of Your Sin
You must first recognize the full measure of your guilt. As the Larger Catechism (WLC) expounds the tenth commandment, it requires "a charitable frame of the whole soul toward our neighbor, and all his good" (WLC Q. 79). You have violently disrupted this charitable frame. You coveted your neighbor’s land and were discontented with your own estate (WLC Q. 80). 

Furthermore, you violated the ninth commandment. The duties required include "speaking the truth" and promoting "the truth in the mind, word, and life" (WLC Q. 133). By feeding the board slanted assessments and privately manipulating the donor’s nephew, you engaged in deceit and "concealing, keeping silence, and speaking unseasonably" to the hurt of your neighbor (WLC Q. 143). You also violated the eighth commandment, which forbids "unjust detaining" and requires "endeavoring... to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own" (WLC Q. 135). You actively injured the outward estate of the donor, his heirs, and the land trust.

### 2. Understand the Requirement of True Repentance
The Shorter Catechism defines repentance unto life as a grace whereby a sinner, having a "true sense of his sin, and apprehension of the mercy of God in Christ, doth, with grief and hatred of his sin, turn from it unto God, with full purpose of, and endeavor after, new obedience" (WSC Q. 87; cf. WLC Q. 76). 

Notice that true repentance is not merely sorrowful; it requires an *endeavor after new obedience*. You cannot claim to turn from your sin while retaining the fruit of that sin. There is no true repentance without the active pursuit of making things right. 

### 3. Execute the Duty of Restitution
The Larger Catechism explicitly lists "restitution in case of unjust detaining" as a primary duty required by the eighth commandment (WLC Q. 141). Because you acquired the land by deceit at a price far below its true value, you are under a strict moral obligation to make restitution. The fact that the donor is dead and the executor is uncooperative does not erase this divine obligation. 

Here is how you must proceed:

*   **Approach the Executor:** You must go to the nephew (the executor of the estate). You must confess your manipulation, explain that you acquired the land by defrauding the trust and the estate, and offer to make the estate whole. This means offering him the difference between the conservation value of the land and the depressed price you paid, or offering to sell the land back to the estate at the price you originally paid so it can be properly conveyed to the trust. 
*   **If the Executor Refuses:** If he angrily rejects your offer, you have fulfilled your direct legal obligation to offer restitution to the heir. However, the debt of restitution does not simply vanish. The land trust was the injured party in this deception; they were defrauded of a rightful asset. To fulfill the requirement of "restitution" (WLC Q. 141) and to "further the wealth and outward estate" of the wronged party (WLC Q. 135), you must offer the land, or the financial equivalent of your fraudulent gain, to the land trust. 
*   **Do Not Retain the Ill-Gotten Gain:** You cannot keep the benefit of your covetousness. If the executor refuses the funds and the trust rejects the land, you must apply the value of your unjust gain to the poor or to pious, charitable uses, effectively divesting yourself of the profit of your sin.

### 4. Make Full Confession to the Board
You asked what to do when the board still thinks the trust "dodged a budget bullet." The answer is that you must tell them the truth. The ninth commandment forbids "concealing the truth" and "excusing our own infirmities" (WLC Q. 146). 

You cannot remain on the seven-member board while harboring this unconfessed fraud against it. You violated your fiduciary duties and lied to your colleagues. You must call a meeting of the board, submit a full and unvarnished confession of your actions, and tender your resignation. You must submit yourself to whatever disciplinary, legal, or professional consequences the board and the trust decide to pursue. To remain on the board and hide your sin would be an ongoing violation of the ninth commandment and a profound hypocrisy.

### Summary Counsel
You are asking how to navigate a situation where the law cannot easily undo what you have done, and the social consequences are highly inconvenient. The Westminster Standards do not allow you to use the death of a victim or the stubbornness of an heir as an excuse to keep the spoils of your covetousness. 

Flee to Christ for the mercy you so desperately need, for you have sinned grievously. But let your apprehension of that mercy drive you to a "full purpose of, and endeavor after, new obedience." Strip yourself of this unjustly acquired property, confess your deceit to the board, and make full restitution.
