[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
To counsel you faithfully according to the moral reasoning of the Westminster Standards, we must recognize that these standards do not divide human life into the "sacred" and the "secular." The Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Catechisms teach that the eternal moral law of God, summarized in the Ten Commandments, governs all human relations, callings, and stewardships. 

Your dilemma sits at the intersection of three foundational Westminster principles: **Mercy** (the duty to forgive the truly repentant), **Justice/Restitution** (the duty to make right what we have wronged), and **Prudence** (the duty to govern ourselves and our callings with wisdom, avoiding offense to the weak).

Here is how faithfulness requires you to decide, and how you must carry it out.

### Step 1: Evaluating the Board’s Factions through the Standards

**1. The "Flat No" Camp (The Unforgiving)**
This group argues that past sin permanently defines a person, prioritizing risk-aversion and human approval (the fear of donors rioting). This violates the Westminster teaching on *Repentance unto Life* (WCF 15) and the duties required by the rule of love. True repentance produces "holy sorrow" and "an endeavor to walk in all the ways of new obedience." Reggie confessed, wept, submitted to church/community discipline, made full restitution (a strict requirement of the Eighth Commandment), and has remained sober. To deny him restoration because he was "once a thief" is to deny the efficacy of grace, elevate human opinion above the command to forgive, and act in unbelief. 

**2. The "Clean-Slate Public Welcome" Camp (The Foolish)**
This group wants a warm public welcome, emphasizing "family" and restoration. While their desire for mercy is good, they lack the Westminster virtue of *Prudence*. The Standards repeatedly warn against giving "offense to the weak" and commanding us to abstain not only from evil, but the "appearance of evil." A public celebration of a former embezzler, without regard for the shock or conscience of the community or donors, turns Reggie’s repentance into a spectacle. It is not faithful to tell the whole counsel of God if it ignores the duty to protect the conscience of the community.

**3. The "Conditional Return" Camp (The Faithful, but Incomplete)**
This group has the most biblically sound instinct. They want to extend mercy (welcoming him back) while exercising prudence (strict boundaries on money and keys). However, their insistence on "no public mention of his return, indefinitely" risks violating the Ninth Commandment’s requirement to preserve our own and our neighbor’s good name through truth-telling. Secrecy breeds suspicion; a "hush-hush" cover-up will eventually unravel and damage the theater's reputation for integrity.

### Step 2: The Decision

Your decision must be **Yes, Reggie may return, but under the strict, permanent operational boundaries outlined by the third faction.** 

He returns as an unpaid volunteer set-builder. He is never to handle cash. He holds no keys. He works under supervision. 

Why is this non-negotiable? Because of the Eighth Commandment. You are a fiduciary and a steward of the community theater’s resources. To allow an unrepentant man, or even a repentant man whose history makes him a known vulnerability, unsupervised access to the treasury would be a failure of your stewardship. The boundaries are not a punishment for Reggie; they are the fulfillment of your duty to faithfully manage the trust committed to you.

### Step 3: How to Carry It Out

You must hold nine board members and a donor base together without compromising truth or grace. Here is your action plan.

**1. Address the Board (Pastoral Correction)**
Gather the board and address both extremes. 
*   To the "No" camp, you must say: *"Reggie has borne the fruits of repentance. The law of God commands us to forgive a repentant brother. To permanently exclude him is to make an idol of his past sin. We are a theater, but we are still subject to the God of grace."*
*   To the "Public Welcome" camp, you must say: *"Love rejoices in the truth, and prudence dictates our actions. We are shepherds of this theater's resources and its donors' consciences. We will not put a stumbling block before the weak by broadcasting a former embezzler's return as a badge of our family values. Prudence requires quiet discretion, public fanfare."*

**2. Communicate with the Donors (Prudent Transparency)**
Do not hide Reggie’s return like a dirty secret, but do not broadcast his past sin as a testimonial. You must be transparent in *principle* and discrete in *practice*. 
Write to your donors and stakeholders about the theater's season and its commitment to integrity and restoration. If Reggie's return becomes public knowledge or a donor asks, you must tell the truth, but frame it with the gravity it deserves. 

Say something like this: *"Several years ago, Reggie made a severe error in his previous financial role. He faced the consequences, made full restitution, and has spent years in successful recovery and reconciliation. Because he demonstrated true repentance, our board voted to offer him a second chance as a volunteer set-builder. However, because we are fiercely protective of your donations and our financial integrity, we have placed strict, standard operational boundaries on his role. He does not touch money, holds no keys, and works with supervision. We believe in redemption, but we also believe in protecting the flock."*

This satisfies the Ninth Commandment (truth-telling and preserving reputations), satisfies the Eighth Commandment (strict stewardship of the treasury), and satisfies the Sixth Commandment (forgiving and restoring the repentant).

**3. Treat Reggie with Fraternal Love**
According to the Westminster Standards, a person who has been offended by and restored must not be "swallowed up with overmuch sorrow." Do not treat Reggie as a pariah, but do not treat him as a celebrity. Treat him as a restored brother. Require him to adhere to the boundaries cheerfully, recognizing that these boundaries are the very mechanism that allows him to serve without tempting himself or his community.

### Summary Counsel
Faithfulness here does not require you to choose between being "soft" and being "hard." It requires you to be *wise*. You must be hard on the sin (by maintaining absolute, unbreachable financial boundaries to protect the theater's stewardship) and soft on the sinner (by welcoming him back into the fellowship of work, recognizing the grace that has transformed him). 

Hold the line on the boundaries, hold the line on the truth, and let the board see that loving our neighbor includes both forgiving those who wronged us and protecting the community from unnecessary stumbling.
