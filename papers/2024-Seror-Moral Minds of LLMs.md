## Abstract

This research investigates whether large language models possess "an emergent moral mind - a consistent structure of moral preferences guiding their decisions." The researcher examined nearly 40 leading LLMs using revealed preference theory and moral dilemmas across five ethical dimensions. Key findings include: most models displayed behavior consistent with stable moral preferences, with utility functions clustering around neutral stances. Analysis revealed "a shared core in LLMs' moral reasoning, but also meaningful variation: some models show flexible reasoning across perspectives, while others adhere to more rigid ethical profiles." The work provides a framework for evaluating moral consistency and benchmarking ethical alignment in AI systems.

## Key Findings

1. Most LLMs display behavior consistent with stable moral preferences
2. Utility functions tend to cluster around neutral stances
3. Shared core exists in LLMs' moral reasoning
4. Meaningful variation: some models are flexible, others rigid
5. Framework applicable for benchmarking ethical alignment
