# Graham Caron

## Biographical Summary

Graham Caron is a researcher affiliated with UNC Chapel Hill who has worked on the intersection of psychology and artificial intelligence, particularly exploring how personality traits manifest in language models.

## Research Focus

Caron's research explores how psychology research concepts like extroversion, agreeableness, and emotional stability - part of the 'Big Five' personality traits - apply to language models. His work investigates whether the perceived personality in language models is exhibited consistently in their language generation, and whether such personality traits can be controlled.

## Notable Contributions

His major publication, "Identifying and Manipulating the Personality Traits of Language Models" (with Shashank Srivastava), examines whether language models such as GPT-2 respond in consistent ways that reflect personality traits, and whether these traits can be manipulated. The research showed that when provided different types of contexts (such as personality descriptions, or answers to diagnostic questions about personality traits), language models such as BERT and GPT-2 can consistently identify and reflect personality markers in those contexts.

In their research, Caron and Srivastava surveyed Reddit users about their "Big Five" personalities and trained LLMs with these user-specific contextual data. Their results show that LLMs can reliably imitate personality markers in various contexts.

This work was also developed as an undergraduate honors thesis at UNC Chapel Hill in 2022, titled "Identifying and Manipulating Personality Characteristics in Pretrained Language Models."
