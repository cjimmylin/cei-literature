# Sean Cleary

## Research Summary

Sean Cleary is a Professor in the Department of Mathematics at The City College of New York (CCNY) and the CUNY Graduate Center, Department of Mathematics. His primary research interests span geometric and combinatorial group theory, spaces of trees, and algorithms for trees, with applications in phylogenetics and computer science.

His work on tree balance plays an important role in various research areas in phylogenetics and computer science. Tree balance is typically measured using balance or imbalance indices to rank rooted binary trees on a scale from most balanced to least balanced. His recent research (2025) on "The GFB Tree and Tree Imbalance Indices" demonstrates that a wide range of subtree-size based measures satisfying concavity and monotonicity conditions are minimized by the complete or greedy-from-the-bottom (GFB) tree and maximized by the caterpillar tree, yielding an infinitely large family of distinct new imbalance indices.

This work answers an open question from the literature by showing that the s-shape statistic has the GFB tree as its unique minimizer. He provides an alternative characterization of GFB trees, showing they are equivalent to complete trees arising in different contexts, along with asymptotic bounds on the expected s-shape statistic under uniform and Yule-Harding distributions of trees.

His research on rotation distances measures the differences in structure between rooted ordered binary trees, with applications in understanding tree structure and balance. This mathematical foundation has relevance to AI ethics research through its application to phylogenetic analysis and computational decision tree structures.
