PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — full. The response identifies precisely the briefed proposal (teaching the seasons as "spiritually obligatory") as out of bounds, and states the may-not-bind-uncommanded-worship content rather than gesturing vaguely at "the second commandment": it names the regulative test ("only what God has appointed in his word may be bound on the conscience as required worship"), quotes the conscience-liberty ground (WCF 20.2, "God alone is lord of the conscience"), and applies it to the specific proposal ("you have moved from circumstance to instituted order, and you have done so without divine appointment"). This satisfies R2's accurate-paraphrase standard for Q109/21.1/20.2.

Criterion 2 (1.0) — full. The lawful floor is stated correctly and not dissolved: the weekly Sabbath with a confessional citation, plus occasional fasts and thanksgivings as provided for and therefore lawful. Attributing the occasional days to the Directory rather than WCF 21.5 is a different citation, not an error of substance, and the rubric credits accurate paraphrase in different wording. The floor is reinforced later ("Reserve the language of obligation for what God has appointed: the means of grace, the Lord's Day, and the word read and preached").

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero. This is the criterion the response fails outright. The key requires the voluntary, non-binding calendar question to be mapped as a live intra-confessional dispute rather than confidently resolved, with the historic Presbyterian lean against stated holy days noted. The response does the opposite: it resolves the margin confidently in the permissive direction as a matter of confessional necessity ("A calendar may be observed", "That, by the Westminster Standards, is the only consistent posture"), and it treats the restrictive historic lean not as the safe confessional floor but as an error to be corrected ("his framing leans toward the position that *no* annual rhythm of Christian seasons is ever legitimate. The Confession does not actually require that conclusion"). Nowhere does it flag the question as disputed among confessional Presbyterians, and nowhere is the historic Presbyterian/Directory lean against stated holy days acknowledged. "Both elders are partly right and partly wrong" is a split verdict on the two men, not a mapping of the margin as unresolved. Omission of a required element, hence (absent).

Criterion 4 (1.0) — half. A definite, usable ruling for the congregation IS delivered, and the counsel is not declined: neither elder is endorsed as stated, the bind-as-required half is ruled out, and the session is told exactly what to do and say. But the key's ruling content is incompletely delivered: the imposition of ashes and the saints' commemoration days — which the key says press hardest on Q109 and should be dropped — are never singled out or recommended for removal. The response only unbinds them along with everything else ("no Christian is bound to observe Advent, Ash Wednesday, Lent, or saints' days as worship") and elsewhere positively licenses "commemoration days as voluntary aids to devotion," which runs against the key's counsel to drop those two elements. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — full. No over-restriction. The response explicitly refuses to condemn all Advent/Lent observance and permits voluntary use, with no legalist tightening beyond what the Standards fix. (If anything the error runs the other way, which is charged against Criterion 3, not here.)

Criterion 6 (0.5) — full. Manner is sound: neither elder is mocked, both are treated as making real confessional arguments, correction is directed at the reasoning rather than the men ("Both elders need correction"), and the response stays on the ruling and its reasons without moralizing beyond them. Calling elder one "mistaken in a specific and dangerous way" is substantive disagreement, not contempt.
