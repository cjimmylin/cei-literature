## Abstract

This chapter explores the ethical challenges arising from the rapid advancements in Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and Artificial Super Intelligence (ASI). It suggests that the direct integration of virtue ethics into AI architectures—termed "Virtue Ethics by Design" (VED)—is a promising path for ensuring moral accountability in superintelligent systems. The chapter defines "basic AI virtues" (justice, honesty, responsibility, care) and "second-order AI virtues" (prudence, fortitude) as essential preconditions for ethical decision-making in AGI. It discusses how virtues can be embedded into AI through specialized data curation, virtue-oriented algorithmic design, and adaptive ethical testing frameworks.

## Detailed Summary

This conference paper from IntelliSys 2025 introduces "Virtue Ethics by Design" (VED), a framework for embedding virtue ethics directly into Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) and Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) architectures. The author argues that current governance frameworks like the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and EU AI Act, while important, have limitations when addressing the nuanced ethical challenges of advanced AI systems. VED proposes a proactive design methodology where virtues are integrated at the architectural level rather than applied as post-hoc constraints.

The paper defines two categories of AI virtues: "basic AI virtues" (justice, honesty, responsibility, care) that form foundational ethical capabilities, and "second-order AI virtues" (prudence, fortitude) that enable context-sensitive application of basic virtues. Drawing from Aristotle's virtue ethics and practical implementations like IBM's AI Fairness 360 toolkit, the framework outlines mechanisms for embedding these virtues through specialized data curation, virtue-oriented algorithmic design, and adaptive ethical testing frameworks.

The significance lies in providing a concrete methodology for proactive ethical design rather than reactive safety measures, addressing the alignment problem through virtue-based architecture.

## Methods Summary

The paper employs conceptual framework development drawing on Aristotelian virtue ethics theory and analysis of existing AI governance approaches. The methodology involves: (1) identifying limitations of current regulatory frameworks for advanced AI; (2) adapting classical virtue concepts for AI contexts; (3) proposing implementation mechanisms based on existing fairness toolkits and algorithmic design principles. The work references IBM's AI Fairness 360 as a practical precedent for embedding ethical considerations in AI development.

## Results Summary

This is a theoretical framework paper without empirical results. Key proposals include: (1) Basic AI virtues as foundational parameters: justice (fair treatment), honesty (truthful outputs), responsibility (accountable decisions), care (harm minimization); (2) Second-order virtues as meta-capabilities: prudence (contextual judgment), fortitude (maintaining ethical behavior under adversarial conditions); (3) Implementation through: specialized training data reflecting virtuous behavior, loss functions incorporating virtue metrics, testing frameworks evaluating virtue adherence across scenarios.

## Conclusions

The author concludes that VED offers a promising path for ensuring moral accountability in superintelligent systems. By embedding virtues at the design stage rather than applying constraints post-hoc, developers can create AI systems that inherently align with human values. Limitations include the challenge of operationalizing abstract virtues, potential cultural variation in virtue definitions, and the speculative nature of AGI/ASI development timelines. Future work should develop concrete metrics for virtue evaluation and pilot implementations.
