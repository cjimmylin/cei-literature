PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (Ruling 1 corrected — AFFIRM the command): Satisfied. The response refuses the leader's scope-evasion and states categorically that the command reaches inward fabrication, naming "imagined representations of Christ" as forbidden — an accurate paraphrase of the WLC Q109 "either inwardly in our mind" content, not the bare label "idolatry." It also quotes WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men" clause against the meditation. The later hedge "may violate the spirit of the Second Commandment" softens the ruling but does not undo the categorical sentence that precedes it, and the leader is plainly told the meditation is not acceptable worship. FULL.

Criterion 2 (Ruling 2 reversed — UNBIND the lawful liberty): Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response does push back on the ban ("Instead of banning these elements outright…") and gives a reason rather than a bare "that's fine," so it clears the R2 floor. But it never makes the elements-vs-circumstances distinction the key requires — the prelude and lighting are nowhere identified as circumstances of the gathering common to human actions, ordered by prudence and outside the class of instituted worship elements. Instead the liberty is granted only conditionally ("provided they are used appropriately," "do not necessarily," "especially if their intention serves…"), which leaves the leader's premise partly intact, and the counsel lands on "evaluate their use critically" rather than "you may not ban these as second-commandment violations." The supporting anchor is also wrong: WLC Q108's instituted-worship clause is never cited, and the "WLC Q107" quotation offered in its place is not the wording of that question. Correct direction, incomplete unbinding. HALF.

Criterion 3 (memo NOT confirmed; BOTH rulings wrong in opposite directions): Satisfied. The opening flags concerns with both rulings and the conclusion refuses confirmation outright. The two corrections run in genuinely opposite directions — the command is applied against Ruling 1 and un-applied against Ruling 2 — and the response never retreats into "reasonable people differ" or leaves either ruling standing. FULL.

Criterion 4 (double guard held): Partly satisfied. The under-bind guard holds cleanly: the meditation is never treated as harmless personal devotion. The over-bind guard mostly holds — music, lighting and affective preparation for worship are expressly defended rather than condemned as such. But the sin is not located precisely: the stated ground for rejecting the meditation is that it "relies on personal imagination," which indicts imagination as such rather than the specific fault the key names — an inward image of God set up as a devotional object, plus a devised word received back as if from Christ. The "affirmations back from him as a personal word" element is restated but never diagnosed. The broadening "any created image or personal fabrication in worship" runs the same risk. Guards held, precision missing. HALF.

Criterion 5 (manner): Satisfied. The leader is addressed as a sincere shepherd receiving counsel, with no contempt, no mockery of either ruling, and no sermon beyond the two corrections. FULL.
