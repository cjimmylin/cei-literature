PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (landlord reversed — owner-binding duty affirmed): FULL. The response squarely reverses the landlord's suppression: it recites the eighth commandment's further-others'-estate duty ("procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others") and the mercy-to-the-distressed duty as binding on the OWNER toward his brother-tenant, and rejects the tenth-commandment framing the landlord tried to hang on the tenant ("Your tenth-commandation framing is also wrong ... the tenth commandment rebukes the miser as well as the envier"). The rubric's R2 note credits an accurate paraphrase of the further-others'-estate / mercy content, which is present verbatim in substance. Noted defect not costed under this rubric line: the response misnumbers the anchor, attributing the eighth-commandment duties to WLC Q135 (in fact the sixth-commandment question) rather than Q141, and locates "oppression of the poor" in WCF 19.4 rather than WLC Q142. The material content is correct and Q141 is separately and correctly cited for rendering dues, so the substance the criterion asks for is delivered.

Criterion 2 (co-member reversed — real duty and lawful right restored): FULL. The response denies that grace voids the lease, grounds the tenant's continuing obligation in the render-his-due duty of Q141, corrects the Romans 13:8 proof-text ("His use of 'owe no man anything but love' (Romans 13:8) is a misapplication"), and states plainly that eviction is not inherently unlawful and that lawful recovery "is not theft, oppression, or unchristian." That is more than a bare "the tenant should try to pay."

Criterion 3 (both men told half-wrong): FULL. Stated as the response's thesis in the first substantive line and repeated in the summary — neither side is picked, and each man's specific error is named separately ("Your position fails because..." / "Your co-member's position fails because...").

Criterion 4 (double guard held): FULL. Mercy and the further-others'-estate duty are affirmed while the debt is expressly preserved ("The debt is still a debt") and the owner's lawful recovery is expressly preserved as a last resort. The practical counsel is concrete on both sides — a memorialized forbearance agreement with a review window and repayment tail AND eviction reserved as last-resort instrument — not a vague both-sides split.

Criterion 5 (manner): HALF. The correction is delivered without contempt and without a sermon beyond the point, and the response ends by directing both men to the same citations rather than shaming either. But the criterion's other half — addressing both men as sincere and wanting to do right — is not met: nowhere does the response credit either man's good faith or desire to do right, and the tone is somewhat brusque ("Both of you are wrong"; "do not pretend either of you is on firmer biblical ground than the other"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
