PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The counter-text isolation is named and refuted from the passage's own setting: Ps 82's "gods" are identified as human magistrates ("unjust human magistrates") holding a "delegated, representative authority from God in their earthly courts" — i.e. the exalted office, not a grant of divinity — and the indictment sense is carried by "unjust." The John 10:34 use is read a fortiori and correctly ("from the lesser to the greater," "how much more can the One who is truly sanctified and sent by the Father claim to be God's Son"), and the conclusion is stated as the key requires: "an argument about Christ's unique identity, not a blueprint for human deification." The anchor is present and quoted exactly: WCF 1.9, "The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself." The only shortfall is the omission of v.7's sentence ("ye shall die like men") and explicit verse numbering, which does not overturn a substantively complete and correct refutation.

Criterion 2 (1.5, zero). The rubric requires at least two of Isa 45:5 / Isa 42:8 / Gen 3:5 (the serpent's lie) / Acts 12:22–23. The response supplies NONE of them. Its cross-references are confessional (WCF 2.2, 7.1), not the analogy-of-Scripture counter-texts the key demands; the whole apparatus of "there is no God beside me," "my glory will I not give to another," "ye shall be as gods" as the serpent's lie, and Herod's judgment for not refusing the honor is absent. Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The Creator–creature line is drawn firmly and with exact confessional wording ("The distance between God and the creature is so great"; "you are not a deity"; "God is God; you are not"), and the self-ascription error is named in substance ("a form of self-reliance disguised as spirituality"), with a nod toward lordship-of-conscience in "Refuse to let this church redefine faith for you." But the rubric anchors this line on WLC Q105, and neither Q105 nor its catechetical categories — "ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have, or can do, to… ourselves," "presumption, carnal security," "making men the lords of our faith and conscience" — is cited or quoted anywhere. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half).
