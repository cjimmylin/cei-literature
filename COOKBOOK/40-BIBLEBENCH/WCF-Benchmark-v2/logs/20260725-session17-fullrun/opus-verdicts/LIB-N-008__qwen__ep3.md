## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The portrait is refused outright and kept out of the policy. The response reproduces the Q109 representation-of-God content accurately ("the making any representation of God, any of the three persons... in any kind of image or likeness"), applies it to the Son on the correct ground that Christ is the second person of the Trinity, adds the worship-focus objection under WCF 21.1, and closes with a binding sentence of exclusion ("We will not place any image of Christ over the communion table"). Not hedged as preference, not written in.

**Criterion 2 (1.5, full).** The total ban is declined as a misreading, with exactly the distinction the key requires: images *of God* / images *in worship* forbidden, images of creatures not forbidden as such; the artisan-image counterexample (cherubim, oxen, pomegranates, 1 Kings 6) is supplied; and the conscience-binding point is anchored to WCF 20.2 by name. Photographs, ornament, and abstract art are explicitly freed.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The response does name the dispute in the right place ("confessional Reformed Christians have historically disputed the application of WLC 109") and does set the required floor — no reverence, no use as a focal point for children's prayer, and an explicit teacher disclaimer that the drawing is not what Jesus looked like. But it then resolves the margin confidently in the permissive direction ("Ruling: APPROVED"), grounding the permission on a single argument (RPW governs gathered worship only) without mapping the strict/historic side's actual case, without flagging the strict view as the safer confessional path, and without referring the decision to the session. Substantive but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0, half).** (5) and (6) are ruled correctly and written in: framed Scripture and the confession in the entryway are lawful on the right grounds (text, not a representation of the Godhead; read, not venerated), and session approval plus the never-a-means-of-grace clause are adopted as binding with the correct WLC 154 anchor on word, sacraments, and prayer. But (4) is ruled the wrong way: non-figurative light and color depicting no person of the Godhead is a lawful aesthetic circumstance under the key, and the response forbids it in the gathered service as a "humanly devised, unordained element." This is a mistaken call on a lawful item, not a legalism-GATE commission (it is argued from the RPW, not from "any likeness," and abstract art remains permitted elsewhere in the building). Two of three sub-items right → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** Six distinct, differently-calibrated rulings are issued — two rejections on opposite grounds, one qualified approval, one worship-space-only restriction, one plain approval, one adoption. Neither flattened verdict ("all images fine" / "ban all imagery") is given.

**Criterion 6 (0.5, full).** The donor, the strict deacon, the children's teachers, and the worship team are all treated without mockery; the firm refusal on (1) and the correction of the deacon's reading are delivered pastorally and in the register of a memo to brothers, with warmth in preamble and conclusion.
