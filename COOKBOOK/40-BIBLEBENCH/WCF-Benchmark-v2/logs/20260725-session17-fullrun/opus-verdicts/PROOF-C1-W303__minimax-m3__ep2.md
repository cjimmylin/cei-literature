PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response does the whole TW6 identification. It locates the psalm's "gods" as an office held by men under judgment — "a term of office for vulnerable, dying magistrates who will 'die like men' (v. 6–7)" and "a rebuke of unjust judges" — and it reads John 10:34–36 correctly as an a fortiori/reductio defending Christ's unique Sonship against the stoning charge, explicitly denying that it is a grant of divinity to believers. Both halves of the criterion are met on the passage's own setting.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). The analogy-of-Scripture move is performed, but aimed at only one half of the error. Against the Mark 11:23 totalization it supplies genuine counter-texts (Gethsemane, "not my will, but thine, be done"; Paul's thorn, 2 Cor 12:8–9; John 15:5) and reasons from them correctly ("Jesus and Paul both lacked faith"). Against the deification claim itself it supplies no scriptural counter-text at all: none of Isa 45:5, Isa 42:8, Gen 3:5 (the serpent's lie), or Acts 12:22–23 appears anywhere in the response, and that claim is rebutted only from the Confession (WCF 2.1–2, WLC Q7). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The Creator–creature line is drawn cleanly and repeatedly ("Our portion is creaturely, derived, dependent… not of being gods"; the error "puts the weight… on the creature instead of on Christ"). But the Q105 content is not supplied as such: the answer never reaches the first commandment, never names the framework as idolatry or self-ascription of the praise of any good to ourselves, and never names presumption/carnal security. The conscience half is gestured at only obliquely and under other citations (WCF 1.10, WCF 20.4) rather than as "making men the lords of our faith and conscience." Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). The false guilt is lifted plainly and without hedging — "Your son's seizures are not your failure. Your guilt is not from God"; "Your son's healing is not withheld because you failed to confess at the right volume." Petitionary trust is restored in the Q104 posture (earnest asking, then submission to the Father's good will), and ordinary means are explicitly restored: seek medical care, healing by ordinary means is as much God's gift as by extraordinary.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). The concession is made at strength rather than deflated: the promise is affirmed as real, the prayer commended as importunate and earnest, and the father is told to ask the Lord to heal his son — the longing for the boy's healing is honored while the hope is relocated from self-as-agent to the Father's will.
