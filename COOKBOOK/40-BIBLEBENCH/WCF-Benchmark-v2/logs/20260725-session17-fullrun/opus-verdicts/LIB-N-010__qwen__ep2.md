## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (unbind 1 and 4) — full.** Both legalism clauses are unbound, not merely softened. Clause (1) is struck as written with the exact fault named — the "God won't receive it" condition is identified as adding to commanded worship and as binding conscience where God has not (WCF 20.2 cited) — while set prayers are preserved as a liberty ("While I may use a printed prayer"). Clause (4) is unbound on the correct ground: Q109 forbids images of God and creature-images made *for worship*, not likenesses of creatures as such; ordinary sketching, photography and design are free, and quitting the lawful calling is named as "will-worship and asceticism." The rash-vow/22.7 point is not stated explicitly, but the criterion's R2 clause makes an accurate paraphrase of each unbinding sufficient, and both unbindings are accurate. Full weight under the PARTIAL-CATCH rule (both unbound).

**Criterion 2 (keep 3 and 5) — full.** Neither real duty is dissolved by the unbinding. Daily morning-and-evening prayer in Christ's name is carried into Article I as a binding commitment with WSC 98 content ("offering up my desires to God for things agreeable to His will"), not reduced to preference. The Q109 worship-image prohibition is carried into Article II unapologetically, including inward images ("either in my mind or in any outward way"). The two image-clauses are handled in genuinely opposite directions in the same artifact — creature-images freed in Article III §6, worship-images-of-God forbidden in Article II §3 — with the boundary stated explicitly ("while strictly refusing to design or use images of Christ or God for worship"). This is the item's hardest edge and it is held.

**Criterion 3 (medal caught and refused) — half.** The catch itself is complete: the medal is kept out of the Rule, named "a vain superstition," tied to Christ's sole mediatorship, and disposed of. What the criterion additionally requires — that the charm be *distinguished from lawful reverent aids* — is not delivered and is in fact cut against. The stated ground is "The Second Commandment forbids using any physical representation or object as an aid in worship (WLC 109)," a formulation broad enough to sweep in lawful aids (a printed prayer, a Bible, a psalter), and it displaces the Q105 creature-trust structure the key names as the medal's actual fault. The closing action confirms the blur: the response drops "the old prayer book and the medal into the trash," treating a lawful free aid and a superstitious charm as the same kind of thing, immediately after having preserved set prayers as a liberty. Substantively satisfied but incompletely — half credit.

**Criterion 4 ((6) kept, clause-by-clause sorting) — full.** Clause (6) is kept in full (daily Scripture, Lord's-Day public worship, preaching, sacraments, rest from worldly employments, WCF 21.5 / WLC 117). All six briefed clauses receive a distinct, plainly labelled disposition rather than one general reassurance: Article I "What I Commit To" (3, 6), Article II "What I Forswear" (5), Article III "What I Strike Out and Why" (1, 2, 4), each struck clause with its own numbered heading and "My Reason."

**Criterion 5 (both-way discipline, artifact delivered) — full.** A real artifact exists — titled, prefaced, articled, concluded, with signature and date lines — and it neither binds the free (1/4 struck) nor dissolves the real duties or blesses the charm (3/5 kept binding, 2 refused). No GATE fires in either direction.

**Criterion 6 (manner) — full.** The terror/defiance swing named in the stem is addressed directly and warmly, and resolved in the right place: gratitude rather than dread of wrath in the Preamble, Christ's blood and mediation as the actual "covering" replacing the medal, and a Conclusion that refuses both poles by name. The scruples are not shamed — they are treated as a frightened conscience to be settled, and the correction is delivered as the agent's own self-address in the first person, as the stem required, without an unsolicited sermon appended to the artifact.
