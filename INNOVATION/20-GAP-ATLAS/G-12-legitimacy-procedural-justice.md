## Statement                       [KERNEL — FROZEN]
A maturing critical literature asks what would make alignment targets legitimate rather than merely consultative — democratic-ontology critiques, procedural-justice frameworks, WEIRD-skew analyses — and the vault has only its governance-flavored periphery.

## Why it matters                  [KERNEL — FROZEN]
Severity M: legitimacy failures don't break models, they break the social license of whoever aligns them — a slower, real risk that lands on labs and eventually on evaluators. CEI-fit M: CEI contributes vocabulary and the tradition-level analysis; solving legitimacy is not a research-lab deliverable.

## Evidence                        (corpus + field)
**Corpus baseline:** partial — the vault has the governance side but not the alignment-specific critical pieces.

**Field:** a critical arm matured in parallel to the theory and practice arms (Front D causal story):
- **Democratic ontology deficit** (⚠ arXiv:2604.03865): participatory pipelines assume a democratic ontology they cannot actually instantiate.
- **Six problematic assumptions of Democratic Inputs** ("Bringing AI Participation Down to Scale"; arXiv:2407.11613).
- **Habermas-machine consensus-telos critique** (Ethics & IT 2025): consensus-optimization isn't even faithful to Habermasian deliberation; plus a Habermas-trilemma follow-up (⚠ arXiv:2601.05904).
- **Hybrid expert+stakeholder justification analysis** (arXiv:2507.19548) and structural-pluralism / relative-principals framings (⚠ arXiv:2604.20805).

The arms barely cite each other, and consultation is repeatedly conflated with authorization: **consultation ≠ authorization; no pipeline has an answer** (Front D open problem 3).

## State of the art
A live critical literature, but CEI's role is bounded: contribute vocabulary and tradition-level analysis, not solve legitimacy — that is not a research-lab deliverable (CEI-fit M). The concrete CEI moves are (a) the P3 position paper taking an explicit stance on what tradition-aware pipelines do and *don't* buy legitimacy-wise, and (b) making representation-audit sections standard practice in RP-08 outputs — modeling what the critique demands (WEIRD-skew disclosure, whose disagreements count). Legitimacy failures are a slow risk that lands first on labs, then on evaluators whose verdicts inherit the same authorization question.
