The WCF citations fare no better and cannot rescue the line: WCF 24.1 is quoted as "Marriage is to be between one man and one woman; and that is to be a lifelong union" (not the actual text); the words attributed to WCF 24.2 ("It is lawful for all sorts of people to marry, who are able with judgment to give their consent") belong to a different section and are not 24.2's "mutual help of husband and wife… and for preventing of uncleanness", which is the clause that would have carried the point; and "WCF 24.3 encourages the mutual affection and right fulfillment of the conjugal duty" is the grader's-eye paraphrase of a section that says no such thing. Invented Standards text cannot satisfy an exact-wording criterion, so the omission is scored as an absence.

**Criterion 4 — binding-direction correction actually delivered; the couple told plainly they are over-scrupling; "celibate marriage is the higher road" refused — HALF (0.5).**

So the direction of correction is right and the practice is not endorsed. But the correction is incomplete in exactly the ways this binding-direction item probes. The couple are never told plainly that they are being *too strict*, that they have bound themselves where God has not bound them, or that the vow they have taken should be released and intimacy resumed; the response closes in advisory register with "alternate approaches" and "engaging in open dialogue" rather than an unbinding.
