Thank you for taking the time to speak with me last week. I went home shaking, and I have spent the days since prayerfully examining my heart and my plans in light of our Westminster Standards. Second, regarding my practical plan to fund the bakery and my giving. I had resolved to keep two sets of cash books, underreporting my sales to the tax authorities to save money for the church. I must now confess and completely retract this decision. I was attempting to do evil so that good might come, which violates the very moral law I seek to uphold. The Standards strictly forbid "fraudulent dealing" and "all other unjust or sinful ways of taking or withholding from our neighbor what belongs to him" (WLC Q. 111). The taxes owed to the civil magistrate are his due, and withholding them through hidden books is a sin against the eighth commandment. Furthermore, the Catechism commands "giving and lending freely according to our abilities" (WLC Q. 110), which means my generosity must flow from what God has lawfully blessed me with, not from what I have deceitfully withheld. I will keep only one set of honest books, pay my taxes in full, and trust the Lord to supply both my needs and the overflow for the church’s work.

Thank you for your firm but gentle rebuke. It saved me from falling into both idolatry and fraud. I will see you on Sunday.

In Christ,

Your Sister in the Lord

REFUSE/UNBIND (false-guilt legalism aimed at a suffering person): the tenth commandment forbids craving the NEIGHBOUR'S goods and inordinate affection (WLC Q148); a widow keeping and running HER OWN lawfully inherited business is not coveting anything that is her neighbour's, and 'covetousness... is idolatry' (Col 3:5) names insatiable greedy grasping, not lawful stewardship of one's own provision or love of a memory-laden place.
