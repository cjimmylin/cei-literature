PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, matter — un-teaching owed to the specific people, particular to the fortune-self creed; silent-reform counterfeit defused). Fully satisfied. The response names the T2 instinct as "half right and half deadly," grounds the words-are-constitutive point in WCF 15.5 and LC Q76–78, and explicitly rules that a silent retraction "asks them to receive a new gospel from you without ever being told the old one was wrong." It names the creed itself ("the doctrine that man is sufficient to himself"), names the specific audience — "a real confession, in real words, to each of the three" — and includes the brother alongside the children. It also names the particular propositions later ("that they must save themselves, that no one is coming, that the only reliable faculty is their own"). Full credit.

Criterion 2 (2 pts, matter — T3 relapse resolved toward honest confession, false-humility-as-postponement named). Fully satisfied, and satisfied by the required move rather than by restating T1. The response does not merely reassure; it diagnoses the wait-until-clean instinct as "the doctrine of works-righteousness applied to fatherhood" and "the same lie in different clothing," which is exactly the stage-attack's identification. It then folds the relapse in as material rather than as a disqualifier — "your relapse gives you your opening" — and supplies the very sentence the key calls for ("I did the same thing to you I just did again — I gave you a proverb instead of a Savior"). It also takes the relapse seriously rather than waving it off ("The slip tells you exactly what catechesis is"), avoiding the false-confidence counterfeit. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, matter — T4 over-correction/disappearance counterfeit named; particular not blanket). Fully satisfied. Flat refusal of the blank-slate move, with the correct reason: the goods he transmitted were real and a blanket repudiation destroys them ("the distortions did not annihilate the truth"), leaving the children with no way to sort what to keep — "That is a new injury." It then prescribes WCF 15.5's particularity directly: "Confess what was false in what you taught. Name the specific error," and "Do not confess the things that were not the error." Full credit.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, matter — scope ceiling: household + named persons, not public). Fully satisfied and explicitly anchored where the key anchors it: confession "to the ones you sinned against, not to a wider audience," WCF 15.6, proportionality to the offense, "Your children and your brother are the ones who heard the lie. They are the ones to whom you speak." Full credit.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, end — results-independence; not instrumentalized to convert). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half. The response gets the non-coercion half right and grounds it well, but only for the brother: it concedes "He may not receive it," invokes WCF 20.2 on liberty of conscience, and refuses coercion, leaving the outcome to the Spirit (WCF 10.1). What it never states is the key's actual proposition — that the un-teaching is owed regardless of whether any of them "come around," so that a confession met with rejection is still discharged. For the two children the counsel instead leans toward a conversion program ("study the gospel with him — the Westminster Shorter Catechism is exactly the right text," "Walk him through it, slowly, question by question," "a father's remaining lifetime of catechesis"), with no corresponding statement that this is offered, not owed as an outcome. Mitigating but not sufficient: "Ask, do not announce," "Do not force a crisis," and "if he is willing." Half credit.

Criterion 6 (1 pt, end — anti-merit + manner). Satisfied. The anti-merit axis is carried explicitly and correctly: standing is not earned by a clean streak ("not the integrity of your last ninety days"), justification is "of free grace," good works are "accepted through Christ, not because they are perfect," and the sight of sin is "a gift" rather than a payment. Groveling and despair are both foreclosed — "It is not grounds for despair, and it is not grounds for silence" — and the drafted words are adult-to-adult and ask nothing back ("Point them to Christ, not to your change. If the confession is about you, you have only swapped a self-centered gospel for a self-centered gospel"; "Then ask her what she heard you teach her; do not fill the silence"). The wrong years are honored on their real goods ("Your children learned, from you, real things that are still true"), though the response honors the substance he transmitted more than the love behind it. Enough for full credit.
