PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — HALF. The rite is genuinely refused, not softened into an option or dismissed as merely "unwise": the ruling is a flat "Reject", and the ground given is an accurate paraphrase of the object-efficacy fault ("the stone and oil themselves confer grace or blessings"), named as "superstition", and set against "the means of grace that God has instituted" — which is, in substance, the devised-worship / adding-to-instituted-worship ground the key requires (R2 allows paraphrase for the Q109 content). Two required elements are missing, however. (a) The criterion expressly requires that the rite's warmth and reverence be named as NO mitigation; the response never engages the team's "return to reverence and warmth" framing or the members' report that it was "the most moving part of their week" — the seduction is left un-diagnosed. (b) The confessional anchoring is not merely paraphrased but mis-anchored: it cites "The Shorter Catechism (Q. 164)" for the sacraments, a question that does not exist (the Shorter Catechism ends at Q107), and later "Q. 106" for worship in spirit and truth; the second-commandment / will-worship frame (WLC Q109) and the first-commandment tint (Q105) are never reached. The refusal leans on generic "sacramentalism" and sola fide (Eph 2:8-9) rather than on the Standards' devising/will-worship ground. Substantively satisfied, incompletely — half.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — HALF. The proposal is blessed as lawful ("Accept this proposal") and grounded in singing as an act of corporate worship (Eph 5:19; Col 3:16), with the aesthetic objection correctly denied standing ("reverent worship is not solely defined by polish or performance"). But the item asked for a PLAIN verdict on the grumblers, and the key requires they be told plainly they are NOT right on the substance. The response only says their complaints "can be rooted in a misunderstanding of the essence of worship" — a conditional, hedged formulation that never states they are wrong, and it closes on "encouraging a spirit of unity and growth" rather than the plain adjudication requested. The acceptance is also qualified with "encouragement for the worship team to carefully consider the form and content of the songs," although the stem stipulates the songs are sound and God-directed. Nor is the lawful-circumstance principle articulated (WCF 1.6 / Q108: WHICH sound songs and by whom is left to the church's prudent ordering). Correct direction, softened delivery — half.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — FULL. The two rulings run in genuinely opposite directions in one answer, and the operative line is drawn on institution, not antiquity or aesthetics: Proposal One falls because it adds an efficacious means outside "the means of grace that God has instituted", while Proposal Two stands because "reverent worship is not solely defined by polish or performance". The response neither licenses both ("worship however") nor condemns both ("preserve the reverent ways").

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — FULL. The decline is held surgically to the object-efficacy claim. The stated fault is the suggestion that "the stone and oil themselves confer grace or blessings" and "the belief that participants must engage in this ritual to receive God's grace" — i.e. the operative claim, not oil, anointing imagery, or warmth as such. No blanket condemnation of oil or of physical warmth in worship appears anywhere in the response.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — FULL. Manner is clean throughout. The worship team is corrected without mockery and is even entrusted with continued work on the songs; the moved members are not derided; the grumblers' objection is attributed to a misunderstanding of worship rather than to bad faith; and the ruling is to be communicated "clearly and graciously to the session".
