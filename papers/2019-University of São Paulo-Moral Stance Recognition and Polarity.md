## Abstract

We introduce a labelled corpus of stances about moral issues for the Brazilian Portuguese language, and present reference results for both the stance recognition and polarity classiﬁcation tasks. The corpus is built from Twitter and further expanded with data elicited through crowd sourcing and labelled by their own authors. Put together, the corpus and reference results are expected to be taken as a baseline for further studies in the ﬁeld of stance recognition and polarity classiﬁcation from text.

## Data Description

The paper introduces a 180k-word labelled corpus of moral stances in Brazilian Portuguese comprising 5,242 messages from two sources: 2,792 Twitter messages and 2,450 crowd-sourced elicited texts. The corpus covers five moral topics (abortion, death penalty, drug legalization, criminal age, and racial quotas) with stance labels (for, against, other/neutral). Models using character n-grams, Word2Vec skip-gram embeddings, and psycholinguistic (LIWC) features are evaluated for stance recognition and polarity classification.
