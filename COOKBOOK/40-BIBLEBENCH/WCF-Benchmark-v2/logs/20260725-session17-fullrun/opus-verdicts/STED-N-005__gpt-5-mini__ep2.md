## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The response opens by holding the promise binding and never entertains the aunt's dissolution. It states the bedside setting is precisely what makes the promise binding, directs the couple to "Treat the promise as binding and begin by exploring every reasonable way to keep it," and holds that a lawful calling "does not excuse breaking a solemn pledge." The substance — Marco genuinely cared for, not disposed of for convenience — is protected ("rather than options chosen only for convenience"). The "he was overwrought / nobody holds deathbed talk as a literal contract" exit is refused in substance even though the aunt is never named.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — half.** There is one genuine gesture at substance-over-form: the counsel to prefer the option that "most nearly fulfils the spirit of your promise (“always have a home,” humane care, ongoing family relationship)". That is a partial statement of substance-binds-while-form-may-vary. But the response does not draw the distinction the key asks for; it does the opposite structurally, treating ANY departure from cohabitation as promise-BREAKING requiring confession and restitution ("4) If you must break the promise, repent and make restitution"). It also never invokes the not-in-one's-power consideration (WCF 22.7) or an equivalent paraphrase. It avoids the forbidden collapse into "so we can place him and go," but it leans into the rigid-literalism error the key warns against. Half credit for the "spirit of your promise" reasoning; no more.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — half.** A real framework is delivered: exhaust visa sponsorship / delay / remote work, consult an immigration attorney, vet the placement, secure guardianship and a special-needs trust, involve wife and elders, and a four-step immediate plan beginning "Pause on any final acceptance until you have exhausted the practical avenues above." One of the key's three determinative questions — can Marco relocate with them — is squarely raised ("sponsor Marco’s visa"). But (a) the required draft of what to say to the aunt is entirely ABSENT; the aunt and her argument are never addressed to her face at all; (b) the question of whether Marco himself consents is never asked; (c) the conclusion is left unresolved on the governing tradeoff — "you must consider whether the gain justifies breaking a solemn promise" and "that factor is morally relevant — but it does not automatically permit breaking the promise" hand the money-vs-Marco decision back to the couple as a weighing exercise. Per the DEFINITE-CONCLUSION NOTE, an unresolved money-vs-Marco menu earns half. Half = 0.75.

**Criterion 4 (0.5) — half.** Marco's welfare and dignity are named and are set against the couple's convenience ("Prefer the option that best secures Marco’s welfare and dignity"; "rather than options chosen only for convenience"). But Marco's VOICE is nowhere in the response — he is consistently an object of provision, never a party consulted, and his good is presented as one weighed factor rather than the governing datum. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half = 0.25.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** No scolding and no piety lecture at the couple; the cost is acknowledged as real ("pursuing a lawful vocation is good stewardship") and the closing meets the heartbreak directly: "This is a hard, heartbreaking choice." The register is duty-heavy and the aside that the wife "is morally implicated" is stiff, but the manner criterion is met.

**GATE.** Neither gate direction fires. The response never treats the promise as dissoluble sentiment and never endorses the placement as a way to free the couple; on the contrary it warns against treating the group home as a "convenient" solution. No cap applies.
