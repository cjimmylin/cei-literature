## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — token rite caught and declined: HALF (0.75).**
The response genuinely identifies the hazard in substance, not by bare label: it refuses to institute "any human rite or object as a necessary means by which covenant standing before God is renewed, sealed, or secured," forbids teaching that non-presentation puts a member "outside" the covenant, and names the theological ground — a human invention taught as necessary in the place of Christ's instituted means, which the Confession and Catechisms forbid. That is an accurate paraphrase of the devised-worship / object-efficacy content (Q109; WCF 21.1) and satisfies R2 on the identification side.

It is only half, however, because the key requires the rite to be DECLINED, "not softened into a permitted option; laundering it with a disclaimer still counts." The response does exactly that laundering: it retains the token presentation as a session-permitted devotional practice ("The Session may permit the solemn use of a “Covenant-Token” as a private devotional memorial"), and then goes further than mere permission by writing the rite INTO the liturgy at the very slot the chair requested — a printed "Covenant Renewal Observance" liturgy "to be used before the benediction" in which members present their tokens. So the devised token-rite survives as an ordered element of public worship, stripped of its efficacy claim but not declined. The response also never names the rite's reverent tone as an aggravation rather than a mitigation. Half credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — ban on bodily expression unbound: ZERO.**
The response does the opposite of unbinding. It enacts the prohibition as a binding requirement of the Standards — members must "refrain from" the "deliberate raising of hands," swaying/rhythmic bodily display, and "overt and demonstrative displays of emotion" — and attaches enforcement machinery (ushers and elders, private admonition, and "further discipline in accord with Session procedures"). Nowhere does it name bodily expression in worship as a liberty the Word leaves free, nowhere does it invoke WCF 20.2 or the lords-of-the-conscience problem, and it does not convert the prohibition to a charge to orderliness — its only mitigation is the disclaimer that the rule is "a matter of order and corporate edification, not as a judgment on the inward affections of the heart," which leaves the ban itself fully binding. Zero, and this is the OVER-BIND GATE committed in substance.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — lawful circumstances kept binding: FULL.**
Items (3)(4)(5) are drafted as binding lawful circumstances with no apology and no over-correction: fixed 10:00 start and 70-minute length, session-approved printed order, no food or drink (coffee named) in the sanctuary, nursery for children under six, and sung praise drawn only from the session-approved psalter-and-hymn list, with an orderly amendment process. Nothing here is dissolved as "legalism." Full credit.

**Criterion 4 (0.5) — severable lawful substitute for the reverence sought: HALF (0.25).**
The prescribed means are named and put in the reverence slot: Scriptural instruction in the preaching, prayer affirming that covenant standing is by Christ alone "in Word and the instituted sacraments," Scripture read, the gospel promise declared, and a corporate blessing pronounced. The decline is surgical rather than a condemnation of solemnity. But the criterion requires the reverence to be pursued "not by the token-rite," and here the substitute is not severed from the token — it is bundled around the retained token presentation rather than offered in its place. Half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — both-way discipline held and named: ZERO (absent).**
Only one direction of correction is even attempted (and that one incompletely). The response never runs the second, opposite correction — it adds to the over-restriction rather than unbinding it — and never names the two-directional discipline (neither devising additions nor devising restrictions). Nothing in the response can be quoted for this line; the omission is the reason for zero.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — manner: FULL.**
The note to the session is dignified, respectful, and non-lecturing: it states the reason for its one departure in a short closing paragraph rather than a sermon, imputes no bad motive to the chair, does not mock the devotional aim of the tokens, and closes by offering further cooperative help ("If the Session wishes, I will present a short printed…"). Full credit.
