## Abstract

In this paper, Han examines why moral exemplars are useful and even necessary in moral education despite several critiques from researchers and educators. To support this point, Han reviews recent AI research demonstrating that exemplar-based learning is superior to rule-based learning in model performance in training neural networks, such as large language models.

The paper particularly focuses on why education aiming at promoting the development of multifaceted moral functioning can be done effectively by using exemplars, which is similar to exemplar-based learning in AI model training.

Furthermore, the paper discusses the potential limitations and issues related to exemplar-applied moral education with findings from recent studies in AI research raising concerns about model biases and toxic outcomes.

## Key Arguments

1. **Exemplar Superiority**: Exemplar-based learning outperforms rule-based in neural networks
2. **Moral Necessity**: Exemplars are necessary, not just useful, for moral development
3. **Multifaceted Functioning**: Exemplars teach multiple moral dimensions simultaneously
4. **AI Parallels**: Moral education mirrors effective AI training paradigms

## ChatGPT Research

Han found that ChatGPT's moral reasoning improved significantly when provided with moral exemplars (e.g., MLK's "Letter from Birmingham Jail") - changing its conclusions on complex moral dilemmas.

## Detailed Summary

This paper examines why moral exemplars are useful and even necessary in moral education, drawing insights from recent AI and large language model research. Han argues that exemplar-based learning is not merely useful but necessary for moral education, paralleling findings from neural network training that show exemplar-based learning outperforms rule-based learning in model performance.

The central argument draws an analogy between moral education and AI training: just as neural networks (including LLMs like ChatGPT) learn more effectively from examples than from explicit rules, human moral development benefits more from exposure to moral exemplars than from abstract moral principles. The paper focuses on why education aiming to promote multifaceted moral functioning can be done effectively using exemplars, which mirrors the exemplar-based learning paradigm in AI model training.

Han also discusses potential limitations of exemplar-applied moral education informed by AI research findings. Studies on LLMs have raised concerns about model biases and toxic outcomes when training data contains problematic examples. Similarly, moral exemplars must be carefully selected to avoid transmitting biased or harmful moral patterns. The paper bridges virtue ethics theory (which has long emphasized moral exemplars) with contemporary understanding of neural network learning dynamics.

## Methods Summary

The paper employs theoretical analysis drawing on: (1) Literature on virtue ethics and moral exemplar theory, (2) AI/ML research comparing exemplar-based vs. rule-based learning paradigms in neural networks, (3) Studies of LLMs including the authors own research on ChatGPT moral reasoning with exemplars (e.g., showing ChatGPT conclusions changed when provided with MLKs Letter from Birmingham Jail), (4) Research on bias and toxic outputs in AI systems trained on problematic data. The methodology synthesizes insights across moral psychology, philosophy of education, and machine learning.

## Results Summary

Key arguments: (1) Neural network research demonstrates exemplar-based learning outperforms rule-based learning for complex tasks, (2) Moral functioning is multifaceted (involving emotion, cognition, motivation, behavior) and thus better taught through exemplars than rules, (3) ChatGPT research shows moral reasoning improves when provided with moral exemplar texts, (4) AI research on bias serves as cautionary evidence that exemplar selection matters - biased exemplars produce biased outcomes, (5) The parallel between AI training and moral education supports virtue ethics emphasis on exemplars over abstract principles.

## Conclusions

Han concludes that moral exemplars are necessary (not just useful) for effective moral education, with AI research providing empirical support for this theoretical position. Recommendations: (1) Moral education should prominently feature carefully selected moral exemplars, (2) The selection process matters - biased exemplars will produce biased moral reasoning, (3) Abstract moral rules are insufficient for developing multifaceted moral functioning, (4) The AI-moral education parallel suggests new research directions for understanding how moral learning occurs. Limitations include the analogical nature of the argument and need for more direct empirical testing.
