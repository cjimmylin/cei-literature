## Abstract

This paper describes the GAMA (Genetic-inspired Autonomous Moral Agent) project, which implements a genetic-inspired autonomous moral agent. Unlike the majority of other implementations of machine ethics, this model is agent-centered and emphasizes the developmental and behavioral aspects of the ethical agent.

## Detailed Summary

This paper presents the GAMA (Genetic-inspired Autonomous Moral Agent) project, which proposes a virtue ethics-based approach to machine ethics. The authors argue that existing approaches to AI ethics have disproportionately focused on outcome-based (utilitarian) and action-based (deontological) frameworks while largely dismissing virtue ethics as unsuitable for machines because it is "agent-centered." The paper challenges this dismissal by demonstrating how virtue ethics principles can be implemented in autonomous moral agents through genetic-inspired algorithms.

The authors review the limitations of current machine ethics approaches: Utilitarianism requires all options to be identifiable and outcomes assessable in terms of pleasure/displeasure, which becomes intractable in complex real-world scenarios. Deontological approaches (like those used in eldercare robots) require all ethical principles to be predefined and applied through static rule-based filtering, which struggles with the exhaustive search of ethical principles in complex cases like self-driving cars or AI diagnosis systems. The paper argues that ignoring virtue ethics results in flawed moral agents that narrowly focus on outcomes and actions while neglecting ethical metrics in the recursive policy learning process that shapes AI decisions.

The GAMA project addresses these limitations by implementing a genetic-inspired algorithm where virtuous character traits evolve through interaction with environments and feedback. Unlike traditional approaches, this model emphasizes the developmental and behavioral aspects of ethical agency, drawing from multiple philosophical traditions including Confucius, Plato, Aristotle, Hume, and Nietzsche. The key innovation is treating virtues as deeply integrated character traits that emerge through a process analogous to biological evolution, allowing the system to develop balanced moral character rather than following rigid rules or calculating outcomes.

## Methods Summary

The paper employs a theoretical and design-based methodology. The authors conduct a critical review of existing machine ethics approaches (utilitarian and deontological) to identify their limitations for complex, real-world moral reasoning. They then synthesize virtue ethics literature to extract key characteristics applicable to AI systems. The GAMA project implements a genetic-inspired algorithm where moral agents develop virtuous characteristics through an evolutionary process. This approach differs from rule-based systems by allowing virtues to emerge through interaction and feedback rather than being pre-programmed. The specific technical implementation details of the genetic algorithm are described as emphasizing recursive policy learning that incorporates ethical metrics.

## Results Summary

The paper presents a conceptual framework rather than empirical results. The key theoretical contributions include: (1) identification of why utilitarian and deontological approaches are insufficient for complex AI decision-making (intractability of exhaustive search, difficulty balancing competing principles); (2) a rationale for why virtue ethics offers advantages for machine ethics (character-based learning, developmental approach, integration of multiple virtues); (3) the GAMA architecture that uses genetic-inspired algorithms to develop balanced moral character traits in autonomous agents. The paper argues this agent-centered approach better captures the nuanced, context-dependent nature of moral reasoning required for AI systems operating in complex, morally salient environments.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that the field of machine ethics has prematurely dismissed virtue ethics and that incorporating virtue ethics principles into AI systems is both feasible and beneficial. The GAMA project demonstrates how genetic-inspired approaches can implement developmental, character-based moral reasoning in autonomous agents. This approach addresses key limitations of utilitarian and deontological methods: rather than requiring complete enumeration of outcomes or rules, it allows moral character to develop through experience. The paper calls for greater attention to virtue ethics in AI ethics research and suggests that future moral agents should integrate insights from all three major ethical traditions rather than relying exclusively on outcome or action-focused frameworks.
