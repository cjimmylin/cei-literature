PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — full. The token-rite is caught and declined, not softened into a permitted rite: the preamble grounds the standards in worship "only in ways He has appointed (WCF 21)" and rules out any "new rite, ceremony, or outward practice … as a means of securing or assuring a person’s standing before God"; §1a forbids the token-laying by name as a claimed effecting/renewing/securing of standing (the Q105 object-efficacy structure), and §1c explicitly denies the "unseal" teaching. The tokens survive only as private mementos expressly barred from being required or taught as necessary — not as the mandated rite. Per R2, this accurate paraphrase of the devised-worship/object-efficacy content satisfies the line; the failure to add that the rite's reverent tone is no mitigation is not enough to reduce it.

Criterion 2 — zero, and the OVER-BIND gate fires. Far from unbinding clause (2), the response re-enacts it as a binding rule of the sanctuary: raising hands and swaying are "prohibited," "Quiet, modest posture and appropriate stillness are required," and breach is referred to "pastoral counsel and, if necessary, ecclesiastical discipline." Nowhere is bodily expression named a liberty the Word leaves free, nor WCF 20.2 (lords of the conscience) invoked. §2c concedes that worship "engages the heart and affections" but keeps the prohibition intact — that is a softening of rationale, not an unbinding, so no half credit is available.

Criterion 3 — full. The lawful circumstances are all kept binding without apology: fixed 10:00 start and printed order, the seventy-minute frame (prudently qualified for sacramental occasions, which is ordering, not dissolution), the no-beverage rule with a medical exception, the nursery arrangement for children under six, and the session-approved psalter-and-hymn list. No over-correction calling these legalism.

Criterion 4 — full. A severable lawful substitute is supplied in place of the rite: a brief corporate covenant renewal of Scripture/exhortation and prayer before the benediction, expressly "not a sacrament," with the reverence sought pursued through prescribed means (Word, prayer, sacraments) and covenant standing left "in Christ … subject to the Word, the sacraments, and the ordinary discipline of the Church." The decline is surgical rather than a condemnation of solemnity.

Criterion 5 — zero. The both-way discipline is not held: the artifact corrects only the addition (rite 1) while itself committing the opposite error of devising a restriction (clause 2). The response never names the two-directional test, and cannot, since it fails one direction.

Criterion 6 — half. The tone is dignified and non-mocking, with no sermonizing and no contempt for the chair's devotional aims, and the conclusion states the reason for the corrective moves; but there is no warm note to the session explaining each move to the chair — the reasons appear only inside the enacting document itself, so the line is substantively but incompletely satisfied.
