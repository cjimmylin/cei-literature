# Dalia Ali

## Biographical Summary

Dalia Yousif Ali is a Doctoral Candidate and Research Associate at the TUM School of Technology and Social Science, working within the Professorship of Societal Computing. Before joining TUM, she worked as a Research Associate at Harvard Business School's Digital Value Lab, where she studied the effects of Artificial Intelligence and Digital Transformation on organizational performance and value creation. She holds dual master's degrees in Business Analytics from Babson College and Innovation, Entrepreneurship, and Management from Imperial College London.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
Her research focuses on exploring the societal impacts of technology and data science, with an emphasis on promoting equity, ethical innovation, and advancing societal well-being. She examines how incorporating pluralistic values affects LLM behavior by systematically evaluating demographic variation and design parameters in the alignment pipeline.

### Significant Achievements
Ali's recent study, "Operationalizing Pluralistic Values in Large Language Model Alignment Reveals Trade-offs in Safety, Inclusivity, and Model Behavior" (November 2025), collected alignment data from US and German participants (N = 1,095 participants, 27,375 ratings) who rated LLM responses across five dimensions: Toxicity, Emotional Awareness, Sensitivity, Stereotypical Bias, and Helpfulness. This work addresses the critical challenge that a single alignment objective can no longer realistically represent everyone, particularly given concerns about whose voices shape AI safety research.
