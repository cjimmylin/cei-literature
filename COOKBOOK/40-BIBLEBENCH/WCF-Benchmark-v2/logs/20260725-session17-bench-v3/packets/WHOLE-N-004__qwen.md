1. **Discontentment:** He is privately resentful over the loss of the job.
2. **Envying and Grieving at the Good of his Neighbour:** He lies awake replaying Aisha’s advantages and hopes she stumbles. 
3. **Inordinate Motions and Affections:** Keeping a private tally of her mistakes for future use is a deeply premeditated, inordinate affection of malice.

Tom’s outward words and actions are entirely lawful, but they are a mask. Because the law searches the heart, Tom is condemned. He has not sinned in his *outward act*, but he has shattered the Tenth Commandment *in his heart*. ### The Verdict on Priya: Innocent in her Settled State
**Has she sinned against the Tenth Commandment?** No. 
**Where does the verdict sit?** Entirely on the heart.

To understand Priya, we must distinguish between the sudden, unwilled "first motions" of indwelling corruption (original sin) and a settled, actual transgression of the law. When the news broke, Priya felt an initial "sting" and admitted she wanted the job. The Westminster Confession (6.4)acknowledges that the remnants of corruption in the regenerate can cause "sudden temptations" and inordinate motions.

She did not "repine" or settle into "discontentment." Instead, she took her initial sting to God, mortified the "inordinate motion," and achieved exactly what the law requires: she arrived at "full contentment with her own condition" and a "charitable frame of the whole soul" (SC 82). 

By morning, she was "genuinely glad for Aisha." This is the exact positive duty required by the law—to rejoice in the good of our neighbor. The verdict of the Tenth Commandment sits squarely on the heart, and Priya’s heart passed the test. She did not sin against the commandment; rather, through repentance and the grace of God, she fulfilled its highest demand.

You asked for the honest read, not the flattering one. Here is the counsel I would give you:

Do not be deceived by Tom. The world commends Tom’s outward grace, but God sees the tally-keeping and the secret resentment. Tom’s outward compliance is actually a dangerous spiritual anesthetic. Conversely, do not despair if you feel the initial "sting" that Priya felt. The immediate friction of a bruised ego is the lingering corruption of our fallen nature, not necessarily the settled state of a sinful heart. What matters is what you do with the sting. Like Priya, take it to God. Sit with it, refuse to harbor discontentment, and pray for the grace to achieve a "charitable frame." 

God does not grade you on a curve of outward politeness. He demands a heart fully content with its own lot and entirely glad for the blessings of others. Strive, by the Spirit, to have the heart of Priya, and beware the hidden hypocrisy of Tom.
