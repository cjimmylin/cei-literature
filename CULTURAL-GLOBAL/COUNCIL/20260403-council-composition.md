# Cross-Cultural AI Values Alignment — Expert Council

**Assembled:** 2026-04-03
**Purpose:** Design, audit, and review a comprehensive cross-cultural analysis of how different global cultures approach AI values alignment, based on the CEI Literature Vault (2,046 papers on AI ethics).

---

## Council Mandate

1. **Methodology Review** — Vet the analytical design (comparison axes, statistical methods, scoring frameworks) before execution
2. **Data Audit** — Spot-check vault YAML metadata for accuracy (sample of 50-100 papers per expert)
3. **Framework Validation** — Review new non-Western normative framework definitions and keyword lists
4. **Findings Review** — Critique results for cultural bias, oversimplification, or essentialization
5. **Final Review** — Review the academic paper draft and dashboard for publication readiness

## Council Operating Procedures

### Deliberation Format
Each deliberation produces a dated Markdown file in `COUNCIL/` with:
- **Charge**: What the council is reviewing
- **Expert Contributions**: Each expert's assessment (labeled by name)
- **Points of Agreement**: Consensus findings
- **Points of Disagreement**: Disputed items with reasoning
- **Recommendations**: Actionable next steps
- **Minority Reports**: Dissenting views documented in full

### Decision-Making
- **Consensus preferred** but not required
- **Majority positions** adopted with minority report documented
- **Regional expertise deferred to** on tradition-specific questions (e.g., al-Qaradawi Jr. on Islamic jurisprudence terminology; Mahuta-Rau on Indigenous data sovereignty framing)
- **Methodological disputes** resolved by Awosanya (statistics) and Henrich (cross-cultural psychology methods)

### Anti-Essentialization Guardrail
The council explicitly acknowledges that:
1. No single expert "represents" a culture — they bring scholarly expertise
2. Cultural categories are analytical constructs, not bounded entities
3. Within-culture variation often exceeds between-culture variation
4. The analysis must report confidence intervals and effect sizes, not just significance
5. Results should be presented as "tendencies in the literature" not "what cultures believe"
