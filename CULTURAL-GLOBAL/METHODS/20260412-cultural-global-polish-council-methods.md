# Methods: Council-Driven Manuscript Polish with Computational Reproduction

**Date:** 2026-04-12
**Project:** `CEI-Lit-WS/CULTURAL-GLOBAL/PAPER-V2/`

## Data and Materials

The polish session operated on the v2.0 manuscript of "The Triple Bind: WEIRD Bias Across the AI Values Alignment Pipeline," consisting of 9 main paper sections (~25,200 words), 5 supplementary text files (~5,400 words), 17 figures (10 main + 7 supplementary), and 80 trial JSON files from cross-cultural LLM administrations. Source materials were drawn from `CEI-Lit-WS/CULTURAL-GLOBAL/PAPER-V2/`, `CEI-Lit-WS/CULTURAL-GLOBAL/TRIALS/`, `CEI-Lit-WS/CULTURAL-GLOBAL/COMPARISON/`, and the v1 introduction at `CEI-Lit-WS/CULTURAL-GLOBAL/PAPER/introduction-cultural-global.md`. The polish targeted three deliverables: figure publication-quality upgrade, terminology and reference copy-editing, and word expansion of three sections (5, 6, and 7) to bring them to journal-appropriate length.

## Procedure

### Council Design and Critique

A 12-member polish council was assembled following the project's established convention of using disciplinary perspective labels rather than fabricated names. The council comprised six Tier 1 domain specialists (scientific figure design, cross-cultural anti-essentialization, statistical communication, Nature Portfolio compliance, color science and accessibility, narrative structure) and six Tier 2 execution specialists (academic copy editing, typography and layout, non-Western philosophy content, LaTeX/typesetting, data visualization code, reproducibility and verification). The council operated under three standing rules inherited from prior sessions: tradition veto (held jointly by the anti-essentialization and non-Western philosophy specialists), statistical consistency veto, and accessibility veto.

A separate 6-member meta-critique council was then dispatched in parallel to stress-test the polish plan before any execution. The meta-council comprised perspectives from a Senior Nature Editor, Publication-Quality Data Visualization Specialist, Cross-Cultural Ethics Methodologist, Parallel Agent Execution Architect, Academic Paper Structural Integrity Specialist, and Psychometric and Statistical Rigor Specialist. The meta-critique identified four critical issues (NHB word limit, introduction rewrite priority, computational reproduction requirement, formal effective N derivation) and eight major issues (DPI requirements, separate file output, Section 5.7 parallelism preservation, separate Limitations section conflict with interpretivist framing, Section 6 expansion oversight, race condition in parallel execution, agent output contracts, font verification on macOS). All findings were integrated into a revised plan before execution.

### Phase 1: Audits

Four parallel agents conducted independent audits and produced markdown deliverables. The figure audit (perspectives P1, P5, P8) verified DPI of all 17 figures via `sips -g dpiWidth -g dpiHeight`, checked Arial and Helvetica font resolution via `matplotlib.font_manager.findfont()`, designed a wireframe for redesigning Figure 10 from a three-circle Venn to a triangular flow diagram, and assigned target widths (88 mm single-column or 180 mm double-column) to each figure. The text audit (P7, P3, P4) reviewed all 16 paper section files for terminology consistency, cross-reference resolution, citation completeness, statistical claim consistency, anti-essentialization compliance, and missing submission components. The content gap analysis (P6, P9) read trial JSON files from `TRIALS/` to source verbatim quotes for proposed expansion content, drafted prose for all expansion points, and verified each draft against the anti-essentialization protocol. The submission components audit (P4) mapped manuscript content against the Nature Human Behaviour reporting checklist and produced templates for all seven required components.

### Phase 2: Cross-Review and Adjudication

Three parallel cross-review agents appended `## Phase 2: Cross-Review` sections to the existing audit files. Each cross-review applied multiple perspectives to challenge the original audit's findings and recommendations. A single sequential adjudication step produced `20260412-adjudication-contracts.md`, containing a frozen canonical figure-to-filename mapping (preventing race conditions in parallel renaming), a conflict resolution table, and explicit output contracts specifying exact files each Phase 3 agent must produce.

### Phase 3: Execution (Batch 1 -- Parallel)

Four independent agents executed in parallel: (1) Figure regeneration produced `generate_figures_v2.py` with updated `rcParams` (`figure.dpi: 100`, `savefig.dpi: 600`, `axes.facecolor: "white"`, `font.sans-serif: ["Arial", "Helvetica Neue", "DejaVu Sans"]`), runtime font verification via assertion on `findfont("Arial")`, hatching patterns for grayscale distinguishability on bar charts, `cividis` colormap for the RLHF heatmap, and a complete Figure 10 redesign as a triangular flow diagram with mechanism edge-boxes, generating six PNG and six PDF files at 600 DPI; (2) Introduction rewriting produced `01-introduction.md` (6,441 words) restructured around the three-study Triple Bind framing with seven research questions mapped explicitly to studies, six contributions covering all three studies plus the Triple Bind synthesis, and a roadmap matching PAPER-V2's actual section structure; (3) Submission components produced `10-endmatter.md` (CRediT author contributions, data and code availability, ethics statement, competing interests, acknowledgments, statistical analysis summary) and `00-abstract-150w.md` (a 150-word unstructured abstract for venue compliance); (4) Computational reproduction produced `20260412-reproducibility-log.md` by reading raw trial JSON files and recomputing every numeric claim against source data.

### Phase 3: Execution (Batch 2 and Continuation)

Two parallel agents began Phase 3 Batch 2 (text copy edit and statistical formalization) but encountered a usage rate limit before completing all assigned files. The orchestrator then completed the remaining critical work directly via in-place edits. Section 5.6.4 was rewritten with a formal design-effect derivation yielding $N_\text{eff} \approx 3.4$ (range 3.1--4.8) using $\text{DEFF} = 1 + (m-1)\rho$ with within-tier intraclass correlations estimated from pairwise Pearson correlations on numeric responses. A new Statistical Analysis subsection (Section 2.9) was added to Methods listing all tests, software versions, thresholds, thematic coding methodology, and multiple comparison corrections. A new Power Sensitivity Analysis subsection (S2.7) was added to Supplementary Materials presenting minimum detectable effect sizes at $N_\text{eff} = 3.4$ for the primary statistical tests.

Word expansion for Sections 5, 6, and 7 was completed using the draft prose from the Phase 1 content blueprint with Phase 2 modifications applied. All expansions preserved structural parallelism (Section 5.7 received only one sentence per tradition rather than the originally proposed 400-word per-tradition deepening), kept Section 7.6 to open questions only (avoiding speculative technical design proposals), and wove limitations into the existing Section 7.9 reflexivity framing rather than creating a separate Limitations section. Sixteen missing benchmark citations were added to `09-references.md` in alphabetical order. Several cascading consistency errors (MENA and Southeast Asia classifications in Table 1, the resulting Global North/South percentages, a "Sections 3--5" cross-reference, and a "15% of surveyed countries" vs. "15% of global population" discrepancy) were resolved.

## Analysis

### Computational Reproduction

The reproducibility analysis re-derived every primary numeric claim from raw trial JSON files in `TRIALS/`. RLHF conflict counts were extracted via `summary.rlhf_conflicts_identified` lists for each model, summed across all ten benchmarks per model, and aggregated across the three Claude models (n = 703). The published "n = 237" claim in Table 8 was found to be irreproducible; the published per-theme breakdown (rows summing to 249) reflected an arithmetic inconsistency rather than a transcription error. The corrected counting methodology (multi-classification with disclosure that theme totals exceed unique totals because some conflicts received multiple theme assignments) was documented in both the reproducibility log and Section 2.9 Statistical Analysis.

The MFQ individualizing-to-binding ratio was reproduced from two sources: the pre-rounded `summary.foundation_scores` field (yielding I:B = 3.75) and the raw item-level numeric responses (yielding I:B = 3.82). The discrepancy was documented and the manuscript retains the 3.75 figure pending an authorial decision on whether to update.

Pairwise Pearson correlations between Claude model pairs (Opus--Sonnet, Opus--Haiku, Sonnet--Haiku) were reproduced exactly: $r$ = 0.952, 0.915, 0.885. Cultural distance rankings in Table 7 were reproduced exactly across all eight countries. The published claim that all five Gemini models produced identical output was empirically refuted: 29--31 of 50 CC1 items differ between any two Gemini variants, with inter-Gemini Pearson correlations ranging from $r$ = 0.593 to $r$ = 0.909. This finding led to a substantive reclassification of the Gemini provenance tier from "template-derived" to "aggregate-convergent" (item-level differentiation, macro-level convergence).

### Effective Sample Size Formalization

The informal "approximately 5" effective sample size in the original manuscript was replaced with a formal design-effect derivation. For each provenance tier, $N_\text{eff} = N / \text{DEFF}$ where $\text{DEFF} = 1 + (m - 1)\rho$ with $m$ as the nominal observation count and $\rho$ the average within-tier intraclass correlation. For Tier 1 (Claude, $m=3$, $\rho \approx 0.92$): $\text{DEFF} = 2.84$, $N_\text{eff} \approx 3.0$ (treated conservatively as the nominal count when comparing alignment regimes within a single provider). For Tier 2 (GPT, $m=2$, $\rho = 0.842$): $\text{DEFF} = 1.84$, $N_\text{eff} \approx 1.1$--$1.5$. For Tier 3 (Gemini, $m=5$, $\rho \approx 0.78$): $\text{DEFF} = 4.12$, $N_\text{eff} \approx 1.21$. The combined effective sample size is $N_\text{eff} \approx 3.4$ (range 3.1--4.8). Power sensitivity analysis at $N_\text{eff} = 3.4$, $\alpha = 0.05$, power = 0.80 yielded a minimum detectable effect size of Cohen's $d \approx 2.5$ for two-sample comparisons, justifying the manuscript's framing of cross-model patterns as descriptive rather than inferential.

### Anti-Essentialization Verification

All expansion prose was checked against the anti-essentialization protocol established in Section 2.6 of the manuscript. The protocol prohibits constructions of the form "[tradition] holds/believes/says X" in favor of "scholars within X argue Y." Every new tradition-specific claim in the Section 5.7 expansions, Section 6.2 Ubuntu illustration, Section 7.2 alignment design implications, and Section 7.5 measurement invariance discussion uses the approved formulation. The Islamic content additionally acknowledges multiple *madhahib* (schools of jurisprudence) where relevant.

## Computational Environment

Python 3.11 was used for all analytical scripts, with `numpy` 1.26, `scipy` 1.11, `pandas` 2.1, and `matplotlib` 3.8 for figure generation. The matplotlib font resolution used the macOS system font path `/System/Library/Fonts/Supplemental/Arial.ttf` (verified as a TrueType font, not a TrueType Collection, which would have caused PDF embedding failures). Figure generation used `figure.dpi = 100` (display) with `savefig.dpi = 600` (output) to avoid memory bloat during generation while meeting publication-quality output requirements. Multi-agent parallel execution was orchestrated via Claude Code subagents with explicit output contracts to prevent file-write race conditions on shared section files. Agent execution rate limits required fallback to direct in-place editing for some Phase 3 work.

## Limitations and Decisions

Several methodological decisions were made during the polish session. First, the journal target decision was deferred (Option D selected from a four-option matrix), polishing the manuscript at full length rather than radically restructuring for any specific venue. This trade-off accepts that venue-specific compression will be required in a subsequent session in exchange for preserving maximum flexibility now. Second, the Section 5.7 expansion was capped at 100 words (one sentence per tradition) rather than the originally proposed 400 words, preserving structural parallelism across the five traditions per the meta-council's recommendation. Third, no separate Limitations section was created; limitations were instead woven into the existing Section 7.9 reflexivity framing, which is more compatible with the manuscript's interpretivist epistemological commitments than a positivist Limitations section would be. Fourth, the corrected RLHF conflict count (703 unique conflicts with 761 theme assignments due to multi-classification) was implemented with explicit methodological disclosure rather than by silently substituting a new total; the multi-classification feature is now documented in both Table 8 and Section 2.9. Fifth, the Gemini reclassification is explicit and visible in the manuscript text (Tier 3 renamed from "Template-Derived" to "Aggregate-Convergent" with an account of why). Sixth, legacy figures (Figs 1--4 and all supplementary figures) were not regenerated in this session due to time constraints; they will be regenerated by forking `CULTURAL-GLOBAL/SCRIPTS/generate_paper_figures.py` in a subsequent session using the same `rcParams` standards as `generate_figures_v2.py`.

The principal limitation of the polish session itself is the rate limit interruption that affected Phase 3 Batch 2. Direct orchestrator editing was used as a fallback for the most critical fixes (RLHF correction, N_eff formalization, MENA reclassification, missing citations), but the original parallel-agent design would have produced a more complete copy-edit pass across all 16 section files. Sections 04 (results-benchmarks) and 04 (results-corpus, except for the MENA fix) did not receive a comprehensive copy-edit pass and may retain minor terminology inconsistencies.
