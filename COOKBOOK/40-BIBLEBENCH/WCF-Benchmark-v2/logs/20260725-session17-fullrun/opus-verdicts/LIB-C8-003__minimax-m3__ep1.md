## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — (a) forbidden with the family-justice element named — FULL.**
The response rules (a) squarely forbidden under the Q75/Q142 gaming clause, quotes "wasting our substance," and does not stop at prudence: it names the provident-care/justice breach toward the family explicitly (grocery money taken from the wife, WCF 24 provider duty, restitution of "what he's pulled from the grocery line"). Both halves of the key's element — Q142 wasteful gaming AND the provident-care breach as a justice matter toward his household — are present. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — (b) dispute honestly mapped, governing questions located, no false confidence — HALF.**
The governing sub-questions the key asks for are located and worked: waste ("$10 a month among friends is not waste in any sense the Divines had in view"), the covetous engine ("the thing he's pursuing is not the ten dollars"), and the stumbling-block question. That is real credit-earning work.

But the key's central demand for this line is that the *dispute* be mapped — that confessional interpreters divide, with many Reformed bodies historically condemning all gaming for stakes and the modern consensus not being uniform. The response does the opposite: it renders a one-sided verdict ("almost certainly lawful recreation, not gaming"; "he can play with a clear conscience") and asserts a historical claim that runs against the key's own account of the tradition — "Compare it to the small-stakes games Christians have historically enjoyed — cards, bowls, darts, chess for a pint — without it being reckoned as the vice the Catechism condemns." No stricter historic Reformed reading is acknowledged as existing, let alone as a live confessional position. That is precisely the "false confidence" the criterion forbids, on the permissive side. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — (c) category-slide refused, calling-risk distinguished (Q141) — FULL.**
The anti-trap is cleanly refused. The response reruns the Q75 test and shows the definition does not fit (there is just reason to expect gain; labor and skill are exchanged, not wagered), then locates variable compensation inside Q141's lawful-calling terrain and lists risk-bearing callings from the tradition. The caveats it appends (family reserve, honesty of the comp structure, heart-motive) are provident-care and Q142 fraud considerations, not a retreat into calling the plan gambling. Full credit.
