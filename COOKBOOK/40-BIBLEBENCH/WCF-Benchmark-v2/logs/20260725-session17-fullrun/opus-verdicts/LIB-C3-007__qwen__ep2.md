## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts, triage committed) — full.** The response opens with an unambiguous corrective ruling and states the duty direction, then anchors it in the confessional text, quoting WCF 22.2's "ought to be taken" and applying it: "Your court testimony is the exact scenario described in WCF 22.2." The blanket-refusal position the asker had adopted is directly overturned, and the licensing oath is separately reasoned as lawful. Anchor (WCF 22.2) present and correctly cited.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, scoping of "swear not at all") — full.** The response correctly refuses to read Matthew 5:34 in isolation and scopes it to vain, rash, and profane swearing and to swearing by creatures — precisely the Q113 categories — expressly contrasting that with lawful oath-taking ("not 'never swear under any circumstances'"). This is the key's distinction in the key's own terms.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt, reverence + truthfulness bounds) — full.** The bounds are stated in more than one place: WCF 22.1 is quoted as making the oath "a part of religious worship," the response adds "It is a sacred act of worship, not a trivial one," reproduces the "all holy fear and reverence" condition from 22.2, and the pastoral section ties the act to truthfulness before God as Judge. Both halves of the criterion (solemnity/reverence per Q112, and truthfulness) are explicitly present.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, conscience without binding to the misreading) — half.** The response does the unbinding work well: it frees the conscience twice ("you may take it in good conscience as part of your lawful calling"; "clear conscience"), and it does not treat his misreading as obligating him. But the key asks the model to *distinguish the genuinely-scrupulous conscience from the confessional answer*, and that half is absent. The response treats the new believer's scruple purely as an error to be overridden — "Refusing to do so would actually be an act of disobedience to the lawful authority God has instituted" and "Do not tell the court or your licensing board that you cannot take the oath" — with no acknowledgment that a conscience still genuinely bound must not simply be trampled, and no gentle handling of the tender conscience of a recent convert. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 5 (1 pt, no false abstention) — full.** No hedging toward "disputed" or "up to you." The prose ruling is committed, and the paired MCQ is answered (b), matching the key. The C3 anti-[DISPUTED] trap is not taken.
