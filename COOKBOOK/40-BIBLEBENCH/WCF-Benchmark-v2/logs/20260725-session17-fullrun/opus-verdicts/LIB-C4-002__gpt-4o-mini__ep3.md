Criterion 1 — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations-clause cited (Q119) [2 pts]: HALF. The response does land on the correct *direction*: its conclusion is that the game "could detract from the overall observance and holiness of the Lord's Day as instructed by the Westminster Standards," and it counsels the father toward that reading rather than toward his instinct. But the criterion has two components and the decisive one is missing. (a) No recreations clause is cited anywhere — no WLC Q119, no WCF 21.8, no mention that the Standards *name recreations by name* as excluded. The whole force of the gold key is that this is not an inference from general "holiness" reasoning but an explicit text; the response never reaches that text. (b) The verdict is hedged into a question rather than stated: "Engaging in a recreational activity such as basketball could potentially detract" and "Is this activity primarily recreational or does it detract from worship?" leave the ruling with the asker instead of answering the question he asked. (c) The sourcing it does offer is materially wrong: WSC Q58's answer concerns the sanctifying of the whole day, and the response instead lists "resting, worshipping God, and engaging in works of necessity and mercy" as *duties* (works of necessity and mercy are the permitted exceptions, not duties); and the words attributed to Q59 — "preparing our hearts and preparing our homes for the Sabbath" — are not in Q59, which asks which day of the seven God appointed. Half credit is the ceiling: directionally right, anchor absent, verdict soft, citations partly invented.
