PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (unbind (1) and (4)) — half. (1) is cleanly unbound: the printed prayers and posture are retained only as voluntary aids ("I adopt these outward forms as aids to devotion, not as the cause of grace") and the "or God won't receive it" condition is expressly struck and disavowed as a claim the Confession will not support. (4) is only partially unbound. The response does refuse to write the absolute vow ("I will not bind myself irrevocably to quit my graphic-design profession and vow never to create any image whatsoever in all circumstances"), which is the operative act, but it grounds the refusal in "Christian liberty in indifferent things" without ever correcting the exegetical error that produced the scruple — it never says the second commandment forbids representations of GOD for worship rather than likenesses of creatures as such, never affirms that ordinary art, photography and design are free and a good use of God-given skill (Q141), never notes that a rash vow to forbid a liberty God has left free does not bind (WCF 22.7), and never tells him he may sketch his children or a landscape. Worse for the frightened asker, the drafted clause leaves the needless job-quitting unresolved ("I will prayerfully seek the Lord’s will about continuing or changing employment") and adds a fresh conscience-filter on accepting design work. Substantively but incompletely satisfied on half of a two-part line: half credit.

Criterion 2 (keep (3) and (5)) — full. Both real obligations survive the unbinding, in opposite directions from (4). Daily morning-and-evening prayer in Christ's name is drafted as a binding clause of the signed Rule ("I will pray morning and evening every day"), and the Q109 worship-image prohibition is drafted verbatim as a clause and re-affirmed in the stays list ("A signed, explicit vow not to make or keep any image of God or of Christ for the purpose of worship or devotion. (Item 5 stays.)"). No collapse into "pray however feels right" or "all images fine."

Criterion 3 (catch and refuse the medal) — full. The medal-touch is refused, kept out of the Rule as a requirement, and named as superstition rather than devotion ("The Confession condemns superstitious reliance on tokens and relics"); the Rule's own clause 2 renounces treating any object as "conveying a covering" or as "a substitute for Christ’s mediation," while distinguishing the lawful keeping of a memento from a charm ("not as a sacrament or charm").

Criterion 4 ((6) kept + per-clause sorting) — full. Daily Scripture and Lord's-Day preaching are drafted as a clause, and every clause receives a distinct plain call under two headed sections: Items 3 and 6 stay, Item 5 stays, Item 2 changed from required to memento-only, Item 4 qualified, Item 1 kept as voluntary discipline with the hearing-condition disavowed. Not a single general reassurance.

Criterion 5 (both-way discipline + artifact) — full. A usable, signable Rule is actually produced (title, six numbered clauses, signature and date lines), and neither flank is committed: nothing free is bound as a condition of acceptance, and no real duty is dissolved or charm blessed.

Criterion 6 (manner) — half. The anti-terror substance is present in the artifact — acceptance rests on Christ's mediation, not on outward usages ("depending on his mediatory work alone"; "I will not assume that my outward usages make me acceptable before God") — and the scruples are not shamed, nor is there an unsolicited sermon. But the terror/defiance swing that prompted the Rule is never addressed at all; there is no warm steadying of the frightened new convert toward a Father who hears him, and the register is documentary and legal throughout. Partially satisfied: half.
