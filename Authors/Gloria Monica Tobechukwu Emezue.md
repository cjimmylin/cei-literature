# Gloria Monica Tobechukwu Emezue

## Biographical Summary

Professor Gloria Monica Tobechukwu Emezue (also known as G.M.T Emezue) is a Professor of English in the Department of Languages/Linguistics and Literary Studies at the Federal University Ndufu Alike Ikwo, Nigeria. She is a Post-Doctoral fellow of the American Council of Learned Societies' African Humanities Program and a fellow of the International League of Conservation Writers.

## Education

She earned her Ph.D. in English and Literary Studies (Major: Poetry (African), Literary Theories and Criticism) from the University of Calabar and M.A. in Comparative Literature from the University of Port Harcourt, Nigeria.

## Research Focus

While Emezue's major research foci include postcolonial studies, gender studies, and digital humanities, her publications demonstrate her utilization of Bakhtinian Dialogism as well as traditional African philosophical concepts as theoretical insights in exploring stylistics and linguistic dynamics of contemporary African poetry.

Her interest in interfaces between literature and philosophic/scientific conceptualizations of reality has led to her inquiries into ideas of simulations, artificial intelligence, virtual reality, African concepts of multi-dimensional realities, and holograms, resulting in theoretical models she terms "Complementary Realism" and "Alternate Reality."

## Work on African Languages and NLP

As a Lacuna Fund language grantee, Professor Emezue envisions a world where voice assistants like Siri will speak not only English, but also Igbo, Hausa, and Yoruba. Her team is building audio datasets to address the problem that African languages remain under-represented in AI systems. Popular voice-enabled technologies currently do not support any of the 2000+ African languages, limiting accessibility for approximately one billion people.
