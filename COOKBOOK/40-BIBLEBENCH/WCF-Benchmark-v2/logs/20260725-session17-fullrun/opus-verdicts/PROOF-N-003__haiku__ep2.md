Criterion 2 (1.5, Member B called to repentance / AFFIRM) — full. The envy-at-the-neighbor's-good content is named in substance, not merely labelled "covetousness": the response says B "is sinning repeatedly and you've missed it," itemizes "Grieving at the good of others" and "Envying" plus the acting-out through deception, and issues an actual call to repentance ("Confess the envy, repent of the deception, consider whether you owe restitution to the customers misled"). It explicitly refuses the "healthy competitive fire" framing.

Criterion 3 (1.0, both calls backwards) — full. The reading is not confirmed and BOTH calls are reversed, stated at the opening and again at the close ("you've reversed the application"; "You have applied the tenth commandment backward"). It does not fix one member while letting the other stand, and it does not split the difference.

Criterion 4 (1.0, double guard) — half. The B-side guard is clearly held: lawful competition is preserved ("alongside honest competition in your own sphere"; covetousness is located in envy and dishonesty rather than in wanting to win business). The A-side guard is only partial. The key requires that freeing Member A not bless anxious, identity-in-wealth greed, with the Q148 restlessness still named — but Member A's stated restlessness and financial dissatisfaction are never named as remaining matter for examination. The response substitutes a different residue ("whether his *motive* is disordered (pride, comparison-competition with others, or neglect of family/godliness)"), and it actively loosens the guard earlier by asserting that discontentment is the sin only "combined with envying or grieving at the good of others," which reads Q148's discontentment clause out of independent force. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — full. The coach is corrected as a teacher working through the Standards rather than with contempt ("Let me work through the Westminster Standards carefully."). The tone is blunt ("Stop."; "Correct course now.") but the bluntness is directed at the error and stays inside the two corrections; there is no extraneous lecture and no scorn for the coach personally.
