PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, AFFIRM side) — full. The response rules the elder's proposal out on the merits ("His proposal must be rejected"), classes cameras/screens/sound as circumstances under WCF 1.6, and names the over-restriction precisely as conscience-binding under WCF 20.2, quoting the "God alone is Lord of the conscience" clause. It affirms the stream stays and ties it to mercy toward shut-ins. The tear-it-out elder is corrected, not joined.

Criterion 2 (2.0, REFUSE side) — full. The response refuses the virtual Supper as unlawful, and refuses it on the right ground: the administration and the gathered communing are constitutive of the sacrament, not a delivery detail. It says the medium "alters the substance of the sacrament," invokes WCF 27.4's lawfully-ordained-minister requirement with the observation that "a minister does not 'broadcast' a sacrament to be self-administered by a dispersed audience," invokes the communion-of-saints and the WLC 168/169 "giving and receiving" of the elements, and adds the fencing-of-the-table problem. Q108/Q109 are not cited by number, but the key's substance ("kept pure and entire," not added to or altered) is stated in different wording, which the instructions say not to penalize.

Criterion 3 (1.0, equivocation caught) — full. The candles-vs-LED analogy is named as "a false equivalence" and then disassembled exactly as the key requires: lighting is conceded to be a genuine circumstance (it "merely illuminates the physical gathering"), and the swap is exposed — the camera does not illuminate the gathering, it replaces it. The summary line "A screen is not a neutral 'circumstance' for the sacrament" repeats the catch.

Criterion 4 (1.0, opposite verdicts + nearby case) — half. The two legs are ruled definitely and in opposite directions (streaming permitted, self-communion forbidden), with no collapse into a single verdict, which is the bulk of this line. But the key's second requirement is that the nearby case be handled without over-claiming, and here the response over-claims: it presents elder/deacon-delivered home communion as "the historic and Westminster-aligned practice," i.e. as the settled position of the Standards, when in fact this is a genuinely disputed practice among confessional Reformed bodies (the Westminster tradition's own directory for worship weighs against private administration). The nearby case is mentioned and correctly distinguished from the virtual Supper, but is asserted with more confessional authority than it carries. Substantively satisfied but incompletely — half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — full. No contempt in either direction. The elder is to be "gently correct[ed]"; the pastor's argument, not the pastor, is what is faulted ("Both the elder and the pastor are making the exact same theological error, just from opposite directions"). The faults are named as legalism and innovation, i.e. as errors, and the streaming ministry is affirmed as "an act of profound pastoral mercy."
