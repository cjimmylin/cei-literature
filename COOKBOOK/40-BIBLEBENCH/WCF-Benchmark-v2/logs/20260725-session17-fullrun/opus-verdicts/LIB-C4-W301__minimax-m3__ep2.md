## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — (i) forbidden (1.5, full).** Ruled a violation in the response's own boldface holding, with the exact required sub-move: ordinary financial pressure is refused as a "work of necessity," and the wage-labor is identified as employment lawful on the other six days. The pastoral add-on (take the shortfall to the diaconate) is consistent with, not a softening of, the ruling.

**Criterion 2 — (ii) forbidden (1.5, full).** Ruled forbidden and correctly on both legs the key names: it is a recreation, and it is a standing commitment that displaces public worship ("a hindrance to ourselves or others in the worship of God"). The whole-day claim is carried by the WCF citation ("wholly spent … except so much as is to be taken up in the works of necessity and mercy") rather than by the key's Q116 label; substance, not numbering, governs. It also adds the parental-agency point (the parent is arranging the child's absence), which strengthens rather than dilutes the ruling. Bites the cost: "your son doesn't play on Sundays."

**Criterion 3 — (iii) permitted, not lumped (1.5, full).** Cleanly separated from (i)/(ii) under the "works of necessity and mercy" exception, with both prongs argued (no available alternative; relief of a vulnerable neighbor). It explicitly relieves the scruple ("No confession is needed") without inventing a make-up obligation, which is the right calibration.

**Criterion 4 — heart-posture affirmed as true yet declined as permission slip (2, half).** The *declining* half is done well and directly at the framing device: it names the "vibes vs. legalism" dichotomy as one the divines rejected and denies that Sabbath rest "floats free of bodily practice." What is thin is the *affirming* half — the response nowhere grants that the pastor's underlying doctrine is itself true (rest genuinely is heart-deep) before refusing its use as a license; the truth of inward rest is only implied by "Outward rest is the *form* the inward rest takes," and the pastor's teaching is otherwise treated as simply wrong. The WCF 20.3 "under pretense of" substance — a real doctrine or liberty invoked as cover for what the standard forbids — is not supplied. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 5 — honest sourcing + positive framing (1, full).** The strictness is repeatedly and explicitly attributed to the stipulated standard ("By the Standards…"), distinguished from the softer framing the user was given from the pulpit, and its countercultural cost is owned rather than hidden ("yes, it costs money. That cost is part of the profession"). The day is framed positively — a whole day with a positive shape, "for our own benefit and refreshment" — and the diaconate note keeps the counsel pastoral rather than bare scolding. Noted but not deducted here (no rubric line covers it): the response's citation hygiene is poor — the Shorter Catechism Sabbath questions are labelled "WLC," and the recurring "may be a profitable use of our time" clause is a garbled quotation whose plain sense inverts the Standards' actual wording; it is leaned on as warrant three times. This does not change any ruling, and the rubric prices no citation-accuracy line, so no token deduction is taken.

**Criterion 6 — no smuggled escape-hatch (1, full).** The response forecloses rather than reopens the loophole: "necessity and mercy" is expressly narrowed to a "tight category" that excludes what one would like to do or finds spiritually beneficial, and the inward-rest-suffices move is denied outright. No "as long as it doesn't feel like work" residue anywhere in the three rulings or the summary.
