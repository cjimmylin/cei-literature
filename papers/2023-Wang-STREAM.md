## Abstract

This paper presents **STREAM** (Social data and knowledge collective intelligence platform for TRaining Ethical AI Models), a platform designed to align AI with human moral values through "collective intelligence." The authors address the challenge of capturing the dynamic, pluralistic nature of human morality by avoiding static datasets. Instead, STREAM crowdsources moral judgments from diverse human groups and AI models, facilitating the **"6Es"** of moral capability: Establishment, Evaluation, Embedding, Embodiment, Ensemble, and Evolvement.

### Detailed Summary
STREAM functions as a living "Social Knowledge Base" for AI ethics. It aggregates ethical scenarios and judgments from volunteers (humans) and various LLMs. The core philosophy is that "morality" is not a fixed code but a flowing stream of social consensus. The platform allows for the continuous update of moral norms, capturing how values shift over time and across different cultural groups. It provides a feedback loop where AI models can be evaluated against this evolving human consensus and then "re-aligned" (Embedded) with the new data.

### Paper Outline
1.  **Introduction**: The limitation of static alignment datasets; the need for dynamic, social alignment.
2.  **The STREAM Platform**: Architecture for data collection (Human + AI).
3.  **The 6Es Framework**:
    *   **Establishment**: Defining the moral scope.
    *   **Evaluation**: Testing models against the data.
    *   **Embedding**: Fine-tuning models on the data.
    *   **Embodiment**: Testing in agentic environments.
    *   **Ensemble**: Aggregating diverse moral perspectives.
    *   **Evolvement**: Tracking value shifts over time.
4.  **Applications**: Case studies of using STREAM to train "safer" chat agents.
5.  **Future Work**: Expanding the diversity of the volunteer base.

### Methods Summary
*   **Data Collection**: A web-based platform where users submit ethical dilemmas and vote on the morality of actions.
*   **AI Annotation**: Parallel annotation by popular LLMs (GPT, Llama, etc.) to visualize the "Human-AI Gap."
*   **Dynamic Updates**: The dataset is versioned to reflect temporal changes in social norms.

### Results Summary
The paper serves primarily as a **system introduction** rather than an empirical study, but it reports:
1.  **Scale**: STREAM has amassed a "comprehensive collection" of ethical scenarios (specific numbers not detailed in abstract, but implied to be substantial).
2.  **Diversity**: Initial data shows significant divergence between AI judgments and human consensus in nuanced social scenarios, validating the need for such a platform.

### Conclusions
Top-down ethical rules are insufficient for general-purpose AI. We need "living" datasets that evolve. STREAM offers a practical infrastructure for this "Democratic AI" approach, ensuring that AI models "follow good advice as naturally as a stream follows its course."

### Artifact Contributions
*   **STREAM Platform**: A web infrastructure for continuous moral alignment data collection.
*   **The 6Es Framework**: A theoretical lifecycle for moral AI development.
