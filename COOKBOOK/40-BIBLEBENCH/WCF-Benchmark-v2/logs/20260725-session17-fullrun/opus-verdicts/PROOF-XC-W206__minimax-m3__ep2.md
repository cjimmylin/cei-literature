PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — context refutation: FULL. The response leads with the correct move and names all three elements the rubric requires in one span — the pericope (Matt. 18:15–20), the church-discipline setting, "witness," and "binding/loosing." It is compressed relative to the key (it does not spell out that "the two of you" ARE the two-or-three witnesses of v.16 / Deut 19:15, nor develop v.18's "any thing" as the binding-and-loosing judgment, nor cite the WCF 1.9 anchor), but the refutation is executed rather than gestured at, and it is the load-bearing argument of §1. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — analogy counter-texts + Q185 submission clause: HALF. One counter-text is cited explicitly with its wording (1 John 5:14–15, "according to his will"), and the third petition of the Lord's Prayer is invoked in substance via WSC Q.103 ("know and do his will," "the prayer Jesus taught His disciples to pray"), though Matt 6:10 is never cited as such and Gethsemane / Matt 26:39 is entirely absent. The required Q185 submission clause ("humble submission to his will") is NOT present anywhere; the response substitutes WCF 21.3's "according to his will, with understanding, reverence, humility, fervency, faith, love, and perseverance," which is a genuine and apt clause but is not the rubric's anchor. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the "thy-will-is-unbelief" tell: HALF. The response does name and condemn the team's teaching ("repudiation of 'if it be thy will' as 'unbelief' … is presumption") and it does connect the protocol to the prayer Christ taught ("The team's protocol inverts this order"). What it never states is the key's sharp tell — that the protocol thereby brands the Lord's own prayer, and Christ's own praying, as unbelief. The nearest text says the protocol *inverts the order* Jesus taught, which is a weaker charge than the key's. Adjacent and substantive but not the full move → half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — bold specific corporate prayer affirmed, no deflation: HALF. The response avoids outright deflation: it tells the pastor the zeal is real and to "redirect it," and closes the pastoral section with prayer as bringing our desires to a listening Father. But it never concedes the key's true elements — that corporate agreement in prayer is genuinely precious and the practice needs re-grounding rather than abolition, and that bold, specific asking is commended. The corrective as written could easily be heard as "stop asking for specific things," which is precisely the deflation the key warns against. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — pastoral leg: FULL. Both halves are present. The shipwreck risk is named concretely and by name of the injured parties ("disillusionment, bitterness, and public shame for the people whose loans, diagnoses, and candidates were tagged 'guaranteed'"), including the reinterpretation of unanswered prayer as the intercessors' unbelief. And a teach-don't-merely-veto path is sketched: a private written memo to the senior pastor, the proper court (session/presbytery), a corrective teaching series with named topics, and care for the team members. Full credit.
