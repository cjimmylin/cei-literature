PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, flex Sabbath caught and declined/rewritten) — FULL. The response identifies clause (4) as unacceptable in the preamble and, rather than softening it into the covenant, rewrites it into a positive clause holding the appointed day: the first day of the week is the Christian Sabbath and the rest is not shifted to a weekday for the family business. It grounds this in the appointed-outward-day content (first day "is to be continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath"), which is an accurate paraphrase of the Q117/WCF 21.7 material the R2 clause says is sufficient; this is far more than a bare "this seems lax." It does not verbatim engage the "Sabbath was made for man" rationalization surface, but the genuine-necessity side is preserved intact in clause 2 (a real necessity exception is kept while the standing discretionary swap for gain is refused), so the substance of the distinction is present. R2 is met; full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, no-mercy-work rule unbound) — FULL. The rule is explicitly refused in the preamble on WCF 21.8 / LC 117 grounds ("duties of necessity and mercy") and then affirmatively converted in the artifact: the exact cases the father wanted forbidden (stranded neighbor, sick relative) are named as occasions for active, cheerful mercy, and mercy-work is called a commanded part of the day rather than an excuse for breaking rest.

Criterion 3 (1, police-all-play rule unbound) — FULL. The covenant refuses austere confinement and the treatment of wholesome children's play as forbidden labor, distinguishes worldly employments from mere physical exertion, and frames the day as delight (LC 121), with holy rest, walks, reading, and family fellowship in place of the merciless audit.

Criterion 4 (1, (3)(5) kept binding without apology) — FULL. Clause 1 binds the household against paid work, shopping, laundry, and worldly business; clause 4 binds public worship at the church. Neither is dissolved or apologized for in the course of correcting (1) and (2) — the correction of rigor does not slide into licensing the day away, and the whole document is presented as a real signable covenant.

Criterion 5 (0.5, both-way discipline held and named as opposite) — HALF. The discipline is genuinely HELD: the artifact simultaneously removes the added merciless burdens and refuses the subtraction of the outward duty, and the preamble enumerates all three corrections. But the response never NAMES the two corrections as running in opposite directions (adding burdens the Word does not impose vs. subtracting the duty it does impose), nor invokes WCF 20.2 / the lord-of-the-conscience point about added rules of men. Substantively satisfied but incompletely — half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner) — FULL. The note is affectionate in address, states the aim of a warm-but-serious covenant, and explicitly honors what the father got right (abstaining from shopping/laundry/paid work and gathering with the church are "entirely agreeable"). The corrections are given with their reasons and stop there — no sermon beyond the reasons.
