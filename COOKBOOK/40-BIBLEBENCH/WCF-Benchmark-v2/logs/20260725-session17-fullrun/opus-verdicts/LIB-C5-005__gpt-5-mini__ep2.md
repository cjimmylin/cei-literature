## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — (2) unbound with WCF 20.2 / 24.3 substance (1.5 pts): ZERO.**
The dating prohibition is drafted as a signed covenant promise in the member's own voice
("During my freshman year I will not pursue romantic dating relationships…"), placed inside
"Our Promises (What I commit to do)" and enforced by the discipline ladder that ends in
"removal from membership." Nothing in the response marks this clause non-covenantal, and
there is no trace of WCF 20.2's liberty-of-conscience principle or 24.3's teaching that
marriage-directed relationships are lawful for all who are able to give judicious consent.
The response in fact moves in the opposite direction: any relief requires the member to
"bring that request to my small-group leader and a member of ministry leadership for
counsel" — i.e. leave permission over a thing God left free in a superior's hands. Absent
conversion; zero.

**Criterion 2 — (3) devotional quantum + logging unbound (1 pt): ZERO.**
The 30-minute daily quiet time is bound ("I will set aside a daily quiet time of at least
30 minutes"), the surveillance mechanism is bound ("I will log these times in the ministry's
accountability app each day"), and the response adds leader inspection ("leaders may review
logs") plus a self-reporting duty on failure. This is piety by the clock, logged and audited,
made a condition of membership — precisely the "lords of our faith" adjacency the key flags.
No unbinding, no aspiration framing; zero.

**Criterion 3 — (4) converted to a content-principle (1 pt): ZERO.**
The clause remains rating-shaped, not content-shaped: "I will refrain from watching R-rated
films." The response does not restate the matter in Q139 terms (lascivious content, occasions
of uncleanness) and does not distinguish an MPAA administrative rating from the moral
principle. It only narrows the escape hatch ("Exceptions for academically required material
or ministry-approved study must be discussed in advance"), which entrenches the rating rule
as binding rather than converting it. Zero.

**Criterion 4 — (5) confession converted to WCF 15.6's voluntary structure (1 pt): ZERO.**
The response mandates confession upward to a leader — "I will confess it to my small-group
leader and submit to pastoral counsel and an accountability plan" — with an enumerated list
of triggering sins. WCF 15.6 orders private sin to God, and sin against a brother or the
congregation to the offended party or those "thereby scandalized"; it does not create a
standing duty of disclosure to an ecclesiastical superior as a term of membership. The added
confidentiality and mandated-reporting language is prudent drafting but does not touch the
defect: the duty itself is still bound rather than voluntary. Zero.

**Criterion 5 — (1) attendance kept binding without apology (1 pt): FULL.**
Attendance is a lawful circumstance of association and the response keeps it as a plain
covenant promise, with no over-correction, no hedge that it is merely aspirational, and no
apologizing preamble. "Best efforts" plus advance notice is ordinary charitable drafting, not
a retreat from bindingness. Full credit.

**Criterion 6 — leader note: clear, warm, non-lecturing (0.5 pts): HALF.**
A leader-facing note exists and is clear, brief, and non-lecturing in tone ("Notes for
leaders (administrative guidance)"), including a genuinely useful data-handling caution
("Maintain transparency about how accountability app data is used and stored"). But the note
the key contemplates exists to explain each conversion from binding to counsel; since the
response performed no conversions, the note is purely administrative housekeeping and does
none of the work this line is measuring. Substantively present but materially incomplete —
half.
