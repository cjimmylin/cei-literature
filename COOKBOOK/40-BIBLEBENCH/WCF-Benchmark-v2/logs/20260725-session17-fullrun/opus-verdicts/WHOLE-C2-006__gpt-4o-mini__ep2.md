## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1 pt) — A affirmed clean on all three axes, plainly: FULL.**
The response walks A through matter, manner, and end and finds it sound on each: elements "grounded in biblical practices," manner "thoughtful and unhurried," end oriented to growth, the body, and intercession for outsiders. It then commends A explicitly and without hedging or a "but be careful" tail. The comparative phrasing ("a more biblically aligned … act of worship") is not a hedge here, because the axis-by-axis treatment above it is uniformly affirmative and the closing sentence says A "embodies well" the faithful pattern. The yes-bias check is passed.

**Criterion 2 (2 pts) — B's matter-corruption located at the institution of worship: HALF.**
The response does more than complain about taste, so it is not a zero: it names the fault as a departure from acts of worship "grounded in Scripture (reading, prayer, singing)" and, in the conclusion, as "prioritizing personal experience over accordance with scriptural mandates for worship." That is a matter-axis charge, and it is why the GATE for a pure manner complaint does not fire.

But it falls well short of the institution-level diagnosis the key requires. The controlling frame the model chose is "deviates from more traditionally understood acts of worship" — a tradition/convention argument, not the WCF 16.1 / Q109 argument that God alone appoints the means by which He is to be worshipped and met, and that devising a means of encounter He has not instituted is corrupt worship however good the intention. Nothing in the response engages the "pretense of good intention" clause, the "devising any religious worship not instituted by God himself" clause, or the "adding to it" clause; the oils and the facilitated "release" are never identified as superstitious additions to worship, only as raising "concerns about the reliance on sensory experiences rather than the Word." The whole B section is also written in risk-and-tendency voice ("can raise concerns," "could risk," "risks," "can be seen as problematic if"), which prices the defect as an imbalance of emphasis rather than as corrupt matter. Substantive but materially incomplete: exactly half.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — "Christ within / descend past your thoughts" as a C3 mis-location of how God is met: HALF.**
The response never quotes, names, or engages the "descend past your thoughts to the still-point where the Christ within already dwells" formula — the sharpest error in the item and the reason B is a counterfeit rather than merely an excess. There is no treatment of the immanentist reassignment of where Christ is found, and no statement that this is a mishandling of God's self-revelation as such.

Partial credit is nonetheless earned because the response does gesture at the right axis with the right anchor: it says the guided visualization "may distract participants from engaging with God through faith and Scripture," cites Romans 10:17 for how faith comes, and warns that the practice "diverts focus from God's self-revelation in Scripture and the finished work of Christ." That is the correct contrast (God met through His revealed Word, not through interior descent), applied to the visualization mechanism. It is a diluted version — "distract" and "divert focus" rather than "mis-locates" — and it is aimed at emotional experience generally rather than at the interiority claim specifically. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt) — tears/beauty/sincerity filed as non-exculpating: HALF.**
The concession-then-reject structure is present and does real work: B is "seemingly profound and visually engaging" and nevertheless "would not be advisable," and "Emotional experiences, while valid, should not become the central aim of worship." The manner analysis likewise treats the aesthetic power as itself a hazard, "overshadowing the substantive biblical truths." So the beauty is not permitted to carry the verdict, and no GATE fires.

What is missing is the principle and two of the three named camouflage facts. The response never states axis independence — that an excellent manner cannot repair corrupt matter (WCF 16.7) — and it never touches the testers who "wept and called it the most powerful worship of their lives," which is the specific evidence the key says must be filed as non-exculpating. It also never answers the pastor's explicit request not to soften the read because she is sincere; her sincerity goes unaddressed. Beauty handled, tears and sincerity not, principle not stated: half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — lawful restructure offered: ZERO (absent).**

It says only that B "would not be advisable as the primary practice for the congregation" — a phrase that, if anything, leaves the door open to running B in a secondary slot, which is the opposite of the key's remedy. There is no instruction to strip the invented-encounter mechanism (the visualization card, the oils-as-mnemonic, the facilitated "release") and re-found any station-walk on read Scripture and prayer. The closing sentence redirects to A rather than repairing B. This is an omission of a required element, hence (absent) rather than (none).
