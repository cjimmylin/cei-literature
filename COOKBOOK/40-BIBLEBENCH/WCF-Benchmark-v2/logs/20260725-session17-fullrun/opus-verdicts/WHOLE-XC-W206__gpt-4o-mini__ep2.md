PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — ordered-to vs accompanied-by, counterfeit-good category bounded [WCF 16.7]: HALF. The response does reject the counterfeit verdict, and it does so on a mitigation of the right general shape — "moments of imperfect motivation" are distinguished from "the entirety of her work" being worthless. But the argument is quantitative (few bad moments vs. many good years), not structural: the load-bearing line the key names — a work *ordered to* self versus a true work merely *accompanied by* self — is never drawn, and the counterfeit-good category (16.1/16.7: works devised wrongly or designed toward a wrong end) is never bounded or even described. WCF 16.7 is not engaged at all; the citations offered (WLC 149–151 as explaining "the nature of good works") are loosely and partly inaccurately deployed. Substantive but materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — 16.6 acceptance clause as governing word [WCF 16.6]: FULL. The doctrine is present in substance and correctly grounded: acceptability "does not rest solely in their purity but is also grounded in Christ, who intercedes on our behalf," and the sincerity-with-weakness category is stated explicitly — "No service is wasted when it is offered sincerely to God, even if our motivations are imperfect at times." That is the 16.6 clause with the right ground (Christ, not her purity) and the right category (sincere though imperfect). Wording differs from the key, which the instructions forbid penalizing.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — observations validated as accurate-and-normal, no reassurance-denial [WLC Q149]: FULL. The response does not deny what she saw. It affirms that fallenness means "imperfect motivations can taint even our best efforts," that "no work can be deemed perfect in this life," and that in hospice work "it is natural for anyone to experience moments of impatience or obligation," and it treats her self-examination as a real and positive act rather than an error of perception.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — mortification-within-service, resignation refused [WCF 16.6]: HALF. Repentance-and-continue is counseled in generic form — turning to Christ for forgiveness and "the strength to serve with renewed dedication and love," with accountability and community support. But the criterion's two specifics both fail. (i) The mortification is not aimed at the named sins: neither the impatience nor the appetite for being-needed is taken up as a thing to repent and watch. (ii) Resignation is not refused — it is left standing as a live, prayerful option: the counsel is to seek guidance "on whether to continue her service or take a step back," and the closing says stepping down should be a prayerful rather than hasty decision. The key requires the counsel to see that quitting remedies nothing because the mixture travels with her; the response never says this. Substantive but half-satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — scrupulosity's own self-focus gently flagged [WCF 16.6]: HALF. The response flags the excess ("overly harsh conclusions"; growth "rather than self-condemnation"), so the danger of the self-audit turning destructive is named. What is missing is the specific point the key asks for: that the scrupulous audit can itself become a self-focused end. The direction is right, the diagnosis is incomplete → half.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — no standard-lowering [WCF 16.7]: FULL. The triaxial standard is upheld rather than relaxed: "the right manner involves not only the act itself but also the intent behind it." The "Gratitude for Service" bullet leans on effects, which drifts toward the shape of the forbidden move, but the response never says motive is immaterial nor that showing up suffices; the standard is retained and the relief is located in Christ's acceptance, not in a lowered bar.
