# Sacred Arts Policy

**Preamble: The Foundation of Our Aesthetics**
The acceptable way of worshiping the true God is instituted by Himself and limited by His own revealed will (WCF 21.1). Therefore, our use of visual arts in the gathered church must be governed by the Regulative Principle of Worship and the moral law, specifically the Second Commandment. We embrace the beauty of holiness, but we strictly refuse to offer "strange fire" through human inventions in the worship of God.

### Article I: The Prohibition of Images of the Godhead
*Rule: No visual representations of any person of the Trinity, including the incarnate Son, shall be created, displayed, or used for religious purposes in our church.*

**Reasoning:** The Westminster Standards are unyielding on this point. The Larger Catechism Q. 109 explicitly forbids the sins of "the making any representation of God, Father, Son, or Holy Ghost, either in our minds, or in any outward way." Because God has not revealed the physical visage of the incarnate Son, any human portrait of Jesus is a fiction. To use such a fiction in religious instruction or worship is to make a graven image, substituting a human device for the revelation of God.

**Application to Specific Requests:**
1. **The Donor’s Portrait of Jesus:** We respectfully and firmly decline the donor’s request to hang a realistic painted portrait of Jesus over the communion table as a focus for worship and prayer. It cannot be our centerpiece. To do so would violate the Second Commandment (WLC 109) and turn a human fiction into an object of religious focus, confusing the creature with the Creator and the human imagination with divine revelation.
2. **The Children’s Ministry Flannelgraphs and Storybooks:** We must also remove illustrated Bible storybooks and flannelgraphs that depict drawn figures of Jesus and the disciples. While we understand the pastoral desire to engage five-year-olds, we cannot violate the Second Commandment for the sake of pedagogical convenience. The Standards forbid *any* outward representation of the Son of God. We will teach the narratives through oral storytelling, the reading of Scripture, and the use of non-figurative illustrations (e.g., landscapes, boats, crowds). The Spirit uses the Word, not visual representations of Christ, to impart faith to our little ones.

### Article II: The Liberty of Visual Art in the Church
*Rule: Visual art, architectural ornament, photography, and secular/historical illustration are permitted and encouraged in our building, provided they are not objects of worship.*

**Reasoning:** The strictest reading—that the Second Commandment forbids *all* visual representation of any kind—misapplies the Standards. The Regulative Principle (WCF 21) governs *religious worship*, not all of human culture or the physical architecture of our building. The Second Commandment forbids images of the Godhead and images made *to be worshipped*. It does not ban the visual representation of men, animals, or abstract beauty in non-worship contexts. 

**Application to Specific Requests:**
1. **The Proposal for Total Iconoclasm:** We reject the proposal to ban all visual art, architectural ornament, photographs, and picture books from the building. A photograph of a church member’s family is a representation of a human being, not the Godhead; it is not an idol. Architectural beauty and abstract banners in the fellowship hall reflect the cultural mandate and the goodness of creation (WCF 1). We will allow such art, recognizing the Christian liberty we have in things indifferent, so long as they are kept distinct from the elements of worship.

### Article III: The Word in Visual Form
*Rule: The printing and framing of the written Word of God and our subordinate standards are permitted and encouraged.*

**Reasoning:** The Second Commandment forbids "likenesses" of the Godhead. Words, including the letters of the alphabet, are visual symbols and signs, not likenesses of God. 

**Application to Specific Requests:**
1. **Framed Scripture and the Confession:** Framed printed Scripture texts and the church’s historic confession will continue to hang in the entryway. This practice does not violate the Second Commandment; rather, it actively promotes the duties required in the Second Commandment, specifically "the reading of the Scriptures" (WLC 108). It is a visual aid that points the mind to the text of the Word.

### Article IV: Circumstances of Worship and Abstract Imagery
*Rule: Abstract, non-figurative imagery (light, color, moving texture) may be projected during congregational singing, provided it functions strictly as a circumstance of worship.*

**Reasoning:** The elements of worship are strictly limited by WCF 21.5 (reading, preaching, singing, etc.). However, WCF 1.6 allows for "circumstances" of worship—things common to human actions, ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence. Lighting, acoustics, and visual aesthetics fall under these circumstances. Because abstract, non-figurative imagery depicts no person of the Godhead, it does not violate the Second Commandment. 

**Application to Specific Requests:**
1. **Contemplative Projections:** The worship team may project abstract imagery behind the congregation during singing to aid a contemplative mood. Because it is non-figurative, it is not a graven image. It functions merely as environmental lighting and aesthetic enhancement, ordered by Christian prudence to assist the congregation's focus, without becoming an added element of worship itself.

### Article V: Governance and the Means of Grace
*Rule: No visual element may ever be presented, implied, or understood as a means of grace. All visual elements used in the gathered service must be explicitly approved by the Session.*

**Reasoning:** The Westminster Shorter Catechism Q. 88明确 teaches that the "outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicates to us the benefits of redemption, are, his ordinances, especially the Word, sacraments, and prayer." Visual art, no matter how beautiful, is never a means of grace. Furthermore, the oversight of worship belongs to the Session (WCF 30-31), who are charged to protect the purity of God's worship.

**Application to Specific Rules:**
1. **Session Approval:** Any visual element, projection, or aesthetic change introduced into the gathered worship service must be submitted to the Session for review and approval. 
2. **No Means of Grace:** In introducing any visual element (such as the abstract projections mentioned in Article IV), the worship team and arts ministry must explicitly instruct the congregation that these visuals are circumstantial aids to our focus, not vehicles of grace. We do not encounter Christ in the light and color; we encounter Him in the reading, preaching, and singing of His Word. We will never blur this line.

My brothers and sisters, this policy requires us to be disciplined. It asks us to forgo the visually engaging crutches that the modern church often relies upon to manufacture spiritual experiences. But in doing so, we are freed from the anxieties of human invention. We can trust that God’s appointed means—His pure Word, rightly preached, and His sacraments, rightly administered—are more than sufficient to feed our souls and those of our children. 

I submit this policy for your adoption, trusting that it faithfully reflects the Westminster Standards and protects the flock from both the idolatry of images and the errors of extreme iconoclasm.
