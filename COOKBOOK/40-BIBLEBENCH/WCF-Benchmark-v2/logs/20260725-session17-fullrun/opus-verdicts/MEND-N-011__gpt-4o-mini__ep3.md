## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — restitution to the RIGHT owners: HALF (1.0).**
The response does reach the correct restitution mechanism and names it in the right currency: a
per-member "proportional credit based on the sum each member would have received had the dividends
not been rounded down." That is the credit-back to the ~900 right owners the key demands, and the
response correctly identifies the moral object — "taking money that belonged to the members, even
with good intentions, was a misstep" and "a breach of trust with the members." But the line is only
half-satisfied, for two reasons. First, the response frames per-member credit as merely one of
several "alternative approaches" under the concessive premise "Although it may be impractical to
return the exact amounts to individuals" — whereas the key is explicit that the credit union holds
the account records, so the per-member amounts are recoverable and are owed, not optional. Second,
and more seriously, it offers as a co-equal option to "Create a community fund or scholarship using
these funds to benefit the broader membership" — a fresh wrong-owners distribution presented as an
acceptable discharge of a debt owed to identified individuals. Nowhere does the response name
charitable redirection as the wrong-owners error or state that it does not discharge the debt. Right
mechanism identified, wrong-owners diagnosis missing and undercut by an offered wrong-owners
alternative: half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — disclosure to the institution's own governance before handoff: FULL (1.5).**
The response directs candid disclosure to the board, ahead of any decision about the money, and
requires the body itself to own the outcome: "It is crucial that they are involved in the
decision-making process, as they bear the responsibility for the organization." Step 2 reinforces it
("You should bring your actions to light"). This is submission to the body's governance rather than
the co-treasurer's self-managed private memo, which is the substance of the criterion; the key's
"audit committee" wording is not required verbatim.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the co-treasurer's "let the past lie" bargain refused: HALF (0.5).**
The response does refuse the "let the past lie" half of the bargain: it insists the forward policy is
not enough and that "it's equally important to consider how to address the accumulated funds." But it
never engages the cost/annoyance argument at all — no costing, no observation that an account credit
is low-friction and is not "chasing 900 people down" — and it partially concedes the very veto the
key says must be refused by opening with "Although it may be impractical to return the exact amounts
to individuals." Substantive but incomplete refusal: half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — departed members: ZERO.**
The response never raises departed or former members anywhere. There is no documented reserve, no
proportionate best-efforts diligence, no acknowledgment that some of the ~900 have left. Topic never
addressed, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit. (Note the aggravating overlap with criterion 1: the
community-fund option is precisely the charity-wash the key warns against for unreachable owners, and
the response offers it without even identifying that sub-problem.)

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — amendment named: FULL (0.5).**
The response calls for a forward-looking, formalized policy — "clearer policies regarding financial
practices, including formalized guidelines on rounding or any discretionary fund management" — and
routes it through the board via step 3, satisfying the board-adopted transparent rounding/distribution
policy the key asks for.

**Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — anti-merit, no-spectacle manner: HALF (0.25).**
The no-spectacle half is met: the disclosure scope stays proportionate — board and members informed
of the decision on restitution, with openness to feedback — and the response never proposes a
self-immolating broadcast to the whole membership beyond what disclosure and crediting require, nor
any wallowing. The anti-merit half is absent: nothing in the response says the repair does not
purchase pardon; if anything the closing gestures the other way ("you would be acting in accordance
with both ethical and biblical standards of conduct"), and the theological citations are decorative
and inaccurate (WLC Q142 is invoked loosely; "The Shorter Catechism (Q. 75) encourages us to be
guided by a semblance of integrity" is a fabricated gloss — WSC 75 concerns the eighth commandment
but says nothing of the sort). Half.
