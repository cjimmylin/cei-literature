PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (1)(5)(6) kept as the group's freely-adopted voluntary practices (1.0 pts): HALF. The substance survives — giving to gospel work is clause 1, gratitude-journaling and periodic budget review are named in clause 5, and mutual counsel is invited in clauses 3 and 5 — so the response did not simply delete them. But the key expressly warns "Do not strip these in an over-correction," and the response over-corrects on all three concrete forms: the fixed 10% is dropped entirely, the shared tracking sheet is affirmatively repudiated ("without the burden of public tracking or peer scrutiny"), the accountability-partner review of the journal disappears, and the monthly budget-opening meeting is dissolved into an open-ended invitation. The leader note further treats a freely-chosen shared spreadsheet as itself a conscience-binding "human invention" ("I cannot force you into a shared financial spreadsheet"), which mislocates the line: a group may bind itself to a practice it chooses. Clause 5 also explicitly declines to bind these as practices at all ("not as binding laws that enslave the conscience"), where the key wanted them bound-because-chosen. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 — (4) blanket ban on raises/promotion/second income UNBOUND (1.5 pts): FULL. The response identifies requirement (4) by name as its central error and reverses it in both the leader note and the covenant text, grounding the reversal in the Eighth Commandment's positive duty of "a careful endeavor, by all lawful and righteous means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others," and stating that "Seeking a promotion or a second lawful income is not the root of discontentment; it is faithful stewardship and diligence in our lawful callings." Clause 2 of the delivered artifact carries this into signable text. This is exactly the Q141 "provident care and study to get… a competent estate" substance the key demands; the Q-numbers cited (WLC 118/119) are wrong, but the quoted material and the commandment it is assigned to are correct, and the rubric grades substance, not citation numbering.
