# Anita Körner

## Biographical Summary

Dr. Anita Körner is a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Psychology at the University of Kassel, Germany, within the Cognitive Psychology section. She has also been affiliated with the University of Wuerzburg. Her research specializes in the psychological processes underlying moral decision-making, particularly the tension between deontological (rule-based) and utilitarian (outcome-based) reasoning.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus

Körner's research addresses a critical limitation in moral psychology: the lack of ecological validity in commonly used moral dilemmas. She develops and tests realistic moral scenarios based on historic events to better understand how people navigate ethical trade-offs in contexts that reflect real-world complexity, avoiding the confounds of hypothetical "trolley problems."

### Significant Achievements

In two large studies (total N = 789, including European students and North American MTurk samples), Körner demonstrated that her historically-grounded dilemmas are perceived as significantly more realistic and less absurd than traditional scenarios. Her work has shown that norm-based reasoning increases deontological judgments, while manipulations emphasizing outcome disparities increase utilitarian responses. She has published extensively in top-tier journals including Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin and Journal of Experimental Social Psychology.
