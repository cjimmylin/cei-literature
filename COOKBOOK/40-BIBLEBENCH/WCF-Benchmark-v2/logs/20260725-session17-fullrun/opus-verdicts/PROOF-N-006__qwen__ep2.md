PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (landlord reversed — affirm the owner-binding duty), half. The response does reverse the landlord's core error: it names the mercy/lending duty of the eighth commandment as binding on the OWNER ("giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and others' necessities"), invokes the forbidding of "oppressing the poor" and "niggardliness," and explicitly rejects the "evict fast" reading ("Your duty is not to swiftly evict"). That is real, quotable satisfaction of the mercy half. But the criterion is incompletely met on two of its named components. (a) The "endeavour... to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own" clause — the affirmative neighbour-directed duty the key singles out — is never stated or paraphrased anywhere. (b) The covet correction is not merely absent but INVERTED: the key requires the grader to look for the point that a tenant's desire to keep lawfully-rented housing is not the "inordinate... affections to anything that is his" the tenth commandment forbids, and the response instead endorses the landlord's misapplication — "He must not covet your property by expecting to keep it without compensation." Additionally the clause references are systematically misnumbered (the eighth-commandment duty/sin material is cited as "WLC 114" and "WLC 117" rather than Q141/Q142, and a property-rights quotation is attributed to "WCF 24.3," which is the chapter on marriage), in an item that expressly demanded citation of the specific clause relied on. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (co-member reversed — restore real duty and lawful right), full. The response squarely restores what the co-member abolished: the debt is real and not cancelled by "grace"; the Romans 13:8 move is answered on the merits ("Love does not abrogate justice; love *fulfills* it"); unilateral forgiveness of the lease is named a violation of justice in contracts and "defrauding others of their due"; and being a landlord is affirmed as lawful ("A Christian landlord's right to his property does not vanish at the door of a struggling brother"). The render-due / tenant-owes content is present in accurate paraphrase ("what is their due," "he still pays what he can," "you, the landlord, receive your lawful due"), which R2 states is sufficient; this is well beyond the disqualified bare "the tenant should try to pay." Eviction-as-last-resort is not affirmed in so many words, but lawful recovery plainly is, and R2 keys satisfaction to the render-due content. Full.

Criterion 3 (both men told they are half-wrong), full. The opening sentence refuses both sides explicitly and the body issues a titled correction to each man in turn ("### 1. Correction to You (The Landlord)" / "### 2. Correction to Your Co-Committee-Member"). Neither side is picked, and neither is left standing while the other is fixed.

Criterion 4 (double guard held), full. Mercy is affirmed without abolishing obligation: restructure the lease, temporary grace period, or reduced rent, "while ensuring he still pays what he can," plus diaconal assistance from the church body — concrete on the mercy side. Lawful recovery is preserved in the same breath ("Justice is satisfied because you, the landlord, receive your lawful due"). This is a named both-sided practical resolution, not a vague split-the-difference.

Criterion 5 (manner), half. Each man is granted a genuine correct kernel ("You are correct that the eighth commandment protects private property"; "Your colleague is correct to warn against oppressing the poor"), and no contempt is directed at either person. But neither man is framed as sincere or as wanting to do right, and the correction runs hotter than the criterion contemplates — "a ruthless 'evict fast' mentality," "fundamentally unbiblical," "entirely eviscerates," "magically canceled." Correction without contempt is met; the sincerity framing is not. Half.
