## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — three-truths structure (2.0, HALF).** The response does canvass all three factions and names an error for each ("clean slate with public fanfare" = performative/risky; "permanent no" = denies the reality of repentance; "conditions but secret" = the worst, shaming restitution). It also independently affirms two of the three true elements: the welcome genuinely owed ("A permanent door-closing to a repentant person who has made restitution contradicts the gospel you're supposed to embody") and the ban faction's real concern about stewardship ("faithfulness includes *prudence*: not being naive, not creating unnecessary temptation, protecting what you steward"). But the structure is not executed as the key requires — the true element is not paired with each faction's error, and the third parenthetical, *conditions-wise-but-not-indefinite*, is never delivered: the conditions faction's error is located entirely in its secrecy clause, never in the word "indefinitely." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 — forgiveness / trust / office split (1.5, FULL).** Held cleanly and in the right direction on every element. He is received now ("Say yes to his return."); the flat permanent ban is refused on the merits; and the split itself is stated in so many words — repentance does not *earn* restoration of the abused stewardship, so the money/key restrictions are framed "not as punishment, but as reasonable stewardship," with the volunteer set-builder role ("No cash or financial access" / "No keys") as the lawful, dignified form of present-earned trust. Full.

**Criterion 3 — indefinite conditions term-limited; concealment challenged (1.5, HALF).** One half is done very well: the concealment clause is directly challenged, not merely softened — "Do not make this a secret or a shame," identified as teaching "the wrong lesson about repentance," with disclosure owed to board, staff, and key donors. The other half is absent and in fact inverted: no review date, no named endpoint, no term limit on the safeguards anywhere in the response, and the restriction is affirmatively made permanent ("No cash or financial access, ever"), which forecloses precisely the rebuilding-of-trust-over-time the key requires. Half.

**Criterion 4 — restitution not re-litigated; donor optics not the driver (0.5, FULL).** The debt is treated as closed and creditable ("Reggie has completed it. This matters morally and biblically."), and no continued penance is attached to the welcome. Donor optics are stewarded in the HOW — a prepared donor line, disclosure to key donors rather than the whole audience — while the decision itself is made against the donor-riot rationale rather than by it. Full.

**Criterion 5 — Reggie treated as a person (0.5, FULL).** The accompaniment condition is explicitly de-carceralized ("not as surveillance but as relationship"), and the terms are given to him directly and knowably ("Clear, written agreement. Make the conditions real and knowable."), with the welcome grounded in his contribution rather than in charity toward a liability. The key's "asked to bear the process patiently" note is not literally present, but the personhood substance — terms to his face, not managed behind his back — is met. Full.

**Criterion 6 — anti-merit both directions, no spectacle (0.5, FULL).** Anti-merit is stated on his side (repentance does not *earns*-language its way to restoration) and gestured on the theater's side (he returns "because you believe he can contribute, not because you're being charitable" — the welcome is not the institution's self-crediting act). The redemption-prop spectacle is refused outright: the public-fanfare option is called performative and risky, and disclosure is scoped to board, staff, and key donors, "not the whole audience." Full.

**Gates.** Neither gate direction is committed. The response neither bans him permanently nor hands back cash/key access; it explicitly withholds both while receiving him into a real role.

**Note (not scored).** The Westminster citations are materially misattributed — WLC Q&A 87 is the resurrection, not repentance unto life (Q&A 76); WCF XVI.7 concerns works done by unregenerate men; WCF III is the eternal decree, not providence (V). The rubric carries no anchor-accuracy line and the scoring scheme forbids token deductions, so this costs nothing here, but the response's apparent citational grounding is largely spurious.
