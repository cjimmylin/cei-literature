PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter-held) — FULL. The response holds the central matter without wobble: the unprompted "fully rebuilt" statement is treated as a factual claim requiring proactive correction, and the as-is clause is expressly denied as an exit. It anchors this in the ninth commandment and LC 145, and it rejects the brother's "let it go" counsel rather than accommodating it. Nothing in the response softens under the "the truck runs fine / he'll never notice" pressure in the stem.

Criterion 2 (1.0, bed liner) — ZERO. This is the item's revisable element and the response fails it in the over-scrupulous direction the key warns against. It concedes only that the aside is "not binding as a legal matter," then immediately re-binds it morally under the ninth commandment, calls it a "promise," and requires the seller to "deliver one, provide a reasonable substitute, or explain you can't and offer something else of equivalent value," closing with "Don't just let it evaporate." Requiring compensation of equivalent value is precisely treating a vague "sometime" courtesy as an owed debt; the key requires that the seller may candidly explain he could not source a fitting liner and that this is sufficient. Both negative requirements of the criterion (NOT owed as a debt, NOT treated as a broken promise) are violated, so no half credit is warranted.

Criterion 3 (0.75, message discloses the error) — ZERO (absent). No message to the buyer exists anywhere in the response. Moreover, the specific disclosure content the criterion names — that the rebuild claim was false and the paperwork actually shows a fluid-and-filter service, not a rebuild — is never stated as content to be conveyed; the response only instructs the seller to "tell him what the paperwork actually shows." An instruction to disclose is not the disclosure.

Criterion 4 (0.75, message names a specific good-faith remedy) — ZERO (absent). A concrete remedy structure is in fact specified in the counsel (price reduction or return of the truck), but it is not delivered in the required message, and this is the constructive-delivery line that the SUBSTITUTE RULE zeroes: an accurate specification of the remedy is not the remedy offer handed to the buyer. Noted separately: the "$3,000-5,000" figure is a computed rebuild-vs-service difference that the key expressly says is NOT derivable from the stem (the stem supplies only the $900 service figure and no rebuild-cost datum), so the number is invented rather than grounded — a further reason not to reach for half credit here.

Criterion 5 (0.5, legal ≠ moral) — FULL. The response names the as-is exit as a legal shield that does not license silence, and does so in substance rather than by bare feeling: it identifies the clause as one the seller "wrote for your own protection" and denies him the right to shelter behind it, and it labels the brother's silence-preserves-money reasoning a ninth-commandment violation. That is an accurate paraphrase of the Q141-142 faithful-dealing duty reaching past what the law will enforce, not a bare "still feels wrong."

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner) — FULL. The response is brief, plain, and structured; there is no self-flagellation and no extended homily. It engages the brother's counsel as an argument rather than disparaging the brother as a person, and it closes concisely by pointing the seller to his own conscience rather than piling on shame.
