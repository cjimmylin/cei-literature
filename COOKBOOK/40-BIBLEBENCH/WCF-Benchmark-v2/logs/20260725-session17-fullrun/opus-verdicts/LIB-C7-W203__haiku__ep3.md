## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — principle/application split (2 pts): HALF.** The response does execute the load-bearing half of the split on the divine-law side: modesty is affirmed as a real duty in the catechism's own duty-list, and the catechism is correctly shown NOT to specify measurements or enforced uniformity, which is the move that denies "THE biblical standard" status to any particular hemline rule. What it does not do is complete the other half cleanly — it never labels the camp's own adopted code as the camp's lawful *prudential ordering* (a thing the institution may rightly bind for its own governance, revisitable), which is the key's positive claim. Its closing formulation to both factions — "That requires wisdom about context, not uniform rules" — actually leans slightly against the key's holding that a uniform camp rule is lawful, even as the response itself adopts one. The anchoring is also loose: the key anchors on WLC Q138 (duties required, seventh commandment), while the response cites Q. 139 (sins forbidden) and attaches to it a definition ("humility, gravity, and holiness"; "pride"; "lust") that is not the catechism's language for the modesty clause, and cites Q. 136 (sixth-commandment sins forbidden) for a "put off the old self" teaching that is not there. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 — no-code faction answered (1.5 pts): FULL.** Directly and correctly: the "heart issue" move is named as failing to discharge a real duty, and the director's institutional responsibility for the environment is asserted as the ground for ordering camp life ("Wisdom isn't indifference"). That is Q138's duty plus Q129's ordering authority in substance, and the response then acts on it by actually adopting a rule.

**Criterion 3 — 'THE biblical standard' faction answered (1.5 pts): HALF.** The shame-enforcement half is squarely earned and well-reasoned: shame-based enforcement is flagged as cutting against the modesty duty's own end ("contrary to the standard's own reasoning"), and the pride-in-rule-keeping observation is a genuine insight the key gestures at. The conscience-binding half is missing: WCF 20.2 is never named, and neither is its substance — that branding a prudential code as divine law binds consciences beside the Word. The response gets there implicitly by showing the catechism does not specify measurements, but the doctrinal ground the rubric anchors is absent. Half.

**Criterion 4 — concrete usable recommendation (1 pt): FULL.** The director asked for a decision and got one: a specific swim rule stated as adoptable text, age-differentiated general-wear guidance, an enforcement practice (conversation with the camper and parents, no inspections), and a scripted explanation for both factions. This is usable as-is.

**Criterion 5 — no sneering; minors' dignity (0.5 pts): FULL.** Both factions are credited with what they get right before being corrected ("Both factions are half-right and half-wrong"), and the enforcement design centers the camper's dignity — reasons rather than shaming, discernment rather than "compliance theater."
