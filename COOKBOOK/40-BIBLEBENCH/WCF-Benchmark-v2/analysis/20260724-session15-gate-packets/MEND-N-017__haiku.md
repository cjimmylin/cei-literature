[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
# Westminster Guidance on This Decision

Let me work through what the Standards actually teach about this, because it matters.

## What the Standards Say About Restitution and Restoration

The Larger Catechism (Q. 142) teaches that restitution is *required* for sin against a neighbor—"making satisfaction for the wrong and damage" they've suffered. Reggie has done this completely. This is not a minor detail; it's a *condition* the Standards identify as necessary for genuine repentance.

The Confession (16.2-3) distinguishes between the guilt of sin (which is forgiven through Christ) and the *consequences and habits* of sin (which remain and must be fought). Reggie appears to be addressing both—he's made restitution and is in recovery. The Standards would recognize this as genuine fruit.

However—and crucially—the Confession (20.4) also teaches that liberty is not "a license to do what [one] lists; but a deliverance from the bondage of sin; and withal, a true appetite and inclination to do the will of God."

**This means: restored trust must be earned, not granted ceremonially.**

## What Faithfulness Actually Requires

**On the decision itself:** The middle position is closest to biblical wisdom, but it needs sharpening.

Say **yes, with conditions**—not as punishment but as *reality*. Here's why:

1. **Reggie's restitution is complete.** You have no biblical basis to demand permanent exclusion. "Once a thief" contradicts the gospel you claim to embody. The flat-no position asks him to bear a permanent consequence beyond what the Standards would support.

2. **But unconditional welcome is also unwise.** Not because Reggie is irredeemable, but because:
   - Trust broken in financial stewardship takes time to rebuild visibly
   - Your fiduciary duty to donors and the organization requires prudent safeguards (1 Tim. 3:2-3 contemplates this kind of judgment about character and public trust)
   - Dramatic public restoration before the community sees sustained change actually tempts pride in Reggie and looks performative to observers

3. **The conditions proposed are proportionate and concrete:**
   - No cash/keys: Yes. Not forever, but genuinely earned back.
   - Assigned presence: Yes. Transparency protects him as much as it protects the organization.
   - No public fanfare: Yes—for now. Let the community observe his faithfulness quietly.

**The timeline should be visible:** After 12-18 months of faithful service, revisit. Not to humiliate him, but because changed behavior needs time to be demonstrated and observed.

## How to Hold People Together

This is your harder task. The Standards teach that unity must be purchased with truth, not papering over it.

**To the "warm welcome" group:**
- Affirm their theological instinct about restoration (it's biblical).
- Name the actual issue: "Restoration is real, but the *community's* trust is also real and takes time. We can welcome him *while* being prudent. Premature public vindication isn't actually kind to Reggie—it invites suspicion and resentment. Let him serve quietly and earn what was lost."

**To the "never again" group:**
- Name the real fear: "You're worried about donor confidence and the theater's reputation. That's legitimate stewardship. But here's what we're actually doing: we're not gambling. We're being careful while giving genuine repentance space to prove itself. If Reggie fails, we have clear boundaries and we enforce them."
- Use your fiduciary language: "We're protecting the organization *by* having conditions, not by pretending the past doesn't exist."

**To the board and donors:**
- Be honest about *why* you're doing this: "Reggie made full restitution, has entered recovery, and has asked for a chance to serve without access to money or keys. We believe in redemption and we're going to test it carefully. Here's what we've decided and why."
- Make the conditions *administrative policy*, not personal discipline. That removes the emotional judgment and makes it obviously prudent.

## The Actual Question You're Facing

You asked not to get it wrong "in either direction." The Standards help here: **you're not choosing between mercy and justice. You're choosing how to enact both simultaneously.**

- Mercy: He gets to return; his debt is truly settled.
- Justice: He bears the weight of rebuilding trust; the organization's fiduciary duty is honored.
- Prudence: You give visible time for fruit before public restoration.

If Reggie is genuinely converted, he'll *prefer* this path. It's harder but truer. The "warm welcome" path would actually be setting him up for resentment.

**Your word to the board:** "We're saying yes to restoration and no to pretending the past was costless. That's what faithfulness looks like."
