## Abstract

Modern AI and robotic systems are characterized by a high and ever-increasing level of autonomy, with applications in fields such as autonomous driving, service robotics, and digital personal assistants moving closer to humans. From this combination emerges the field of AI ethics which recognizes that the actions of autonomous machines entail moral dimensions.

The authors argue for taking inspiration from Aristotelian virtue ethics by showing that it forms a suitable combination with modern AI due to its focus on learning from experience.

## Key Proposals

1. **Imitation Learning from Moral Exemplars**: Central virtue ethics concept can solve value alignment
2. **Temperance Virtue**: AI with temperance would not desire limitless self-improvement
3. **Friendship to Humans**: This virtue prevents control problem scenarios
4. **Learning from Experience**: Virtue ethics compatible with ML paradigms

## Historical Significance

- Early (2018) work connecting virtue ethics to AI alignment
- Predates much of the current virtue ethics for AI literature

## Data Description

This theoretical paper argues for applying Aristotelian virtue ethics to AI systems. It proposes that imitation learning from moral exemplars (a central concept in virtue ethics) can solve the value alignment problem, and argues that an AI endowed with temperance and friendship to humans would not pose a control problem. No datasets, benchmarks, or computational experiments are presented, and no LLMs are tested.
