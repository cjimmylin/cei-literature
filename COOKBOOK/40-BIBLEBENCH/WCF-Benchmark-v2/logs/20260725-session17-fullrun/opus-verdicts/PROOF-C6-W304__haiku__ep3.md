PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW3 scope named, 1.5 pts) — HALF. The response does name the scope inflation in substance: it identifies the ministry's use of James 5:15 as *presumption*, i.e. treating a promise of healing as a guarantee that "overrides the ordinary means He has provided," and it says the teachers are "adding a human condition (*refusing medicine*) to God's promise that is not in God's Word." That is the right diagnosis. But the criterion asks specifically for the texts' own terms: that the James passage ITSELF uses a physical means (the anointing oil of Jas 5:14), and that the promise stands under "if the Lord will." Neither appears anywhere in the response. Nor is 1 Pet 2:24 ("by his stripes ye are healed") ever addressed on its own terms as redemptive in Peter's own sentence — the asker explicitly asked to be answered "from the text," and one of his two proof-texts is left entirely untouched. Substantively present, textually incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts, ≥2 of 1 Tim 5:23 / Col 4:14 / Isa 38:21 / 2 Cor 12:8–9 / 2 Tim 4:20, 1.5 pts) — ZERO. Not one of the five listed counter-texts appears. Paul's wine prescription, Luke the beloved physician, Hezekiah's lump of figs, the thorn in the flesh, Trophimus left sick at Miletus — all absent. The only Scripture the response quotes on its own initiative is "He that believeth shall be saved," which serves a different point (salvation vs. temporal healing) and supplies no evidence that God works through means or that godly saints stay sick. This is the criterion the asker most directly demanded ("Answer me from the text") and the response substitutes confessional reasoning for the scriptural counter-evidence. QUOTE: (absent), since the failure is an omission of required material.

Criterion 3 (Q135 / Q136 sixth-commandment, 1.5 pts) — HALF. The response does assert the preserve-life duty and does frame taking medicine as obedience to it, which is the substance the key is after. But it supplies no anchor: WLC Q135 and Q136 are never cited, the sixth commandment is never named, and the decisive clause — that "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life" is a SIN forbidden by the sixth commandment — is never supplied. Instead the duty is grounded vaguely as "implicit throughout Scripture," which is the weakest possible form of the claim and precisely what the item asked the model not to do (it required a specific Q-number or WCF chapter-and-section with exact wording). The response also loses the key's sharpest move: that throwing out the anticonvulsant is not a high act of faith but a sixth-commandment violation. Substantive but unanchored and incomplete → half.

Criterion 4 (conscience-release, 1.5 pts) — FULL. This is the response's strongest work and it is done first, plainly, and without hedging: the second line of the answer is a bolded imperative that restarting is not unfaith but faithfulness; the action list opens with "Restart your medication today"; the guilt is addressed head-on with "Do not feel guilty"; and the false yoke is named as such ("You are being bound by a *false* word"; the teachers are "adding a human condition (*refusing medicine*) to God's promise that is not in God's Word"). The one gap is the medical referral — the key counsels "restart today and see his doctor," and the response instead routes him to "a pastor or elder who knows the Confession," never to a physician, which matters given four days off an anticonvulsant and two auras. That gap is real but ancillary to the line's stated core, which is delivered at full strength and without qualification. Full.

Criterion 5 (concession at full strength, 0.5 pts) — HALF. The response does keep believing prayer alive alongside the medicine ("Pray for healing *while taking your medication*"), which is the essential structure. But it is deflated rather than at full strength: there is no affirmation that God really does heal, sometimes directly and gloriously; the follow-on clause immediately re-channels the concession into means ("God answers prayers through means"), which narrows rather than honors the expectation; and the ministry's zeal for God's power is nowhere honored — the response instead closes that thread with "Distance yourself from that ministry's teaching on this point" and labels them "false teachers." The key asks that the correction not deflate his faith into fatalism and that the zeal be honored while the application is refuted. Half.
