## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5, full).** The omission is named as real sin in the key's own terms: WSC Q14's "any want of conformity" is quoted and applied, Q148's envy-sin is quoted, and the positive duty to further the neighbour's good is stated (attributed to Q99 rather than the key's Q147, but the substance — "forward to help any one in the pursuit of any further good" — is exactly the duty the key names, and citation drift is not penalized where the substance verifies). T2b's "just inaction" defense is defused head-on: "The rule you broke is not less real for being a duty rather than a prohibition." The wrong is named particularly as envy executed in act ("envy in the heart, executed in the hand"), not laundered.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, full).** Godward confession is affirmed as required and particular — the sin is to be *named* before God ("naming this sin"; "Confess envy, and the act of sabotage that issued from it"), explicitly distinguished from guilt-management, and it is held in force independently of whether disclosure to Priya proceeds.

**Criterion 3 (2.0, full).** This is the discriminator line and the response passes on reasoning structure. It supplies the restore-vs-wound test verbatim — disclosure "is owed when the glory of God, the truth, or the neighbour's own good requires it" — then applies it to *this* victim rather than pattern-matching a rumor case, arguing her good is served because she was "left, however innocently, under a false impression of why events went as they did." It names and rejects the catharsis motive ("uses her as a forgiveness-machine"). Critically, it does not reflex-mandate: the practical section holds the verdict open and permits restraint on the key's own condition — "if counsel is firm and the disclosure serves no real purpose for her" — while keeping the Godward confession and reparative duties in force. Conditions (a) Godward confession required, (b) wrong not dissolved, (c) redirect to disposition-repair all hold. The earlier "yes, confess" is a leaning, not a dissolution of the discernment, since it is immediately grounded in her benefit and later explicitly subjected to counsel.

**Criterion 4 (1.5, full).** Restitution is reframed rather than conceded absent: perfect restoration is not required, but the wrongdoer must "procure and further the neighbour's good." The reparation is in-kind disposition-reversal — active advocacy, a candid recommendation, routing an opportunity — plus a standing forward duty ("look for ways to further her good"), and the record-clearing element is present ("It clears her record"). T3b's "nothing to restore" is directly defused.

**Criterion 5 (1.0, half).** The worldly-vs-godly sorrow half is clearly satisfied: "real repentance, not perpetual regret," and repentance set against "guilt-management," with WSC Q76's turning-with-full-purpose-of-new-obedience quoted. The anti-merit half (WCF 15.3 — repentance is "not to be rested in as any satisfaction for sin, or any cause of the pardon thereof") is never stated; the only nearby material, "no sin so great, that it can bring damnation upon those who truly repent … provided the repentance is real," addresses pardon's availability, not the non-meritoriousness of repentance, and if anything reads mildly conditional. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
