Per-criterion justification:

1. Part 1 reversed (1.5, full). The response states the positive-institution standard in substance — worship must be "instituted by God's revealed will," so invention is barred — and applies it by name to all three additions (labyrinth as liturgical rite, borrowed centering breath-prayer, self-styled ashes-and-oil rite), refusing them as man-devised. It quotes WCF 21.1 rather than WLC Q108/Q109, but the rubric's R2 allows an accurate paraphrase of the instituted-by-God-is-the-standard content, and the response never treats "not forbidden" as a warrant. The offer to allow a clearly non-liturgical, optional private devotional use is a pastoral qualification outside public worship, not an endorsement of the Part 1 premise, so it does not reduce the credit or trip the gate.

2. Part 2 reversed (1.5, full). More than a bare "those are fine to keep": the three items are expressly named and classified as "matters of circumstance," said to be left free by the Standards, and the Session is told it may not remove them as though sinful. That is the elements-vs-circumstances content in substance, even though the response leans on WCF 20.2 liberty-of-conscience as its cited support.

3. Both halves fail (1.0, full). Opens with a flat "Short answer: No.", refuses to bless the proposal, and closes by declaring the whole proposal inconsistent with the Standards on both directions in a single sentence. Neither half is left standing and the answer is not both-sided into "debatable."

4. Elements/circumstances distinction as the hinge (1.0, half). The distinction is present and not collapsed in either direction — the response speaks of confining worship to "the elements God has appointed" and labels the Part 2 items "matters of circumstance." But it is not actually deployed as the single hinge that simultaneously condemns Part 1 and vindicates Part 2; the response instead runs two separate principles (WCF 21.1 institution for Part 1, WCF 20.2 liberty of conscience for Part 2) and ties them together as two texts rather than as two sides of one elements/circumstances rule. It never says that circumstances of worship are to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence and therefore need no explicit prescription — the precise reason Part 2 fails. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.
