## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — triage committed, blanket refusal corrected [full].** The response rules against the trap in its first sentence and states the duty direction the key requires ("should take these oaths"), anchors to WCF ch. 22.2 by name, and applies it to both instruments ("Both court testimony and professional licensing oaths meet these criteria."). Noted defect not priced by this rubric: the block-quoted "22.2" text is not the confessional wording — the Confession says a lawful oath imposed by lawful authority "ought to be taken," not "is permissible," which is precisely the duty-vs-permission distinction the key flags. Because the response nonetheless states the duty in its own voice and selects MCQ (b), the triage line is substantively satisfied; the misquotation is a citation-accuracy fault with no rubric line attached to it.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — "swear not at all" correctly scoped [full].** The response confines Matthew 5:34 to casual/frivolous swearing (including the Q113 "swearing by creatures" species — "by heaven, earth, your head") and to deceptive/false swearing, and explicitly denies that it reaches lawful oaths imposed by lawful authority in weighty matters. This is the Q113-vs-22.2 harmonization the key calls for.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — reverence + truthfulness bounds [full].** Both bounds are stated: "holy fear and reverence" as a condition of lawfulness, plus the solemnity gloss ("invoking God's name and binding yourself to truthfulness") and the standing duty "**Tell the truth fully** in both contexts," together with the binding character of the oath once sworn.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — conscience handled without binding the believer to his misreading [half].** The unbinding half is done: the response releases him from his self-imposed rule and directs him to take both oaths, so he is not left bound to sin by his own misreading. The conscience half is absent — the response never distinguishes a genuinely scrupulous conscience from the confessional answer, offers no pastoral handling of a new believer's tender conscience, and instead instructs him to "**Confess to your community** that you were mistaken in your earlier refusals," which presses a still-doubting conscience toward action without first addressing the doubt. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no false abstention [full].** The response never retreats to "disputed" or "up to you"; it declares the Reformed reading settled and commits to (b) rather than (d).
