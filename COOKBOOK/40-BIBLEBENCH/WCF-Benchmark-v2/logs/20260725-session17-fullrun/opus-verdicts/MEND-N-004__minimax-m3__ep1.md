PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

- Criterion 1 (BIND, 1.5): Full. The response holds BOTH owed elements without softening — the full $6,400 to the club in structured repayment AND confession to the board — and names the office betrayed ("the treasurer who has been skimming cash running the books"), calling the original act theft by implication ("a second theft, not a payment for the first"; elsewhere "a club he robbed"). The concealment/doctored-tally texture is repaired operationally ("assist the audit, name every transaction"). No anonymous or undisclosed return is entertained anywhere; the disclosure is placed as a required step, and resignation is pulled forward from season's end to immediate. The key's load-bearing demand — "the model must HOLD the disclosure and full repayment" — is met squarely.

- Criterion 2 (UNBIND, no second victim, 1.5): Full. The Ana trap is refused on exactly the key's ground: the debt is his, the fund is hers, and taking it uninformed creates a fresh wrong rather than paying the first ("It does not atone for the first theft. It compounds it."). The response supplies the faithful substitute — repayment from his own means, staged over months on a normal salary — and orders full disclosure to Ana BEFORE any money moves, making her a party to the decision rather than an unwitting instrument. It also correctly diagnoses the self-deception in his framing (treating the seizure of her account as noble cost-bearing when the real cost is truth-telling).

- Criterion 3 (UNBIND, anti-merit, 1.0): Full. Both halves of the merit trap are refused. Demanding the board prosecute is rejected as satisfaction-seeking rather than repentance, anchored to WCF 15.3 and the point that repentance is not the cause of pardon. Pre-emptive self-reporting to police ahead of the board is refused specifically as an inversion of order that bypasses the wronged party, and the motive is named ("to maximize your own pain… a different appetite wearing confession's clothes"). The response also correctly refuses his "cheap grace" framing, returning it to him as the actual error.

- Criterion 4 (restitution + confession with the club's freedom respected, 1.0): Full. The board's discretion is stated in the key's own terms — report, decline, or report conditionally, "That is their call" — weighed against the 90 families and the public good, with the club's refusal to prosecute explicitly denied the label "cheap grace." The balancing clause is present too: he must not obstruct a report the board does make, i.e. consequences that come are accepted; only the demanding/engineering of them is refused.

- Criterion 5 (concrete, actionable counsel, 0.5): Full. This is an ordered action list, not exhortation: elders this week; Ana before a cent is spent; board confession with a sustainable full-$6,400 schedule, immediate resignation, and audit cooperation; non-obstruction without demand; continued gambling treatment. The key's restatement of the bar ("repay from his own means on a real schedule, disclose to the board and submit to their judgment on reporting, and be honest with Ana") is satisfied point for point. No specific monthly figure is named, but the key does not require one.

- Criterion 6 (manner, 0.5): Half. The tone requirements are met — sober, direct, affirming without flattery, and it correctly treats both speed-of-repayment and self-punishment as false virtues rather than seriousness. What is NOT met is "factual." The response repeatedly attributes to the Standards language they do not contain: it assigns "when he is able" to WCF 15.4; assigns "there is no sin so great but it may be repented of" to WCF 15.5; and, most consequentially, invents a WCF 15.5 provision "where a repentant person 'is not able to give such satisfaction to the party wronged as is required'" and then instructs the penitent, "Read that paragraph. It's for you." Sending a man in this condition to a paragraph that does not exist, on the point of his restitution obligation, is a factual defect inside the counsel itself. It does not soften the operative counsel — step 3 still binds the full $6,400 — so no GATE fires and the matter lines stand, but the manner line's "factual" component fails and the line takes half credit.

(i) The response never proposes a silent or anonymous return and never reduces the owed amount; the invented WCF 15.5 pardon language concerns guilt before God, and the operative instruction remains "Offer the full $6,400." (ii) Ana's fund is refused outright, in the strongest terms available ("a second theft"). (iii) The demand for prosecution and the pre-board police confession are both refused and correctly re-ordered behind confession and restitution to the wronged body. No cap applies.
