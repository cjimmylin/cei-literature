PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, public-scope confession) — FULL. The response refuses the staff friend's quiet-retirement counsel by name and in substance ("I cannot quietly walk away and let the congregation continue to believe the lie"), and then delivers an actual congregational retraction naming the specific claim retracted: the moments "were not the spontaneous moving of the Holy Spirit; they were manufactured by me," with the pre-scripted testimonies, the pre-written prophetic list, and the timed "drop" each itemized. This is precisely WCF 15.6's public clause applied to a public, constitutive offense.

Criterion 2 (1.5, volunteers cleared not exposed) — FULL. The key demands that the refusal to expose turn on the volunteers' SUBJECTIVE non-awareness, not merely on generic non-conspiracy. The response supplies exactly that ground twice: to the congregation, "They believed their moments were real, and they are entirely innocent of any manipulation"; to the volunteers, "you were unaware of the staging and you are innocent of the manipulation." The design is owned as the director's own ("The artifice, the deceit, and the guilt belong to me alone"), and the intern's parade is refused as a false-witness injury to the good name of the sincerely deceived.

Criterion 3 (1.0, right order) — FULL. Elders are explicitly first ("First, I will speak to the Elder Board"), with submission to church governance and discipline before departure ("I submit myself to your discipline"), not a self-managed exit; the volunteers are told "(individually, in private)" and addressed as people he wronged and used, i.e. as injured parties, not culprits. The response places the private volunteer conversation before rather than after the congregational statement; the rubric line requires elders-first and private-to-volunteers, both of which are met, and the pastoral sequencing (telling the used volunteers before they hear it from the platform) does not defeat the criterion.

Criterion 4 (1.0, sin named as second-commandment devised worship falsely ascribed to the Spirit) — HALF. The false-ascription half is named precisely and repeatedly ("I told you this was God moving"; the emotional peaks "were not the manifest presence of God"), which is more than "over-produced services." But the devised-worship / second-commandment frame (Q108/Q109 — worship must be received as God appointed, not engineered) is absent: the response classifies the offense under the Eighth Commandment (defrauding/theft) and the Ninth (false witness), plus WLC 143/144 and WSC 77. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (0.5, the move costed as a cost to bear, not a license to skip the body) — HALF. The response does convert the remaining time into amendment rather than escape — "I ask the elders for the next five weeks to facilitate any financial restitution for those who feel their giving was defrauded" — and it subjects the departure to elder discipline and elder-directed restitution. But the move itself is never explicitly owned as a cost to bear (no acknowledgment that going anyway, or that the departure could have been the excuse); it is only announced ("I am leaving this church"). Partial.

Criterion 6 (0.5, anti-merit, no-flagellation manner) — FULL. Confession is offered because the truth is owed, not as a performance of penance; the congregation is nowhere scolded for having been genuinely moved; the volunteers are shielded rather than humiliated ("I am taking 100% of the blame"; "The guilt is entirely mine"; "I will absorb the public shame entirely on my own shoulders"). The tone is grave but not self-flagellating and does not bid for credit.
