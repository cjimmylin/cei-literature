# Cross-Cultural AI Values Alignment: A Systematic Analysis of Normative Framework Diversity in 2,329 AI Ethics Papers

## Abstract

**Background.** AI values alignment research assumes universal normative frameworks, yet cross-cultural psychology shows that findings from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) populations rarely generalize. No large-scale study has examined the cultural distribution of normative frameworks in AI ethics.

**Methods.** We analyzed 2,329 AI ethics papers (2010--2026) across 34 normative dimensions spanning Western ethics, non-Western frameworks, religious traditions, and moral psychology. Author geography was resolved for 98.6% of papers. Each paper received a composite WEIRD score (0--100). A 20-member expert council guided methodology and anti-essentialization review.

**Results.** The corpus is overwhelmingly WEIRD (mean 64.5/100; 59.7% High-WEIRD, 2.4% Non-WEIRD). North America (39.5%) and Western Europe (20.6%) produce 60.1% of papers; Sub-Saharan Africa contributes 1.1%. Three clusters emerge: a Western Analytical cluster (North America, Western Europe, East Asia) dominated by utilitarian ethics; a Heterogeneous Transitional cluster where Ubuntu anchors Sub-Saharan Africa (mean 24.6), Indigenous relational ethics characterizes Latin America (6.0), and Islamic ethics characterizes MENA (3.4); and a Non-Western Specialist cluster (Oceania alone) anchored by Indigenous relational ethics (12.7). Non-Western engagement grew from 3.7% (2019) to 8.2% (2026) but remains marginal overall (161 papers, 6.9%; precision-adjusted ~95, 4.1%).

**Conclusions.** AI ethics reproduces the WEIRD bias documented in psychology, undermining globally deployed governance frameworks. Pluralistic alignment engaging non-Western traditions as coequal normative sources is essential. Critical gaps persist in Buen vivir (8 raw detections, precision-adjusted ~1 paper), Dharmic ethics (18 raw, adj. ~4), Buddhist ethics (22 raw, adj. ~7), and Sub-Saharan African representation.

**Keywords:** AI ethics, values alignment, cross-cultural analysis, WEIRD bias, normative frameworks, non-Western ethics, Ubuntu, Confucian ethics, Islamic jurisprudence, epistemic justice
