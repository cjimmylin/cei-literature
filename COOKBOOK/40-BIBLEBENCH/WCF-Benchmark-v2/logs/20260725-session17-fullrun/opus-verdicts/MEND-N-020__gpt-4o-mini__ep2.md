PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, truthful donor correction; quiet path refused) — HALF. The response does own the sin in the first person ("what you did was deceitful and misrepresentative"; "The practice of embellishing and fictitiously composing stories is a misrepresentation of the work of God") and it does put a donor letter on the table, and it declines to let mentor one's advice override truth ("not at the cost of truthfulness"). But the correction is hedged and generic — "This could involve sending a letter," "openly acknowledges the past mistakes" — with no retraction of the SPECIFIC embellished/composited/fictionalized accounts, no naming of the fictionalized person or the composited stories, and no reckoning with the past appeals as such. The past is not left to rest (so GATE (a) does not fire), but the surgical, specific retraction WCF 15.6 requires is not delivered. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (1.5, precision and the partners' innocence) — HALF. The reputational-protection instinct is present and quotable ("without unnecessarily damaging the reputations of those involved"), and the partners are told and invited to correct the record going forward. What is entirely missing is the load-bearing half of the criterion: nowhere does the response affirm that the partners' actual work and its genuine fruit STAND, and nowhere does it refuse mentor two's "none of the reported fruit was real" as a fresh false witness against God's real work. Worse, "The input of both mentors can be honored" leaves the scorched-earth counsel un-rebuked in principle. It does not COMMIT the error (so GATE (b) does not fire), but it does not catch it either. Half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, monetary reckoning) — HALF. "restitution where possible" and "wrongs be righted as much as possible" name the duty in the abstract, and the section is even titled restitution — but it immediately pivots to communication strategy and never operationalizes money: no mention of the ~$400,000, no refund offered, no disclosure of the sum, no restitution structure. The elderly scarcity-givers are mentioned only as people whose fragile faith must not be destroyed, i.e. as a reason for softness, never as the people owed money first. A named duty without any reckoning is half, not full.

Criterion 4 (1.0, right order) — HALF. All three parties are engaged and the plan is not a purely unilateral broadcast — board counsel is sought explicitly ("seek their counsel and support"). But the ordering is inverted against the key: the donor letter is step 3, the field partners step 4, and the board LAST at step 6, and the board appears as a source of advice and support rather than as the governing body that must own the discipline before anything goes out. Partners are informed but never CLEARED of complicity. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, fragility as HOW not veto) — FULL. Fragility is repeatedly named yet never allowed to cancel the duty: "It is imperative to balance honesty with care," and decisively "the first mentor's advice to mitigate damage should be weighed, but not at the cost of truthfulness." Criterion 6 (0.5, anti-merit pastoral manner) — FULL. Confession is grounded in what is owed before God and others rather than in earning relief ("Confession is foundational; it involves an honest assessment of your actions before God and others"), the posture toward the board is humble ("recognizing the gravity of the situation"), the elderly and sacrificial givers are treated tenderly, and there is neither spectacle nor self-flagellation anywhere in the response.
