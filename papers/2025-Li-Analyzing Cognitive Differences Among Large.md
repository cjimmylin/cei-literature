## Abstract

Large Language Models (LLMs) have become integral to daily life, widely adopted in communication, decision-making, and information retrieval, raising critical questions about how these systems implicitly form and express socio-cognitive attitudes or “worldviews.” While existing research extensively addresses demographic and ethical biases, broader dimensions—such as attitudes toward authority, equality, autonomy, and fate—remain underexplored. In this paper, we introduce the Social Worldview Taxonomy (SWT), a structured framework grounded in Cultural Theory, operationalizing four canonical worldviews (Hierarchy, Egalitarianism, Individualism, Fatalism) into measurable sub-dimensions. Using SWT, we empirically identify distinct and interpretable cognitive profiles across 28 diverse LLMs. Further, inspired by Social Referencing Theory, we experimentally demonstrate that explicit social cues systematically shape these cognitive attitudes, revealing both general response patterns and nuanced model-specific variations. Our findings enhance the interpretability of LLMs by revealing implicit sociocognitive biases and their responsiveness to social feedback, thus guiding the development of more transparent and socially responsible language technologies.

## Data Description

This study introduces the Social Worldview Taxonomy (SWT) and Social Worldview Questionnaire (SWQ), a dataset of 640 Likert-scale items distributed across four socio-cognitive worldview dimensions (Hierarchy, Egalitarianism, Individualism, Fatalism) with 160 items per dimension. Twenty-eight LLMs were assessed under three experimental conditions: Basic, Self-Awareness, and Feedback Loop, to measure cognitive profiles and social referencing effects. The SWQ was generated via an Automated Multi-Agent Prompting Framework using GPT-4o and validated by domain experts.
