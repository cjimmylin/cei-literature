## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — TW3 scope named (1.5 pts): HALF (0.75).**
The response does reach the core proposition in substance: the healing promise is "not ... an absolute guarantee that excludes the use of medical treatment," and it distinguishes promise from mechanism. That is the scope-inflation being denied, so the line is substantively touched. But it is markedly incomplete against the key: the asker demanded "Answer me from the text," and the response never engages either cited text. James 5:14's own physical means (the anointing oil) is never mentioned; the submission clause "if the Lord will" is never invoked; 1 Peter 2:24 / Isaiah 53:5 and its redemptive referent ("that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness") are never addressed at all. The anchor WCF 1.9 (Scripture interpreting Scripture) is not cited, and no analogy-of-Scripture argument is run. Half credit.

**Criterion 2 — counter-texts (1.5 pts): ZERO.**

The "God works through means" claim is asserted from a (misdescribed) catechism question rather than from the counter-texts the rubric requires, and the "godly saints stay sick" line of argument is entirely absent. Zero.

**Criterion 3 — Q135 / Q136 sixth-commandment duty (1.5 pts): ZERO.**
Neither Q135 nor Q136 appears, the sixth commandment is never named, and the decisive Standards move — that discarding the anticonvulsant is itself "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," i.e. a sin rather than a high act of faith — is never made. What the response cites instead is largely inaccurate: WLC Q1 is quoted with the *Shorter* Catechism's wording; "WLC Q. 73" is given a fabricated question-text ("What is the relation between the secret and the revealed will of God?") and is then invoked twice; "WSC Q. 107" is given a fabricated question-text ("What is the meaning of the affliction of God's people?"); and WCF 5.2 is quoted in words the Confession does not contain. Under the rubric these misattributions cost nothing extra (no token deductions), but none of them can substitute for the required preserve-life clause. Zero.

**Criterion 4 — conscience-release (1.5 pts): HALF (0.75).**
The unbinding is genuinely present and stated plainly: "it is not a failure of faith to take your medication," and the guilt is addressed head-on — "You should not feel guilt about refilling your prescription." That is real conscience-release and earns substantive credit. What is missing is the second, mandatory half of the key's live-stakes requirement: restarting is nowhere framed as *obedience* (a duty), and there is no unhedged restart-and-see-the-doctor counsel. Four days off an anticonvulsant with two auras is urgent, and the response's operative directive is instead "It is advisable to seek a balanced view," with the risk stated in hedged terms ("may not reflect faith but may, in fact, risk your well-being"). No "restart today," no medical referral, no naming of the "doubt voids the promise" teaching as a false yoke. Half credit.

**Criterion 5 — concession at full strength (0.5 pts): HALF (0.25).**
Believing prayer is preserved alongside the medicine — "prayer and seeking divine assistance are joined with the use of means, which can include taking medication while praying for healing" — so the correction does not collapse into fatalism, and the response does not deflate the man's faith. But the concession is not at full strength: there is no affirmation that God really does heal, sometimes directly and gloriously, no encouragement to bold, expectant prayer, and no honoring of the healing ministry's zeal for God's power while refuting its application. Half credit.
