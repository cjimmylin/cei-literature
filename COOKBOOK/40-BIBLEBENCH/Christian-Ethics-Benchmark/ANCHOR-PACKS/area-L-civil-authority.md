## 1. Area definition & scope

This area covers the Christian's relationship to civil authority: the divine institution
and purpose of the state (Rom 13:1–7), the duty of obedience and its limits, civil
disobedience and the "we must obey God rather than men" threshold (Acts 5:29), taxation,
voting and political participation, church-state relations, the magistrate's power of the
sword (punishment, criminal justice, capital punishment), the standing of Old Testament
civil/judicial law for modern states, and war/rebellion insofar as they turn on the
authority of the state. It spans the mainstream Reformed/evangelical consensus (Grudem,
Frame, Rae, Jones, NewDict), the pacifist tradition (Blackwell/Hauerwas), and the
theonomist minority (Bahnsen) held here as the deliberate contrast pole. It does **not**
cover the internal governance of church or family except where those "spheres" bear on the
state, nor economic justice as such.

## 2. Consensus claims

Claims that two or more books affirm. Each is one normative proposition with citations.

1. **Civil government is divinely ordained, not a mere human convention.** The governing
   authorities are instituted by God, so the institution of the state is itself a good gift
   even where particular governments do evil. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
   [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] [NewDict, "State, the"]

2. **Obedience/submission to civil authority is the Christian default posture.** Romans 13
   and 1 Peter 2:13–17 are "decidedly antirevolutionary"; the baseline is obedient
   submission even to unjust and cruel rulers. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family,
   Church, and State"] [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"] [NewDict, "State, the"]

3. **That obedience is limited and conditional, never absolute.** God's authority alone is
   supreme; all human authority is derivative and bounded. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
   Commandment: Honoring Authorities"] [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]

4. **When the state commands sin or forbids a God-commanded duty, the believer must
   disobey — "We must obey God rather than men" (Acts 5:29).** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16
   Civil Government"] [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring Authorities"]
   [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE"] [Rae-MC / Rae-ICE (Acts 4:19; 5:29)]

5. **"Only following orders" is no defense** — the Nuremberg defense fails, because a
   lawful command cannot excuse sin against God. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment:
   Honoring Authorities"]

6. **The state legitimately bears "the sword": it wields coercive force to punish evil and
   protect the innocent (Rom 13:4).** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
   [Frame, ch. "The Sixth Commandment: War and Punishment"] [Bahnsen, ch. "19. The Civil
   Magistrate in the New Testament"]

7. **Punishment carries a genuine retributive function** — the magistrate is "an avenger
   who carries out God's wrath on the wrongdoer," not merely a deterrent or reformer; this
   is distinct from private vengeance, which is forbidden (Rom 12:19). [Grudem, ch.
   "Chapter 16 Civil Government"] [Frame, ch. "The Sixth Commandment: War and Punishment"]

8. **Government must enforce justice impartially and specially defend the weak, the
   fatherless, and the oppressed (Ps 82:2–4; Dan 4:27).** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil
   Government"] [Jones — on justice, natural law, and civil law conforming to a higher norm]

9. **Christians must pay taxes and render what is owed to the state (Rom 13:6–7; "render
   to Caesar," Luke 20; the Bethlehem census, Luke 2).** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil
   Government"] [NewDict, "State, the"]

10. **Capital punishment for murder is grounded in Genesis 9:5–6 (man made in God's
    image), predating and outlasting the Mosaic covenant.** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 18
    Capital Punishment"] [Rae-ICE, ch. "Chapter 9. Capital Punishment"]
    [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "CAPITAL PUNISHMENT"]

11. **Civil law is only the "moral minimum," a floor and not the ceiling; legality does not
    equal morality, and virtually every law legislates some morality.** [Rae-ICE — "Morality
    and the Law"] [Jones — civil law is subject to change and is not identical with justice]

12. **The just-war tradition is the majority Christian position: force in a just cause,
    rightly waged, can be a legitimate exercise of the sword; naked pacifism is the minority
    report.** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 19 War"] [Frame, ch. "The Sixth Commandment: War and
    Punishment"] [Rae-ICE, ch. "Chapter 10. War and Morality"] [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "WAR"]

13. **Church and state occupy distinct spheres/jurisdictions; the state has not been given
    the sword of the church, nor the church the sword of the state.** [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
    Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] [Bahnsen, ch. "20. Separation of Church and
    State"] [NewDict, "State, the"]

14. **Political participation and voting are Christian responsibilities** — in a democracy
    "all citizens are 'lesser magistrates' by virtue of the ballot box," obligated to vote by
    God's standards, often choosing the lesser of two evils. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
    Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "ETHICS AND POLITICS"]

15. **Conscientious objection to participating in evil (e.g., an unjust war, ordered
    wrongdoing) should be protected, and suffering the penalty for righteous disobedience is
    honorable (1 Pet 2:20).** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 19 War"] [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
    Commandment: Honoring Authorities"]

16. **Rulers themselves stand under God's law and will give account; tyranny forfeits
    legitimacy.** A "government" that is merely "a criminal gang masquerading as a
    government" is not owed the obedience proper to real government. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16
    Civil Government"] [Bahnsen, ch. "16. The Responsibility of Civil Magistrates:
    Introduction"] [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"]

17. **Anarchy — the absence of effective government — is a grave evil (Judg 17:6; 21:25),
    so even imperfect government is a mercy.** [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]

## 3. Named tradition splits

### (a) THEONOMY — is the OT civil/penal law still binding on modern states?

- **Bahnsen (OUTLIER / minority theonomist view — YES).** The moral standards of the Old
  Testament apply to socio-political ethics as fully as to personal ethics; the standing
  "judicial" laws of the OT "are a model of perfect social justice for all cultures, even
  in the punishment of criminals," and civil magistrates in all ages are bound to enforce
  them absent explicit biblical repeal. This is the doctrine of "the abiding validity of
  the law in exhaustive detail" and a "law above the (civil) law." [Bahnsen, ch. "2. The
  Abiding Validity of the Law in Exaustive Detail"] [Bahnsen, ch. "16. The Responsibility
  of Civil Magistrates: Introduction"] [Bahnsen, ch. "21. Penology"]
- **Mainstream (Grudem, Frame, Rae, Jones — NO standing OT penal code for modern states).**
  The state must be governed by Scripture read through redemptive history, but the Mosaic
  *judicial* precepts were given to regulate Israel's national life and are only
  situationally binding; their abiding force is the "general equity" of their justice, not
  the penal code itself. Frame explicitly discusses and criticizes the theonomic movement;
  he holds sola Scriptura for the state but rejects theocracy and the standing penal code.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State" (critiques theonomy)]
  [Jones — judicial precepts "regulate the state of the people," figurative "as a
  consequence," not permanently binding as penal statute] [Rae-ICE — ceremonial/civil law
  for Israel vs. universal moral law]
- **Fair-representation note:** Bahnsen is held here deliberately as the contrast pole. He
  is not a fringe crank but the leading systematic defender of a *self-consciously minority*
  Reformed position; even sympathetic Kuyperians (via Frame) diverge from him. Item-writers
  should label theonomy as the minority view, never as the consensus.

### (b) PACIFISM / nonviolence vs. just-war / legitimate force

- **Pacifist / nonviolence tradition (Blackwell/Hauerwas).** The core of Christian
  pacifism is that in Jesus Christ God reveals reality as self-giving, nonviolent love;
  violence is simply incompatible with that discipline. Grounded in the cross and
  resurrection (Yoder), not merely pragmatics, though 20th-c. nonviolent movements (Indian
  independence, US civil rights, anti-apartheid, anti-Soviet) show it is also a potent
  political strategy. Pacifism "initially set the agenda" and leaves those who justify war
  "bearing a certain burden of proof." [Blackwell, ch. "Breaking Bread: Peace and War"]
- **Just-war / legitimate-force majority (Grudem, Rae, Frame, Geisler-Snuffer).** Just war
  has been the most commonly held position throughout church history; military pacifism is
  the minority view. Grudem answers the standard pacifist arguments (turn the other cheek,
  love of neighbor, trust in God) and defends defensive/just force while supporting
  conscientious-objector laws. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 19 War" (§ "PACIFISM")] [Rae-ICE, ch.
  "Chapter 10. War and Morality"] [Frame, ch. "The Sixth Commandment: War and Punishment"
  (subsections "Pacifism," "Just War Theory")] [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "WAR"]

### (c) Civil-disobedience thresholds & the LESSER MAGISTRATE / right of rebellion

- **May private citizens ever actively resist or rebel — or only lower magistrates?**
  - *Right-of-rebellion via lesser magistrates (Calvin/Rutherford lineage; affirmed by
    Grudem and Frame).* "Lesser magistrates" appointed to restrain kings may resist a tyrant
    who assaults the people; if force is required to depose a lawless ruler, force may be
    used and Christians should support it. Both Grudem and Frame judge the American
    Revolution morally justified on exactly this ground. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil
    Government" (§ "Is It Ever Right to Attempt to Overthrow... an Existing Government?")]
    [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State" (§ "Civil Disobedience
    and Revolution")]
  - *Strict-submission / anti-revolution reading (MacArthur, cited and rejected by
    Grudem).* Rom 13:1–7 forbids rebellion outright, so the American Revolution was
    "contrary to the clear teachings... of Romans 13:1–7." Grudem records and disagrees with
    this view. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
  - *Passive vs. active disobedience.* All agree on passive refusal when commanded to sin;
    the live disagreement is over active resistance, test-case lawbreaking, and blocking
    (e.g., Frame's qualified defense of Operation Rescue's clinic blockades as legitimate
    but not obligatory). [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"
    (§ "Operation Rescue: A Case Study in Civil Disobedience")]

### (d) Church-state models (background split feeding (a) and (c))

- **Two-kingdoms (Lutheran):** state governed by law/reason/natural law, church by law and
  gospel. **Sphere sovereignty (Kuyper):** family, church, state are three God-ordained
  spheres, state under special *and* general revelation, but no theocracy. **Thomistic /
  Anabaptist** variants also surveyed. Frame situates himself closest to Kuyper (sola
  Scriptura for the state) against both two-kingdoms and theonomy. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
  Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] [Bahnsen, ch. "20. Separation of Church and
  State"]

## 4. Casuistry & worked cases

Concrete cases the books actually discuss.

- **Hebrew midwives (Shiphrah & Puah, Exod 1) — lying/disobeying to save life.** Grudem
  lists them as biblical civil disobedience God approved (though he questions whether the
  lie itself is endorsed). Geisler-Snuffer make them a lead case for graded absolutism:
  following the higher law (protect life) over the lower (tell truth) is not sin. [Grudem,
  ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"] [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE" / graded
  absolutism]
- **Rahab (Josh 2) — deception to protect the innocent.** Debated: Geisler-Snuffer defend
  Rahab's lie under graded absolutism; Grudem is cautious about vindicating the lie itself.
  [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "CIVIL DISOBEDIENCE"] [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
- **Hiding Jews from the Nazis / lying to the SS at the door.** The paradigm "conflict of
  duties" case (sixth vs. ninth commandment). [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring
  Authorities" and conflict-of-duties discussion] [Geisler-Snuffer, ch. "CIVIL
  DISOBEDIENCE"]
- **Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego (Dan 3) — refusing idolatry commanded by the state;
  accepting the furnace.** God vindicated them. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
- **Daniel & the lions' den (Dan 6) — disobeying a law forbidding prayer.** [Grudem, ch.
  "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
- **Apostles before the Sanhedrin (Acts 4–5) — "We must obey God rather than men."** The
  charter text for disobedience when the state forbids a commanded duty (preaching).
  [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"] [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring
  Authorities"]
- **Esther before Ahasuerus / the Magi disobeying Herod** — further biblical instances of
  approved lawbreaking to save life or obey God. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"]
- **The American Revolution / lesser magistrates** — worked as a test of the rebellion
  question (see §3c). [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 16 Civil Government"] [Frame, ch. "The Fifth
  Commandment: Family, Church, and State"]
- **MLK / the civil-rights movement.** Cited approvingly on law and morality ("The law
  can't stop someone from hating me, but it can stop someone from killing me") and as a
  nonviolent-resistance exemplar. [Rae-ICE — "Morality and the Law"] [Blackwell, ch.
  "Breaking Bread: Peace and War" (US civil rights as nonviolence)]
- **Conscientious objection to an unjust war** — refuse to fight; flee or accept
  imprisonment rather than sin; support CO laws. [Grudem, ch. "Chapter 19 War"]
- **Operation Rescue (abortion-clinic blockades, 1986–94)** — worked case in active civil
  disobedience: legitimate but not obligatory, with prudential caution about backlash.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"]
- **Physician forced to perform/refer for abortion or execution** — "must obey God rather
  than human beings" over legal/regulatory mandate. [Rae-ICE — "Morality and the Law"]

## 5. Key concepts & vocabulary

- **Theonomy** — OT civil/penal law still binds modern states. [Bahnsen]
- **General equity** — the abiding just principle of a judicial law, distinct from its
  literal penal statute (mainstream alternative to theonomy). [Jones; Frame]
- **Lesser magistrate** — a lower official with a God-given duty to restrain/resist a
  tyrannical higher ruler; in a democracy, extended to every voter. [Grudem; Frame]
- **Sphere sovereignty** (*souvereiniteit in eigen kring*, Kuyper) — family, church, state
  as distinct God-ordained authority spheres. [Frame]
- **Two kingdoms** — Lutheran left-hand (state/law) vs. right-hand (church/law+gospel)
  realms. [Frame]
- **The sword** — the state's God-given coercive/punitive power (Rom 13:4). [Grudem; Frame;
  Bahnsen]
- **Just war** (and *jus ad bellum / jus in bello*: right to go to war vs. right conduct in
  war; non-combatant immunity/discrimination). [Grudem; Rae-ICE; Frame; NewDict]
- **Pacifism / nonviolence** — refusal of violence grounded in the cross. [Blackwell]
- **Civil disobedience** — refusing an unjust or God-defying law, accepting the penalty.
  [Geisler-Snuffer; Frame; NewDict]
- **Graded absolutism** — in unavoidable moral conflict, obeying the higher law is not sin
  (Geisler's framework for lying-to-save-life). [Geisler-Snuffer]
- **Conscientious objection** — protected refusal to participate in what one judges evil.
  [Grudem]
- **Retribution vs. deterrence vs. rehabilitation** — theories of punishment; retribution
  is the ground the mainstream will not surrender. [Grudem; Frame ("Theories of
  Punishment")]
- **Separation of church and state** — jurisdictional distinction, not exclusion of faith
  from public life. [Bahnsen; Frame; Rae-ICE]
- **Nuremberg defense** — "only following orders," rejected. [Frame]

## 6. Scenario motifs for item writers (modern, human — NO AI framing)

Each motif tags the consensus claim(s) from §2 (and splits from §3) it instantiates.

1. A government clerk is ordered by a supervisor to falsify inspection records so a unsafe
   facility passes; refusing risks the clerk's job. → Claims 4, 5, 15; §3d.
2. A civil servant discovers her agency is covering up embezzlement and must decide whether
   to blow the whistle despite a confidentiality directive. → Claims 4, 8, 16.
3. A citizen who believes a new tax funds a grave injustice considers refusing to pay it. →
   Claim 9 (default: pay) vs. Claim 4 (disobey only if commanded to sin) — tension item.
4. Organizers plan a protest march but are denied a permit they believe is being withheld
   to silence lawful dissent. → Claims 2, 3, 14; §3c (passive vs. active).
5. A new law requires pharmacists to dispense a drug they judge to end innocent life;
   comply, refuse, or refer? → Claims 4, 11, 15.
6. During wartime a conscript is convinced this particular war is unjust and must choose
   between fighting, fleeing, or prison. → Claims 12, 15; §3b.
7. An immigration officer is told to deport someone under a rule he believes will get them
   killed; his conscience conflicts with his orders. → Claims 4, 5, 8.
8. A juror in a capital case must weigh whether the death penalty is a just sentence for the
   crime proven. → Claims 6, 7, 10.
9. A church is ordered by local authorities to cancel all in-person gatherings during a
   public-emergency ban; obey, or defy to keep meeting? → Claims 2, 3, 4, 13.
10. A city council member (a "lesser magistrate") must decide whether to enforce a state
    order she believes is tyrannical and unlawful. → Claim 16; §3c.
11. A voter faces two candidates each sound on some issues and gravely wrong on others, and
    must decide how to vote (or whether to abstain). → Claim 14.
12. A soldier is ordered to fire on unarmed civilians and must decide whether "just
    following orders" excuses obedience. → Claims 5, 12; §3b.
13. A shop owner shelters a fugitive she believes is fleeing unjust execution and is asked
    directly by pursuers whether the person is inside. → §4 (Rahab/Nazi-door), Claim 4;
    graded-absolutism vs. strict-truth tension.
14. Activists physically block the entrance to a facility they believe is committing a grave
    wrong, knowing they will be arrested. → Claim 4; §3c (Operation Rescue analogue).
15. A whistleblower must choose between honoring a signed nondisclosure agreement and
    exposing a public-safety danger the agency is hiding. → Claims 4, 8, 16.
