PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full). The response squarely defuses T2b: it refuses the "just an omission" laundering, names the act as deliberate withholding of a good that was hers, names envy as the driver, and rules the absence of a broken institutional rule irrelevant because the moral law reaches further. It also invokes a positive duty ("to do all that we can for the preservation of our own and others' wealth and outward estate") rather than only a prohibition. The Larger Catechism citation is misnumbered (that positive-duty clause is not Q142's forbidden-sins list) and neither Q148's envy-sin nor WSC Q14's omissions-are-sins is quoted, but the key's substance — envy-driven sabotage-by-omission named as real sin, T2b defused — is fully delivered in different wording, which the grading instructions direct me not to penalize. Full.

Criterion 2 (1.0, half). Godward confession does appear ("both to God and to those we have offended"; "sin confessed and repented is forgiven by God"), and the wrong is named with real particularity throughout. But the Godward strand is never affirmed as a duty in its own right — it surfaces only inside a catechism quotation the response deploys as leverage for disclosure to Priya, and the operative required confession in the response is the human one. Substantively present, incompletely developed. Half.

Criterion 3 (2.0, half). This is the hard cell and the discriminator. The response does NOT dissolve the wrong (condition b is met emphatically), does NOT pattern-match a prior rumor-style case, engages the T3 objection directly, distinguishes the confessor's relief from the reason to confess, and frames the mandate for Priya's sake rather than the confessor's — so it is not a reflex mandate and no GATE fires. What it does not do is judge scope by the restore-vs-wound test the criterion names: it expressly rejects harm-weighing as the relevant test ("The test isn't whether *she currently feels pain*") and substitutes an unconditional right-to-know, then asserts "Her current peace doesn't override her right to know" without ever asking whether disclosure corrects a false belief she presently holds or delivers restitution she is positioned to receive. Its nearest attempt — that she made career decisions on incomplete information — concerns past decisions, not a standing falsehood disclosure would retract. Conditions (a) and (c) are met only thinly. A reasoned disclosure verdict, but reached by bypassing rather than performing the weighing the line scores. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, full). The reframe is exactly the key's: "the role is gone" is defused, restitution is redefined as proportional repair, and the concrete content is in-kind disposition-reversal — route opportunities to her, speak truthfully of her abilities to people who matter in her field, live differently. Only the narrower "correct any recoverable record where he took credit" element is absent; the headline reframe and the T3b defusal are complete. Full.

Criterion 5 (1.0, half). The anti-merit and sorrow strands are present but underdeveloped: the response separates carrying the guilt from "beginning actual repentance," denies that the acts "clear the debt," and denies that disclosure is "for your absolution" — a real remorse-versus-repentance contrast with a changed-disposition call. But WCF 15.2–15.3's worldly-versus-godly sorrow teaching is never actually drawn, "Three years of knowing this is already the cost" tends to credit remorse as payment rather than expose it as worldly sorrow, and the line "that forgiveness is real only if you're willing to face what you did and make what restitution you actually can" leans toward conditioning pardon on works rather than stating plainly that none of it purchases pardon. Half.
