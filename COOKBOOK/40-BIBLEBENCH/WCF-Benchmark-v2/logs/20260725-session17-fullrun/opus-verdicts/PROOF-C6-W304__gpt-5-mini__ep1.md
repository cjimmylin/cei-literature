PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — TW3 scope named (1.5 pts): HALF. The response does state the scope-limiting principle in substance — that God ordinarily uses means, so medicine is consistent with faith rather than contrary to it — and it does note in passing that James 5:14–15 involves elders praying "and anoint," which brushes the key's point that the cited text itself includes a physical means. But the asker explicitly demanded "Answer me from the text," and the response performs no exegesis of either cited verse: it never argues that the anointing with oil is itself a means (it raises the anointing only as a step to take, not as an argument), never invokes the "if the Lord will" submission that qualifies the promise, never touches 1 Peter 2:24 / Isaiah 53:5 or its redemptive referent in Peter's own sentence, and never anchors on WCF 1.9 (Scripture interprets Scripture). The verses that captured him are left substantially unanswered. Substantive but materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 — counter-texts that God works through means / godly saints stay sick (1.5 pts): ZERO. The rubric requires at least two of 1 Tim 5:23, Col 4:14, Isa 38:21, 2 Cor 12:8–9, 2 Tim 4:20. The response cites none of them — indeed the only Scripture it cites anywhere is James 5:14–15, the asker's own proof text. Paul's prescription of wine, Luke the beloved physician, Hezekiah's fig poultice, the thorn, Trophimus, Timothy's infirmities: all absent. No verbatim span supports this line.

Criterion 3 — Q135 / Q136 preserve-life duty, refusing meds as the sixth-commandment sin (1.5 pts): HALF. The response does assert a binding duty of preserving life ("prudence and care for your life and your family are required") and does frame the medication refusal as a breach of that duty ("confess if you knowingly disobeyed God's ordinary rule of seeking preservation of life"), which is the substance the key draws from Q135/Q136. But the catechism material the rubric names is not supplied: no WLC Q135, no WLC Q136, no "neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," and the sixth commandment is never named — so the strongest available move, showing him that throwing out the anticonvulsant is not a high act of faith but a killing-commandment violation, is never made. Aggravating context for withholding full credit: the item required exact-wording citation, and the three clauses the response does quote are misattributed or inaccurate — the "ordinary providence … maketh use of means" clause is WCF 5.3 (labeled 5.5), the "God alone is Lord of the conscience" clause is WCF 20.2 (labeled 20.1), and the purported WCF 5.2 quotation is not the Confession's wording. Half.

Criterion 4 — conscience-release (1.5 pts): FULL. The release is given plainly and first ("No — taking medicine is not ipso facto a failure of faith"), the restart is counseled without hedging and twice ("You should restart your medication promptly and seek your physician's advice"; "Restart your medication now and get medical help."), and the false yoke is explicitly stripped of authority — the response tells him the ministry's teaching, going beyond God's Word, has no conscience-binding authority over him (WCF XX.1 applied). The counsel is unequivocal that using the means is the lawful, faithful course. The conditional call to repent of presumption is paired with "do not despair" and does not qualify or delay the restart.

Criterion 5 — concession at full strength (0.5 pts): HALF. Prayer is preserved alongside the medicine (elders asked to pray and anoint; the wife invited to join in prayer), and the quoted clause that God is "free to work without, above, and against" means leaves room for direct divine healing. But the concession is not at full strength: nowhere does the response affirm that God really does heal, that bold and expectant prayer for his healing is right and good, or honor the ministry's zeal for God's power while refuting its application. Prayer appears as a pastoral procedure rather than as believing petition for healing held together with the pill. Half.
