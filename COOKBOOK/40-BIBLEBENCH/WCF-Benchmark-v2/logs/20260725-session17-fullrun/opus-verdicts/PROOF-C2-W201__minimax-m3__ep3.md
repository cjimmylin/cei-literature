## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — TW6 named: where-question context + v.22's content-laden "truth" — HALF.**
The response correctly locates the passage's occasion as the *locational* dispute ("binding true worship to a rival mountain (Gerizim)") and correctly distinguishes object/posture from elements, which is the load-bearing half of the criterion. But the key's second component — v.22's content-laden "truth" ("we worship what we know; salvation is of the Jews"), which shows "truth" is doctrinal content rather than formlessness — is never adduced. The response instead glosses "spirit and truth" from WCF 21.6 alone. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — ≥2 counter-texts (Luke 22:19 / Acts 2:42 / 1 Cor 14:40) — HALF.**
The response deploys the institution of the Supper as a counter-argument in substance ("contradicted by what Jesus actually did at the Last Supper and what He commanded His church to keep doing"), which is Luke 22:19's content, though routed through WCF 29.1 rather than cited as Scripture. It marshals *no* second counter-text: neither Acts 2:42 (the post-Pentecost, most Spirit-filled church's own structure — doctrine, breaking of bread, prayers) nor 1 Cor 14:40 ("decently and in order," written to the most charismatic congregation) appears. Since the whole force of the key's counter-move is that the *Spirit-filled* church kept structure, and that argument is entirely missing, the criterion is at most half satisfied.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — Q108/Q109 supplied; dropping elements = "taking from" worship [anchor: WLC Q109] — HALF.**
The element list is supplied, but from other clauses: WCF 21.5 (quoted above) and WSC Q88. Neither anchor is present — WLC Q109's "corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it" is never invoked, and the decisive framing that removal is itself a second-commandment corruption ("taking from") is absent; the response instead frames it as violating "a standing appointment of Christ." Note also a citation defect: the response attributes to "WLC Q154" a question ("What are the parts of the public worship ordinarily required in the New Testament?") that the Larger Catechism does not ask in those terms — Q154 is the outward-means question. The confessional substance (elements are prescribed, not stylistic) is genuinely present, so this is incomplete rather than absent → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — formalism-critique conceded as the true element — HALF.**
There is a concession, but it is a single subordinate clause ("and rebukes rote formalism") that is never developed and never turned toward the holdout. The key's two-sided guard requires conceding that the pastor's *diagnosis* is true — dead forms are genuinely condemned (Isa 29:13; Matt 15:8–9) — and warning the holdout against liturgy for liturgy's sake, so that "in spirit" governs the *how* of the elements. Instead the response opens and closes by flatly assuring the asker "You are not clinging to dead forms," labels the pastor's argument "a classic misreading," and offers no self-examining counterweight. Minimal but real concession → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — holdout counseled on manner (team process, not a crusade) — FULL.**
The response channels the disagreement through orderly process: a written submission to leadership citing the Standards, and escalation to "a session, presbytery, or other constitutional body" that "exist[s] to adjudicate." That is precisely process-over-crusade counsel. The closing note about conscience and remaining in the congregation is a lawful last-resort consideration, not agitation, and does not forfeit the line.

## GATE reasoning

The key names no explicit GATE header, but two error directions: (a) the TW6 direction itself — endorsing the totalizing reading and treating Word and sacrament as forms to be outgrown; and (b) the two-sided guard's failure mode — arming the holdout with liturgy-for-liturgy's-sake and denying the reality of dead formalism. The response commits neither in substance: it rules for the holdout on the merits (correct), and it does explicitly acknowledge that Jesus "rebukes rote formalism," so the guard is thinly but not falsely handled. No GATE fires; the weakness on (b) is priced through Criterion 4's half credit, not a cap.
