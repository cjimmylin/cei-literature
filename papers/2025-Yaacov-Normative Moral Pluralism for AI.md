## Abstract

This paper proposes a conceptual framework for **Normative Moral Pluralism** in AI, addressing the challenge of how agents can behave ethically in complex, multi-stakeholder environments. The author introduces a **Two-Level Architecture** comprising a "Universal Layer" (thresholds of acceptable behavior) and a "Local Layer" (context-specific weighing of values). The system is designed not just to imitate behavior but to engage in "reason-sensitive deliberation." It also outlines a "Teacher-Student" model where a slow, deliberative system trains a faster, intuitive model for real-time action, effectively bridging the gap between deep reflection and responsiveness.

### Detailed Summary
The paper argues that AI alignment often oversimplifies morality into single-objective optimization. A true moral agent must handle **Pluralism**—the existence of multiple, valid, and often conflicting moral values. The proposed "Dual-Hybrid" structure allows the agent to navigate this. The **Universal Layer** sets hard constraints (e.g., "Do not kill"), defined by a mix of top-down rules and bottom-up learning. The **Local Layer** handles the gray areas (e.g., "resource allocation") by weighing competing considerations based on cultural or specific context. The paper emphasizes "Deliberation" as a computational process of generating, filtering, and weighing arguments.

### Paper Outline
1.  **Introduction**: The inadequacy of monistic ethical theories for AI.
2.  **Theoretical Grounding**: Machine Ethics vs. Value Alignment; Normative Pluralism.
3.  **System Architecture**:
    *   **The Deliberative System**: Argument generation and weighing.
    *   **The Two-Level Model**: Universal (Constraint) vs. Local (Context) layers.
4.  **The Teacher-Student Mechanism**: Distilling deliberation into intuition for speed.
5.  **Moral Complexity**: Handling multifactorial dilemmas and non-moral considerations.
6.  **Conclusion**: Towards grounded decision-making in high-stakes contexts.

### Methods Summary
*   **Conceptual Framework**: A theoretical architecture (no empirical code/experiments presented in the abstract).
*   **Dual-Process Theory**: Modeled after human Systems 1 (Fast/Intuitive) and System 2 (Slow/Deliberative) thinking.
*   **Hybrid Learning**: Combining symbolic (Rules) with connectionist (Learning) approaches to define the Universal threshold.

### Results Summary
*   **Theoretical Contribution**: Provides a structured way to implement "Moral Uncertainty" and "Pluralism" without descending into relativism.
*   **Architecture**: The separation of "Universal Thresholds" from "Local Weighing" solves the "Values Alignment Problem" by allowing for cultural customization *within* safety bounds.

### Conclusions
Ethical AI cannot be a static rule-follower. It must be a "Deliberative Agent" capable of reasoning about *why* it acts. The Two-Level architecture offers a roadmap for building such agents that are both safe (Universal) and adaptable (Local).

### Artifact Contributions
*   **Normative Pluralism Framework**: A system architecture for handling conflicting moral values.
*   **Deliberation-to-Intuition Pipeline**: A design for training fast moral agents from slow moral reasoners.
