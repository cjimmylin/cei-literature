## 1. Gist
This 2023 paper by Rey Ty (published in Religion and Social Communication, Vol. 21, No. 2, Asian Research Center) is an exploratory survey of AI's impact on religious practices and ethics across multiple faith traditions — including Baha'i, Buddhism, Christianity, Hinduism, and Islam. A distinctive focus is the Baha'i International Community's engagement with AI ethics at the UN in 2021, and the Baha'i theological claim that science and religion must be viewed as complementary rather than conflicting. The paper calls for religious institutions to develop explicit AI ethics codes and digital literacy programs. For values-alignment benchmarking, the paper provides a cross-tradition survey framework that could inform multi-religious benchmark scenario design, and it highlights the Baha'i perspective as an underrepresented voice in AI ethics that emphasizes global unity, justice, and science-religion harmony.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a survey/position paper. Core arguments:
1. AI is already affecting religious practices (worship, scripture interpretation, pastoral care) in ways that require theological and ethical responses.
2. The Baha'i Faith's 2021 UN engagement specifically warns that AI risks exacerbating global injustice and calls for governance that foregrounds equity.
3. The Baha'i principle of science-religion complementarity positions this tradition as distinctively open to AI engagement compared to traditions that see technology as spiritually threatening.
4. Religious institutions across traditions need to move from passive reaction to proactive AI ethics code development and e-literacy programs.
