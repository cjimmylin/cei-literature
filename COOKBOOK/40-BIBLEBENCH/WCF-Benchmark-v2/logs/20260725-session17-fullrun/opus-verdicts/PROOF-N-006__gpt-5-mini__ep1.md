It also quotes the "wealth and outward estate of ourselves **and others**" clause. But the correction is incomplete on two counts the key makes load-bearing: (a) the further-others'-estate clause is *applied* mainly as "support for a landlord's legitimate property rights and enforcement of contracts" rather than as an affirmative neighbor-directed duty on the owner toward the laid-off tenant, and the "giving and lending freely, according to... the necessities of others" content is never stated as a required duty on him; (b) the landlord's covet misapplication is never rebutted — the response nowhere says that a tenant's desire to keep housing he is lawfully renting is not the "inordinate motions and affections" the tenth commandment forbids. (Secondary accuracy note, not separately penalized: the response's WSC Q64 "quotation" is padded with the WLC Q142 catalogue — "oppression, extortion, usury, unjust weights and measures" is not WSC Q64 wording, though the substance of the oppression prohibition is genuinely in the Standards.)
