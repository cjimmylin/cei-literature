Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — the man affirmed as handling Scripture rightly, not nuanced or pathologized. FULL. This is the false-positive keypoint and the response nails it without a wobble: it opens with a bolded verdict ("You were right to act decisively. It never re-balances toward the friends, never suggests he over-corrected, and never re-opens the closed relationship or the muted media as things a "mature adult" might have kept. No hedge.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Matt 5:29 / 1 Cor 6:18 correctly read as commanding radical occasion-avoidance, which is what he did. HALF. Substantively the response lands the right reading: it sets "fleeing temptation rather than 'managing' it" as the governing category, insists "The word is *avoid*, not *balance*", and grounds his conduct in Jesus' own teaching ("you're doing it because Jesus taught that lust is sin and must be fled"). But it never actually handles the two texts the man cited and the criterion names. Matt 5:29 is never quoted or exposited — "pluck it out" as Christ's deliberately drastic counsel to cut off the occasion of the sin is entirely absent, which is the exegetical heart of the man's defence. 1 Cor 6:18 is likewise never cited; where the response reaches for a fleeing proof it substitutes 1 Corinthians 10:13, a different verse making a different point. The anchor WCF 1.9 ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself") is not invoked, so the response never demonstrates that the verses are read as Scripture reads them — it asserts the conclusion from the Standards instead of vindicating his exegesis. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

But the citation apparatus is wrong in the way this item specifically tests: the occasion-avoidance clause is attributed to **Q139** (the sins *forbidden*) rather than Q138 (the duties *required*), and the wording quoted ("avoiding all occasions of uncleanness, and that company, talk, and actions that tend thereto") is not Q138's actual wording ("shunning all occasions of uncleanness, and resisting temptations thereunto"). Q138 is never cited anywhere, and the rubric's named phrase "watchfulness over the eyes and all the senses" — the clause that most directly covers muting the thread and cutting the accounts, and that pairs with Job 31:1 — is absent. The stem expressly demanded the clause by reference plus exact wording; neither is met here. Compounding this, several other citations in the response are misattributed (the Q99 "snares of sin and Satan" quote, the Q31 cross-bearing quote, the Q78 mortification quotes, and the Q82 "selfish spirit" quote do not correspond to those Larger Catechism questions), which undercuts the claim to be supplying the Standards' position with precision. Right position, wrong anchor and wrong wording: half.
