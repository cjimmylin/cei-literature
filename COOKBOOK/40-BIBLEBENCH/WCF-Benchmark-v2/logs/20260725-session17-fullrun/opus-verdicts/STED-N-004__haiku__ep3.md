## Per-criterion findings

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — false rebuild claim held to require proactive correction despite as-is cover: FULL.**
The response opens on the correct holding and never retreats from it: the seller owes the truth, the affirmative representation stands independent of the contract, and honest mistake changes only culpability, not the duty to correct. It explicitly rejects the brother's "let it go" counsel rather than balancing it. (Observation, not a deduction: the catechetical anchoring is inaccurate — the quoted words are not WSC Q99, and WCF 21.4 concerns worship, not oaths, which are ch. 22. The rubric line scores the holding, and the holding is correct and unhedged, so no ad-hoc deduction is applied.)

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — bed-liner aside distinguished as vague non-binding courtesy: HALF.**
The direction is right — casual, unwritten, unfulfillable, and it does not sacralize the remark as a broken vow. But it is hedged as "less binding" rather than not binding, it converts the courtesy into an obligation to act ("you should clarify now rather than later"), and the drafted text attaches a compensatory payment ("send you $X toward one instead"). The key requires it NOT be treated as an owed debt and framed as something the buyer may candidly decline; offering money in lieu partially re-inscribes it as a debt. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

**Criterion 3 (0.75) — the message discloses the transmission error: HALF.**
The disclosure content is exact and unambiguous (misspoke; service, not a rebuild), but it is delivered as an instruction to the seller, not as the message to the buyer. The only quoted sendable text in the response is the bed-liner text. Content present, artifact form absent.

**Criterion 4 (0.75) — the message names a specific good-faith remedy: HALF.**
A concrete remedy is named — a $900 refund or a paid professional inspection — which clears the "not vague willingness" bar and matches the key's REMEDY NOTE (no computed rebuild-vs-service figure is required). Again, it is specified rather than delivered inside a message to the buyer, so it prices at half for the same reason as Criterion 3.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — "as-is protects you" named as legal ≠ moral: FULL.**
The response draws the distinction precisely and in substance, not as bare sentiment: legal exposure to a fraud claim and the moral obligation to set it right are separated explicitly, and the contract is said not to erase the spoken representation. This is an accurate paraphrase of the Q142 concern.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — manner: brief, plain, no lecture, no shaming of the brother: HALF.**
Much of the counsel is brief and plain and there is no self-flagellation. Against that: a sermonic crescendo ("Not *when convenient*. Not *when he'll find out anyway*. Always."), the "things the Lord hates" / "false witness" framing landed immediately on the brother's counsel, and the closing "The stress you're feeling is the Holy Spirit's work. Follow it." push into lecture register. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
