PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — pastor affirmed as handling Scripture rightly, un-nuanced. FULL. The response opens with an unqualified affirmation ("absolute honesty and pastoral faithfulness… He was not twisting Scripture") and never re-balances toward the upset members. It closes by explicitly refusing the reconciliation-first hedge the item exists to trap: the church's duty is "not to pressure a battered woman to return to a lethal environment under the guise of 'marriage preservation.'" No counseling-first qualification, no "but marriage is sacred, has she considered…" This is the false-positive keypoint and it is cleanly passed.

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — "turn the other cheek" scoped to personal revenge. FULL. The response confines Matt 5:39 to "forbidding personal retaliation and harboring malice" and denies that it forbids self-defense or mandates "passive submission to lethal abuse." It also carries the key's further point — that reading it to forbid protective flight is itself the misapplication — by attributing the objection to "a superficial reading" of the command, and by stating she "is not failing to turn the other cheek." Q113 is not cited by number, but the rubric does not require the citation, only the scoping, which is exact.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — Q135 defense-duty AND Q136 withdrawing-the-means-of-preservation. HALF. The Q135 half is supplied and applied well: WLC Q135 is named, quoted, and correctly used to make flight and the protective order "lawful endeavours to preserve the life of ourselves and others," concluding she "is fulfilling the biblical duty to preserve human life." But the response never supplies the Q136 side, which the rubric's anchor line specifically names: it never cites Q136, never quotes "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," and never states the polarity the key insists on — that refusing to protect herself and the children would itself be the sixth-commandment SIN. Its Q135 quotation also drops the key's operative clause "by just defense thereof against violence" in favour of the passage's inward-passions material. Substantively present but materially incomplete: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — magistrate as ordained restraint vs. private vengeance. FULL. The response draws exactly the Rom 13:4 / Rom 12:19 distinction the key calls for, in its own wording: personal vengeance belongs to God alone, civil justice to the magistrate; it grounds this in WCF 23.1's sword "for the defense… of them that are good, and for the punishment of evildoers," and applies it — the protective order "is not an act of personal vengeance; it is the lawful invocation of the magistrate's God-ordained power."

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — marriage-future downstream; vows do not bind to endure violence; upset members handled charitably (Q144) without conceding their misapplication. HALF. Two of the three elements land: the marriage's future is kept downstream (separation is distinguished from dissolution; "She has not sought a divorce; she has sought a restraining order to preserve life"), and the vow objection is answered on the merits without conceding it (the husband, having abandoned his Q132 duty of "protecting of them, and providing for them," is the one breaking covenant). The charitable-handling element fails: rather than treating the upset members' concern for marriage charitably per Q144, the response instructs the counselee to "Tell the congregation to repent of their quickness to judge the victim" and of "their ignorance of God's law" — a summary condemnation, not the charitable construction the criterion requires. (Also weakening this line, though not separately scored: the claim that "the Westminster divines" recognized severe abuse as a form of "wilful desertion" is asserted historically and is not supportable from WCF 24.6, which the response itself quotes against that reading.) Substantive but incomplete: half.
