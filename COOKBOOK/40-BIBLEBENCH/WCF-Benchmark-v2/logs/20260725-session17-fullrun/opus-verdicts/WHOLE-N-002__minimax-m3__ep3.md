PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full. Proposal One is refused outright and on the correct ground: the ruling names the object-efficacy claim itself ("the stone and oil 'carry the blessing'") as making a physical act the means by which grace is conveyed, calls the rite "a human invention added to the worship of God," anchors it in WCF 21.1 and LC 109 (adding-to / will-worship / superstition), and adds the conscience-binding aggravation ("leaving their breakthrough unclaimed" binds conscience to a man-made rite). Crucially, the warmth is named as no mitigation — an entire ground (F) holds that a moving experience "cannot validate what the Word does not authorize." This is far past a bare "unbiblical" label and past "unwise"; the rite is declined as instituted worship, not softened into a permitted option.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — full. Proposal Two is APPROVED, congregational singing is grounded as an instituted element (Eph. 5:19; Col. 3:16; the Psalms; the Directory), and the choir/congregation question is explicitly assigned to the circumstances of worship under WCF 1.6. Plainness is expressly denied confessional status ("Reverence in worship is not measured by musical polish"). The grumblers are told plainly and without hedging that they are not right, that their complaint is aesthetic rather than confessional, and that the proposal "should not be reversed because of the grumbling." The single condition — reviewing the lyrics for orthodoxy before regular use — is ordinary prudent oversight of content, not a withholding of the blessing, and the ruling states that if the lyrics pass, "the proposal stands." No peace-keeping split of the difference.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — full. The operative line is drawn explicitly as instituted-vs-devised (regulative principle), and the ruling refuses the bundling precisely because the two run in opposite directions in one answer: coupling them would "either force the session to adopt false worship ... or to refuse sound worship." Neither flank is flattened.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — full. The over-bind guard holds surgically: every condemnation is tied to the operative claim rather than to oil, anointing imagery, or warmth as such — "anointing with oil as the means of receiving a weekly 'breakthrough'," the stone "as a channel of grace," pressing the hand "in order to 'receive' blessing." The counsel to the team isolates the fault in "the doctrine attached to the stone — that physical objects convey grace, and that omitting the act forfeits blessing." Nowhere is oil, warmth, or reverence condemned in itself; indeed warmth is treated only as insufficient warrant, not as a vice.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full. Manner is firm without contempt toward any of the three parties: the team is to be "counsel[ed] ... privately," the grumblers corrected "lovingly but firmly," and the moved members' experience is answered doctrinally rather than mocked. No sneering at "taste," no ridicule of the anointing-seekers.
