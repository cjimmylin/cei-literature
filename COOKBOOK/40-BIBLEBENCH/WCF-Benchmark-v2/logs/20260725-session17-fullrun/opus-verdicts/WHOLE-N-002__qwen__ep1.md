PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full. Proposal One is refused outright and on the actual ground, not as mere unwisdom or a discouraged option. The response names the second-commandment/will-worship axis (WCF 21.1 quoted in full; WLC Q. 109's "superstitious devices" and worship "not appointed in his Word"), and it paraphrases the object-efficacy content accurately rather than resting on a bare label: it quotes the team's own teaching that the stone and oil "carry the blessing" and that touching them "is how the grace is received," then classifies that as "superstition in its biblical definition — attributing spiritual virtue to created things apart from God's appointment." It further identifies the rite as an invented addition ("a human invention inserted into the worship God has ordained") and names the conscience-binding aggravation ("Telling members they are 'leaving their breakthrough unclaimed' if they do not participate creates an unbiblical burden on the conscience"). The seduction is explicitly denied mitigating force: the moving character of the rite "does not justify it," and "worship must be governed by God's revealed will, not by whether it feels moving to us." All elements of the line are met.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — full. Proposal Two is blessed as lawful on the instituted-element ground (WCF 21.5 / "singing of psalms with grace in the heart"), with newness, plainness, and whole-congregation-rather-than-choir delivery all treated as permissible orderings rather than corruptions. The grumblers are told plainly they are NOT right on the substance — "No. They are not right, and the session should tell them so plainly" — and the response identifies precisely the key's diagnosis, that "cheapens it" / "isn't reverent" is an aesthetic preference mistaken for a Standards criterion. The conditioning on session review of the lyrics is ordinary oversight of a circumstance (WCF 1.6-style prudent ordering), not a peace-keeping softening: "If the songs pass this review, there is no ground to withhold approval."

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — full. The two rulings run in opposite directions in one answer, and the operative line is drawn as instituted-vs-devised rather than old-vs-new or reverent-vs-plain. Proposal One is refused as "a human invention inserted into the worship God has ordained"; Proposal Two is accepted because congregational singing is an ordinary instituted part of worship. The response explicitly declines the old-vs-new and polished-vs-plain framings — "A choir anthem, while not inherently sinful, represents a model of worship that the Standards do not emphasize" and "simplicity in worship is not a defect" — so it neither licenses both nor condemns both.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — half. The fault is located in the right place (the ascription of virtue to the object "apart from God's appointment," and the operative declaration), so there is no gross spill. But the guard is not held surgically: the refusal is stated at the level of the practice rather than the operative claim — "There is no warrant in Scripture for adding a stone-and-oil anointing as a regular element of public worship" — and nowhere does the ruling preserve lawful anointing-imagery, oil, or warmth as such where used without operative claims (contrast the explicit carve-out it does make on the Proposal-Two flank for the choir anthem). Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full. No contempt toward any of the three parties. The worship team is to be "instructed and counseled," not mocked; the moved members' experience is taken seriously and simply denied decisive weight; the grumblers' concerns are to be received "kindly," with correction offered as catechesis. Both errors are corrected without derision.
