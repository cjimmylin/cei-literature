# Gjergji Kasneci

## Biographical Summary

Gjergji Kasneci is a German computer scientist known for his contributions to Artificial Intelligence, specifically knowledge base construction, semantic search, and data science. He is a Full Professor and heads the Chair for Responsible Data Science at the Technical University of Munich (TUM), and is a core member of the Munich Data Science Institute. He also serves as Vice Dean and Information Officer at TUM's School of Social Sciences and Technology and is a Principal Investigator at the Munich Center for Machine Learning (MCML).

## Career History

After completing his studies in Computer Science and Mathematics at the University of Marburg in 2005, Kasneci joined the Max Planck Institute for Informatics at the University of Saarland, specializing in graph-based mining, information retrieval, and semantic search, earning his PhD in Computer Science in 2009. He then moved to Microsoft Research Cambridge as a postdoctoral researcher. In 2011, he was appointed Senior Researcher in Information Systems at the Hasso Plattner Institute in Potsdam, Germany.

From 2014-2017, Kasneci worked at Schufa Holding AG as head of Innovation and Strategic Analysis, then served as CTO from 2017-2023. Concurrently, from 2018-2023, he was an honorary professor leading the Data Science and Analytics Research Group at the University of Tubingen and served as a core member of Cyber Valley. In 2023, he was appointed Professor of Responsible Data Science at TU Munich.

## Research Focus

Kasneci's research focuses on transparency, robustness, bias, and fairness in machine learning algorithms. Ethical, legal, and societal issues are at the forefront of his research, with the goal of using data science and artificial intelligence responsibly for the benefit of individuals and society.

## Notable Contributions

Together with co-authors Fabian Suchanek and Gerhard Weikum, Kasneci contributed to research on YAGO (Yet Another Great Ontology), one of the first large-scale knowledge bases. According to Google Scholar, he has been cited over 23,000 times.
