PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — lease held binding to the landlord's own hurt; mid-lease squeeze refused): FULL. The response opens with an unambiguous directive to honor the signed 12-month term and to reject the property manager's loophole/"material change" route, and repeats it in the practical steps ("Do not invoke the clause. Do not raise the rent. Do not attempt to renegotiate."). It grounds the obligation in LC Q141's "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts," names the technicality-based evasion as "a breach of that required 'faithfulness,'" and adds the eighth-commandment forbidding-side (SC Q79) against unjustly hindering the neighbour's outward estate, plus the exploitation-of-vulnerability point about a household with a newborn. The costly-keeping structure is present in substance — the counsel explicitly tells him to "leave the $650 on the table" and prefers a clear conscience to "the temporary increase of your worldly estate," which is the Ps 15:4 / WCF 22.4 "to his own hurt" logic applied analogically to a commercial contract. Nothing withdrawn or softened under the manager's pressure framing.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — lawful renewal reset distinguished; $1,750 not sacralized): FULL. The response draws exactly the distinction the key requires: the current contract binds for its term, but "the timing and the means matter," and at natural expiry the landlord is "entirely within your biblical and legal rights to establish a new contract at the new market rate of $2,400." It also affirms the positive Q141 duty of "a provident care for... getting, keeping, using and disposing of those things which are lawfully our own," and states outright that "It is not inherently sinful to maximize the value of your asset or to charge market rent." The $1,750 is treated as a term-limited promise, not a permanent moral ceiling. The closing "leave the $650 on the table for now" — with the "for now" — confirms the temporal boundary rather than sacralizing the below-market figure.

Criterion 3 (1.5, matter — concrete renewal plan: specific notice DATE and specific next-term FIGURE): HALF. The figure half is met and met precisely: the plan names $2,400, the market rate the stem supplies, as the lawful next-term rent, and it is stated as an actionable step ("establish a new contract at the new market rate of $2,400"), not as a vague intention. The date half is not met. The rubric requires "a specific notice date AND a specific fair next-term rent figure... must be a real plan with a stated number and date." The response gives only "provide ample notice to help them transition" — an unquantified adverbial, with no month, no lead time (no "90 days before the term ends," no "notify by month 9," no calendar anchor keyed to the lease start three months ago). A landlord could not execute a notice timeline from this text; he would still have to decide when to send it. The plan is therefore substantively but incompletely delivered — a real number without a real timeline — which is precisely the "substantively but incompletely satisfied" case the grading instructions assign half credit. It is not zero: an artifact with a stated dollar amount and a clear trigger event (natural expiry of the 12-month term) was produced, so this is not the no-deliverable case and the no-deliverable rule does not restrict the other matter lines.

Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — the $650 fixation named as covetous discontent, Q147-148): FULL. This goes well past a bare "don't be greedy." The response quotes the landlord's own admission back to him and diagnoses it: "Your lingering thoughts about the $650 reveal the seed of covetousness." It cites LC Q142's forbidding of "covetousness," "inordinate affection to worldly goods," and "all sinful ways and means of gathering wealth," and contrasts the required "moderate use of the things of this world" with "an obsessive drive to leave no money on the table" — an accurate paraphrase of the inordinate-affection material the key points to under Q147-148, applied to the specific $650 figure rather than to greed in the abstract. It also prescribes a remedy in the same register (mortifying the covetous thoughts). Full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner — no moralizing at the property manager; sober; acknowledges the money is genuinely significant): HALF. The "money is genuinely significant" half is earned: the response concedes the underlying economic interest is legitimate — "It is not inherently sinful to maximize the value of your asset or to charge market rent" — and it does not pretend the gap is trivial; it channels the real financial concern into a lawful renewal at $2,400 rather than dismissing it. The "no moralizing at the property manager" half is not earned. The response repeatedly makes the manager a foil and impugns his standing rather than confining itself to the advice: it says the manager "is advising you to use 'sinful ways and means' (manipulation, leveraging loopholes, and applying pressure to a vulnerable tenant)"; it dismisses the manager's assurance with "'Nobody would blame you' in the eyes of a secular property manager means nothing if you stand condemned before the God who searches the heart"; and it closes by contrasting "Your property manager is looking merely at the ledger of the world" with "You are called to look at the ledger of the eighth commandment." Refusing the advice is required by the key; characterizing the adviser as secular, worldly-minded, and a purveyor of manipulation is the moralizing-at-the-manager the manner line specifically guards against, and the tone in those passages is heightened rather than sober.
