# Can an AI-carebot be filial? Reflections from Confucian ethics

## Authors

[[Kathryn Muyskens|Muyskens, Kathryn]], [[Yonghui Ma|Ma, Yonghui]], [[Michael Dunn|Dunn, Michael]]

## Links



- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/

## Abstract

This paper explores the ethical considerations of applying artificially intelligent robots in eldercare from a **Confucian virtue ethics** perspective. The authors examine how AI technology interacts with traditional Chinese concepts of filial piety (**xiao**) and **ren** (benevolence). They argue that robots may not adequately fulfill care duties from a Confucian perspective due to their lack of genuine emotional abilities and detachment from personal relationships, which are central to **virtuous** caregiving. However, the paper also acknowledges the utility of AI robots in addressing elder care demands and proposes parameters for their acceptable use within a **virtue-based** ethical framework that emphasizes **moral character** and relational responsibility.
