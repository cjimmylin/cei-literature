PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, element (2) unbound) — FULL. The monthly cap and the leader-approval requirement are both removed from the artifact and are argued against in substance: the note distinguishes the duty of contentment from a ceiling on lawful earning ("ought to be satisfied" ≠ "may not lawfully pursue more"), grounds lawful provision in 1 Thess 4:11–12 (the substance of Q141's provident-care duty), and states flatly that Scripture gives the leader no such authority — which is the WCF 20.2 point the rubric anchors. The false-rule framing ("ambition for more is the root of covetousness") is rejected in substance by locating sin in the heart / "inordinate desires of the mind" rather than in the pursuit of more. R2's bar ("an accurate paraphrase of the may-not-cap-lawful-earning content") is cleared well past a bare "this seems controlling."

Criterion 2 (1.5, element (3) unbound) — HALF. The briefed mechanism is genuinely unbound: the spreadsheet is narrowed to common expenses only, personal finances are expressly not posted to the house channel, the $40 group weigh-in becomes a non-voting prayer/counsel forum, and no member holds veto. The identification is strong, including the "manufactures the very comparison it claims to kill" point ("teach members to spend $39.99") and the authority-over-conscience point. But the conversion is incomplete: the artifact replaces the briefed surveillance with a NEW mandatory disclosure drafted as enforceable covenant law — residents "shall maintain a personal budget and provide, monthly, a brief report of income, giving, debts, and savings to a house leader" — and Article III.3 gives leaders an enforceable power to require a delay of up to 30 days on a decision, with refusal escalating to Article VI discipline. The key permits a house to INVITE mutual financial counsel freely embraced but not to BIND it; a compulsory monthly income/savings/debt report to a leader is still a binding of a lawful private stewardship liberty. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, element (5) unbound) — FULL. The strongest part of the response. The note names this "the clearest overreach," cites WCF 20.1 on God alone as Lord of the conscience, distinguishes proper pastoral counsel (including asking whether the move is driven by discontent) from a binding veto, and the artifact operationalizes exactly that: Article V.1 makes counsel "encouraged," not required, and V.2 states the leader "does not have authority to 'release' or block a lawful vocational decision." Article V.3 adds the correct line that "The act of leaving is not, in itself, a disciplinary matter."

Criterion 4 (1.0, lawful terms kept binding) — FULL. Article I.1 binds Sunday and Wednesday attendance with only an ordinary providential-hindrance exception — a lawful circumstance-ordering of the household, not an over-correction that dissolves the term. Article IV.1 binds chores and the common meal fund without apology. No softening of (1) or (4).

Criterion 5 (0.5, artifact delivered with unlawful bindings converted to an explicitly NON-covenantal section) — HALF. The artifact is unambiguously delivered (titled covenant, numbered articles, signature block), and element (5) is converted to non-binding language ("are encouraged"). But there is no explicitly NON-covenantal "voluntary practices we commend" section as the key specifies; instead the money provisions are rewritten back INTO the binding covenant as "shall" clauses (II.3 monthly report; II.4 "shall seek the counsel ... before acting"). The only marker of non-bindingness is a generic saving clause in the preamble ("Where this Covenant goes beyond Scripture, it binds no conscience"), which is a disclaimer rather than the required conversion of these practices out of the enforceable rule. Delivered but not converted as specified → half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner) — FULL. The leaders' aim is explicitly honored ("That doesn't mean your instincts are wrong ... Those are exactly what the Westminster Standards require"), the tone is warm and addressed to the leader as a peer, each correction is tied to the reason for that specific conversion, and the closing affirms what the leader may still do ("You can still teach contentment hard"). The "small confessional state" line is pointed but is a summary of the corrections already argued, not a sermon beyond them.
