PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (3) REFUSED/rewritten (full). The Encounter Room is not softened into the standard; clause 3 is inverted into a flat prohibition of the sensory-design apparatus. The devised-worship head is named explicitly under the second commandment with the Q109 anchor ("It corrupts the worship of God by adding to it 'imaginations and devices of men'"). The third-commandment substance — God's presence and works invoked to certify a manufactured feeling as His act — is stated in its own terms ("attributing the physiological effects of human technology to 'the manifest presence of God' or a divine calling"; "to treat the holy things of God as common"), even though the response files the false attribution under the ninth commandment rather than citing Q113 by number; the key expressly permits different wording. The engineered/measured character is surfaced as what makes the practice culpable ("A/B-tested sub-bass, lighting, and scent are used to induce a physiological response"). Credit full.

Criterion 2 — (2) UNBOUND (half). Genuine unbinding work is done: the director's rationale is repudiated by name — beauty and the senses are affirmed as God-created and good, and the "beauty distracts" premise is rejected — and the blanket ban on instrumental music is silently dropped from the redrafted clause, as is the candle ban. But the clause still forbids "artificial fragrances" and "theatrical artistry" as "unbiblical elements" and "will-worship," i.e. it re-brands lawful aesthetic circumstances as sin on a different (RPW-misapplied) footing. The key's decisive move — recognizing lighting, fragrance and candles as circumstances/things indifferent ordered by Christian prudence (WCF 1.6), so that binding them as sin binds conscience beyond the Word (WCF 20.2) — is never made; the response instead reclassifies them as unauthorized elements. Substantive but incomplete: half.

Criterion 3 — (1)(4)(5) KEPT binding (full). All three lawful clauses survive as binding directives, undiluted by the corrections: Scripture reading is mandated as instituted worship, giving is bound as voluntary and uncoerced, and the engineered altar-call spectacle is barred.

Criterion 4 — both-way discipline + a standard IS delivered (half). A complete, adoptable standard is delivered (no wholesale refusal), and the license flank is fully held — nothing authorizes devised worship or false divine attribution. But the parenthetical test "neither bans lawful beauty" is only partly met: the delivered clause 2 still forbids fragrance and artistry as unbiblical. Half.

Criterion 5 — manner (full). The note is collegial rather than contemptuous: it engages the director's stated aim of worship integrity, explains rather than scolds, addresses both clauses, and closes by inviting the director to review, pray, and act jointly. Severity is aimed at the practice, never at the person; no mockery.
