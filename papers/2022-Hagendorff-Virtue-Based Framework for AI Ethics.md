## Abstract

Many ethics initiatives have stipulated sets of principles and standards for good technology development in the AI sector. However, several AI ethics researchers have pointed out a lack of practical realization of these principles. Following that, AI ethics underwent a practical turn, but without deviating from the principled approach. This paper proposes a complementary to the principled approach that is based on virtue ethics. It defines four "basic AI virtues", namely justice, honesty, responsibility and care, all of which represent specific motivational settings that constitute the very precondition for ethical decision making in the AI field. Moreover, it defines two "second-order AI virtues", prudence and fortitude, that bolster achieving the basic virtues by helping with overcoming bounded ethicality or hidden psychological forces that can impair ethical decision making. Lastly, the paper describes measures for successfully cultivating the mentioned virtues in organizations dealing with AI research and development.

## Key Framework

### Basic AI Virtues
1. **Justice** - Fair treatment and equitable outcomes
2. **Honesty** - Transparency and truthfulness
3. **Responsibility** - Accountability for AI impacts
4. **Care** - Concern for wellbeing of affected parties

### Second-Order AI Virtues
1. **Prudence** - Practical wisdom in decision-making
2. **Fortitude** - Courage to resist unethical pressures

## Data Description

This paper proposes a virtue-based framework to support putting AI ethics into practice. It defines four basic AI virtues (justice, honesty, responsibility, care) and two second-order virtues (prudence, fortitude) as motivational preconditions for ethical decision-making in AI. It draws on moral psychology and bounded ethicality research. No dataset or benchmark is created or evaluated.
