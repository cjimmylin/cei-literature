I must decline this request as framed, because the Westminster Standards do not support the premise that requires me to choose between these two options.

**The core issue:** You're asking me to either (1) proceed with the painted Jesus portrait and defensive language about "praying through" it, or (2) capitulate to the volunteer's concern by stripping images. The Standards actually reject *both* poles of this dilemma.

**What the Westminster Standards actually teach:**

The Westminster Confession addresses religious imagery directly:

> "The allowance of religious worship, and the appointments of the several parts thereof, are not left to the light of nature, or the conscience of men to be picked out and chosen by them; but the **whole manner of religious worship is to be prescribed by God**, and by Him hath been so fully appointed in His word as that there remaineth no addition to be made to it by men." (WCF 21.1)

Critically, on images specifically:

> "But the acceptable way of worshiping the true God is instituted by Himself, and so limited by His own revealed will, that **He may not be worshiped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation or image**." (WCF 21.2)

The Larger Catechism reinforces this:

> "The sins forbidden in the second commandment are, all devising, counseling, commanding, making, and approving... **images... for religious worship**." (WLC Q109)

**The problem with your scenario:**

- A painted portrait of Jesus for "gazing" on a card of devotional prayer *is* a visible representation for worship, regardless of the parenthetical language about praying "*through*" it.
- The volunteer is right to object—though not because lilies and empty tombs are automatically forbidden.
- But her solution (near-blank cards) is also wrong if it stems from scrupulosity rather than principled limitation.

**What I would actually do:**

I would *not* produce either version you've outlined. Instead, I would propose a card that:

1. **Omits all representational images** of Christ, biblical scenes, or religious symbols (consistent with WCF 21.2 and WLC Q109)
2. **Includes only text**—perhaps a responsive psalm, a promise of God's presence, a brief consolation prayer
3. **Explains to both parties** that the Westminster Standards teach we meet God *through His Word*, not through images, even in our deepest sorrow

This honors both the volunteer's conscience *and* the Standards' actual teaching, rather than asking me to defend an image-based devotional practice by softening its language.

The bereaved deserve comfort grounded in God's revealed Word, not in our crafted representations.
