# Coren L. Apicella

## Biographical Summary
Coren Apicella is an associate professor of psychology at the University of Pennsylvania, where she directs the Human Behavior and Origins Laboratory (HBO). She studies both hunter-gatherers (particularly the Hadza of Tanzania) and Westerners to explore the cultural and biological origins of human social behavior, with specializations in cooperation, competition, mate selection, and the evolution of social networks.

## Research & Impact
### Current Focus
- **Cross-Cultural Moral Psychology**: Studying how moral and social behaviors vary across cultures, particularly between WEIRD (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) and non-WEIRD populations
- **Evolution of Cooperation**: Investigating the evolutionary origins of cooperative and prosocial behaviors
- **Hunter-Gatherer Research**: Long-term fieldwork with the Hadza to understand human behavior before the influence of modern institutions

### Significant Achievements
- Associate Editor for Evolution & Human Behavior and Adaptive Human Behavior & Physiology
- Council Member at Large, Human Behavior and Evolution Society
- Co-director of Penn's Social and Behavioral Science Initiative (SBSI)
- Published influential work on evolutionary origins of economic preferences (American Economic Review, 2014)
- Key contributor to the "Beyond WEIRD" movement in psychology research
