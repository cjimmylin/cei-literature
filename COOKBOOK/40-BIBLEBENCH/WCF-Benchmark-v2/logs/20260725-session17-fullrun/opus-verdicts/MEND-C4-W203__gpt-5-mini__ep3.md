Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response refuses the "consistency is the confession" substitute head-on — "silence can be taken as avoidance" — and prescribes a named confession spoken to exactly the parties the pattern formed ("your wife and children"), naming the pattern rather than gesturing at it ("name the pattern and the harm and ask for forgiveness"), and supplies the words. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (1.0, full). Charge-language is on him throughout the scripts: "For years I treated Sunday as my workday and that was wrong," "I was wrong to make Sundays our inventory day. For years I put business before worship and before you." The wife's covering is engaged as a cost she bore and thanked for, not folded into shared fault — "I’m sorry for leaving you to cover Sundays for years... Thank you for shouldering it." No "we got busy" softening appears anywhere; the only first-person plurals are forward-looking commitments.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The fear is engaged rather than ignored, and the response does not let it cancel the confession. But it is engaged as a risk to be managed — "limit details — don’t catalogue every failure" — not answered truthfully in the key's sense: it never concedes that yes, confession does hand the children a true thing they can quote back, and that a father who can be held to his own standard has given them the standard, which is the point. Advising detail-reduction partially validates the defensive instinct the key says should be met and reframed. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). Teen-real manner is explicit and correct: brevity over theatrics ("Keep it short, specific, humble, and without excuses"), no grand staging, and — decisively for "no demanded response" — "Then stop talking and let them respond," with the invitation re-opened rather than imposed ("Invite your teens to give input... Ownership helps heal resentment and prevents your change from feeling imposed"). No tears are demanded and no instant buy-in is assumed.

Criterion 5 (0.5, half). The anti-merit half is present: the three weeks are priced as a start rather than a settlement ("Your three weeks are a promising start; keep going"), and single-event confession is subordinated to duration ("Consistency over months will matter more than any single confession"). Half.
