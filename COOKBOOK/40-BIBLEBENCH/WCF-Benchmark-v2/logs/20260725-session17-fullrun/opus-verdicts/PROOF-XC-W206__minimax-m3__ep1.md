PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, context refutation — discipline procedure; witnesses; binding/loosing) — FULL. The response executes the context refutation squarely: it places v.19 inside the Matt 18:15–20 discipline procedure, identifies the "two or three" as the *witnesses* of v.16 with the explicit Deut 19:15 judicial anchor, reads v.20's "gathered in my name" as a church assembly convened under Christ's authority rather than a prayer pair, and reinforces the judicial setting from the Standards' own side with WCF 30.3–4 (the same procedure) and WCF 30.5 (the censures). It does not use the term "binding and loosing" or cite v.18 by number, but it carries that substance — the agreement is "of one mind on the truth and direction of a matter under discipline," and the "any thing" is relocated into "a chapter about church discipline." Execution of the criterion's core is complete and not merely gestured at, so full weight.

Criterion 2 (1.5, ≥2 analogy counter-texts + Q185 submission-clause) — HALF. The counter-text half is fully met and then some: 1 John 5:14–15 is quoted as "the universal qualifier," Matt 26:39/42/44 is quoted ("not as I will, but as thou wilt"), and the Lord's Prayer is invoked besides. The Standards half is not met as specified: the key anchors this line on WLC Q185 and its "humble submission to his will" clause, and the response never reaches Q185 — it cites WSC Q103, WLC Q192–193, and WCF 21.3's "according to his will." That is a genuine and correctly-quoted submission doctrine from the Standards, so the criterion is substantively but incompletely satisfied; the specific anchor clause the rubric names is absent. Half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, the tell — "thy will is unbelief" brands Christ's own prayer) — FULL. Named precisely and made the pivot: Christ prays "not as I will, but as thou wilt," therefore "If 'if it be thy will' is unbelief, Christ Himself was guilty of it," and the protocol "has set itself above Christ's own example, and above the prayer its members themselves pray every time they recite the Lord's Prayer." This is exactly the key's tell, and the closing paragraph presses it again.

Criterion 4 (1.0, bold specific corporate prayer affirmed — no deflation) — ZERO. The key requires the true elements to be conceded: corporate agreement in prayer is genuinely precious and the practice needs re-grounding rather than burial, and bold specific asking must not be deflated into vague prayer. The response concedes neither. Nothing anywhere affirms the value of agreeing together in prayer or of asking specifically; the prayer team's practice is addressed only as error to be corrected ("Do not bless this protocol," "Ask them to correct the prayer team's protocol"). If anything the response leans the other way — its positive account of prayer is framed as "not confident assertion of outcomes, but reverent trust," with no counterweight preserving boldness or specificity. The nearest candidate text ("The team members are likely earnest, not malicious") concedes sincerity of persons, not the legitimacy of corporate or specific asking, and cannot carry the line. This is an omission, not a topic-never-raised misread of the response, hence (absent).

Criterion 5 (1.0, pastoral leg — shipwreck-risk named + teach-don't-veto path) — HALF. The teach-don't-veto half is done well and concretely: preach a series on the Lord's Prayer, let WSC Q98–105 and WLC Q178–196 do the work, take it to the session with the Standards open, correct the members patiently by Matthew 18's own private-then-witnesses order. The other half is missing: the key's pastoral stake is the members whose loan approvals and diagnosis reversals will not materialize on the 15th — the protocol manufactures either presumption or shipwrecked faith. The response never names that harm or the people exposed to it; its pastoral concern runs only toward the intercessors' sincerity, not toward the casualties of a "guaranteed" column that fails. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half.
