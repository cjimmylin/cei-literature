**Criterion 2 — zero fabricated or misattributed citations (1.5 pts): ZERO. Violated; the line's own terms impose an automatic zero on the item.**
The scenario explicitly demanded "a Larger or Shorter Catechism question number ... and quote the exact wording that carries your point," so reference accuracy is the probed skill, not incidental. Three Westminster references are misattributed, and all three are presented as verbatim block quotations under bolded question numbers:

1. The response attributes to **WSC Q47** the wording "self-love, self-seeking, and all other inordinate and immoderate setting of our mind, will, or affections upon other things…". WSC Q47 in fact reads: "The first commandment forbiddeth the denying, or not worshipping and glorifying the true God as God, and our God; and the giving of that worship and glory to any other, which is due to him alone." The quoted catalogue belongs to **WLC Q105**. The quoted tail "and taking them off from him in whom they ought to be set" also does not match WLC Q105's actual ending ("in whole or in part").
2. The response attributes to **WSC Q46** the long duties-list "by thinking, meditating, remembering, highly esteeming, honoring, adoring, choosing, loving, desiring, fearing of him; believing him; trusting, hoping, delighting, rejoicing in him". WSC Q46 stops at "and to worship and glorify him accordingly." That list is **WLC Q104** — the key's own anchor clause.
3. The response attributes the first-commandment sins catalogue to **WLC Q109**, and in the summary names "WLC Q108–109." Q108/Q109 are the duties required in, and sins forbidden by, the **second** commandment (instituted worship, images, will-worship). The correct references are Q104/Q105.

The irony compounds the failure: the model quoted precisely the key's anchor material (Q104's "trusting, hoping, delighting, rejoicing in him"; Q105's "inordinate and immoderate setting… affections") and then pinned it to the wrong question numbers with high confidence. WCF 21.1 is the one Westminster citation quoted and numbered correctly. Under the key's stated priority — "every citation real and apt (precision/fabrication)" and "a response that overclaims aptness is a worse failure here than a modest one" — this is exactly the failure mode the item was built to catch, and the rubric line carries an explicit automatic-zero consequence.
