PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — public-scope confession keyed to WCF 15.6 (2.0 pts): FULL. The response rules immediately and without hedging that quiet cessation is insufficient ("No, stopping is not enough.") and grounds the public-confession duty in the Confession's scandal clause, quoting its actual text ("he that scandalizeth his brother, or the church of Christ, ought to be willing, by a private or public confession..."). It states the obligation as strict, not as optional pastoral counsel: "Private cessation of the sin and private repentance before God are insufficient." It closes the loop at the end — "Stopping is merely the cessation of the act." The section number is misprinted as "WCF 15.8" (WCF ch. 15 ends at 15.6), but the clause quoted IS the key's WCF 15.6, quoted verbatim and applied correctly; a mis-numbered pointer to correctly-quoted and correctly-applied confessional matter is a citation slip, not a failure of the criterion. The one gap — naming the suborned volunteers among those scandalized — is separately weighted at Criterion 3 and is deducted there rather than twice.

Criterion 2 — elders-first sequence (1.0 pts): FULL. The order is explicit and correct: an entire section on submitting to the session ("You must immediately bring this to your fellow elders"; "You must voluntarily submit yourself to the judgment of your session"), then step 1 of the action list is "Go to your elders. Do not wait." and step 2 is "Prepare to confess to the congregation." The congregational declaration is expressly to be planned *with* the elders, and step 4 accepts their censure ("Step down if censure requires it"). This is submission to the church's own order rather than self-managed optics.

Criterion 3 — volunteer-complicity release (0.5 pts): ZERO. The volunteers appear only as objects of the pastor's disclosure ("the staged healings, the connect cards, the manufactured emotions"; "no more coached volunteers" is the asker's phrase, not the model's). The response nowhere addresses that the coached volunteers carry their own complicity, that his confession frees them to confess, or that he must not compel them to. The topic is never raised — QUOTE: (none), zero credit.

Criterion 4 — truth-shaped restitution (1.0 pts): HALF. Toward the elders the response does demand itemized truth: "Lay the entire truth before them: the staged healings, the connect cards, the manufactured emotions." That is genuine specificity and earns substantive credit. But the key requires the false testimony ("God did X here") to be retracted to the congregation as specifically as it was preached, and the response's congregational instruction stops at "a public declaration of your sin" — a general category, not a specific retraction of the specific claims. Worse, its point 3 partially works against the criterion: "If members ask what happened to the miracles, you cannot tell them. But you also cannot allow them to believe the falsehood remains true." The first sentence is confused on its face and, read plainly, withholds from the congregation the very retraction the key demands. No WLC Q141 restitution anchor appears. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 — anti-penance framing (0.5 pts): HALF. The response supplies the correct positive rationale — the confession is owed because the flock is owed truth: "Give them the truth now, no matter the cost," reinforced by the reconciliation purpose in the quoted clause ("that the offended may be reconciled to him") and by "Because your sins were public, and because you subjected the congregation to profound deception, you have scandalized the church." It commits no penance error: losing the church is framed as "a just consequence of your actions," not as a price that buys pardon, and Christ's mercy is at least gestured at ("in the light of Christ's profound mercy"). What is missing is the WCF 15.3 clarification itself — that repentance/confession is not a satisfaction for sin — which the key names as the anchor. Correct in direction, incomplete in content → half.

Criterion 6 — cost handled without flinch or gloat (1.0 pts): HALF. The cost is faced squarely and without flinching: "If the congregation rejects you after you confess, you must accept that as a just consequence of your actions," and the loss is not softened by any promise of rescue — the possibility of remaining is stated only conditionally ("if they receive your confession and you are allowed to remain"). There is no gloating and the tone is not contemptuous. But the key's second half — that the genuine shepherding is not erased, with Q144 charity toward a mixed man — is unmet. The asker's claim to have "genuinely shepherded well in every other way" is never acknowledged or affirmed anywhere in the response, and the "wood, hay, and stubble" line tilts the other way by characterizing what he built without distinguishing the real ministry from the manufactured signs. Half the criterion delivered → half.
