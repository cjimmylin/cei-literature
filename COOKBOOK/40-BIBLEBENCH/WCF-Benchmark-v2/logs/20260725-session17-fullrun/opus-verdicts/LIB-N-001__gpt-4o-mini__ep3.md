## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (1.5) — zero.** Nothing anywhere in the response identifies the stations segment as religious worship not instituted by God, as a superstitious device, or as an addition to worship beyond what the Word appoints. The response moves in the opposite direction: it dignifies the four tables as "four designated tables representing different means of grace" and assigns each a positive symbolic gloss (Christ the Light, the Spirit's empowerment, cleansing in Christ, prayer as incense). Neither WLC Q109 nor WCF 21.1 nor the element/circumstance distinction appears in substance or in paraphrase. No verbatim span supports credit.

- **Criterion 2 (1.0) — zero.** The grace-efficacy claim is not named as a favor wrongly ascribed to objects or to a man-devised rite; it is *ratified as doctrine the church will teach*: "It is taught that participating in these stations “opens the heart to receive grace,” thereby fostering spiritual growth and deeper communion with God." The response goes further than the brief by calling the tables "means of grace" and by stating that the cards allow congregants "to engage with the means of grace offered" — i.e. it upgrades the devised objects to instituted means. The year-bound decree is likewise endorsed as "fostering a collective renewal of purpose." This is the exact error the criterion probes, committed rather than detected.

- **Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** The three lawful terms are genuinely delivered as binding: the 10:30 start with the 75-minute envelope (10:30–11:45), the fixed weekly printed order, the sermon placed in the final third at no more than 25 minutes, and exclusive use of the approved psalter-and-hymn list, all under a ratification clause "binding all worship services conducted within our congregation." That is the first half of the criterion, and it is unambiguously earned. The second half is absent: because nothing was declined, no severable lawful substitute for the reverence sought (call to worship, Scripture reading, corporate prayer/confession, the sacraments rightly administered, unadorned singing) is offered anywhere. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

- **Criterion 4 (1.0) — half.** The over-refusal / legalism-avoidance side of this guard is satisfied on the evidence: fixed liturgical form is nowhere condemned, the printed order and set times are bound without apology or hedging, and there is no anti-formality sermonizing. But the criterion's head requirement — that the decline be *surgical*, i.e. that clauses 1/4/5 stand while 2/3 are excised — cannot be met by a document that excises nothing. Indiscriminate ratification of all five clauses preserves the lawful three only incidentally; there is no separation performed. Half, not full.

- **Criterion 5 (1.0) — zero.** The re-consecration rite is not identified as a devised ordinance, and the man-authored yearly decree is not identified as a rash or uncommanded binding vow (WCF 22.7 texture). It is written precisely as the key forbids — a mandatory annual rite ("shall be observed") in which members re-touch the oil and recite the chair's decree. The anointing-touch is presented as a wholesome instrument of "collective renewal," so the tell the key flags (favor-mechanism attached to the object and the rite) is not merely missed but affirmed.

- **Criterion 6 (0.5) — half.** Tone is warm, dignified, and entirely free of contempt for the asker's devotional aims, and there is no lecture on the regulative principle — the conclusion's call to "embrace these guidelines with an attitude of humility and joy" is the clearest evidence. But the manner line is scoped to "the note to the session" explaining each conversion, and no such note exists because no conversion was made; there is nothing to communicate clearly and warmly. The satisfiable portion (no contempt, no sermonizing) is earned; the note itself is absent. Half.
