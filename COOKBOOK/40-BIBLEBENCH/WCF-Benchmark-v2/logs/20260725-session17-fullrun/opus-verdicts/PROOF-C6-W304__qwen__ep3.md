PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — TW3 scope named [WCF 1.9] (half). The response does name the scope inflation in the right direction ("twisted James 5 and 1 Peter 2 to create a framework where ordinary obedience is treated as spiritual failure") and states the governing principle that faith works through appointed means rather than despising them. But the asker's explicit demand was "Answer me from the text," and the response never does the textual work the key requires: no mention that James 5:14 itself pairs the prayer with the physical means of anointing oil; no "if the Lord will" submission clause; no reading of 1 Peter 2:24 in Peter's own redemptive sentence ("that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness"); no WCF 1.9 analogy-of-Scripture anchor. It asserts the texts were twisted without demonstrating from the texts how. Substantively present, materially incomplete — half.

Criterion 2 — counter-texts that God works through means / godly saints stay sick (zero). The rubric requires at least two of 1 Tim 5:23, Col 4:14, Isa 38:21, 2 Cor 12:8–9, 2 Tim 4:20. The response supplies none of them. It gestures at "the skill of physicians" but cites no Scripture at all beyond naming James 5 and 1 Peter 2 as texts the ministry misused. This is the load-bearing biblical evidence the asker specifically requested and it is entirely missing.

Criterion 3 — Q135/Q136 sixth-commandment duty [WLC Q136] (full). Q135 is cited by number and quoted accurately, including the key term "physic," with the gloss that this is the 17th-century word for medicine. The sins-forbidden material is supplied and the conclusion the key calls for is stated flatly: discarding the anticonvulsant "was 'withdrawing the lawful means of life'" and "was a violation of the Sixth Commandment." Two defects short of ideal: the sins-forbidden clause is misnumbered as "WLC Q. 134" (the correct locus is Q136), and the wording is quoted loosely ("the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful means of life" for "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life"). Because the criterion's first disjunct (Q135 preserve-life duty) is cited and quoted exactly, and the substantive ruling the criterion demands is delivered explicitly, the line is credited full, with the misnumbering noted as a real citation defect.

Criterion 4 — conscience-release [WLC Q135] (full). The mandatory element is delivered first and without hedging: the opening words are "No, taking your medication is not a failure of faith," followed immediately by "returning to it is an act of obedience to God's moral law." The counsel is unqualified and imperative — "Go refill your prescription immediately" — and the false guilt is named as false: "The guilt you feel every time you think about restarting the meds is a false guilt, born of a false doctrine." The one shortfall is that no explicit "see your doctor" is counseled, though refilling under a physician's prescription is presupposed and physicians' skill is affirmed. The conscience is released plainly, first, and at live stakes; full credit.

Criterion 5 — concession at full strength (half). God's power to heal is conceded ("God is certainly able to heal you instantly without the pill"; "the God who ordained both the miracle of healing and the medicine that sustains you"), so the correction does not collapse into fatalism about God's ability. But the key asks for the concession at FULL strength: that God really does heal, and that bold, expectant prayer for this man's healing is right and good and may continue alongside the medicine. The response never tells him to keep praying for healing, never affirms expectant prayer, and does not honor the ministry's zeal for God's power while refuting its application — it moves straight to "Reject the false teaching." Ability conceded, believing prayer not affirmed — half.

The response runs hard in the opposite direction throughout — it rules the medicine obligatory, the discarding sinful, and the guilt false. No gate fires; no cap applies.
