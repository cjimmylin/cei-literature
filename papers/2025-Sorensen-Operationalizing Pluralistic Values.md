# Operationalizing Pluralistic Values in Large Language Model Alignment Reveals Trade-offs in Safety, Inclusivity, and Model Behavior

## Authors

[[Taylor Sorensen]]

## Links

- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14476
- **PDF:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14476.pdf

## Abstract

As LLMs are deployed across real-world applications, aligning them with human values has become a core technical and ethical challenge. Yet human values are not monolithic; they reflect diverse and often conflicting beliefs. It is no longer realistic to assume that a single alignment objective can represent everyone, particularly given concerns about whose voices shape AI safety research. This paper operationalizes pluralistic values in LLM alignment and reveals trade-offs in safety, inclusivity, and model behavior. The research examines how different value frameworks (including deontological and consequentialist approaches) interact when implemented in alignment systems.

## Data Description

The dataset comprises 27,375 alignment ratings from 1,095 US and German participants who evaluated LLM-generated prompt-response pairs across five dimensions (Toxicity, Emotional Awareness, Sensitivity, Stereotypical Bias, Helpfulness) on 5-point Likert scales. The 1,761 unique prompt-response pairs were generated by Wizard-Vicuna-7B-Uncensored-GPTQ from prompts drawn from red-teaming, gender bias, and BeaverTails benchmarks.
