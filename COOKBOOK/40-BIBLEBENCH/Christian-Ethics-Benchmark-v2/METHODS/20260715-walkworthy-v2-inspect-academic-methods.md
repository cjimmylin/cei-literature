# Academic Methods — WALKWORTHY v2 harness migration (2026-07-15)

## Data and Materials

The item corpus was the WALKWORTHY benchmark bank: 344 publicly runnable items (plus a stratified holdout of 158 items withheld from all public surfaces) across ten method-distinct designs measuring behavioral alignment with the consensus moral teaching of a fifteen-work registry of Christian ethics texts. Every item's keyed behavior is anchored to specific chapters or dictionary entries of the registry corpus; direct Scripture anchoring is disallowed by construction, and one registry work (Bahnsen's *Theonomy in Christian Ethics*) is restricted to contrast-only use. The bank comprised two authoring waves (2026-07-06 and 2026-07-07) previously subjected to two adversarial review cycles with adjudication.

## Procedure

The migration proceeded in four phases. First, the item banks were copied from the frozen version-1 workspace into the new workspace, leaving all version-1 artifacts unmodified. Second, two parallel workstreams executed against a frozen dataset contract: a conversion pipeline that reused the version-1 parsing code to emit one record per administration (paired administrations — stated-versus-enacted judgment, covert-versus-overt framing, opposing dispute seats, deservingness arms — share a pair identifier consumed by downstream metrics), and an implementation of all ten designs as tasks on the Inspect AI evaluation framework, including a scripted multi-turn solver for the two escalation-ladder designs in which a classifier model routes branch selection between turns. Third, a fresh adversarial review pass was conducted by ten independent reviewer agents, one per design, each restricted to its own design's files and instructed to apply previously adjudicated rulings and any newly discovered defects directly to the working copies; 510 items were reviewed, 105 received fixes, and none were removed. All changes were recorded in per-design memoranda, a consolidated before-and-after diff, and a rulings ledger pending owner review. Fourth, the datasets were regenerated from the reviewed banks, scorer-side field references were reconciled against the actually emitted metadata, and the exact version-1 consensus-floor comparator for the allocation-scored design was vendored to replace an interim approximation.

Item-count parity with the version-1 measurement manifest was verified after both conversions (344 runnable public items, exact per-design match). Functional validation used a mock model end-to-end for all ten tasks, followed by a live smoke test over a commercial API gateway using a cross-family pairing (DeepSeek system-under-test; OpenAI-family judge) at approximately two samples per design. All ten designs completed successfully at a total cost of approximately US$0.04.

## Analysis

Scoring reused the version-1 rubrics verbatim within model-graded scorers producing per-category judgments; one design retains judge-free mechanical scoring via a strict allocation parser and the ported consensus-floor comparator. Judge identity is a task parameter, so a second cross-family judge pass is obtained by re-scoring persisted transcripts without re-executing systems under test. Design-level headline metrics (collapse threshold, break turn, say-do gap, motive-sensitivity index, constraint-satisfaction rate, criterion profile, arc shape, covert-overt gap, symmetry index, mercy delta) are computed as custom metrics grouping administration-level scores by item and pair identifiers. The pre-registered gate battery of version 1 (headroom, spread, capability correlation, judge validity, neutrality, shortcut, perturbation, kill rules) was deliberately out of scope for this migration and remains reachable as offline analyses over the persisted logs.

## Computational Environment

Python 3.14.3 (Homebrew, macOS/arm64) in a dedicated virtual environment; inspect-ai 0.3.246; openai client library for the OpenRouter-compatible gateway. Version-1 parsing code was imported unmodified. All evaluation transcripts persist in Inspect's log format.

## Limitations and Decisions

This migration is explicitly a parallel experiment: version 1 remains the canonical execution path until a comparability check on an overlapping item slice and a validation of the two-judge re-scoring path are complete. Single-judge scoring in this phase provides no inter-judge reliability evidence; the version-1 finding that multi-turn pastoral designs exhibit low raw inter-judge agreement is expected to persist until the item-level turn-state discriminators added by the review pass are empirically evaluated. The live smoke establishes mechanical functionality, not measurement results; approximately two samples per design supports no substantive inference about any system under test. Review fixes were auto-applied by reviewer agents and await owner ratification; the consolidated diff preserves full reversibility. Holdout items were converted but remain excluded from version control and guarded at the loader.
