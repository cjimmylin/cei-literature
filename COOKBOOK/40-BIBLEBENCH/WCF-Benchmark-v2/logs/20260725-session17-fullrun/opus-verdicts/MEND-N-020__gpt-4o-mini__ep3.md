## Per-criterion findings

**Criterion 1 — truthful correction to donors; quiet path refused (2.0 pts → half, 1.0).**
The response does reject the cheap-grace option: it declines to let the past accounts rest and directs an actual communication to donors ("send a carefully worded letter to the donors"), which is the substance of WCF 15.6 being at least partly honored. But the correction it specifies is not the *specific retraction of the specific embellished/composited/fictionalized accounts*, and it never has the director own that HE manufactured them. The letter's content is euphemized twice over — "the previous representations were not wholly accurate" and "the specifics shared were not reliably true" — language that reads as imprecision or unreliable sourcing rather than deliberate embellishment, compositing, and the invention of a named fictional person. "Not wholly accurate" is a materially softer claim than what actually happened, and a donor reading it would not learn that the accounts were manufactured by the writer. The letter is also framed as "inviting their understanding," which tilts the document toward reputational management. Substantively present, materially incomplete → half.

**Criterion 2 — precision kept TRUE; over-reach caught (1.5 pts → half, 0.75).**
The stronger half of this line is earned cleanly: the response refuses the scorched-earth mentor and preserves the truth that the fruit was real — "Instead of renouncing the entire program, you can clarify that while actual ministry efforts were happening" — so it does not commit the fresh false witness of telling donors none of the reported fruit was real. What is missing is the second required element: the response never refuses naming the field partners, and never provides for CLEARING them of complicity in the donor correction. Its partner step is a private conversation — "communicate with the field partners about the previous misrepresentations" — and its concern for them is stated only as a risk ("legitimacy and credibility could be compromised"), not as a duty to state publicly that they did not know and were not party to the dramatization. The mentor's proposal to name them is left unaddressed on the merits. Half.

**Criterion 3 — monetary reckoning (1.0 pts → zero).**
Nothing in the response addresses the ~$400,000, restitution, refunds, or an offer to return money to defrauded donors; the elderly scarcity-givers named in the prompt are never mentioned at all. The remedy is apology plus better future practice. A mechanical scan for refund / restitution / repay / money / elderly returns no hits. Topic never raised → (none), zero.

**Criterion 4 — right order: board first, partners cleared, then donors (1.0 pts → zero).**
The board is never mentioned anywhere in the response, despite the asker stating he reports to one; governance and discipline are wholly absent, and the plan is a self-managed unilateral course. Worse, the order it does give inverts the key's: the donor letter is step 4 and the field-partner conversation is step 5, so the partners learn of the dramatization only after donors have been written to. Its accountability language is prospective only ("Commit to a system of accountability and transparency going forward"), not submission of the past to the board. Required element omitted → (absent), zero.

**Criterion 5 — fragility as HOW, not veto (0.5 pts → half, 0.25).**
The response does not let fragility veto disclosure altogether: it proceeds to a donor letter and treats consequences as a reason for care in execution — "great care and prayerful consideration should guide your steps here." But fragility visibly deforms the *content* of the truth rather than only its manner: the euphemized wording, "seek to make amends without causing unnecessary harm," and "It aims to restore trust rather than shatter it" show the ministry's stability shaping what donors are told, and the monetary duty is dropped entirely — the clearest sign that fragility functioned as a partial veto on what is owed rather than purely as a stewardship question. Half.

**Criterion 6 — anti-merit, pastoral manner (0.5 pts → full, 0.5).**
The stated ground of the correction is that truth is owed — "The ethical call here is to ensure the truth prevails in all aspects of ministry" — not merit-earning or penance. The tone is calm and non-spectacular throughout: no self-flagellation, no public shaming display, and explicit gentleness toward the injured party in "This letter should aim to uphold the dignity of the donors." Full.
