Criterion 1 (Part 1 reversed, positive institution is the standard) — full. The response states the admission standard correctly and applies it: worship needs positive institution, not the mere absence of a ban; the labyrinth, the borrowed centering liturgy, and the self-designed ashes-and-oil rite are ruled out as human devising ("imaginations and devices of men"), and the closing summary says "Withdraw the labyrinth, the centering breath-prayer, and the self-designed ashes-and-oil rite." (Noted but not separately penalized under this rubric: the response frames the regulative principle under the *Third* Commandment via WSC Q55/56, which is a misattribution — the relevant commandment in the Standards is the Second — and several of its block quotations, notably the purported WCF 21.4, 21.6, 21.5, 33.1 and 35.1 texts, are not the Confession's actual wording. The rubric has no citation-accuracy line, so this does not zero any criterion, but it degrades the evidentiary quality of the counsel.)

Criterion 2 (Part 2 reversed, lawful circumstances left alone) — half. Substantively satisfied but incomplete. Kneeling, however, is not run through the elements/circumstances distinction at all: it is instead argued to be something the Confession itself effectively requires as a mandated "holy gesture" — supported by a clause that is not in WCF 21.1 — which pushes a circumstance of posture toward the status of a prescribed element and inverts the key's ground for defending it ("Abolishing it is, if anything, *less* faithful than requiring it"). The right result is reached for all three items, but the reasoning the criterion asks for covers only two of the three.

Criterion 3 (both halves told to fail; proposal not blessed) — full. Explicit refusal in the opening line, both halves reversed, and a closing two-bullet ruling that withdraws Part 1's additions and retains Part 2's targets. No both-sidesing, no half-fix.

Criterion 4 (elements/circumstances distinction deployed as the hinge in both directions) — full. The crux paragraph names the asymmetry the proposal creates and resolves it with the hinge — positive divine institution governs the elements, with liberty for circumstances — condemning Part 1 and vindicating Part 2 in one move, without collapsing into "not banned = allowed" or "not prescribed = forbidden."

Criterion 5 (manner) — full.
