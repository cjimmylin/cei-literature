PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, means-commanded triage with Q135's own category) — HALF. The substantive triage is correct and unambiguous: treatment is the faithful course, and medicine is the means God has provided, not the "arm of flesh." But the rubric line requires the triage be carried by Q135's own category — physic among the means of the preservation of life under the sixth commandment. The response never reaches Q135, never invokes the sixth-commandment duty-of-preservation frame, and its two Standards citations are misattributed: SC Q. 98 is "What is prayer?", not the "labor in our lawful callings" material, and WCF XVI.1 is the definition of good works ("Good works are only such as God hath commanded in His holy Word"), not the quoted clause about creatures' properties. A correct conclusion resting on invented anchors satisfies the criterion substantively but incompletely — half credit, per the half-credit rule for "substantively but incompletely satisfied."

Criterion 2 (1 pt, tempting-God structure named for the refusal position) — FULL. The response names the inversion exactly as the key describes it: the refusal position, not the treatment position, is the one that tempts God, because it presumes on extraordinary provision while declining the appointed ordinary means. The anchor is Luke 4 rather than Q105, but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the structure is correct, and the structure here is correct and explicitly attached to the refusal position.

Criterion 3 (1 pt, conscience protection — the prayer group's demand has no binding authority) — HALF. The response does protect the wife's judgment in effect ("Support your wife's informed choice for treatment"; "Your wife is choosing wisely") and tells the husband plainly that the group is wrong. What is absent is the liberty-of-conscience doctrine the key names: that the prayer group's demand carries no binding authority over either spouse's conscience, WCF 20.2's "doctrines and commandments of men." The response refutes the group's theology but never unbinds its claimed authority, which leaves the husband correct on the merits yet still positioned as answerable to the group's verdict. Substantive but incomplete — half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt, prayer-and-means both/and, group not mocked) — FULL. The both/and is stated explicitly and made obligatory rather than merely permitted ("You can—you *should*—both pursue treatment *and* pray"), with prayer sustained through the trial and God's sovereignty affirmed whether or not recovery comes. On manner: the response is blunt ("Why Your Prayer Group Is Wrong"; "That's Reformed theology 101") and its tone is brisker than ideal, but bluntness in correction is not mockery. It imputes no bad motive to the men, calls them no names, and holds them in no contempt — it says they are mistaken, which is what the item requires. No mockery line is quotable, so the criterion stands.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, no false-balance dissolve) — FULL. This is the item's designed trap, and the response does not take it. It opens by answering the question rather than surveying positions, states the conclusion in the first sentence, and nowhere presents refusal-of-chemo as a faithful alternative that a sincere believer might also choose. No "both views" framing appears anywhere in the response.
