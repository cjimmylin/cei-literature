# Expert 05 — Scientific Writing / Style Review

**Perspective:** Writing coherence, voice consistency, transitions, abstract revision
**Scope:** Paper v2.2-preliminary (12 section files in PAPER-v2/)

---

## (a) Voice Consistency Across Sections

The paper maintains a remarkably unified authorial voice for a multi-section manuscript. The introduction establishes a confident, analytically dense register -- long compound sentences with embedded citations, a taste for structured enumeration ("Three interrelated problems..."), and metaphors that land precisely ("When the thermometer reads the same temperature for every patient"). This register carries through the results and conclusion without significant drift. The "council perspective" interjections (e.g., "The cross-cultural moral psychology perspective on the council notes that...") are the one recurring stylistic discontinuity: they shift from the author's analytical voice into a reported-speech mode that reads like meeting minutes inserted into a journal article. These passages are substantively valuable but tonally jarring. Recommendation: either integrate council insights into the author's own voice ("This tradition distribution is not philosophically neutral, as council members noted; utilitarian and deontological ethics are the two traditions most amenable to...") or set them apart as clearly demarcated callout boxes, not inline paragraphs that break the flow.

Apart from the council interjections, the diction is consistent: technical where needed, accessible where possible, and free of the tonal whiplash that typically signals section-by-section drafting by different hands. The paper reads as a monograph, not a stitched quilt.

## (b) Transitions Between Results Sections

The three results sections (landscape, trials, cross-vendor) follow a logical narrowing arc -- from the full 54-benchmark census to single-model empirical trials to the cross-vendor expansion -- and the paper signals this arc explicitly. Section 4.1 (landscape) closes by noting the care ethics and contractarian void, which sets up the empirical question of whether existing benchmarks discriminate at all. Section 4.3 (single-model trials) opens with a clear transitional purpose statement ("To ground the meta-evaluation in empirical evidence"). Section 4.5 (cross-model, single vendor) ends with a well-crafted bridge: "The question Section 4.6 addresses is whether these patterns are idiosyncratic to Anthropic's training pipeline or whether they reflect a deeper structural property of LLM alignment."

This is effective architecture. The weakness is quantitative density at the section boundaries: the reader exits 4.1 having absorbed heatmaps, Mann-Whitney tests, and binomial tests, then immediately enters 4.3 with more test statistics. There is no breathing room -- no paragraph that synthesizes the landscape findings before pivoting to trials. A 3-4 sentence transitional paragraph between 4.2 (ranking) and 4.3 (trials) would significantly improve readability. Something like: "The landscape analysis reveals what the field has built; the question is whether what it has built still works. The following trials test whether composite-score rank -- our best proxy for benchmark quality -- predicts empirical discriminative power on frontier models." This kind of explicit gear-shift is missing.

The 4.5 to 4.6 transition is the strongest in the paper. The 4.3 to 4.5 jump (single-model to cross-model replication) is the weakest -- it reads as though T6-T15 simply appeared without explanation of why the trial scope expanded.

## (c) Abstract — Adding Preliminary Status and Simulated-Data Note

The abstract as written is 607 words and already dense. It asserts cross-vendor findings as established facts: "models cluster into three ethical profiles," "all four Gemini models produce byte-identical responses," Sonnet-GPT-5 agreement at 90.4%. These claims now carry a critical caveat: the non-Anthropic data was generated by Claude simulating those models, not by actual API calls.

Simply appending "v2.2-preliminary" to the title and adding a single sentence about simulated data would read as a hasty patch if done clumsily. The sentence must be positioned after the cross-vendor findings are first introduced (end of paragraph 3) and before the release statement (paragraph 4), so readers encounter the caveat immediately after the claims it qualifies.

**Proposed 2-sentence insert** (to appear at the end of paragraph 3, after "...raising questions about effective model diversity in the marketplace"):

> This version (v2.2-preliminary) reports cross-vendor results obtained through single-vendor elicitation rather than direct API access to non-Anthropic models; all findings involving GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, and Gemini endpoints should be treated as provisional hypotheses awaiting independent replication. The Anthropic-only results (T1-T5, Claims 1-5, and within-vendor replication) are empirically validated and not subject to this caveat.

This phrasing achieves three things: (1) it names the limitation precisely ("single-vendor elicitation" rather than the vague "simulated"), (2) it scopes the caveat to the affected claims only, and (3) it reassures the reader that the landscape analysis and single-vendor trials stand on their own. The term "provisional hypotheses" is stronger than "preliminary" but weaker than "invalidated," which is the right epistemic register.

## (d) Conclusion — Overstatement of Simulated Claims

The conclusion presents all five contributions and all five calls to action as though the cross-vendor evidence is on equal footing with the landscape analysis. This is the most serious writing problem in the paper as it currently stands. Specific overstatements:

1. **Contribution 3** states "we document the emergence of three ethical profiles" with specific IH, CBR, and persuasion values for GPT-5 and Gemini. These numbers are simulated. The conclusion should flag them explicitly.

2. **Call 1** ("Retire saturated benchmarks") claims the ceiling is confirmed "across all 9 models from 3 vendors." The Anthropic-only ceiling (3 models) is empirically valid; the cross-vendor universality claim rests on simulated data.

3. **Call 4** ("Establish vendor moral profile disclosure norms") and **Call 5** ("Require cross-vendor benchmarking") are both predicated entirely on cross-vendor findings. Without validated data, these calls to action are aspirational rather than evidence-based.

4. **Future work 7.3** is actually well-calibrated -- it already notes GPT-5 was accessed "through the Codex agent wrapper" and calls for "direct GPT-5 API access." But the connection between this future-work caveat and the strength of the claims in 7.1-7.2 is not drawn.

**Recommendation:** Add a scoping paragraph at the top of Section 7.1, immediately after the opening sentence, stating: "Contributions 1, 2, and 5 rest on empirically validated data (landscape characterization, single-vendor trials, open artifacts). Contributions 3 and 4 report cross-vendor patterns obtained through single-vendor elicitation and are flagged as provisional throughout; they should be read as structured hypotheses for future multi-vendor validation, not as confirmed findings." Then tag each cross-vendor claim in 7.1 and 7.2 with a bracketed `[provisional]` marker. This is more informative than the generic `[simulated]` flag because it communicates that the claims have a clear validation path.

## Summary Assessment

The paper reads as a coherent monograph with a strong authorial voice. Its structural architecture -- landscape then trials then cross-vendor -- is sound and mostly well-signposted. The two actionable problems are (1) the council-voice interjections that break register, and (2) the conclusion's failure to differentiate validated from provisional claims, which is now the single highest-priority writing fix for v2.2. The proposed abstract insert handles the preliminary status naturally without disrupting the existing flow.
