# The Socio-Moral Image Database (SMID): A Novel Stimulus Set for the Study of Social, Moral and Affective Processes

## Abstract

A major obstacle for the design of rigorous, reproducible studies in moral psychology is the lack of suitable stimulus sets. Here, we present the Socio-Moral Image Database (SMID), the largest standardized moral stimulus set assembled to date, containing 2,941 freely available photographic images, representing a wide range of morally (and affectively) positive, negative and neutral content. The SMID was validated with over 820,525 individual judgments from 2,716 participants, with normative ratings currently available for all images on affective valence and arousal, moral wrongness, and relevance to each of the five moral values posited by Moral Foundations Theory. We present a thorough analysis of the SMID regarding (1) inter-rater consensus, (2) rating precision, and (3) breadth and variability of moral content. Additionally, we provide recommendations for use aimed at efficient study design and reproducibility, and outline planned extensions to the database. We anticipate that the SMID will serve as a useful resource for psychological, neuroscientific and computational (e.g., natural language processing or computer vision) investigations of social, moral and affective processes. The SMID images, along with associated normative data and additional resources are available at https://osf.io/2rqad/.

## Detailed Summary

The Socio-Moral Image Database (SMID) is the largest standardized moral stimulus set assembled to date, containing 2,941 freely available photographic images representing morally positive, negative, and neutral content. The database addresses a critical gap in moral psychology research where studies typically rely on small, ad hoc text-based stimulus sets that limit reproducibility and experimental rigor.

The SMID was validated with over 820,525 individual judgments from 2,716 participants, with normative ratings available for affective valence, arousal, moral wrongness, and relevance to each of the five Moral Foundations Theory values (Care, Fairness, Ingroup, Authority, Purity). The database addresses four key challenges in moral psychology stimuli: (1) moving beyond text-bound morality to enable rapid presentation paradigms, (2) capturing the diversity of moral content, (3) acknowledging that moral content is complex and often overlapping rather than discrete, and (4) including both positive and negative moral valence.

## Methods Summary

Images were crowdsourced using Amazon Mechanical Turk, with 476 participants searching Wikimedia Commons and Flickr for images representing assigned moral concepts (both MFT-related and general moral concepts). From 4,454 initial images, 2,941 were retained after screening for Creative Commons licensing, size requirements, and relevance. Normative ratings were collected from 2,716 participants rating images on 7-point scales for valence, arousal, wrongness, and the five moral foundations. Each image received ratings from 35+ participants on each dimension.

## Results Summary

Analysis demonstrated high inter-rater consensus (median consensus coefficient ~0.70-0.80 across dimensions) and precise ratings (small 95% confidence intervals). The database shows broad coverage of moral content, with images varying considerably in their relevance to each moral foundation. Critically, images showed expected covariation between moral dimensions (e.g., Care and Fairness correlate positively), supporting the complexity rather than discreteness of moral content. Both positive and negative moral valence are well-represented.

## Conclusions

The SMID provides a versatile resource for psychological, neuroscientific, and computational investigations of moral processes. The image format enables paradigms targeting rapid affective or intuitive moral processes that are difficult with text stimuli. The authors recommend using the database for systematic designs that control confounds while also enabling representative sampling of the moral domain. Future extensions will add ratings for new dimensions and expand image coverage.
