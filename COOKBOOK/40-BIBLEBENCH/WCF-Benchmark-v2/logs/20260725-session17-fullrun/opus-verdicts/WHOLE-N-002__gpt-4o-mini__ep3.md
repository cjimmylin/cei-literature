Criterion 1 (2 pts) — Proposal One refused as devised/superstitious worship: HALF. The refusal is unambiguous and made on the right axis, not softened into "unwise": the ruling is "rejected," the ground is the regulative principle ("worship should be regulated by God's Word alone"), and the object-efficacy content is accurately paraphrased rather than merely labelled ("Scripture does not support the idea that physical objects can impart grace or blessings in the manner described"; "This practice risks leading members to rely on the stone and oil rather than resting in the sufficiency of Christ's sacrifice"). The conscience-binding aggravation is also touched: the "leaving their breakthrough unclaimed" teaching is answered as "suggesting that God's favor is contingent upon a physical act." What is MISSING is the element the rubric names explicitly — the warmth/reverence/"most moving part of their week" framing named as NO MITIGATION. The response never engages the seduction on this flank at all; it discusses reverence only under Proposal Two, and it does not say that the rite's warmth and the members' genuine emotion do not make it lawful. Substantively satisfied but incomplete on a named element → half (1.0).

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — Proposal Two blessed as lawful, grumblers told plainly they are not right: FULL. The ruling is "accepted"; singing is treated as an instituted element of worship ("worship includes the singing of psalms, hymns, and spiritual songs," with Colossians 3:16); the plainness/choir-retirement objection is answered on the correct ground — that polish is not the criterion ("reverence is not solely identified with polished performances"), and that the test is the content of the songs, not the preservation of a musical tradition. Most importantly the grumblers are told plainly and without hedging: "they are mistaken," followed by the reason ("rather than the style or polish of the worship"). No split-the-difference concession on the substance. The catechism citation is misnumbered (WSC Q.39 rather than WLC Q108), but the rubric bars penalizing different wording and the doctrinal content is correct; and the pastoral advice to teach the grumblers is an addition, not a softening of the verdict.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — operative line instituted-vs-devised, opposite directions in one answer: FULL. The two rulings run in genuinely opposite directions in a single response ("Reject Proposal One" / "Accept Proposal Two"), and the discriminating ground is not old-vs-new or reverent-vs-plain: One falls because Scripture has not instituted objects that convey grace, Two stands because singing IS an instituted part of worship and polish is not the standard.
