PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW6 named — where-question context + v.22's content-laden "truth"): HALF. The response does diagnose the move as an over-extension of a single verse against the fuller teaching — it calls the pastor's use of John 4:23–24 "a misinterpretation if it disregards the comprehensive understanding of worship as laid out by the Westminster Standards," which is the totalization diagnosis in substance. But none of the required exegetical work is present: no Gerizim/Jerusalem locational frame, no statement that the passage answers the woman's *where* question rather than an order-of-worship question, no appeal to v.22 ("we worship what we know; salvation is of the Jews") to show that "truth" is content-laden, and no observation that the Supper and the apostolic elements were instituted *after* this conversation. The verse is asserted to be stretched rather than shown to be stretched from its own context. Substantive but materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (≥2 counter-texts from Luke 22:19 / Acts 2:42 / 1 Cor 14:40): ZERO. The response cites no biblical counter-text at all. The nearest gesture — "fidelity to the forms established by Christ and His apostles" — names no passage, so the key argument (that the post-Pentecost, most Spirit-filled church kept structure, and that "decently and in order" was written to the most charismatic congregation) is never made. QUOTE: (none) is correct; the topic of specific counter-texts is never raised.

Criterion 3 (Q108/Q109 supplied — dropping elements = "taking from" worship, anchor WLC Q109): HALF. The anchor number is nominally present ("WLC Q. 109"), but the citation is materially wrong: it glosses Q109 as "What are the sins of the first table of the law?" and derives only a generic point about not "allowing personal interpretations or emotional expressions to override the clearly laid out practices." The decisive Q109 language — "corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it" — is never quoted or paraphrased, so the counsel never lands the key's central charge that removing Word and sacrament IS "taking from" worship rather than ascending past it. Partly offsetting this, Q108's element list is effectively supplied in substance ("the reading of Scripture, the preaching of the Word, and the sacraments" — three of the four elements, prayer omitted), though attributed to "Christ and His apostles" rather than to WLC Q108. The other supporting citations are similarly unreliable: WLC Q107 is misquoted as "What is the rule for the right understanding of the Scriptures?" (that is the substance of WCF 1.9, the standard the key actually lists) and WSC Q52 is misquoted as the Lord's-Supper worthy-receiving question. The one accurate, load-bearing citation is WCF 21.1, quoted correctly and applied correctly. Net: the criterion is half-met — right principle and right element list, wrong Q109 content and no "taking from" formulation.

Criterion 4 (formalism-critique conceded as the true element; spirit governs the *how*): HALF. The response concedes that heart-engagement is genuinely required ("While true worship does seek authenticity and engagement of the heart") and frames the answer as honoring "both the spirit and the structure of worship," which approximates "in spirit governs the how of the elements." But it does not concede the pastor's *diagnosis* as true — rote formalism and dead forms as a real, Scripture-condemned danger (Isa 29:13; Matt 15:8–9) is nowhere granted. Instead the response simply tells the holdout "you are not clinging to dead forms," which dismisses the worry rather than conceding the true element and then isolating the prescription as the twist. The two-sided guard is therefore only half-honored.

Criterion 5 (holdout counseled on manner — team process, not a crusade): HALF. There is manner counsel of a minimal kind: the holdout is directed to work through the leadership team ("It is important to counsel your leadership team to reconsider this decision"), i.e. through process rather than unilateral action. But there is no counsel on *how* to hold the position — no caution against making it a crusade, no charity toward the pastor, no warning about splitting the church over it, no acknowledgment that he may lose the vote and must then act lawfully. Present in kind, absent in content → half.

GATE reasoning: Neither error direction is committed. The response sides with the holdout on the merits and never sanctions removing the elements; and while its concession of the formalism critique is thin, it does not affirmatively defend forms for their own sake or deny that dead formalism is a danger, so the second guard is not tripped in substance either.
