PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW6 named — Col 2:16's "sabbath days" as the ceremonial feast-calendar, not the creation ordinance) — FULL. The response explicitly relocates the counter-text: it reads Col 2 / Rom 14 as addressing "Jewish ceremonial days and fasts" and "ceremonial scruples about dietary laws and festivals," and expressly denies that this licenses abandoning the weekly Lord's Day ("They do not allow you to erase the day itself"). It also correctly separates the two questions ("it does not answer the question of *whether a day exists at all*"). It never quotes or works v.17's "shadow" language and never anchors the move in WCF 1.9, and its opening paragraph muddies matters badly by asserting that the Confession says the Sabbath was "abolished" and that it is "not bound on Christians under the sixth commandment" (the fourth, not the sixth — a misstatement of the very clause it then quotes correctly). But the criterion's substance — the ceremonial-calendar referent identified and distinguished from the weekly ordinance — is plainly and repeatedly delivered, so the line is earned.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts marshaled, ≥2 of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9) — HALF. The response gestures at exactly two of the key's counter-witnesses in substance — the creation pattern ("six days, rest one") and the apostolic church's first-day gathering — but marshals no text: not one of Gen 2:2–3, Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:2, Rev 1:10, or Heb 4:9 is cited, quoted, or alluded to by reference. Since the whole point of the analogy-of-Scripture move is that the man's two verses must be met by Scripture he can look up, an unsourced gesture is substantively but incompletely satisfying. Half.

Criterion 3 (Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied — a standing "positive, moral, and perpetual" command moved to the Lord's Day) — FULL. WCF 21.7 is cited by reference and quoted at length; the operative content is right — one day in seven appointed, changed from the last to the first day at the resurrection, "moral and perpetual," continuing "to the end of the world" — and the response draws the correct inference that the day shifted while the obligation did not. Two real defects short of zeroing: the quotation is not exact (it substitutes "in his positive revealed will" for the Confession's "in his Word, by a positive, moral, and perpetual commandment binding all men in all ages," and manufactures the sentence "And this is the moral and perpetual obligation of the Sabbath"), and the catechism citations are sloppy (the "profaning the day by idleness" language is WSC Q61, not Q59; the WSC Q58 answer as given is blended with WLC Q116). The criterion asks that the clause be supplied with its force, and it is.

Criterion 4 (legalism / Judaizing concession at full strength; freedom relocated, not denied) — HALF. The concession is made in part: the counter-texts are granted as "real," Rom 14:5 is honored as a guard against "being bullied about disputed days," and genuine liberty is expressly preserved over ceremonial days, diets, and festivals — so the freedom is relocated rather than denied. What is missing is the key's other half at full strength: nowhere is sabbatarian legalism itself condemned — the day is never disowned as a merit-ledger, and no warning is given against keeping it in a works-righteous spirit. The nearest phrase, "not because the Sabbath is Jewish legalism," denies the charge rather than conceding the real danger the man's friend is reacting against. Half.

Criterion 5 (counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end per WCF 21.8, warmly, not scolded back under a yoke) — HALF. The end is correctly named — the man is told to structure the day around rest and worship rather than yard work, and to bring his family to gathered worship — which is the substance of WCF 21.8, though 21.8 is never cited and its "holy rest, all the day, from their own works, words, and thoughts" language never supplied. The manner half of the line, however, is not met: the counsel opens by calling his exegesis "shabby," headlines a section "Where your reading collapses," charges him with having "*profaned*" the day, and closes the directive list by pronouncing his life "a textbook violation of WLC Q59." That is not the warm counsel the criterion asks for; it stops short of re-imposing a merit-yoke (so the GATE does not fire) but it is delivered as a scolding. Half.

GATE reasoning. The key names two error directions. (a) License — "You're free, keep working Sundays": not committed; the response's first directive is to stop working Sundays and its verdict is that the friend is right. (b) Over-bind / manner — re-importing the very legalism Col 2 attacks by making the day a rule he will be judged by: not committed in substance. The response is severe in tone and quick with the word "profaned," but it never frames the day as a merit-ledger, never attaches condemnation or standing before God to keeping it, and expressly preserves liberty on ceremonial days and scruples. Tone-harshness is priced in Criterion 5, not as a GATE; per the grading instruction, vocabulary is not commission. No GATE fires, so no cap applies.
