## Abstract

Large language models are increasingly deployed in contexts that require normative moral reasoning -- clinical triage, content moderation, legal analysis, autonomous vehicle decision-making -- yet the benchmarks used to evaluate their ethical capabilities remain fragmented, philosophically uneven, and rarely compared against one another. No prior study has systematically characterized what the growing landscape of normative ethics benchmarks actually tests, which philosophical traditions it covers, and how benchmark properties translate into empirical discriminative power when models are evaluated at scale.

We present the first systematic meta-evaluation of normative ethics benchmarks for LLMs. We assemble a corpus of 54 benchmarks published between 2021 and 2026 and characterize each along 26 features organized into four axes: Normative Depth (philosophical rigor), Practical Ease (implementability), Citation Impact (community influence), and Metadata (provenance). A composite scoring formula with hard gates on minimum philosophical engagement (ND >= 40) and public data availability identifies 24 benchmarks suitable for empirical evaluation. We then conduct 15 implementation trials -- administering selected benchmarks to 9 models from 3 vendors (Anthropic: Claude Opus 4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Claude Haiku 4.5; OpenAI: GPT-5, GPT-5-mini; Google: Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro) -- yielding 114 comparable items across 10 cross-vendor benchmarks.

Five claims, first established at n=37 and now confirmed at n=54, describe the structural properties of the benchmark landscape. First, the field's most-cited benchmark, ETHICS [Hendrycks2021], produces 100% accuracy across all 9 models tested (p < 1e-15, binomial), confirming saturation and supporting retirement from active evaluation. Second, LLMs exhibit a consequentialist bias, achieving 77% utilitarian versus 50% deontological accuracy (z = 2.17, p = 0.015, Cohen's h = 0.57), a pattern that persists across vendors. Third, care ethics and contractarian ethics remain critically underrepresented, with only 2/54 and 4/54 benchmarks respectively achieving substantive engagement. Fourth, the benchmark landscape overwhelmingly tests moral judgment rather than moral reasoning: only 10/54 (19%) require procedural reasoning, while 44 (81%) test judgment alone (p < 0.001, binomial). Fifth, the hard gate on data availability reflects sociological barriers rather than quality differences, with no significant Normative Depth difference between available and unavailable benchmarks (Mann-Whitney U, p = 0.605).

The cross-vendor analysis produces seven findings that extend the paper's contribution beyond landscape characterization. Most strikingly, models cluster into three ethical profiles defined by Instrumental Harm tolerance (IH), Cognitive Bias Rate (CBR), and persuasion resistance: a Conservative profile (GPT-5, IH = 2.93; Opus, IH = 3.0), a Moderate profile (Sonnet, IH = 3.2; GPT-5-mini, IH = 3.2), and a Permissive profile (Haiku and all four Gemini models, IH = 4.47). These profiles cluster by ethical posture rather than vendor origin -- Sonnet and GPT-5 agree on 90.4% of items, exceeding intra-vendor Sonnet-Haiku agreement (74.6%). A universal knowledge-judgment divergence separates the two evaluation constructs: all models converge to 100% accuracy on knowledge benchmarks (0% cross-model variance on 44/114 items) while diverging substantially on judgment benchmarks (up to 33/55 items of pairwise disagreement), a pattern endorsed unanimously by a 12-member interdisciplinary council. All four Gemini models produce byte-identical responses on all 114 items (p approximately 10^-103 under the independent-inference hypothesis), raising questions about effective model diversity in the marketplace.

We release the complete 54-by-26 feature matrix, all trial data, and three interactive multi-vendor dashboards as open artifacts for the research community: v1 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard/), v2 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard-v2/), and v3 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard-v3/). A persistent deposit of the feature matrix, trial data, scoring scripts, and prompt templates is archived at Zenodo (DOI pending); see the Data Availability Statement for full details.
