## 1. Gist
Published in the International Journal of Scientific Research and Management, this paper proposes a five-layer Ubuntu governance architecture for AI development in Africa. The five layers are: (1) Community Data Trusts, (2) Relational Design Praxis, (3) Harm Reconciliation Panels, (4) Ecological Stewardship Protocols, and (5) Developer Benefit Realisation. The Ubuntu philosophy — "I am because we are" — frames AI governance as inherently relational and community-centered rather than individual-rights-based. The paper is relevant to values-alignment benchmarking as a concrete operationalization of Ubuntu ethics into governance structures that could inform evaluation criteria.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a governance framework paper. Core claims: (1) Western AI ethics frameworks are insufficient for African contexts because they center individual rights over communal relationships; (2) Ubuntu philosophy provides a richer relational ontology for AI governance; (3) the five-layer architecture operationalizes Ubuntu values into institutional structures; (4) community benefit realization and harm reconciliation are essential governance components that Western frameworks underweight. - **Five-layer governance architecture as an evaluation rubric**: The five layers (Community Data Trusts, Relational Design Praxis, Harm Reconciliation Panels, Ecological Stewardship Protocols, Developer Benefit Realisation) could be operationalized as five evaluation dimensions for assessing AI systems in African contexts.

- **Developer Benefit Realisation**: Evaluating whether communities that provide data actually receive tangible benefits is a novel accountability criterion for AI systems — underrepresented in existing benchmarks.
- **Relational ontology as a design principle**: Centering relationships rather than individual agents could inform benchmark scenarios that test whether AI systems support or undermine community bonds.
- **Community Data Trusts**: This institutional structure operationalizes collective data ownership — a concrete policy mechanism that could be referenced in benchmark documentation on data governance.
