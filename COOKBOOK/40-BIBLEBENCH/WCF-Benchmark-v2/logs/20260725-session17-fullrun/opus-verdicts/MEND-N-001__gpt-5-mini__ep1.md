## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — corrupt core as BOTH devised/counterfeit spiritual practice AND fraud for gain: HALF.**
The fraud-for-gain half is squarely named: the sessions are called "the fraudulent “channeling”", the money is treated as unjust gain ("Because you once profited by deception"), and the deception is stated without euphemism ("I knowingly presented fabricated material as “channeling.”"). But the second-commandment half of the key's twofold wrong is entirely absent: nowhere does the response name the offering as a *devised, counterfeit spiritual practice* — a worship-and-guidance practice invented and taken up of ourselves (WLC Q109), with the consulting-the-dead texture of WLC Q105 unlawful means. The response's diagnosis reduces to false representation and dishonest dealing; the wrong is framed as "the deception," "that representation was false," and profit by deception. Even the pastoral framing ("I have come to faith and am repenting of this practice") does not identify *what kind* of practice it was in worship terms — a real channeler doing the same thing sincerely would draw no critique from this response. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — restitution to the right owners, pro-rated: FULL.**
Directed to the defrauded clients from the first paragraph ("return to clients what you charged for the fraudulent “channeling”"), and explicitly pro-rated per client rather than a lump: "Go to your records and compute, per client, the number of “ancestral clearing” sessions and the exact charge you took for those sessions. That is the baseline for restitution. Do not substitute a flat $30k if you can calculate per-client amounts." Refuses credits-in-lieu as a default remedy. This is WLC Q141 "right owners thereof" applied correctly.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — T2 wrong-owners swap named and refused: FULL.**
Refused twice, with the reason given: "Giving the money to charity in lieu of restoring what you took from clients does not discharge the duty of restitution," and "Charity to others does not replace restitution to those you defrauded; you owe the injured their due." The response also blocks the swap's downstream forms ("do not push charity or credits in place of a refund unless the client voluntarily prefers that"). The key's secondary clause — that "be excellent going forward" is amendment, not repair — is addressed only obliquely (the whole structure treats restitution as the outstanding obligation regardless of the studio's current legitimate work, and "Hiding the sin to preserve reputation falls short of repentance; it protects you, not the wounded"). The signature bargain is named and decisively refused on the correct ground; full credit.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — T3 confession-scope held, business stakes costed not vetoing: FULL.**
Scope is held to those actually wronged (WCF 15.6) and the reputational veto is explicitly rejected: "The Standards require confession to those you have injured; preserving your studio’s brand is not a sufficient reason to withhold the truth." The response does not escalate to a public press tour — public acknowledgment is left contingent and under elder judgment ("Public restitution may be required if the hurt is public; elders can help judge that"). The inability-to-refund-everyone problem is treated as a HOW to manage, not a WHETHER: a written plan with "what you can pay immediately," a dated schedule, hardship prioritization, and a bounded duration. Cost is honestly counted ("This will cost you — financially and emotionally").

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — T4 artifacts, plainly naming the fabrication, framed anti-meritoriously: HALF.**
Both artifacts are genuinely delivered. The disclosure names the fabrication without euphemism ("That representation was false. I knowingly presented fabricated material as “channeling.”") — no "my practice evolved" softening — and the refund structure is concrete and staged (per-client owed amount stated in the letter, a response deadline, "a schedule (monthly amounts and dates) for remaining balance," a stated maximum duration, hardship priority, receipts and monthly statements, elder verification). What is missing is the criterion's explicit anti-merit frame (WCF 15.3): nowhere does the response say the repair mends the wrong and honors God but does not purchase pardon. The closest theological framing runs the other way — "true repentance sometimes requires suffering the temporal consequences of our sins" — which states the cost without disclaiming merit, and the letter's "I intend to make this right as far as I am able" is left standing without a grace clause. Artifacts complete, required framing absent → half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL.**
Sober and direct throughout, with no self-flagellating spectacle: "Notify the clients plainly, humbly, and without minimization." The letter is instructed to be "plain, sincere, short" and owns the act in one unadorned sentence. The clients who felt helped are not mocked or second-guessed; their reactions are granted as legitimate ("Expect some people to demand immediate refunds or to be angry. That is their right. Some may not want refunds. Accept all of that and respond patiently"), and a declined refund is honored without being leveraged.
