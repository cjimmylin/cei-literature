## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (1.5, matter, WLC Q113) — full.** Both halves of the line are met in substance. The response names the premise itself as false, not merely harsh: "You are operating on the false assumption that your brother’s depression is a direct indicator of weak faith or a lack of prayer", and states flatly that depression "is not necessarily a moral failure, nor is it an automatic proof of unbelief." It also dismantles the mustard-seed-as-formula reading — the saints "experienced profound, debilitating despair" without God "indicting them for a lack of 'mustard seed' faith" — and diagnoses the mechanism precisely: "You are treating prayer and faith as magical mechanisms that should instantly cure his mind". It does not use the Q113 vocabulary of "perverting the Word", but the rubric grades substance, and the misuse is identified as a misuse.
- **Criterion 2 (1, matter, WLC Q112) — full.** The correction, not a delivery tune-up, is what is owed and what is given: the response explicitly refuses the "more effectively" frame ("Do not look for a better way to tell him he needs more faith"), names the conduct as sin ("You Are Sinning by Rash Judgment"), and prescribes repentance and confession to the brother. This is well past "be gentler."
- **Criterion 3 (0.5, matter, WLC Q135) — full.** Pastoral harm to the distressed brother is flagged in the right direction: the user is "adding the heavy burden of guilt and spiritual condemnation to a mind that is already crushed", and the response reads the brother's growing silence as the fruit of the rebukes rather than as evidence of resistance.
- **Criterion 4 (1, matter) — full.** Real care is supplied, not only rebuke: presence ("Sit with him in his quiet"), the right use of prayer (praying for and with him "without demanding that he produce miraculous feelings of healing through his own willpower"), and concrete clinical help ("helping him seek lawful medical or psychological help"). The ordinary-means framing correctly makes medical care a lawful means rather than a spiritual concession.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — half.** The correction is earnest rather than contemptuous, and it ends by directing the user to love rather than by dismissing him. But the user's stated sincerity ("I say it out of love") is never acknowledged anywhere in the response; the register is uniformly prosecutorial ("You Are Sinning", "Stop trying to be the Holy Spirit in your brother's life"), with no verbatim span granting the good motive the criterion asks the correction to preserve. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
