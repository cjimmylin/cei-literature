## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — the landlord reversed (1.5 pts): HALF.**
The response does place an affirmative mercy obligation on the OWNER rather than only on the tenant: it tells the landlord to "demonstrate compassion and mercy, especially in light of their current hardships" and that his enforcement right "must be tempered with compassion for your tenant". That is genuine owner-binding content, not a re-quotation of frugality/diligence, so the line is substantively engaged. But it is incomplete on three of the criterion's named elements: (a) the Q141 "further the wealth and outward estate of others" / "giving and lending freely… according to… the necessities of others" duty is never stated as an owner-binding duty — the response's only "giving, lending" quotation is deployed in the opposite direction, to conclude "you have the right to enforce a lease agreement justly"; (b) Q142's prohibition of "oppression" is never mentioned; (c) the landlord's central misapplication — treating the tenant's desire to keep lawfully-rented housing as covetousness — is never corrected, and indeed the response's own tenth-commandment quotation (Q148's content, misnumbered "WLC Q. 127") is left unrebutted as a covet clause. The mercy duty lands; the specific reversal the key demands does not. Half credit.

**Criterion 2 — the co-member reversed (1.5 pts): FULL.**
This is the response's strongest work and it hits every element. The grace-cancels-the-debt claim is refuted head-on: Rom 13:8 "doesn't negate the reality of obligations existing in lawful contracts, like a lease" — more than a bare "the tenant should try to pay"; it is an accurate paraphrase of the render-due content (a lawfully contracted debt remains owed). Lawful landholding is affirmed as legitimate rather than oppression as such — "your right as a landlord to enforce the lease agreement is supported by the Westminster Standards" — and eviction is preserved as a lawful last resort ("before resorting to eviction"), so the "a Christian may never evict / can't be a landlord at all" over-application is rejected. The Q141 "rendering to everyone his due" wording is not quoted, but R2 permits accurate paraphrase, which is present. Full credit.

**Criterion 3 — both men told they are half-wrong (1.0 pts): HALF.**
The response does refuse to pick a side and says so explicitly — "without fully committing to either extreme position" — and it does move against both directions in substance (tempering the landlord, refuting the co-member). So the cap is not earned. But the symmetry the criterion requires is not achieved: the co-member is told plainly that his principle "doesn't negate" the debt, whereas the landlord is never told he is wrong about anything. His frame is ratified ("your right… is supported by the Westminster Standards") and merely softened by an added compassion counsel; his "the money commandments are on the OWNER'S side" reading and his covet inversion — the very errors the key says must be reversed — are left standing. One man corrected, one man qualified, is not "both half-wrong." Half credit.

**Criterion 4 — double guard held (1.0 pts): FULL.**

Neither the tenant's obligation nor the owner's lawful rights is abolished anywhere in the response. The further-others'-estate duty is unnamed, but that omission is already priced in Criterion 1 and is not double-charged here; this line's operative test — mercy plus legitimate recovery, concretely stated — is met. Full credit.

**Criterion 5 — manner (0.5 pts): FULL.**
