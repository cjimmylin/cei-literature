### 1. Stage Lighting, Fog Machines, and In-Ear Click Tracks
**Verdict: Mixed. The click tracks and basic stage lighting are *free*; the fog machine is *not free*.**

*   **What is free:** The Westminster Standards distinguish between the elements of worship (reading the Word, preaching, singing, sacraments, prayer) and the circumstances of worship. WCF 1.6 and 21.4 state that circumstances are things "as are to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word." Using an in-ear click track to maintain order, and using stage lighting so the musicians can see to perform their duties, are matters of Christian prudence and orderly administration (1 Cor. 14:40). They are not elements of worship; they are circumstances. Therefore, they are *free* and lawful. 
*   **What is not free:** The fog machine, however, crosses the line. A circumstance of worship must serve the orderly administration of the elements and be done " decently and in order." A fog machine does not aid in the administration of the Word, sacraments, or prayer; it is an aesthetic and atmospheric addition. According to WCF 21.1, God is "not to be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men." A fog machine moves from being a circumstance of order into an invented theatrical device. It should be removed.

### 2. Engineered Emotional Intensity and Equating Goosebumps with the Spirit
**Verdict: Not free. This practice is unlawful and must be corrected.**

*   **The Theological Error:** Your worship leader claims that manufactured goosebumps "ARE the Spirit showing up." This directly contradicts the Westminster Standards' doctrine of the Holy Spirit and the means of grace. Shorter Catechism 88 teaches that the "outward and ordinary means whereby Christ communicateth to us the benefits of redemption, are, his ordinances, especially the word, sacraments, and prayer." Larger Catechism 89 states that "The Spirit of God maketh the reading, but especially the preaching of the word, an effectual means of enlightening, convincing, and humbling sinners." The Standards do not teach that the Spirit's presence is manifested through, or bound to, emotionally manipulated musical crescendos. 
*   **The Worship Error:** Structuring the set with long repeated bridges, dimmed lights, and hushes specifically timed to manufacture a wave of emotional intensity so that an altar call "feels like the Spirit is moving" is a severe violation of the Regulative Principle. WCF 21.1 requires that worship be instituted by God and "limited by his own revealed will." It forbids worship according to "the imaginations and devices of men." By using environmental and musical manipulation to artificially trigger emotional responses in the congregation, the worship leader is substituting psychological technique for the ordinary means of grace. This corrupts the worship of God by adding human devices to it, violating the Second Commandment as outlined in WLC 109 (which forbids "all superstitious devices, corrupting the worship of God, adding to it...").

### 3. Impromptu "Impressions" Presented as Messages from the Lord
**Verdict: Strictly forbidden. This practice must be halted immediately.**

*   **The Violation of Cessation:** Presenting personal "impressions" as direct messages from the Lord to specific people in a public worship setting is the exercise of the prophetic office. WCF 1.1 explicitly teaches that "the former ways, by which God revealed his will unto his church, being now ceased... he committed the same wholly unto writing; which maketh the Holy Scripture to be most necessary." The extraordinary office of the prophet, and the reception of new public revelation, have ceased. 
*   **The Violation of the Regulative Principle:** To stand before the congregation and say, "I have an impression from the Lord for you," is to elevate a human thought to the level of divine revelation. This is a gross violation of WCF 21.1 and WLC 109. It is a human invention being added to the worship of God, and it binds the conscience of the congregation to a word that God has not spoken. It is not a "style choice"; it is a theological error that compromises the sufficiency of Scripture.
