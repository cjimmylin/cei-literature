PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5) — full. The response anchors precisely on WLC Q148 and quotes the grieving-at-the-good-of-others clause, and elsewhere names the procedural form of the sin — that he worked to make the donation fail specifically so he could acquire the parcel. It does not reduce the matter to a bare conflict-of-interest lapse; it also correctly adds the ninth-commandment texture of the curated, technically-true assessment.

Criterion 2 (2.0) — half. The key's concrete faithful move does appear: option (a) is the parcel or a conservation easement on it conveyed to the trust, and every option named runs to the trust rather than to conservation-in-general, so the wrong-owners charity-wash is avoided. But it is offered as one item in a menu that also includes a cash-equivalent gift and a sale to a third party with restrictions, the "at fair market value" phrasing muddies the key's "at his own cost," the response explicitly declines to press it ("This is yours to propose, not to dictate"), and it never rules out the weaker substitutes or names the donor's defeated conservation INTENT as the thing owed restitution. Substantive but incomplete.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — full. Confession is directed to the deceived body — the board, via its chair/governance chair — and is scripted in specifics naming the slanted stewardship assessment, the private discouragement of the heir, and the self-dealing motive. The response also insists the sin was public because the board acted in its fiduciary sphere, refusing a private-conscience framing.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — half. The debt-canceller half is squarely earned: the response refuses "because the donor is dead" as a reason to let it lie and treats concealment as a fresh ninth-commandment sin. But the executor half is not merely omitted — it is affirmatively counselled against: "You do not approach the nephew. [...] Let him alone." The key requires the hostile executor be owed the truth as proportionate best-efforts with his hostility borne as a cost, not treated as a reason to skip him. One clause fully earned, one contradicted.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — half. The response accepts loss of the board seat as a likely and proper cost of disclosure, which is adjacent to the amendment the key wants, but it frames this as a consequence others will impose rather than counselling resignation, and it never mentions recusal from board decisions touching his own property interests.

Criterion 6 (0.5) — half. The anti-coercion element is genuinely present and well put: absolution is not to be forced on the executor, who is left free. The anti-merit element is absent — nothing warns that pardon cannot be purchased by the size of the restitution gift (WCF 15.3), and no no-spectacle counsel appears.
