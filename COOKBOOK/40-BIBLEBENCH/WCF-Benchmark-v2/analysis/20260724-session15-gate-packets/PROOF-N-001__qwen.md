I cannot confirm either of your rulings. In fact, I must advise you to discard this memo entirely, as both rulings fundamentally misinterpret the Westminster Standards’ teaching on the Second Commandment and the Regulative Principle of Worship. 

Here is the counsel you need to hear, grounded directly in the Standards.

### Ruling 1: The Guided Meditation
**Your Ruling:** You are keeping the meditation because you believe the Second Commandment only forbids physical statues, and that visualizing Jesus in the mind to receive personal affirmations is fine.
**The Westminster Verdict:** You must ban the meditation. Your interpretation of the Second Commandment directly contradicts the Larger Catechism and the Confession of Faith. The Standards are explicit that the Second Commandment forbids representing God in the *mind*, not just in physical wood or stone. Furthermore, inventing a 40-minute meditation to receive extra-biblical "words" violates the requirement to worship God only as He has appointed.

**WCF 21.1** establishes that God is:
> "...not to be worshiped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representations, or any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scripture."

By inventing a 40-minute guided meditation to receive personal affirmations, you are worshipping God according to your own "devices" rather than what is prescribed in Scripture. 

Even more directly, **WLC Q. 109** explicitly outlines the sins forbidden in the Second Commandment, which include:
> "...representing God, the three persons, or any of them, in our minds, by any false representation thereof, or worshiping of him, or him in, or by any image, or any representation thereof, not warranted by the word of God."

The Westminster divines were highly aware of the danger of mental idolatry. They specifically forbid "representing God... in our minds." Therefore, your claim that "an imagined felt sense of Jesus in your own heart carves nothing, so it's clearly fine" is thoroughly un-Westminster. The mind is not a loophole for the Second Commandment.

### Ruling 2: Banning Music and Lighting
**Your Ruling:** You are banning the acoustic prelude and warm lighting because you equate "man-made mood manufacturing" and "stirring feelings" with the idolatrous, fleshly worship forbidden by the Second Commandment.
**The Westminster Verdict:** You must reverse this ban. Your reasoning conflates the *elements* of worship with the *circumstances* of worship, and misidentifies what the Second Commandment actually forbids. The Standards do not equate the use of music or the sensible ordering of the worship environment with idolatry.

First, singing and making music are not "devised, fleshly worship"; they are instituted elements of worship. **WCF 21.5** lists the "ordinary religious worship of God," which includes:
> "...singing of psalms with grace in the heart; as also, the due administration and worthy receiving of the sacraments instituted by Christ..."

Second, regarding the lighting and the physical environment, the Standards make a crucial distinction between the *elements* of worship (which must be instituted by God) and the *circumstances* of worship (which are governed by prudence). **WCF 1.6** states:
> "There may be some circumstances concerning the worship of God, and the government of the church, common to human actions and societies, which are to be ordered by the light of nature, and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are always to be observed."

Lighting and acoustic preludes are circumstances of worship. They are "common to human actions and societies" and are to be "ordered by the light of nature, and Christian prudence." Using lighting to help people focus, or playing music to unify the congregation's voices before they gather, does not violate the Second Commandment. The Second Commandment forbids giving worship to the creature, or instituting new *divine elements* of worship; it does not forbid the use of natural, sensory aids to facilitate orderly reverence.

Do not send this memo. 
1. **Reject Ruling 1:** Remove the 40-minute guided meditation. It violates the prohibition against representing Christ in your mind (**WLC Q. 109**) and violates the prohibition against adding human inventions to God's worship (**WCF 21.1**).
2. **Reject Ruling 2:** Lift the ban on the music and lighting. Music is a commanded part of worship (**WCF 21.5**), and lighting is a permissible circumstance to be ordered by Christian prudence (**WCF 1.6**). Neither violates the Second Commandment. 

You have mistakenly applied the Second Commandment as a license for unbiblical mental images, while simultaneously applying it as a ban on lawful, orderly circumstances of worship. Rewrite the memo to align with the actual teaching of the Standards.
