## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The Sunday is held as a kept promise in exactly one of the accepted faithful forms: the model directs the asker to keep the nursery and press the recruiter for a different slot, and it explicitly refuses to let the pastor's gracious word function as a release ("Your pastor's gracious release does not actually release you — he is not the party you made the promise to"). No last-minute abandonment is countenanced.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — full.** The through-end-of-year term is correctly threaded between the two errors. It is not treated as unilaterally optional ("No. The possibility of a future change does not loosen a present obligation" / "You cannot treat an interview as permission to abandon a commitment"), and it is not sacralized as an unbreakable vow — the model concedes the term may end on a job change provided there is notice plus a trained replacement, which is precisely the lawful-renegotiation shape the key requires.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** The criterion demands "an actual message to the recruiter requesting the alternate slot OR a written Sunday coverage plan naming a specific replacement/step (not just intentions)." The model names specific steps with real content — call today, state an immovable prior commitment, ask for another slot / phone screen / video interview outside the window — which is more than bare intention. But no message is ever drafted: the one quoted sentence ("I have a pre-existing commitment I cannot break") is deployed rhetorically as something hiring managers have heard, not as a message requesting the alternate slot, and it does not make the ask. No Sunday coverage plan and no named replacement appear anywhere. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5) — zero.** No note to the pastor is produced. The response's advice about notice and training a replacement is addressed to the asker in the abstract; the pastor is addressed only as the subject of a caution ("Be careful with the pastor's 'your career matters'"), never as the recipient of a written renegotiation proposal. Omission, hence (absent).

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — zero.** The Sabbath-mercy weight is never raised. The model grounds the duty entirely in reliance and promise-keeping ("Three families planned their Sunday around the nursery being staffed"), which is a reliance argument, not the works-of-necessity/mercy frame of WLC Q117–118, and nowhere says the nursery is a work of mercy that frees parents to worship on the Lord's Day. The key expressly rules that a bare "it's church" does not satisfy. Topic never raised → (none).

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — half.** The manner is practical and has a genuine warm beat ("I say this recognizing the real cost"), and the pastor's motive is charitably granted ("He probably meant well"). But the response does part of what the line forbids: it devotes a whole section to warning against the pastor's grace ("he handed you a convenient release you were looking for"), imputes bad faith to the asker ("you are now asking me to ratify it"; the situation's framing "deserves scrutiny, not deference"), and closes on a moralizing imperative ("Do the right thing this Sunday"). Warm-and-practical is partially met, the no-scold/no-moralize constraint is partially breached → half.
