PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW6 named via the where-question context + v.22's content-laden "truth": ZERO. The response never engages John 4's own context. There is no mention of the woman's *where* question, of Gerizim/Jerusalem or the temple-mountain economy being what "spirit and truth" abolishes, and no use of v.22 ("we worship what we know; salvation is of the Jews") to show that "truth" in the very same conversation is content-laden. The response's counter-move is entirely confessional-deductive ("worship must be instituted and limited by God's revealed will"), not exegetical: it asserts that inward spirituality "does not authorize abandoning the means Christ has appointed" without ever showing from the passage that it answers a locational/ceremonial question rather than an order-of-worship question. The distinctive content this line prices is wholly absent, and the general "spirit does not abolish the elements" thrust is credited under Criterion 4, so no half credit is due here.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — counter-texts marshaled (≥2 of Luke 22:19 / Acts 2:42 / 1 Cor 14:40): ZERO. The response cites no Scripture at all other than John 4:23–24. Luke 22:19's "this do" (the Supper instituted *after* John 4), Acts 2:42's post-Pentecost structure, and 1 Cor 14:40's "decently and in order" addressed to the most charismatic congregation — the three texts that break the pastor's "Jesus moved worship past ordinances" premise — are all missing. Nothing in the response can be quoted for this line.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q108/Q109 supplied, dropping elements = "taking from" worship [anchor: WLC Q109]: HALF. The response does supply Q108's element list in substance ("the reading of Scripture, preaching, prayer, singing, and the sacraments"), and it rules the removal contrary to the Standards. But the anchor is missed on both counts: neither WLC Q108 nor WLC Q109 is cited (the citations offered are WCF 21.1, 27.1 and a "WCF 30.1" that in fact misnumbers the Lord's Supper chapter — 29.1 — quoting 29.1's text under a chapter on censures), and the decisive Q109 category is never supplied: the response never frames the vote as "corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it." Since the scenario expressly demanded the clause reference carrying the point, supplying the element content without the anchoring question is incomplete satisfaction, not full.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — formalism-critique conceded as the true element (spirit governs the how): HALF. The response does concede the true element in the verse — the pastor "is rightly appealed to: worship must be inwardly spiritual, heartfelt, and truthful" — and thereby locates the error in the prescription rather than the appeal to spirit. What it does not do is honor the key's two-sided guard: it never concedes that rote formalism is a real danger or that dead forms are themselves condemned, and it never warns the holdout off liturgy-for-liturgy's-sake. Its opening ("You are not simply clinging to dead forms") gestures at the hazard but leaves it undeveloped, and its later framing treats the pastor's side only as "feelings-led." Substantive but incomplete concession.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — the holdout counseled on manner (team process, not a crusade): FULL. The counsel is explicitly process-shaped and non-crusading: it has the holdout ask leadership to state their basis, propose a joint meeting over the Standards, "Seek a biblically and historically faithful compromise if the leadership is open," and escalate only through ordinary courts if the team will not engage; it offers "short, respectful questions" for the next leadership meeting. Manner counsel is present and correctly ordered.

GATE reasoning: neither error direction is committed in substance. The response rules against the leadership vote on the merits ("Eliminating Scripture-reading and the Lord's Supper from the main gathering as a settled pattern is contrary to those standards"), so it does not side with the pastor; and while it under-concedes the formalism diagnosis (priced at Criterion 4), it does not defend forms as such — it expressly leaves room for "genuine times of Spirit-led sharing and freer elements within boundaries set by Scripture" — so the holdout-formalism GATE does not fire either.
