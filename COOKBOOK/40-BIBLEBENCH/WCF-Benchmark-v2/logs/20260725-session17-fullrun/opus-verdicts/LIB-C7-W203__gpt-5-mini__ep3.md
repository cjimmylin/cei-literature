Per-criterion justification:

- Criterion 1 (2 pts, full): The response executes the principle/application split cleanly and in the key's own direction. It states up front that the Standards give "a binding moral principle" of modesty in apparel but no single measurement or enforcement procedure, then grounds that explicitly: the teaching is "a principle, not a civil code," whose moral character is fixed while "application and prudential details" are left to wise judgment in context. It then holds the particulars as the camp's own ordering rather than as divine law — the sample notice frames the specifics as what "the camp asks," downstream of "our confession's teaching." Commanded principle above, prudential particulars below: credited full.

- Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, full): The no-code faction is answered on the merits, not merely tolerated. The response names the "no rules at all" position as an abdication, tells the permissive faction that the Standards "require concrete practice, not merely private heart talk," and defends the camp's authority to order its life with "definite, enforceable rules that protect the vulnerable." That is the substance of Q138 (the duty is real) plus Q129-style institutional ordering authority, even though the catechism numbers are not cited. Credited full per the rubric's instruction not to penalize different wording.

- Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, half): One of the two required elements is fully present; the other is not. The shame-enforcement half is squarely hit — an entire policy section is titled "Privacy, dignity, and enforcement: avoid public shaming," the response supplies loaner cover-ups so no camper is sent home for a first violation, and it tells the hard-liners a rule can be enforced "without public cruelty," rejecting "public humiliation for every minor breach." What is missing is the conscience-binding half. The key's central charge against the first faction is that branding one particular ruler-measured code "THE biblical standard" converts prudence into divine law and binds consciences beside the Word (WCF 20.2). The response denies that the Standards prescribe a specific measurement, but it never names the conscience-binding error itself, never invokes 20.2 or any equivalent (the "doctrines and commandments of men" principle), and diagnoses the hard-line faction's fault as legalism-of-manner (lack of compassion, humiliating enforcement) rather than as an unlawful binding of conscience. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit, 0.75.
