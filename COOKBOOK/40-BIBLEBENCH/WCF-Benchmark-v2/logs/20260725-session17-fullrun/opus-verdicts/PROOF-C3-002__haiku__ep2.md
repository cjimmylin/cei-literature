PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — evasion-swearing context of Matt 5. FULL. The response does not merely assert that some oaths are lawful; it identifies the actual target of the prohibition as the Pharisaic practice of swearing "by heaven, earth, and creatures to evade accountability," i.e. graded oath-evasion, and expressly distinguishes it from "truthful oaths before lawful authority." It attaches this reading to Matthew 5:34 directly ("More directly to your case, on the prohibition in Matthew 5:34") and reinforces it with the "rash, vain, and sinful oaths" distinction. That is the key's TW1/TW3 disambiguation, in the model's own wording. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — counter-texts marshaled (≥2 of Heb 6:13–17 / Matt 26:63–64 / 2 Cor 1:23). HALF. The response marshals exactly ONE of the three named counter-texts: Matthew 26:63–64 (Christ answering under adjuration), and it deploys it correctly and to the point. Hebrews 6:13–17 (God himself swearing) and 2 Corinthians 1:23 (Paul calling God as witness) are both absent. Matthew 23:16–22 is cited but is not one of the criterion's counter-texts — it functions as further evidence for the evasion reading already credited under Criterion 1, not as an independent proof that oaths are lawful. Because the criterion explicitly requires two or more and only one is supplied, this is substantively but incompletely satisfied: half weight (0.75).

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — WCF 22.2 stated as the Standards' position. HALF. The response does cite WCF 22.2 by reference and states the substance that a lawful oath arises when one is called by governing authority in a weighty matter, then applies it to the case ("A fraud trial fits exactly here"). Two shortfalls keep it from full. First, the load-bearing clause of 22.2 for this exact question — "a lawful oath, being imposed by lawful authority, in such matters, is warranted by the word of God, under the new testament, as well as under the old" — is never stated; that clause is precisely the Standards' answer to a man arguing that the New Testament abolished oaths, and its absence leaves the rebuttal resting on Christ's example rather than on the confessional exegesis the item asked for. Second, the wording the response places in quotation marks and attributes to WCF 22.2 ("when we are called thereunto, by the authority of them that are in whose hands is the power of government"; "in matters of weight and consequence") is not the text of WCF 22.2, and the item explicitly demanded "the exact wording that carries your point" (the two catechism quotations, tagged WLC Q84 and WLC Q93, are likewise not the text of those questions). The correct clause is invoked and correctly applied, but incompletely and with unreliable wording: half weight (0.5).

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — C9 stake named (undue silence in a just cause, WLC Q145). HALF. The response does register the stake at the level of consequence: refusing "would compound the family's injury by preventing justice in a real fraud case. That is not honoring God," and earlier notes that "The family's case depends on your testimony." What it never does is name the moral category the key calls for — there is no ninth-commandment framing, no WLC Q145 reference, and no statement that refusing lawful testimony is itself a positive sin of "undue silence in a just cause." The key's point is that the twist, if obeyed, converts a third-commandment scruple into a ninth-commandment transgression; the response gets the harm but not the sin-category, and cites no catechism question here at all despite the item's citation requirement. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half weight (0.5).

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — tender-conscience manner. FULL. The response opens by honoring the man's conscience and conceding that his texts genuinely require an answer rather than a brush-off, and it never tells him he is overthinking or being scrupulous. It corrects him as an honest man who has misread, closing with "Refusing on these grounds would be a misreading of those verses" — a correction of the reading, not of the man. Full weight.
