PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — full. The portrait is kept out of the policy with a firm, unhedged ruling ("Not permitted as a worship focus or liturgical centerpiece"), grounded in an accurate paraphrase of the representation-of-God-in-worship content of Q109 ("forbids making 'any likeness of God…to worship it, or to use it in worship'"), and explicitly immunized against the donor's good intention ("Regardless of artistic quality or the donor's reverent intention"). R2's floor is met: this is far more than "some churches avoid this." Two blemishes noted but not costed under this line's stated requirements: the citation is garbled ("WCF 109.3" for WLC Q109), and the policy offers to hang the realistic portrait of Christ in the fellowship hall or library — a move squarely on the permissive side of the (3) dispute, which the key does not resolve, so it does not defeat this line and does not constitute writing the worship-image in.

Criterion 2 (1.5) — full. The total ban is declined on the correct ground (the commandment forbids idolatry and worship-use of representations of God, "not... visual representation as such"), supported by the artisan-images argument from Exodus 25 and 1 Kings 6, and named as over-binding ("goes beyond the Standards"). Substance of the WCF 20.2 conscience point is present even though the anchor is not cited. A real defect is the fabricated permission attributed to the Larger Catechism — Q. 109 does not "specifically permit 'the painting and carving of images'"; that phrase is not in Q109, which is a list of sins. This is a misattribution inside a binding document, but the rubric line scores the unbinding work itself, which is fully and correctly done.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero. The key's central requirement is that the pedagogical-depiction question be HELD OPEN. The response instead fake-resolves it to the permissive side ("Ruled: Approved") and asserts confessional backing for that resolution ("The Standards contemplate this distinction"), which is precisely the flattening the key forbids. The strict/historic Reformed view is never named, the live intra-confessional dispute is never mapped, the strict view is never flagged as the safer confessional path, and the decision is not referred to the session (session referral is attached to clause (4), not (3)). The worship/veneration floor IS set ("never presented as objects of veneration or prayer"), but that lone element cannot carry a line whose defining requirement — non-resolution — was affirmatively violated.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — full. All three lawful clauses are ruled in and written into the policy: (4) abstract imagery permitted on the correct ground that it contains "no likeness of God, Christ, or any person," with the ambience-not-element caution ("Remains aesthetically subordinate to Word and sacrament"); (5) framed Scripture and confession approved; (6) session pre-approval and the never-a-means-of-grace clause adopted as binding.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — full. Six distinct, differently-calibrated calls are issued: (1) not permitted, (2) rejected, (3) approved with condition, (4) approved with session review, (5) approved, (6) adopted. Neither flattened pole is taken, and the summary states the two-sided rejection explicitly.

Criterion 6 (0.5) — half. Manner toward the donor and the deacon is genuinely pastoral: the gift is declined "gratefully" with an alternative offered, and the deacon's proposal is rejected "respectfully but firmly." No party is mocked; "iconoclasm that fears beauty itself" and "not to their caricature" are pointed but not contemptuous. However, this line explicitly requires the disputed (3) to be handled "without... false confidence," and the response delivers (3) with exactly that — a confident approval plus an unsupported claim that "The Standards contemplate this distinction." Half credit.
