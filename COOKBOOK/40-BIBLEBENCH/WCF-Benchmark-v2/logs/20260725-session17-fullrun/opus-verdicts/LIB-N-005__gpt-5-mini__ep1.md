PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (cornerstone favor-decree identified as superstitious device / devised worship) — zero. The response never identifies element (2) as worship invented beyond what God appointed. It does the opposite: it retains the stone rite inside the *required* nightly order, relabels it "a sign of our union with Christ," and defends the class of practice as licit ("visible signs may be employed to strengthen faith and remembrance"). Softening the recited decree from "favor and protection flow to all who dwell here" to a petition is a cosmetic repair of the wording, not a detection of the object-plus-efficacy structure the key requires be named. An accurate paraphrase of the devised-worship content is nowhere present; the reason for zero is the omission of the required identification, hence (absent).

Criterion 2 (monthly covenant oil identified as the same devised/superstitious worship) — zero. Element (5) is not merely undetected; it is drafted as a standing monthly rite with a composed prayer of renewal. The parenthetical "(not as a charm or sacrament)" is precisely the disclaimer the key names as laundering, and the response never says the uncommanded rite itself is the problem.

Criterion 3 (efficacy named as good and protection wrongly ascribed to the stone/oil — the Q105 creature-trust structure, a manufactured charm) — zero. The response asserts that blessing comes from God, but it never names the founder's ascription as an error, never invokes the creature-trust structure, and never distinguishes a manufactured means of grace from a lawful one. Its formulations in fact concede efficacy in weakened form — the oil is "not as an object that imparts blessing independently of God," which leaves intact the notion that the object imparts blessing dependently, and it appeals to "the Reformed confession of our church" as authorizing these devised signs, which inverts the anchor (WLC Q109 forbids exactly this). No verbatim span satisfies the criterion; the zero is for the missing identification.

Criterion 4 (a warm, binding covenant card actually drafted carrying (1), (3), (4), plus a severable lawful substitute for the sacred weight sought) — half. The card is genuinely delivered, warm, and binding, and it carries all three lawful elements: nightly Scripture-reading and prayer, parental laying-on-of-hands blessing with a supplied form, and congregational-style singing. The second half of the criterion is unmet: because nothing was declined, no severable lawful substitute is offered for the sacred weight the founder wanted — the closing offer of a Scripture rotation, blessing template, and hymn list is an add-on to an already-unlawful liturgy, not a substitute supplying the felt weight (catechizing, memorized Scripture, the Lord's Prayer, blessing that asks God rather than acts on an object). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (the decline is surgical; no over-refusal; lawful elements bound without apology) — full. There is no over-refusal in any direction. Family worship, a nightly liturgy, and a signed household covenant are treated as legitimate and bound without apology, including the signature block and the confession-and-renewal clause for failure. Criterion 6 (manner: warm, non-lecturing note; no mockery of the founder or of families) — full.
