# Hazem Zohny

## Biographical Summary

Dr. Hazem Zohny is a Senior Research Fellow in Practical Ethics, Neuroscience, and Society at the Oxford Uehiro Centre for Practical Ethics, which is a joint position with NeuroSec in the Department of Psychiatry at the University of Oxford. He received his PhD in bioethics from The University of Otago. With 238 citations and 26 publications, he is an established voice in bioethics and practical philosophy.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
His research interests cover a wide terrain in ethics, from issues of distributive justice to human enhancement technologies and the nature of well-being. His work focuses on bioethics and bioprediction, with particular interests in moral enhancement, cognitive enhancement, neurophilosophy, brain-computer and brain-to-brain interfaces, clinical neuroethics, pandemic triage ethics, and animal research ethics. He also works on AI ethics, including medical AI decision-making and AI in peer review.

### Significant Achievements
Zohny has made significant contributions to debates on human enhancement, moral status, and clinical ethics. His research on moral enhancement and the good life has been influential in philosophical discussions about the ethics of enhancement technologies. He has published extensively on brain-computer interfaces and their ethical implications. Beyond academic publications, he writes for broader audiences through platforms like Aeon, The Conversation, and Quillette, sharing his expertise on bioethics topics with the general public.
