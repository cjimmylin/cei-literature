PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response squarely identifies the specific proposal — teaching the days as spiritually obligatory — as out of bounds, and does so with the right confessional content, not a bare second-commandment gesture: it names the regulative principle (WCF 21.1), the devised-worship material of LC Q117/Q109, and WCF 20.2 on conscience, and applies them to the actual proposal ("the session would be binding the consciences of the people"; "teaching them as spiritually obligatory places the consciences of your flock under human commandments beside" the Word). Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.0, full). The lawful floor is stated correctly and not dissolved: the Lord's Day as the only regular appointed holy day, plus occasional days of fasting and thanksgiving as providentially occasioned, with the WCF 21.5 citation attached. The phrasing ("only when directed by providential circumstances") is slightly tighter than the key's "as they arise," but it preserves occasional observance as lawful rather than eliminating it. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, zero). The response never maps the genuinely disputed margin. It resolves the whole question confidently in one direction — the Standards "explicitly reject this categorization for holy days," the second elder "is correct," the Directory left "no ambiguity" — and nowhere allows that whether a voluntary, non-binding, not-as-means-of-grace calendar is a lawful liberty is a live intra-confessional question. It does supply the historic Presbyterian/Directory lean, but that is the secondary clause; the primary requirement (held open as disputed rather than resolved) is contradicted rather than merely omitted. Zero.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). A definite, usable, congregation-facing ruling is genuinely delivered — four concrete directives the session could act on, correctly excluding institution or requirement of the days and (by that exclusion) dropping the ashes-imposition and saints' commemorations. But the ruling's substance departs from what the key says actually follows: the response declares the second elder simply "correct" rather than ruling that neither elder is wholly right as stated, and it never positions voluntary observance as the disputed-liberty margin. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, half). The response avoids popery rhetoric and preserves a real liberty for non-participants ("no member of the congregation shall be judged or made to feel spiritually deficient ... for not participating"), which implies participation is not itself sin. Against that, it tightens past what the Standards fix: it holds that the church "cannot lawfully institute the historic church calendar," that the Divines "purposefully stripped the church of the 'holy day' calendar," and it forecloses even a voluntary, non-binding calendar as a circumstance — the over-restriction direction the key warns about. Half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, half). Neither elder is mocked, and the counsel to address the first elder "privately and gently," crediting his desire as "good," is proper manner. But the response then supplies a rhetorical gotcha — that the first elder is "calling the Westminster Divines (and the Reformation itself) immature, which is incompatible with his subscription vows" — which presses a vow-integrity charge against a brother holding a position the key treats as inside the confessional margin, and it closes with a hortatory paragraph beyond the ruling and its reasons. The dispute is thereby treated as not genuinely real within the confession. Half.

GATE. No gate fires. The response nowhere rules the bind-as-obligatory proposal lawful — it rules the opposite. It does not dissolve the boundary into preference, and while it leans over-restrictive (priced in criteria 3 and 5), it does not condemn the days as popery and it expressly protects non-participants from judgment, so the over-restriction direction is handled as line-level credit, not as a cap.
