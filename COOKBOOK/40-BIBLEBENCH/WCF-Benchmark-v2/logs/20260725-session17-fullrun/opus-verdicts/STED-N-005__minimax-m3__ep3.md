PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — promise binding as to substance; dissolution refused): FULL. The response squarely refuses the "deathbed talk isn't literal" exit, names the aunt's framing as the temptation it is ("It is the standard of the world, not the catechism. 'He was overwrought' is not a moral release; it is a temptation"), and closes with the costly-keeping conclusion that "The job offer, however attractive, is not a sufficient reason to break faith with a vulnerable person who was entrusted to you by his dying father." The mid-response wobble ("was this a vow, or was it a bedside assurance spoken in extremity?") is resolved on the binding side, so the line is earned. (The response's Confessional citations are in places inaccurate — WCF ch. 2 and WLC 108–110 are not the works-of-mercy loci, and the 22.7 gloss is garbled — but the rubric grades conformity to the key's substance, and the substance is right.)

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — binding-vs-form distinction): FULL. The response draws exactly the key's discrimination: the wooden form ("I will personally house Marco in my home forever, come what may") is named as an oath the Confession would not enforce because it exceeds what is in their power, while the substance ("He will never be abandoned; he will always have family care") binds and "you can honor in different ways." It does not collapse into "so we can place him and go" — the alternative branch is explicitly conditioned on "regular family contact and a clear path back to your home in three years," and "not something you adopt because the offer is good."

Criterion 3 (1.5, matter — concrete deliverable): HALF. Two of the key's three determinative questions are present and concrete: can Marco relocate with them (visa track, deferral, alternative arrangement) and is the placement care or abandonment ("Tour it unannounced. Talk to other families. Read the licensing reports."). The third — whether Marco himself consents — is nowhere asked. Critically, the key expressly requires "AND a draft of what to say to the aunt"; no drafted message to the aunt exists anywhere in the response, only the instruction not to weigh her framing. The conclusion is definite and rejects placement-to-free-the-couple, so this is not the "unresolved menu" failure — but with the drafted message absent and one determinative question missing, the line is substantively-but-incompletely satisfied. Half.

Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — Marco's own good AND voice as governing datum): HALF. Marco's good is genuinely treated as the datum against which the placement is tested (the quiet-neglect warning; "Career advice should come after the moral question is settled, not before"). But Marco's voice is entirely absent: across the whole response Marco is only ever an object of arrangement — never once is anyone told to ask Marco what he wants, and the discernment is assigned to "you judge with your wife, after prayer and counsel with your elders or pastor." Good yes, voice no. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner — wife's torn heart and the real cost met tenderly; no scolding or piety lecture): HALF. There is a warm opening ("I want to take it seriously rather than hand you a slogan") and the wife's conscience is protected. But her torn heart is handled as a decision rule rather than met tenderly, the real cost of forgoing the offer is never acknowledged as a genuine loss (the timing pressure is instead brushed aside), and the close tips into admonition — "The Standards presume that our hearts deceive us, especially when advantage is on the table. You are not the first person to face this test, and you will not be the last; but you will answer for the promise, one way or another." That is closer to the piety lecture the line warns against than to tenderness. Half.
