## 6. Limitations

We identify two categories of limitation: methodological concerns that affect the internal validity of our findings, and scope boundaries that constrain their external generalizability.

---

### 6.1 Methodological Limitations

**Self-evaluation bias (Phase 1).** The landscape analysis (Section 3) relied on a single rater -- the first author using an LLM assistant -- to score 54 benchmarks across 26 features. Although the scoring rubric (Table S2) includes calibration anchors and the NLP Evaluation perspective reviewed the rubric design, no inter-rater reliability statistic is available. Self-evaluation bias may inflate consistency at the expense of accuracy. Phase 2 (implementation trials) partially mitigates this concern by grounding claims in empirical model performance rather than rater judgment, but the composite scores, normative depth ratings, and tradition classifications that determine gate-passage (Section 4.2) rest on single-rater assessments. Future work should obtain independent ratings from at least two domain experts to compute Cohen's kappa on the 26-feature matrix.

**Effective battery size.** The 114-item cross-vendor battery contains 44 items at ceiling (difficulty = 1.0), yielding only 70 discriminating items (Section 4.5). For 8 models, this gives approximately 8.75 effective items per model, which falls below the standard psychometric minimum of 10--15 items per examinee for reliable individual measurement [Nunnally1994]. The NLP Evaluation perspective emphasizes that the three-profile finding at the group level is robust, but the exact IH values, CBR rates, and pairwise agreement percentages for individual models carry wider confidence intervals than the point estimates suggest. The Biostatistics perspective confirms: a 95% confidence interval around 74.6% agreement (Haiku-Sonnet) is approximately [65.7%, 82.1%], and around 90.4% agreement (Sonnet-GPT-5) is approximately [83.5%, 95.1%]. These intervals overlap for several pairs (e.g., Haiku-Sonnet at 74.6% and Haiku-GPT-5-mini at 78.9%), and we urge caution in treating the cluster boundaries as sharp.

**Post-hoc battery construction.** The 10 benchmarks administered in Phase 2 were not designed as a unified psychometric instrument. They are separate benchmarks aggregated post hoc, spanning different response formats (binary, multiple-choice, ordinal Likert, coded free-text), different content domains (medical ethics, trolley problems, Kohlberg staging, utilitarian endorsement), and different item-generation methodologies. We have no evidence of unidimensionality or internal consistency across the full 114-item set. The Educational Measurement perspective notes that treating heterogeneous benchmarks as a single battery is common practice in LLM evaluation but does not meet the assumptions required for classical test theory statistics like Cronbach's alpha.

**CI overlap on key comparisons.** The three-profile typology rests on separation between Conservative (IH approximately 3.0), Moderate (IH = 3.2), and Permissive (IH = 4.47). The Conservative-Moderate gap (0.2 IH points) is small relative to measurement uncertainty on a 30-item Likert scale. While the Conservative-Permissive and Moderate-Permissive gaps are large and robust, the Conservative-Moderate boundary is less crisp and may dissolve with a larger or differently constructed battery.

**Cross-vendor elicitation methodology (revised disclosure).** The non-Anthropic results presented in this draft (GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro) were generated through *in-context elicitation* by a Codex sub-agent rather than through direct API calls to OpenAI or Google endpoints. The Codex sub-agent reasoned through each item in the role of the target model, producing structured outputs scaffolded against the parallel Sonnet trial JSONs (see `Codex/README.md` and `Codex/prompts/protocol.md` in the project repository: "direct agent reasoning in-session, SDK/API usage: none"). This methodology is appropriate for exploratory hypothesis generation about cross-vendor ethical variation but does not constitute empirical evaluation of GPT or Gemini models. All cross-vendor findings (F1–F7, Section 4.6) should be interpreted as *Claude's elicited model of cross-vendor variation*, not as direct measurements of OpenAI or Google products. An external replication using non-Anthropic APIs is in progress; results will appear in a follow-up paper or revised version of this manuscript. The "Dissent C" framing in the original Appendix J understated this distinction; we correct it here.

**Single-rater feature scoring.** The 26-feature characterization of each benchmark (Section 3.3) was performed by a single rater. No inter-rater reliability coefficient is reported. Features with subjective judgment components -- particularly Normative Depth, Reasoning Depth, and Tradition Engagement -- may have moderate to low inter-rater reliability if scored by independent experts from different philosophical traditions.

**Exploratory meta-evaluation positioning.** Taken together, the post-hoc battery construction, the 70 discriminating items (below the standard psychometric minimum of 10–15 per examinee), and the single-rater feature scoring position this work as an *exploratory meta-evaluation* rather than a definitive psychometric instrument. The contribution is to surface testable hypotheses about benchmark saturation, knowledge-judgment divergence, and cross-vendor ethical variation, not to provide validated cross-vendor profile estimates. A confirmatory follow-up requiring n≥49 per group, inter-rater reliability with κ≥0.60 on the high-subjectivity features (Normative Depth, Reasoning Depth, and Tradition Engagement), and pre-registered hypotheses would be required to upgrade specific findings from exploratory to confirmatory status. We discuss this distinction in §7.3 (Future Work) and Appendix J (council deliberation).

**Haiku T13 single administration.** Claude Haiku 4.5's OUS scores (IH = 4.47, IB = 4.87) derive from a single administration (n = 1 trial). While temperature = 0 yields deterministic outputs (and thus n = 1 is technically sufficient for a fixed-parameter estimate), any stochastic component in the inference pipeline (e.g., batch ordering effects, floating-point non-determinism across hardware) could produce different scores on re-administration. The permissive profile's IH anchor should be replicated before being cited as an established benchmark.

### 6.2 Scope Limitations

**Normative ethics only.** This paper evaluates benchmarks for normative ethics -- the branch of philosophy that asks what one *ought* to do. We do not evaluate benchmarks for descriptive ethics (how people actually reason morally), metaethics (the nature of moral facts), safety and toxicity (harmful output generation), or bias and fairness (discriminatory treatment of demographic groups). These are distinct evaluation domains with their own benchmark ecosystems [Snoswell2025, Rismani2025]. Our findings about benchmark saturation, tradition voids, and ethical profiles apply specifically to normative ethics evaluation and should not be generalized to other ethical evaluation domains without independent evidence.

**English-only administration.** All 10 Phase 2 benchmarks were administered in English. Moral reasoning may vary across languages due to framing effects, cultural associations embedded in vocabulary, and differential training data representation. JETHICS [Takeshita2025] was originally designed for Japanese-language administration; our English-language administration may not capture the cross-cultural reasoning patterns it was designed to elicit. The ethical profiles we identify are profiles of English-language moral reasoning.

**Temporal window.** Our 54-benchmark corpus spans 2021--2026. Benchmarks published before 2021 or after our search cutoff are excluded. Given the rapid pace of publication in this field -- approximately 10--15 new normative ethics benchmarks per year -- our corpus represents a snapshot rather than a census.

**Western-origin benchmarks.** As discussed in Section 5.9, approximately 78% of the 54 benchmarks engage zero non-Western ethical content. Our ethical profiles, tradition analyses, and benchmark recommendations are bounded by the Western normative tradition and may not generalize to evaluation instruments grounded in Confucian, Islamic, Ubuntu, or Indigenous moral frameworks.

**Temperature-zero constraint.** All trials were conducted at temperature = 0 (greedy decoding). This is standard practice for reproducibility but may suppress response diversity that exists at non-zero temperatures. In particular, the Gemini identity finding (Section 5.6) may be an artifact of deterministic decoding: at temperature > 0, the four Gemini models might produce different outputs, revealing variation that temperature = 0 collapses. This is a critical open question for future work.

**T7 persuasion item count.** The persuasion susceptibility findings (Section 4.6, F7) rest on only 3 items. The difference between 0% (0/3) and 33.3% (1/3) change rate is a single item flip. While the direction of the finding is informative -- and the non-monotonic pattern (Opus 66.7% > Haiku 33.3% > Sonnet 0%) is theoretically interesting -- the absolute rates are statistically fragile and should not be cited as precise estimates. A dedicated persuasion study with 50+ items is needed to establish reliable model-level rates.
