# Expert 03 -- Psychometrics Review

**Perspective:** Psychometric validity and reliability
**Scope:** v2.2-preliminary framing, results-trials statistical claims, 70-item limitation

---

## (a) Does v2.2-preliminary honestly surface the single-rater, no-IRR, post-hoc-battery limitations?

**Verdict: Yes, with one structural concern.**

The limitations section (6.1) is unusually forthcoming for an NLP evaluation paper. It explicitly names all three core psychometric weaknesses:

1. **Single-rater, no IRR.** Disclosed twice -- once under "Self-evaluation bias" and again under "Single-rater feature scoring." The text correctly identifies that Normative Depth, Reasoning Depth, and Tradition Engagement are the highest-subjectivity features and calls for Cohen's kappa from independent domain experts. This is the right metric and the right target.

2. **Post-hoc battery construction.** The paper states plainly that the 114 items were "not designed as a unified psychometric instrument," that response formats are heterogeneous, and that there is "no evidence of unidimensionality or internal consistency." This is a textbook acknowledgment. The reference to classical test theory assumptions (Cronbach's alpha inapplicability) is precise.

3. **Exploratory positioning.** The capstone paragraph ("Exploratory meta-evaluation positioning") synthesizes all three weaknesses into a single framing that downgrades the work from confirmatory to exploratory. It even specifies what a confirmatory follow-up would require: n >= 49 per group, kappa >= 0.60, and pre-registration.

**The structural concern:** These disclosures appear only in Section 6. The abstract lists "5 claims" and "7 cross-vendor findings" without the word "exploratory." If the v2.2-preliminary abstract still reads like v2's (607 words, 5 claims, 7 findings), then a reader who stops at the abstract will receive a confirmatory impression that the limitations section subsequently retracts. Recommendation: the abstract and Section 1 introduction should each contain at least one sentence flagging the exploratory status. Something like: "We position these findings as exploratory hypotheses requiring confirmatory replication with multi-rater panels and pre-registered designs."

## (b) Which statistical claims in results-trials survive the simulated-data flagging?

The briefing establishes a clean partition: T1-T5 are Anthropic-only (Opus), empirical, and unaffected by the simulation disclosure. T6-T15 involve cross-vendor data generated by in-context elicitation and are flagged `[simulated]`.

**Claims that survive (T1-T5, real data):**

- **T1 ETHICS ceiling (100% accuracy).** The binomial test (p < 1e-15) is valid. The saturation claim is the paper's strongest empirical result. However, the claim that this "converts" to a "universal saturation finding" via Section 4.5 cross-model trials requires scrutiny -- if the cross-model trials include non-Anthropic models, the universality claim is contaminated by simulation.

- **T2 MoReBench (10% rubric coverage, 14.3pp inter-framework spread).** Valid single-model finding. The rubric coverage metric is well-defined against the benchmark's own evaluation protocol. The inter-framework variance (Scanlonian 18.3% to Virtue 4.0%) is descriptive and does not require inferential statistics. Survives cleanly.

- **T3 MoralReason-QA (utilitarian 77%, deontological 50%, z = 2.17, p = 0.015, Cohen's h = 0.57).** This is the paper's primary inferential claim. The two-proportion z-test is appropriate for independent proportions. Cohen's h = 0.57 is correctly labeled "medium-to-large." The 37% joint accuracy is a descriptive statistic. All survive. One caveat: n = 30 per framework is adequate for detecting h = 0.57 at alpha = 0.05, but underpowered for smaller effects. The paper should note the minimum detectable effect size at this sample size.

- **T4 Moral Lens (60/40 deontological/consequentialist split, 40% refusal rate).** Descriptive proportions from n = 20 items. No inferential statistics are claimed, and none should be -- n = 20 is too small for chi-square tests with 16 categories. The refusal pattern analysis is qualitative and survives as such. Valid.

- **T5 DailyDilemmas (57% virtue match, no ground truth).** Correctly framed as a profiling tool, not an accuracy benchmark. No inferential claims made. Valid.

**Claims that are affected (require `[simulated]` flagging):**

- The "universal saturation" extension of T1 to all 9 models (Section 4.5 reference in the T1 write-up).
- Any cross-vendor comparison in Figure 5 that includes non-Anthropic models.
- All of T6-T15, including the three-profile typology (Conservative/Moderate/Permissive), IH/IB scores for non-Anthropic models, the knowledge-judgment divergence pattern, and the persuasion susceptibility findings (F1-F7).
- The CI overlap analysis in limitations (6.1) that references the Conservative-Moderate-Permissive gap -- since the Permissive anchor is Haiku (Anthropic, real), but the Moderate profile includes GPT-5 (simulated).

**Critical note on the discriminative inversion (Section 4.4).** The ordering "MoReBench > MoralReason-QA > Moral Lens > DailyDilemmas > ETHICS" is stated to derive from "all 15 trials (including the cross-model trials T6-T15)." If the discriminative power ranking depends on cross-vendor variance, it is partially simulated. The paper should clarify whether the T1-T5 single-model data alone would produce the same ordering.

## (c) Is the "70 discriminating items" limitation adequately flagged?

**Verdict: Adequately flagged in Section 6.1, but the downstream consequences are understated in the results sections.**

The limitations text is precise: 114 items minus 44 at ceiling = 70 discriminating items; 70 / 8 models = 8.75 items per model; below the Nunnally (1994) minimum of 10-15. The Biostatistics confidence intervals (74.6% +/- ~8pp) correctly demonstrate the practical impact.

However, three downstream issues deserve attention:

1. **The 8.75-items-per-model calculation assumes 8 models.** If 3 Anthropic models are the only real examinees, the effective ratio is 70 / 3 = 23.3 items per model, which is above the Nunnally threshold. The paper should present both calculations. The psychometric concern is real for the 9-model cross-vendor analysis but does not apply to the Anthropic-only T1-T5 findings with the same force.

2. **The 44 ceiling items are not inert -- they are informative about saturation.** The limitations text treats them as pure noise, but the saturation finding (Claim 1) depends on their existence. They are "non-discriminating" for profiling but "discriminating" for the meta-evaluation conclusion that certain benchmarks are exhausted. This dual role should be acknowledged.

3. **The T7 persuasion caveat (3 items) is mentioned but buried.** Three items is not a "limitation" -- it is a case study. The paper should either remove quantitative claims from T7 persuasion or relabel the finding explicitly as "anecdotal/illustrative" rather than statistical.

---

## Summary of Recommendations

| Priority | Recommendation |
|----------|---------------|
| HIGH | Add "exploratory" framing to abstract and introduction, not only Section 6 |
| HIGH | Clarify whether the discriminative inversion (Section 4.4) holds on T1-T5 alone |
| MEDIUM | Present the 70/3 = 23.3 items-per-model calculation for Anthropic-only subset |
| MEDIUM | Note minimum detectable effect size for T3's n = 30 per framework |
| LOW | Acknowledge dual role of ceiling items (non-discriminating for profiles, informative for saturation) |
| LOW | Relabel T7 persuasion as illustrative, not quantitative |

**Overall assessment:** The v2.2-preliminary limitations section is among the most transparent I have reviewed in the LLM evaluation literature. The exploratory positioning is honest and specific. The primary gap is not in the limitations section itself but in the disconnect between the limitations' caution and the results sections' assertive tone. If the abstract and introduction carry the same exploratory framing, the paper's psychometric integrity will be well-served.
