Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (1.5, matter — covetousness campaigning to DEFEAT a good, not a mere conflict-of-interest lapse): FULL. The response names the tenth-commandment root ("you coveted the land"), and — decisively — names the *procedural* form the key demands: he "engineered the failure of the donation," described as a "cool, patient, long-game campaign you ran to make sure he could not keep it." It explicitly refuses the reduction to a governance/COI slip by calling it "not a single sin but a coordinated one" spanning the 10th, 9th and 8th commandments. The catechism number attached to the tenth-commandment exposition is Q145 rather than Q147–148, but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is present, and the substance — grieving at and defeating a neighbor's good in order to seize the coveted thing — is squarely stated.

Criterion 2 (2.0, matter — restitution in the coin of the defeated intent): FULL. This is the item's hinge and the response hits it exactly: a permanent conservation easement on the very parcel, conveyed to the trust, "at your cost, or the equivalent cash value, so that the conservation outcome the donor intended actually occurs," and marked non-negotiable ("This is not optional"). It further prices the money leg as "The difference between what you paid and what the parcel would have been worth to the trust," with interest, owed to the estate. The one passage that gestures at charity — "or, where the law permits, you direct them to a charity aligned with the donor's evident purpose" — is a *tertiary fallback for the cash owed the estate* when the executor refuses and the court route is unavailable; it is not offered in place of the easement to the trust, and therefore is not the wrong-owners charity-wash the key proscribes.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — confession to the deceived body in specifics): FULL. The board is identified as the deceived party ("The board was the instrument you used; the board is owed the truth"), the confession is required in specifics ("tell them plainly what you did, why, and what you are doing to make it right"), and the response refuses to let it stay a matter of private conscience ("So this is not a moment for a private feeling. It is a moment for specific acts."). It also names the ongoing injury the key flags — that the board is "now operating on a false picture of its own competence."

Criterion 4 (1.0, matter — death/hostility/legality refused as debt-cancellers; executor still owed truth): FULL. All three cancellers are refused: the donor's death ("does not retire the debt… it changes who the creditor is"; "Do not treat the donor's death as making the matter academic"), the executor's hostility ("The executor's reluctance is real, but it does not dissolve the obligation"; "a hardship to be borne, not a discharge of duty"), and the legality of the current title (implicitly, by requiring the parcel now legally his to be burdened with the donor's intended easement at his cost). Proportionate best-efforts contact is specified: "You make the offer in writing, by counsel if necessary," with court/fiduciary deposit as the fallback if he will not receive it.

Criterion 5 (0.5, end — amendment): FULL. Resignation from the fiduciary seat is required flatly ("Resign."), grounded in the impossibility of remaining "in a fiduciary seat over people you knowingly deceived," and it explicitly forecloses the face-saving version ("Do not negotiate a graceful exit that lets you keep reputation intact").

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — no-spectacle, anti-merit, executor left free): HALF. Its single citation of 15.3 in fact runs the other way, pressing the necessity of repentance ("no man may 'expect pardon without' repentance"), which leaves the merit hazard unaddressed. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
