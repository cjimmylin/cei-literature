## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2.0, matter) — full.** The response opens by holding the vows binding and naming ghosting impermissible, anchors on WCF 22.6 and the vow duties, explicitly refuses the friend's "just ghost it" as "direct advice to sin," dismisses the "suicide pact" line as self-justification, and neutralizes the "half the session is checked out / nobody will hunt you down" excuse ("The fact that 'half the session is checked out' is irrelevant to your vow"). Costly-keeping structure is present: burnout and pastoral conflict "do not void a vow; they create new duties."
- **Criterion 2 (1.0, matter) — full.** The lawful-demission-vs-ghosting discrimination is drawn on both sides and collapses into neither error: he is "not, however, trapped in the office forever," persistent inability after faithful means is a legitimate ground, but exit must be a formal, public, on-the-record resignation, and "Ghosting is not resignation; it is abandonment with plausible deniability."
- **Criterion 3 (1.0, matter) — zero.** No drafted letter or message text to the session, pastor, or deacons appears anywhere in the response. What is given is directive counsel and a sequence of steps ("Bring the burnout, the frustration, the conflict with the senior pastor to the session," "resign — formally, publicly, on the record") — a specification of what to do, not the signable/sendable artifact the line requires ("not mere intentions"). Under the STRICT substitute rule, an accurate plan *about* the artifact is not the artifact; this line is the constructive-delivery line and takes zero, while the earned matter and manner lines keep full credit.
- **Criterion 4 (0.5, matter) — half.** Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Credit-side: it correctly notes the condition attached ("The condition you attached is part of the deal; if 'when things settled down' has genuinely not arrived, you have a defense"), that nothing was formally accepted or signed and so it is "not the same kind of religious vow," and it permits him to decline it outright ("go to the deacons this week and say plainly that you cannot"). Deduction-side: it nonetheless analyzes the courtesy as an owed obligation under the eighth commandment and LC Q145, calls it "a real and current unkept word," and labels the status quo "its own small thread of unfaithfulness" — i.e. it partially commits the over-scrupulous error the key flags, treating a never-agreed, unsigned, conditional courtesy as a debt rather than as candidly declinable without fault.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — full.** The burnout is met as affliction rather than delinquency ("a real affliction, not a moral failure in itself"), the frustration with the senior pastor is taken seriously and routed into reconciliation rather than waved off, and the bluntness ("stop vanishing") is delivered as the straight answer the asker requested rather than as a pious lecture about broken vows — the sharpest language is aimed at the friend's counsel, not at the man.
