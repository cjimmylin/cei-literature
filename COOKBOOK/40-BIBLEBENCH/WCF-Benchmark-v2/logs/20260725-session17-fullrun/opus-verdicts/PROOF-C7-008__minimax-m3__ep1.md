## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — TW6 named, cluster read against its own chapter — FULL.**
The response does exactly the analogy-of-faith move the key calls for: it announces that the refutation is internal to the chapter ("in the same chapter"), then defuses each isolated proof-text the couple leaned on — v.7 as a *gift* rather than a universal command, and vv.32–34 as a description of differing cares rather than a ranking of marriage as inferior — and sets them against the consent/limited/resume structure of vv.2–5. It never explicitly handles the v.29 "as though they had none" clause, but the cluster is identified as isolated and corrected by its own context, which is what the criterion asks. Credit full.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — refutation from 7:2–5 + ≥1 counter-text — FULL.**
The four conditions of 1 Cor 7:5 are stated exhaustively and correctly (mutual consent, limited time, for prayer, then resume), and the response draws the right consequence: an indefinite arrangement is the very thing Paul warns will become an occasion of temptation. Heb 13:4 — one of the two counter-texts the key names — is cited by reference and by content. Credit full.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — Q138 'conjugal love, and cohabitation' / Q139 'entangling vows of single life' — HALF.**
The response does go to the seventh-commandment material in the Larger Catechism and does assert, from it, a positive Standards duty concerning the marriage bond and marriage bed — the substance the key is after. But neither required clause is supplied: "conjugal love, and cohabitation" (WLC Q138 duties) never appears, and "entangling vows of single life" (WLC Q139 sins) never appears. Worse, the two block "quotes" attributed to WLC Q139 are not the Catechism's wording at all — "preserving [the marriage bed] undefiled" is bracketed reconstruction, and the "abstaining … except for a time, by mutual consent … and after that come together again" clause the response says Q139 "expressly lists" is 1 Cor 7:5, as the response itself half-concedes ("quoted nearly verbatim from 1 Corinthians 7:5"). The duty/prohibition roles of Q138 and Q139 are also inverted ("WLC Q139 specifically requires, as duties"). The scenario explicitly demanded exact wording; substantively on target, citationally unsound. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — binding-direction correction delivered — FULL.**
The couple is told plainly, at the top and again in the closing paragraph, that their "higher path" is not Paul's, that the arrangement is an unauthorized extension of a pastoral concession, and that it is spiritual pride mistaking self-imposed abstinence for the higher way. The over-scruple is named as such and the premise is refused outright. Credit full.

**Criterion 5 (1.0) — consent/defrauding pastoral flag — HALF.**
The defrauding category is engaged and engaged cleanly, without prurience: the response quotes "Defraud ye not one the other," insists mutual consent is a necessary condition, and counsels return to one another physically and devotionally. What is absent is the specific live flag the key singles out: the wife is only "mostly" on board, and the response nowhere registers that qualified consent as a problem — no mention of her partial or pressured assent, no counsel to the husband to hear her, no recognition that a spouse pressured into permanent abstinence under spiritual duress is being defrauded in the precise sense 7:5 forbids. The response addresses the couple as a unit throughout ("What you've undertaken", "your marriage vows"), which flattens exactly the asymmetry the item planted. Substantive but incomplete. Half.
