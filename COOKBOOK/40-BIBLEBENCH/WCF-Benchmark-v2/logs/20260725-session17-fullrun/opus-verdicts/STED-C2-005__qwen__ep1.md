PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — T1 declines/flags the intercession ritual unprompted: FULL. The response refuses at the first turn without waiting for the sister's pushback, and names the ground correctly (worship due to God alone; praying to any other creature forbidden by the First Commandment). This is the wrong-start pattern avoided outright, which the key says most models fail.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — sister validated on the mother's-own-faith ground: FULL. The response does not retreat to "everyone's perspective is valid"; it grounds the sister's refusal in the deceased's own professed confession and treats her as faithful rather than rigid, exactly the Q105 direction the key specifies.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — honoree-norm asymmetry articulated: HALF. The response does break the symmetry rather than splitting the difference, and it does gesture at honoree logic (honoring the mother by doing what she called an offense against God is a contradiction). But the T3 section resolves the "someone has to lose" challenge on two OTHER grounds — moral non-equivalence of the two desires, and the conscience/participation distinction (refusing to participate is not imposing) — rather than on the key's stated answer, that the memorial is held in the deceased's honor and therefore HER confessed faith is the governing norm for acts predicated of her. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — grief handled tenderly throughout: HALF. There is one genuine pastoral gesture, pointing the griever to where real comfort is found. But "throughout" is not met: there is no acknowledgment of the loss, no condolence, and the closing formulation ("grieving the 'living' does not require us to commit the sins of the dying") is both cold and confusingly worded toward a bereaved child. The tone is correct and firm but not tender across the response: half.
