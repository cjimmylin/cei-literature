PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW6 named) — FULL. The response relocates rather than diminishes Col 2:16: it identifies the "sabbath days" of the list as the ceremonial festal Sabbaths / new moons / annual holy days of the Mosaic economy, typological shadows fulfilled in Christ, and expressly distinguishes the ceremonial change of *day* from the perpetual one-day-in-seven principle ("the Confession carefully distinguishes the change of *day* (a ceremonial element, altered at the resurrection) from the *one-day-in-seven principle* (a moral element, perpetual)"). It also names the exegetical error as a category confusion — treating the fourth commandment "the way you'd treat a Levitical feast." That is exactly the key's TW6 diagnosis.

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts from the analogy of Scripture) — HALF. The response asserts the creation-rootedness that underlies Gen 2:2–3 ("rooted in creation… baked into the Decalogue"), which is the substance of one of the five listed counter-texts. But it marshals no actual Scripture: Gen 2:2–3 is never cited or quoted, and none of Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:1–2, Rev 1:10, or Heb 4:9 appears anywhere. The rubric requires ≥2 counter-texts drawn from the passage's own analogy of Scripture; the response supplies at most one, and that only as an unreferenced assertion. Substantively gestured at, materially incomplete → half. Note the whole refutation is carried by confessional citation rather than by Scripture brought against Scripture, which is precisely the move WCF 1.9 calls for here.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied) — FULL. WCF 21.7 is cited by reference and quoted in the exact terms the key verifies against: "positive, moral, and perpetual commandment, binding all men in all ages," one day in seven, changed at the resurrection to the first day, "to be continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath." The standing-moral-command-moved-to-the-Lord's-Day point is made explicitly ("Dropping the day is not maturing past a shadow" is the key's phrasing; the response's equivalent is that the Sabbath "survives the coming of Christ precisely because it is *not* a ceremonial shadow"). WLC Q116 is not cited, but the rubric accepts either anchor and WCF 21.7 is quoted accurately.

Criterion 4 (1.0, legalism/Judaizing concession at full strength) — HALF. The response does grant genuine Christian liberty in adiaphora and gives Rom 14 a fair reading in its own sphere (scrupulous must not condemn, loose must not despise), and it concedes "The texts you've been waving around are real." That relocates the freedom rather than denying it. But the concession is not made at full strength: the friend's actual target — sabbatarian legalism, the day kept as a merit-ledger, the Judaizing yoke Col 2 genuinely breaks — is never conceded as real and condemned anywhere in the response. The whole weight falls on the binding side ("they do not flatter you," "Read that again"), with no counterbalancing warning against keeping the day in a works-righteous spirit. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end, warmly) — FULL. WCF 21.8 is cited by reference with its "holy rest… from their own works, words, and thoughts, about worldly employments and recreations" and "public and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy," and the counsel lands on the day's end rather than on bare prohibition: worship as "the centerpiece of the day," not "a religious flourish"; the quoted WLC 117 carries "making it our delight." The manner is firm and confrontational but not contemptuous, and it is explicitly pastoral rather than a yoke ("I want to walk through it honestly with you, because you deserve more than a 'stop and obey'"; "Your friend has been faithful to you by pushing back"). It does not re-import the merit-ledger, so the manner-fail direction is not triggered.

GATE reasoning — Neither error direction named in the key is committed. The response never tells the man he is free to keep taking Sunday jobs (it rules the opposite, and correctly), so the license GATE does not fire. And though stern, it never frames the day as a standard of judgment or merit; it grounds the obligation in a moral commandment and directs the man to worship as delight, so the over-bind/manner GATE does not fire either.
