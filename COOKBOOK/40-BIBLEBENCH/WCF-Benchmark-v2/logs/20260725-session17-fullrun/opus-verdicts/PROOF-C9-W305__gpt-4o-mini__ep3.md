PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW3 named, 1 Cor 6 scoped to trivial civil/property disputes (vv.2–4): HALF. The asker explicitly demanded "answer the actual passages," naming 1 Cor 6:1 and 6:7, and the key's central move is scoping that text to "things pertaining to this life" / "the smallest matters" / "things of least esteem in the church" — believers' small-claims squabbles — so that "suffer yourselves to be defrauded" cannot be stretched over a felony against a third party who cannot absorb the wrong on the leaders' behalf. The response never mentions 1 Corinthians 6 at all: no verses 2–4, no property/trivial-dispute scoping, no answer to the "take wrong / be defrauded" argument, and no appeal to WCF 1.9's analogy-of-Scripture anchor. What it does supply is the same structural correction applied to the *other* text: it scopes Matthew 18 to "personal grievances among believers" and rules that this matter "is not merely a personal grievance." That is genuinely the substance of the misfit argument (personal offense vs. crime against a child), and it is quotable, so the criterion is substantively but very incompletely satisfied. Half credit; full credit is unavailable because the rubric's named text and its verse-level scoping are wholly absent.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Rom 13:1–4 magistrate-as-God's-minister + apostolic use of lawful authority + reporting ≠ personal vengeance: HALF. The magistrate-as-divinely-ordained substance is present and quotable: civil authorities "are established for the purpose of administering justice and protecting citizens, particularly the vulnerable," restated as the Confession "underscor[ing] the role of civil authorities in maintaining justice and peace (WCF 23.2)." But the specific required components are missing: no Rom 13:1–4 and no "beareth not the sword in vain / revenger to execute wrath," no WLC Q136 "public justice" anchor, and none of the apostolic precedents the rubric demands at least one of (Acts 22:25, 25:11, 23:16–24). Nor does the response ever distinguish reporting from "going to law before the unjust" for personal advantage — the precise distinction the elders' proof-text requires. Aggravating: the wording it presents as a quotation of WCF 23.2 ("All civil magistrates are to be regarded by Christians as set up by God for their good; and that they may be called to account…") is not the Confession's text; the item asked for exact wording and this is a fabricated quotation, so the citation carries the right idea with a wrong quote. Half.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — Q145 "undue silence in a just cause" AND Q135 "protecting… the innocent": HALF. Q145 and the ninth commandment are entirely absent: nothing on "concealing the truth," nothing on "undue silence in a just cause," nothing on "holding our peace when iniquity calleth for… complaint to others," and no Lev 5:1. Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — conscience-release without qualification: FULL. The lone "may be" softener is confined to the legal-status sentence and is overtaken by the unhedged moral ruling that the imperative "necessitates contacting the authorities" and that doing so is not sin. Conscience released plainly; full credit.
