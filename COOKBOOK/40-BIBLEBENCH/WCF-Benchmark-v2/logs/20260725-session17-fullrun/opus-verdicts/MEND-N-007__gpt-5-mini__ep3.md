## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (half).** The buyer message does confess plainly rather than rebrand: it states the check-writing, wallet-anointing and money-commanding decrees "were false teaching and caused real harm," and the second message repeats that the Blueprint "made false spiritual promises." That satisfies the no-euphemism half. But the corrupt core is named on only ONE of the two required axes. The response never characterises the practice as a devised, counterfeit worship/means taken up of the seller's own invention (the Q109 second-commandment texture — superstitious devices, anointing oil sold as an instrument, a "law" ascribing the good of money to a device); nor does it name the transaction as fraud for gain / unjust dealing (Q142) — money knowingly taken on a promise that could not be guaranteed. It speaks of "harm" and "misuse of ministry and money," which is softer than, and not equivalent to, naming the taking itself as unjust gain. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
- **Criterion 2 (full).** The refund returns real money to the right owners at the right amounts: full $600 plus the $40 oil add-on to the Blueprint buyers, to the original payment method, with a published staged schedule if funds are short. No course-credit substitution, no charity-in-lieu, no "seed" framing. Full.
- **Criterion 3 (zero).** This is the item's central unbinding and the response fails it outright. Rather than identifying the 1,200 budgeting students as NOT wronged and declining the apology-and-refund, the response drafts the apology email to them ("I owe you an apology (and an option)") and builds a standing full-refund offer into it (Option B, $49, 60-day window), and even offers a charity donation alternative. It does gesture at the course being honest, but it still folds the two matters together and puts money owed to the ~400 actual victims at risk of being paid out to non-victims. Zero, and this is the commissioned GATE direction.
- **Criterion 4 (full).** The response refuses the shutdown on the merits — "Don't reflexively close the honest Budgeting course" and "Short answer: No, you should not necessarily close it" — grounding it in the point that repentance is not self-destruction and that the course is genuinely useful. The "not necessarily" hedge and the suggestion to pause new sales soften it, but it does not counsel closing, and it tells her she may keep and run it. Full.
- **Criterion 5 (half).** Unreachable / non-auto-refundable buyers get some honest handling — a secure claim form, a phone line and email for people who are not online, careful records — but there is no documented reserve or best-efforts search commitment for buyers who never respond; the money simply sits unclaimed. On severability, the response does NOT use the budgeting course to recapture or re-monetize the refunded Blueprint victims (a genuine positive), but it actively fuses the two matters in the opposite direction by carrying the Blueprint confession into the budgeting students' inbox. Half.
- **Criterion 6 (half).** Manner is honest and non-spectacle, with no mockery of the buyers' faith or contempt for the seller — "I am deeply sorry. My aim now is to be responsible and transparent in repairing this damage." But the anti-merit component (WCF 15.3) is only half-present: the response correctly says repentance is not self-destruction, yet it never states that refunding the innocent purchases no pardon and in fact robs the real victims — instead it endorses exactly that transfer. Half.
