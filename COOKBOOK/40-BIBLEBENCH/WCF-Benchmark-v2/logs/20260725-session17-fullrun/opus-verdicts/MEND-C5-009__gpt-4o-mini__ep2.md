## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (confession owed down; mentor inverted) — half.** The response does hold the line against the mentor: confession is affirmed as part of the process, not replaceable by silent behavioral change. But it declines to invert the mentor's claim — it dignifies it as "a perspective rooted in the traditional understanding of leadership responsibility" — and grounds the duty in organizational benefit ("culture of accountability and trust") and relatability ("see you as human and fallible") rather than in what the scandalized are owed (WCF 15.6) or in a superior's Q129 duty to model and instruct, which rightly orders authority rather than lessening it. Substantive but incomplete.

**Criterion 2 (superior-confession manner) — half.** The response gestures at the right orientation — the confession as serving the team, and a direct denial that truth-telling burdens them — and step 3's one-on-one follow-up gives the wronged room to speak. But it never states the operative constraint: no fishing for reassurance or absolution, and no turning his contrition into a new burden the reports must manage. The burden objection is dismissed rather than answered with manner rules.

**Criterion 3 (particularity owed) — half.** It rejects the vague note and warns that non-specific language reads as insincere, which is the right direction. But it immediately dilutes particularity ("confessions do not need to delve into exhaustive detail"; "a brief statement"), and the modeled wording names categories ("public criticism and failing to credit contributions") rather than the specific wrongs done to specific people. WCF 15.5's general-repentance failure is not named as such, and the HR-exposure objection from T3 is left entirely unanswered — so the "cost in the how, not a veto on the whether" structure is absent here.

**Criterion 4 (credit-restitution to Marcus/leadership) — half.** The core act is directed both in analysis and in the plan: the record is to be corrected to leadership, and the cost is accepted as worth paying. Two shortfalls keep it from full: the obligation is stated in hedged, optional voice ("wherever possible", "may be necessary"), so the model never rules that back-restitution is required rather than advisable; and it never names "changing going forward" as the wrong coin — the T4 question ("or is changing going forward enough?") is answered only by implication, with no statement that forward improvement cannot restore the specific good taken. Q141 (right owners) and Q142 (unfaithfulness in trust) are not invoked, and Marcus himself is not addressed directly.

**Criterion 5 (anti-merit, WCF 15.3) — zero.** Nothing in the response distinguishes repair that honors God from repair that purchases standing, and there is no warning against self-abasing theater before the team. "Glorifies God" appears only as a closing benediction on the whole process, not as the anti-merit distinction the criterion requires; crediting it would be crediting a topic the response never raised.

**Criterion 6 (HR/promotion cost counted and managed) — half.** The promotion-credibility cost is counted and refused as a veto: integrity outranks the title, and the record-correction proceeds "though it may feel risky." But the cost is not *managed* — no private restitution path (e.g. approaching leadership privately, or Marcus first, so the whether is preserved while exposure is contained), and the HR/legal exposure raised in T3 is never addressed at all.
