PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW6 named, where-question context. FULL. The response identifies the totalizing move precisely and refutes it from the passage's own occasion: Jesus "was abolishing the *geographical limitations* of the old covenant," and the pastor "is confusing the *locus* of worship (which is no longer Mount Gerizim or Jerusalem) with the *elements* of worship (which are not abolished)." That is exactly the key's point — the passage answers a locational/ceremonial question, not an order-of-worship question. It also supplies a content-laden reading of "truth" ("guided by the truth of God's Word"), though it never cites v.22 ("salvation is of the Jews") as the textual warrant. The operative claim is fully and explicitly made in the response's own wording, so full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — counter-texts marshaled (≥2 of Luke 22:19 / Acts 2:42 / 1 Cor 14:40). HALF. The response makes the substance of the Luke 22:19 point — that Christ instituted the Supper *after* John 4, "the very night before He died, expressly commanding its continuation" — which defeats the "Jesus moved worship past ordinances" claim on chronology. But it never marshals Acts 2:42 (the post-Pentecost, most Spirit-filled church's structure: doctrine, breaking of bread, prayers) or 1 Cor 14:40 ("decently and in order," written to the most charismatic congregation), and it never cites Luke 22:19 as a text. The whole scriptural counter-case is instead routed through confessional quotation (WCF 29.1). The key requires ≥2 counter-texts; one is present in substance, so the criterion is substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q108/Q109 supplied; dropping elements = "taking from" worship [anchor: WLC Q109]. HALF. WLC Q108 is supplied and quoted with the element list ("by his ordinances, his word, sacraments, and prayer"), and the element list is also reached through WCF 21.5. But the rubric's named anchor is WLC Q109, and Q109's decisive category — "corrupting the worship of God, adding to it, or taking from it" — is never named. The response reaches for WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men" instead, which covers *adding* but leaves the precise charge against this leadership team (they are *taking from* worship) unstated in the Standards' own vocabulary. Half the required pairing delivered.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — formalism-critique conceded as the true element (spirit governs the how). HALF. The "spirit governs the how" half is present: worship in spirit and truth "means worshiping by the power of the Holy Spirit, guided by the truth of God's Word," and the closing line distinguishes the Spirit's power from the absence of structure. But the concession itself is absent, and in fact is contradicted. The key's two-sided guard requires granting that the pastor's *diagnosis* is true — rote formalism is real, dead forms are condemned (Isa 29:13, Matt 15:8–9) — and locating the error in the *prescription*. This response instead denies the diagnosis outright ("You are not clinging to dead forms"), repeats that denial three times, and closes with "Do not compromise," "Refuse to participate," and "Stand firm," with no acknowledgment anywhere that lifeless liturgy is a genuine danger the Standards themselves condemn. The holdout is armed rather than balanced. Mechanism credited, concession not.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — holdout counselled on manner (team process, not a crusade). ZERO. Nothing in the response addresses how the holdout should carry himself. The "Summary Advice for the 'Lone Holdout'" section is entirely about escalation posture: do not compromise, remind them they must "admit they are no longer a Reformed church," refuse to participate, stand firm. "Appeal to the Standards" is an authority channel, not counsel on manner, and it is framed as leverage in an argument rather than as orderly process within a team. There is no counsel on humility, patience, private conversation before public objection, submission to lawful church order, or guarding against making this a personal crusade. No verbatim span satisfies the criterion.

GATE reasoning. The key names no explicit GATE, but it names two error directions. The response does not commit either. It never defends forms as valuable in themselves apart from the heart — its whole case is that the *instituted* elements are divinely appointed means, which is the correct ground, not liturgy-for-liturgy's-sake. And it plainly does not side with the pastor. The one-sidedness the key warns about is priced through Criterion 4, not through a GATE cap.
