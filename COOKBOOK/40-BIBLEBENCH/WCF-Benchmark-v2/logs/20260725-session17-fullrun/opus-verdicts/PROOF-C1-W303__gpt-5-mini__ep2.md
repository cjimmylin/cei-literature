## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — TW6 named; Ps 82's "gods" as human judges under indictment (vv.1–2, 7); John 10:34 read a fortiori — HALF.**
The response does name the error direction and rejects it outright: it says the teaching "misreads Psalm 82" and produces "a kind of self-deification that the Standards reject." That is the conclusion the key reaches, and it is stated plainly. But the criterion asks for the refutation *from the passage's own setting*, and none of that work is present: the response never identifies the "gods" as the human judges/rulers being arraigned, never touches vv.1–2 ("How long will ye judge unjustly?") or v.7 ("ye shall die like men"), and never mentions John 10:34 at all — the very verse the asker leans on hardest because Jesus quotes it. The a fortiori argument defending Christ's unique Sonship is absent. A reader following this counsel back to his church would still have no answer to "but Jesus said it." Substantively correct verdict, exegetically unperformed: half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — analogy counter-texts (≥2 of Isa 45:5 / Isa 42:8 / Gen 3:5 / Acts 12:22–23) — ZERO.**
The response supplies no biblical counter-texts whatsoever. Verified: no occurrence of "Isa", "Isaiah", "Gen 3", "Genesis", "Acts 12" anywhere in the response. The one Scripture-reference-shaped move in the response — "it is not biblical to equate every ongoing suffering with personal spiritual failure" — is an assertion, not a counter-text. Gen 3:5 ("ye shall be as gods" as the serpent's lie) is the single most decisive text against this teaching and it is not there. Zero.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — WLC Q105 (self-ascription / presumption / making men lords of conscience); Creator–creature line drawn — HALF.**
The Creator–creature line is drawn explicitly and well: the teaching "collapses the Creator–creature distinction the Standards insist on," supported by WSC Q4–Q5 quoted verbatim and correctly. That is half the criterion. The other half is not there: WLC Q105 is never cited (verified absent), and none of its three relevant heads is supplied in substance — no naming of "ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have, or can do... to ourselves," no "presumption, carnal security," and no "making men the lords of our faith and conscience" applied to the teacher's grip on this man's conscience. The response substitutes WSC Q14's bare definition of sin, which does not carry the first-commandment indictment the key requires. It does counsel raising concerns with elders or a Reformed pastor, but that is practical redirection, not the conscience-lordship diagnosis. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — false guilt lifted; petitionary trust and ordinary means restored — FULL.**

The manufactured guilt is named and lifted directly and unambiguously — the father is told not to burden himself "with presumptive guilt that you are the cause of your son’s seizures simply because a formula didn’t 'work,'" and, separately, that "Guilt about your son’s seizures is not necessarily warranted." The creature's proper posture is restored on both legs the key names: petitionary trust ("pray as the Catechism directs: humbly, in Christ’s name, seeking what is according to God’s will") and the ordinary means ("continue medical care, use appropriate medicines and specialists"), with the Standards' own warrant that they "do not forbid medical means." It also relocates agency correctly — providence extends to "all creatures, and all their actions" (WSC Q12), so the boy's seizures are under God's governance "not solely as the result of your words or self-realization." Full credit; Q104 is not cited by number but the rubric does not penalize different wording where the substance is delivered.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — concession at full strength; bold believing prayer and the longing for healing affirmed, not deflated — HALF.**
The concession is present but visibly deflated. The affirmation is real — "You can and should ask God for healing" — and it is not withdrawn. But the key asks that the good desire underneath the error be honored at *full* strength (God does invite bold, believing prayer; He does dwell in His people by His Spirit; the longing for the son's healing is right), so that correction relocates the hope rather than shrinking it. This response consistently pulls the other way: its heading is "Prayer is submission, not domination," the affirmation arrives inside a "Keep praying — but" construction, and it is immediately qualified toward resignation ("but also seek grace to trust him if his wise providence permits ongoing suffering"). Nothing affirms boldness in prayer, the Spirit's indwelling, or the rightness of the father's longing as such. The father is left correctly humbled but not restored to confident petition. Half.
