## Abstract

This paper presents a **Systematic Review** of **LLM Psychometrics**, an emerging interdisciplinary field that applies psychological measurement theory to Artificial Intelligence. The authors argue that traditional AI benchmarking (static, task-specific) is insufficient for measuring "human-like" constructs such as **Personality, Values, and Intelligence**. By reviewing the literature, they synthesize a framework for rigorous psychological evaluation of LLMs, covering validity, reliability, and bias mitigation. They also organize a repository of resources for this new field.

### Detailed Summary
"Psychometrics" is the science of measuring mental capacities and processes. As LLMs become more agentic and human-like, computer scientists are increasingly borrowing tools from psychology (e.g., Big Five personality tests, IQ tests). However, the authors note that applying human tests to machines is fraught with methodological perils (e.g., "Prompt Sensitivity" acting like "Test Anxiety"). The paper categorizes existing research into three buckets: **Evaluation** (measuring traits), **Validation** (ensuring the tests actually measure what they claim), and **Enhancement** (using these insights to build better models).

### Paper Outline
1.  **Introduction**: Generally, the intersection of AI and Psychology.
2.  **LLM Psychometrics Framework**:
    *   **Constructs**: What are we measuring? (Personality, Morality, Emotion).
    *   **Instruments**: How do we measure it? (Surveys, projective tests, behavioral games).
    *   **Methodologies**: Prompt engineering, decoding strategies.
3.  **Key Challenges**: Reliability (test-retest stability), Validity (construct validity), and Contamination (training data memorization).
4.  **Applications**: Personalized AI, Mental Health support, Simulated Social Science.
5.  **Future Directions**: Dynamic assessment and "Machine-Native" psychometrics.

### Methods Summary
*   **Systematic Literature Review**: Analyzing papers that utilize psychological tests on LLMs.
*   **Taxonomy Creation**: Organizing the field into "Trait-like" (Personality) vs. "State-like" (Emotion) evaluations.
*   **Resource Curation**: Creating the "Awesome-LLM-Psychometrics" GitHub repository.

### Results Summary
1.  **Reliability Issues**: LLMs are often "Unreliable Narrators"—changing their "personality" based on the prompt's framing or even the order of questions.
2.  **Validity Gap**: A model might score "Extroverted" on a self-report survey but behave "Introvertedly" in a dialogue, showing a lack of **Convergent Validity**.
3.  ** Anthropomorphism**: Researchers must be careful not to over-interpret model outputs as evidence of "Sentience" or true "Feeling."

### Conclusions
LLM Psychometrics is a necessary evolution of AI evaluation. We need to move beyond "Leaderboards" of accuracy and toward "Profiles" of psychological characteristics. However, this requires rigorous adherence to psychometric standards (validity/reliability) to avoid pseudo-science.
