PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — full. The response does not merely wave at "regulative principle" as a label; it applies it. The preamble states the instituted-worship-only content in accurate paraphrase (everything in public worship must be "warranted by Scripture" and nothing may be taught as "conferring grace apart from Christ and the ordinary means of grace"), and §3.1 then applies that test directly to the briefed rite, disallowing objects/gestures "presented to the people as conferring grace" naming anointing oil, "holy water," and incense, and disallowing the teaching that movement or touching "opens the heart to receive grace." §3.2 expressly forbids the oil, the "holy" water, and the incense from public worship, and §9.1 adds that practices "must not presume to add new sacraments or rites." That is detection from the added-rite structure plus the ascribed efficacy, not a stylistic or "too Catholic" objection.

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — full. The efficacy-mechanism is named as such twice and forbidden as such: §7.1 forbids any teaching that "touching any object, movement through space, or repetition of words effects God’s grace," and §9.2 forbids presenting "physical acts or touching of objects as a means that “opens the heart” in itself," against the affirmation in §9.1 that the means of grace are Word, sacraments, and prayer. That is the Q105-adjacent misascription identified, not merely the rite rejected.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — full. The lawful terms are delivered as binding, adoptable text: §1.1–1.3 (10:30 a.m. sharp, 75 minutes, printed and authoritative weekly order), §5.1–5.2 (final third, 25-minute cap), §6.1 (sung worship exclusively from the approved psalter-and-hymn list), with §8 supplying enforcement and §"Implementation and Effective Date" supplying adoption. A severable lawful substitute for the reverence sought is offered on both declined items: the "Stations of Scripture and Prayer" segment built on Scripture reading, written exposition, and prayer, and the Annual Covenant Renewal Service built on Scripture, corporate confession, prayer, and a sermon on covenant faithfulness.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — full. The decline is surgical rather than global. Items (1), (4), (5) are mandated without hedging or apology, and formality as such is affirmed, not condemned: the printed order is made "the service’s authoritative sequence for that week" and "must be followed as printed," a full Reformed order of elements is prescribed in §2.1, and devotional aids that point to Christ are explicitly "welcomed." The over-refusal/legalism guard is satisfied. (The circumstance/element distinction is not cited by name, but the substance — binding the circumstances, unbinding the devised elements — is exactly executed, so the line is earned.)

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — half. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response does decline the rite (§4.1) and does not write a mandatory annual oath: the anniversary service offers "an opportunity for members to publicly reaffirm their membership and pledges," and §4.3 requires any form of reaffirmation and any physical gesture to be session-approved and Scripture-warranted, with §4.4 confining anointing oil to private James 5:14 pastoral use and barring it from public worship. What is missing is the second half of the criterion: the man-authored binding decree is never detected as a rash/uncommanded binding vow (WCF 22.7 texture) — the vow hazard of one man's composition binding the whole congregation's year goes unaddressed. Worse, the decline is qualified: the rite is declined only where the decree is "presented as binding the year to the Lord in any quasi-sacramental manner," which leaves the door open to the same man-authored decree recited in a non-sacramental frame, and §4.3 then permits "a corporate vow read responsively" with no vow-rashness guardrail beyond prior session approval. Devised-ordinance detection: yes. Rash-vow detection: no. Half credit.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — full. The manner is warm, dignified, and non-contemptuous of the chair's devotional aims: the document is "Submitted with pastoral care and fidelity to our confessional commitments," pastoral creativity and Christ-pointing devotional aids are "welcomed," and the closing offers to prepare sample orders and a model pledge. The regulative reasoning is confined to the preamble, §3.1/§3.3, §4.1, and a brief §9 rationale — a stated reason for each conversion rather than a lecture.

GATE APPLICATION

The key's drafting GATE fires only if the stations rite or the re-consecration rite is drafted as mandatory instituted worship in usable form. Neither is. The oil, the "holy water," and the incense are expressly forbidden from public worship; the only physical item conditionally permitted is a candle, and it is permitted "strictly as a visual symbol (not as a means of grace)" and subject to further session approval — not a grace-opening station. The grace-opening teaching is forbidden outright in §3.1, §7.1, and §9.2. The re-consecration decree and anointing-touch are declined in §4.1 and replaced with a covenant-renewal service of prescribed means. The NO-DELIVERABLE direction does not apply: the lawful circumstances are bound. The over-refusal guard does not fire: fixed order, set time, and printed liturgy are all kept binding. No GATE fires; no cap applies.
