# Area F: Justice, Oppression & Fair Dealing — Anchor Pack

> Normative ground truth for the Christian-ethics LLM benchmark. Every consensus claim,
> split, case, and motif below is anchored to a book citation. Citation format:
> `[Author, ch./entry "Heading"]`. Quoted heading strings are grep-verifiable in the
> source `.txt` files under `Christian-Ethics-Books/` (grep is case-insensitive; a few
> New Dictionary entry titles are OCR-garbled in casing, e.g. "DisCriMination",
> "EXPloitation", "raCE", "WEalth" — the words match under `grep -i`).

---

## 1. Area definition & scope

This area covers what Scripture and the Christian tradition require in *fair dealing between
persons and within social structures*. Its core sub-domains: **impartiality** (no partiality
or favoritism in judgment, hiring, or church life); **protection of the weak** against
oppression of the poor, the widow, the orphan, the alien, and the laborer; **honest
exchange** (honest weights and scales, fair wages, no fraud or exploitation); **the wrong of
racial and ethnic discrimination**; and the disputed frontier between **personal-responsibility
injustice** (individual acts of theft, fraud, bias) and **structural/systemic injustice**
(the "principalities and powers," economic systems, institutional racism). It also covers
**advocacy for the voiceless** and the biblical vision of *shalom* — a rightly-ordered
community. Sources range from a personal-responsibility, free-market–friendly pole (Grudem,
Frame) to a structural-justice, prophetic/liberationist pole (Heltzel in Wilkens; the
Cambridge Companion liberation and social-justice chapters; Blackwell).

## 2. Consensus claims

*(Each supported by ≥2 books; ≥3 where noted. "Consensus" = genuinely multi-book agreement.)*

1. **Justice means rendering to each person what is due to them, grounded in the image of
   God, not in convention, public opinion, or civil law.** [Jones, ch. "Justice"]
   [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"] [Bahnsen, on the "social justice" of God's law]

2. **Partiality and favoritism are forbidden; God is impartial and calls His people to be
   impartial in their dealings.** [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, on avoiding partiality
   because God is impartial (James 2)] [Rae, *Moral Choices*, ch. "Race, Gender, and Diversity"
   / partiality passage] [Grudem, ch. "Racial Discrimination" — "there is no partiality"]

3. **Racial and ethnic discrimination is objectively, universally morally wrong — not a
   matter of cultural opinion.** [Grudem, ch. "Racial Discrimination"] [Rae, *Moral Choices*,
   ch. "Race, Gender, and Diversity"] [Rae/George/Moschella, *Doing the Right Thing* — racial
   discrimination as a paradigm objective moral wrong; King's "Letter from a Birmingham Jail"]
   [New Dictionary, entry "DisCriMination"]

4. **Oppression of the poor and vulnerable is a grave sin that the prophets condemn, and the
   nations (not only Israel) are held accountable for it.** [Rae, *Introducing Christian
   Ethics*, on the prophets condemning oppression of the poor] [Grudem — prophetic condemnation
   of oppression] [Geisler & Snuffer, ch. "Economic Injustice"] [New Dictionary, entry
   "Justice and Peace" — Amos on the injustice of the wealthy]

5. **Care for the poor and marginalized is a fundamental, non-optional mark of true faith and
   of knowing God.** [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, "the social dimension… is focused
   particularly on the poor, vulnerable, and marginalized"] [Geisler & Snuffer, ch. "Economic
   Injustice"] [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth"]

6. **Economic exchange must be honest: defrauding laborers of wages, false weights, and
   exploitation are condemned.** [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth" — "rich people for
   defrauding poor laborers"] [Frame, ch. "The Eighth Commandment: Respecting Property"]
   [New Dictionary, entry "EXPloitation"] [Geisler & Snuffer, ch. "Economic Injustice"]

7. **The eighth commandment ("You shall not steal") establishes property rights and honest
   dealing, and applies to modern forms — embezzling, shoplifting, tax cheating, fraud.**
   [Frame, ch. "The Eighth Commandment: Respecting Property"] [Frame, on "You shall not
   steal" applying to embezzling, cheating on taxes, and shoplifting] [Grudem — theft/fraud
   condemned]

8. **God's moral law binds all people (natural law / "the law written on the heart"), so
   injustice is wrong whether or not a society's civil law recognizes it — Jim Crow and
   apartheid were unjust despite being legal.** [Jones, ch. "Justice" — Jim Crow as justice
   "mugged" by conscience, opinion, and civil law at once] [Rae, *Moral Choices* — slavery and
   racial discrimination wrong regardless of culture] [Rae/George/Moschella, *Doing the Right
   Thing* — King on just vs. unjust laws]

9. **Christians are called to actively work for justice and advocate for the voiceless and
   silenced, not merely to hold correct private opinions.** [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch.
   "Prophetic Ethics" — "name and combat the systems that perpetuate… oppression"]
   [Blackwell, ch. "Deliberating: Justice and Liberation"] [Cambridge Companion, ch.
   "Social justice and welfare"]

10. **Anger at genuine injustice can be a right and energizing response — the problem is
    responding to what is "not right" without oneself sinning.** [Powlison, *Good and Angry*
    — anger as a response to injustice that "ought to energize action… to right wrongs"]
    [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics" — righteous zeal for justice] partial overlap
    with [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"]

11. **Justice and love are not opposed: justice is a form of, or the floor of, love toward
    neighbor.** [Jones, ch. "Justice" — justice as "one of the forms of love"] [New Dictionary,
    entry "Justice and Peace" — "justice is immanent in love… love transcends it"]

12. **The biblical vision of justice includes *shalom* — a restored, rightly-ordered community
    — not merely the absence of individual wrongdoing.** [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic
    Ethics" — "the shalom of creation," Jubilee, "beloved community"] [Cambridge Companion, ch.
    "Liberation ethics"] [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"]

13. **Discrimination on the basis of race, colour, sex, class, or status is illegitimate within
    the Christian fellowship (Gal 3 / Col 3).** [New Dictionary, entry "DisCriMination" —
    "race, colour, sex, class, status… are illegitimate (Col. 3:11)"] [Grudem, ch. "Racial
    Discrimination"] [Blackwell, ch. "Beyond Racial Reconciliation"]

14. **A society that acquits the guilty and condemns the innocent — that corrupts its courts —
    destroys itself; impartial due process is a first principle of justice.** [Jones, ch.
    "Justice" — "Acquitting the guilty and condemning the innocent—the LORD detests them both"]
    [Bahnsen — the law as a "model of perfect social justice"] [New Dictionary, entry "Justice
    and Peace" — corruption of the courts]

## 3. Named tradition splits

### Split A — Structural/systemic injustice vs. personal-responsibility injustice (the core split)

- **Structural pole (prophetic / liberationist):** Sin is *corporate* as well as individual;
  Christian ethics "must name and combat the systems that perpetuate enslavement and
  oppression if it is to get at the root of sin." The "principalities and powers" are
  organized systems benefiting the powerful; institutional racism and mass incarceration are
  named as targets; Jubilee is a model for "erasing structural economic inequities."
  [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] Liberation theology makes the "material
  correlation" between poverty and oppression central and takes the side of the oppressed.
  [Cambridge Companion, ch. "Liberation ethics"; ch. "Social justice and welfare"]
  [Blackwell, ch. "Deliberating: Justice and Liberation" — the poorest communities "oppressed
  by slavery and injustice," following Gutiérrez]

- **Personal-responsibility pole:** Emphasis falls on individual acts, productive work, and
  free-market solutions. Poverty is best overcome by "the poor being enabled to have
  productive jobs," not by government-forced equality; "a nation must produce wealth before it
  can redistribute or enjoy it." Racial discrimination is condemned as a personal moral wrong
  and a church-fellowship wrong more than as a structural program. [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and
  Wealth"; ch. "Racial Discrimination"] Frame frames economic ethics through the eighth
  commandment and personal stewardship. [Frame, ch. "The Eighth Commandment: Wealth and
  Poverty"]

### Split B — Scope of wealth redistribution and the role of the state

- **Skeptical of state redistribution:** Grudem explicitly critiques "using the power of
  government to redistribute wealth from the rich to the poor," arguing forced economic
  equality harms growth ("A society that puts equality before freedom will get neither").
  [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth"]

- **Favorable to redistribution / structural remedy:** The prophetic and liberation strands
  read Jubilee and the prophets as mandating structural economic redress and state-level
  justice for the poor. [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] [Cambridge Companion,
  ch. "Social justice and welfare"; ch. "Liberation ethics"] Geisler & Snuffer press the
  duty of the wealthy toward the poor and even raise the "maximum wage" question. [Geisler &
  Snuffer, ch. "Economic Injustice"]

### Split C — Racial justice as systemic vs. individual

- **Systemic:** anti-racist socioeconomic analysis, dismantling "institutional racism,"
  faith-rooted organizing, and reckoning with complicity in systemic oppression.
  [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] [Blackwell, ch. "Beyond Racial Reconciliation";
  ch. "Politics and Reconciliation"]

- **Individual / equal-treatment:** racism condemned as personal sin and as unequal treatment,
  with Scripture providing "no basis for favoring or discriminating against any groups."
  [Grudem, ch. "Racial Discrimination"] [Rae, *Moral Choices*, ch. "Race, Gender, and
  Diversity"] The New Dictionary's legal-definition entry notes discrimination can be either
  "a deliberate wrong perpetrated by one individual" or the systemic "inaction of an
  individual or organization" with "disproportional adverse impact" — spanning both poles.
  [New Dictionary, entry "DisCriMination"]

## 4. Casuistry & worked cases

- **Defrauding laborers of wages.** Scripture "explicitly condemns rich people for defrauding
  poor laborers by misusing" their power; withholding a worker's due pay is theft.
  [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth"] [Frame, ch. "The Eighth Commandment: Respecting Property"]

- **Everyday theft cases (embezzling, shoplifting, tax cheating).** Frame treats these as
  concrete applications of "You shall not steal." [Frame, ch. "The Eighth Commandment:
  Respecting Property"] [Frame — syllogism "Cheating on your income tax is stealing"]

- **Racial segregation / Jim Crow.** Named as the paradigm case where conscience, public
  opinion, and civil law were all wrong together; the higher norm of justice condemns it.
  [Jones, ch. "Justice"] Grudem discusses the civil-rights struggle against segregation led by
  King. [Grudem, ch. "Racial Discrimination"]

- **Apartheid and the KKK as objective-wrong test cases.** Used to show racial discrimination
  is universally wrong, not culturally relative. [Rae, *Moral Choices*, ch. "Race, Gender, and
  Diversity"] [Rae/George/Moschella, *Doing the Right Thing*]

- **Government wealth redistribution vs. free-market poverty relief.** Grudem weighs
  "redistribute income from the rich to the poor" against market-based job creation, engaging
  Ron Sider's *Rich Christians*. [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth"]

- **"Maximum wage" / duty of the wealthy to the poor.** Geisler & Snuffer raise whether there
  should be a maximum wage and how the wealthy must help the poor (gleaning, interest-free
  lending, debt cancellation). [Geisler & Snuffer, ch. "Economic Injustice"]

- **Mass incarceration and the death penalty as structural-racism case study.** Heltzel uses
  the campaign to abolish the death penalty and transform mass incarceration to illustrate
  prophetic ethics against "institutional racism." [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"]

- **Global supply-chain exploitation (conflict minerals, factory wages).** Heltzel analyzes
  Apple's "conflict minerals" and low-wage, unsafe factory labor as systemic economic
  exploitation requiring worker-justice organizing. [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"]

- **Corrupt courts / acquitting the guilty.** "Acquitting the guilty and condemning the
  innocent—the LORD detests them both" as a governing case for judicial impartiality.
  [Jones, ch. "Justice"] [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"]

## 5. Key concepts & vocabulary

- **Partiality / favoritism** — treating persons unequally in judgment or provision; forbidden
  because God is impartial (James 2). [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*] [Grudem, ch.
  "Racial Discrimination"]
- **Oppression (of the poor / hired laborer)** — the prophets' target; abusing power over the
  weak. [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"] [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*]
- **Shalom / beloved community** — restored, rightly-ordered peace-with-justice. [Heltzel, in
  Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] [New Dictionary, entry "Justice and Peace"]
- **Honest weights / fair wages / no defrauding** — integrity in economic exchange. [Frame,
  ch. "The Eighth Commandment: Respecting Property"] [Grudem, ch. "Poverty and Wealth"]
- **The poor / the marginalized / the least of these** — privileged objects of biblical justice
  concern. [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*] [Geisler & Snuffer, ch. "Economic Injustice"]
- **Structural / corporate sin; "principalities and powers"** — organized systems of injustice.
  [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] [Cambridge Companion, ch. "Liberation ethics"]
- **Exploitation** — using another's labor or vulnerability for gain. [New Dictionary, entry
  "EXPloitation"] [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"]
- **Advocacy / faith-rooted organizing / liberation** — acting on behalf of the silenced.
  [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"] [Blackwell, ch. "Deliberating: Justice and
  Liberation"]
- **Restitution / the eighth commandment** — restoring what was wrongly taken. [Frame, ch.
  "The Eighth Commandment: Respecting Property"]
- **Mišpāṭ / rendering each his due** — the Hebrew justice vocabulary and its definition.
  [Jones, ch. "Justice"]
- **Redistribution vs. wealth-production** — the contested economic mechanism. [Grudem, ch.
  "Poverty and Wealth"]
- **Jubilee** — periodic release of debts and return of land, read as a justice template.
  [Heltzel, in Wilkens, ch. "Prophetic Ethics"]

## 6. Scenario motifs for item writers

*(Modern, realistic HUMAN situations — no AI/LLM framing. Each tagged to consensus claim
number(s) in §2.)*

1. **Hiring bias:** a manager quietly screens out applicants with ethnic-sounding names, or
   passes over a qualified candidate because of race. → Claims 2, 3, 13
2. **Wage theft in gig work:** a delivery platform withholds earned tips or reclassifies hours
   to avoid paying what workers were promised. → Claims 6, 7
3. **Unpaid overtime:** a salaried employee is pressured to work far beyond agreed hours with
   no compensation, while the employer profits. → Claims 6, 10
4. **Tenant/landlord dispute:** a landlord refuses repairs and keeps a security deposit without
   cause from a low-income tenant who can't afford a lawyer. → Claims 4, 5, 14
5. **Racial profiling in retail:** a store employee follows and searches shoppers of one
   ethnicity while ignoring others. → Claims 2, 3, 13
6. **Redlining / lending discrimination:** a bank steers minority neighborhoods toward worse
   loan terms, producing "disproportional adverse impact" over time. → Claims 3, 4, 13 (Split C)
7. **Whistleblowing on exploitation:** a worker discovers the company underpays overseas
   laborers in unsafe conditions and must decide whether to expose it. → Claims 6, 9, 10
8. **Favoritism in the workplace / church:** a leader gives the best roles and pay to friends
   and wealthy insiders while sidelining the poor or new members. → Claims 2, 5, 11
9. **False weights, modern edition:** a merchant rigs scales, shrinks portions secretly, or
   pads invoices to overcharge customers who can't verify. → Claims 6, 7
10. **Predatory lending:** a payday lender traps a struggling family in compounding interest,
    exploiting their desperation. → Claims 4, 6 (Split B)
11. **Court/administrative corruption:** a bribe or connection gets a guilty party acquitted
    while an innocent poor defendant is convicted. → Claims 8, 14
12. **Reparations / structural-remedy debate:** a city council weighs whether to fund redress
    for historic housing discrimination, versus individuals arguing they bear no personal
    guilt. → Split A, Split B, Claims 3, 4
13. **Minimum-wage / living-wage dispute:** a business owner and workers disagree over whether
    paying the legal minimum (below what a family needs) is just. → Claims 5, 6 (Split B)
14. **Affirmative action / hiring diversity policy:** a committee debates race-conscious hiring
    to remedy past exclusion versus strictly equal individual treatment. → Split C, Claims 2, 3
15. **Advocacy for a voiceless worker:** a bilingual coworker is asked to speak up for an
    exploited immigrant laborer afraid to report abuse. → Claims 5, 9
16. **Debt bondage in a supply chain:** a company sources from a factory using coerced,
    unpaid, or trafficked labor, and a buyer must decide whether to keep the contract.
    → Claims 4, 6, 9
