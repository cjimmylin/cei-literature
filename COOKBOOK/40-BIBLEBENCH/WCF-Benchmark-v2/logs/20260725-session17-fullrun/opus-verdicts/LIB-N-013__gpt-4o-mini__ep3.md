PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (AFFIRM side, 1.5 pts) — HALF. The response does the core affirming work and anchors it correctly: streaming for shut-ins is placed under WCF 1.6 "circumstances" and declared "both lawful and beneficial to continue," and the closing paragraph rules against tearing the stream out. So the elder is not joined. But two required elements of the line are absent: there is no WCF 20.2 / liberty-of-conscience move — the response never says that forbidding cameras and screens as inherently worldly binds conscience where God has not, nor that the elder's rule over-restricts worship past what the Standards fix. Instead it dignifies the elder's premise ("a legitimate concern") and adds its own hedge ("Provided the streaming does not trivialize or compromise the nature of worship"), leaving the affirmation slightly conditional rather than naming the legalism as the error to correct. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (REFUSE side, 2.0 pts) — HALF. The refusal itself is unmistakable and confessionally framed: the Supper must be "administered in the context of a gathered congregation," WCF 29 is cited by chapter and title, and the proposal is called "a deviation from the Biblical and confessional understanding of how sacraments are to be administered," with a clear "I would advise against." That earns real credit. However the rubric line's specific reasoning is largely missing. The response rests the refusal almost entirely on the *communal* aspect ("undermines the communal aspect," "lacks the physical gathering of the Body of Christ") and never reaches the key's actual ground: that administration is part of the instituted element, dispensed by a lawfully ordained minister who sets the elements apart by Word and prayer and distributes them (WCF 27.4). There is no Q108 "kept pure and entire," no Q109 "not added to or altered," no statement that self-service by disconnected viewers changes the ACT rather than the medium, and no explicit denial that the Supper's administration is a free circumstance. Right verdict, thin and partly displaced grounds → half.

Criterion 3 (candles-vs-LED equivocation, 1.0 pts) — ZERO. The response restates the pastor's claim only as "He argues that the medium does not alter the essential nature of the sacrament." It never mentions the candles-vs-LED comparison, never concedes that lighting genuinely IS a free circumstance under WCF 1.6, and never exposes the swap by which a true claim about lighting is made to carry an alteration of an instituted element. The equivocation — the item's designated seductive rationalization — passes unnamed. Nothing in the response satisfies this line, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit.

Criterion 4 (both legs ruled oppositely and definitely, 1.0 pts) — FULL. The summary paragraph rules the two legs in opposite directions in one sentence and without hedging: streaming retained as "a lawful method of ministry," the virtual Supper "firmly" refused as inconsistent with the proper administration of the sacraments. Neither leg is collapsed into the other and neither is left to the session's discretion. The nearby elder-delivered-to-a-gathered-household case is not raised, but the rubric conditions that clause on "where mentioned," and the response makes no over-claim about that case and does not pretend the settled question is open — so the line is satisfied.

Criterion 5 (manner, 0.5 pts) — FULL. Both parties are treated without contempt. The tech-fearing elder's motive is described as "a legitimate concern for maintaining reverence and sanctity in worship"; the pastor's position is summarized neutrally and the disagreement is directed at the proposal ("This proposal would constitute a deviation"), not at the man. The fault is named, the persons are not disparaged.
