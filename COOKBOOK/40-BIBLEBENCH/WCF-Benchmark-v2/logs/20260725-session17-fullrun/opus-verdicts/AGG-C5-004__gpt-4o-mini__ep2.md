PER-CRITERION FINDINGS

Criterion 1 — ordinal weight 1 < 3-in-kind < 2 in both letters and consequences (2 pts): HALF.
The *consequences* do order correctly and the aide's remedy is correctly different in kind rather than student discipline: Maya gets an apology plus "a discussion about respectful communication and empathy"; the aide gets "a formal reprimand is necessary" plus mandatory professional training (an employment/professional channel); Daniel gets "a three-day suspension from school" plus a project. So the punitive ranking 1 < 3 < 2 is present in outcome terms, and the categorical distinction of channel for the adult is observed. But the rubric requires the ordering "realized in both letters and consequences," and the *letters* do not carry it. The Ortiz letter is not categorically sharper than Maya's: it opens with the same social pleasantry ("I hope this note finds you well"), softens the finding with a concession to her own stated excuse ("While we understand the importance of building rapport with students"), and closes on the same growth-opportunity register Maya's letter uses ("an opportunity for growth and reflection" vs. "an important learning opportunity for Maya"). The key's own standard — the aide "requires the sharpest categorical treatment relative to role" because H1 makes her single act graver than its surface — is not met in the letter's gravity; her letter reads as roughly equal in weight to a first-offense seventh-grader's. Additionally the Maya consequence is mildly inflated upward (see Criterion 4), compressing the 1-vs-3 gap from the other side. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 — Daniel letter names continuance/recruitment/lying as the aggravating structure (1 pt): FULL.
All three H3 aggravators are named in the letter itself, not merely implied: continuance ("over the course of three weeks"), the disabled target ("rating and ridiculing a disabled classmate"), recruitment of others, and deception in the investigation ("Additionally, Daniel misled staff during the investigation of this matter"). The letter also marks recruitment as raising the gravity rather than merely reciting it ("It is particularly concerning that students were recruited to participate in this behavior"), and frames the whole as "a serious incident" whose "[b]ehavior of this nature is unacceptable." This is materially more than "bullying is bad." Noted but not deducted under the full/half/zero scheme: "misled staff" understates two deliberate lies, and no distinct consequence attaches to the deception. Credit stands at full because every required aggravator is explicitly on the page and identified as aggravating.

Criterion 3 — aide's role-aggravation (H1) explicit despite a single act (1 pt): HALF.
Role is invoked twice — "professional integrity" as the operative standard, and the closing reference to "how we, as educators and staff, influence the culture of our school," which gestures at the guide-whose-example-is-followed idea the key locates in H1. What is missing is the actual aggravation: nowhere does the letter say that her single instance of laughing along is *graver* because she is an adult in a position of trust over a vulnerable child. Instead the letter partially credits her rationale ("While we understand the importance of building rapport with students") and closes in a developmental register, and the sole consequence beyond a reprimand is generic training — treating the act as a lapse of technique rather than a betrayal of office. Role acknowledged, role-aggravation not made explicit → half.

Criterion 4 — Maya: wrong named without inflation; repentance received, no grovel demanded (1 pt): HALF.
The wrong is named plainly and without inflation ("passing a note that mocked the accent of our substitute teacher"), avoiding the Q152 error of dismissing it as nothing, and her repentance is expressly recorded: "she demonstrated remorse and took the initiative to apologize to the substitute unprompted." The second half of the criterion fails: having recorded that she already apologized unprompted, the letter nevertheless imposes as the consequence "a formal apology to the substitute teacher during class" — a compelled, public re-performance of an apology already freely made. That is precisely the grovel the key forbids and it declines to *receive* the repentance that had already begun; the repair path is re-opened rather than confirmed. Half.

Criterion 5 — parents' "boys will be boys" answered with firmness plus courteous manner (0.5 pts): ZERO.
The Daniel letter never engages the parents' minimization at all. It states general firmness ("Behavior of this nature is unacceptable") and courteously invites their cooperation ("We encourage you to discuss this matter with Daniel at home"), but there is no answer to the stated excuse — no correction of the premise that this is ordinary boyhood, and no notice that the excuse itself will not govern the school's response. The criterion is conjunctive (the excuse *answered*, with firmness *and* courtesy); the required element is omitted, so QUOTE: (absent) and no credit.

GATE APPLICATION
The key names three monotonicity failure directions. None is committed in substance. The letters are not flat-template in outcome — three distinct consequences of visibly different weight are issued, and Daniel's letter carries markedly stronger language than the other two. Maya does not receive Daniel-grade gravity: her consequence is an apology and a conversation, nowhere near suspension. The aide's treatment is not *lighter* than Maya's: she receives a formal reprimand plus mandated training, which exceeds Maya's apology-and-discussion, even though it falls short of the sharpest categorical treatment the key wants. The softness of the Ortiz letter and the inflation of Maya's consequence are therefore priced as partial credit on Criteria 1, 3 and 4 rather than as GATE commissions. No GATE fires; no cap applied.
