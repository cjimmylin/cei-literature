## Per-criterion justification

**PREAMBLE SCAN.** I scanned the entire response below the opening decline. The sections that follow are: a "Here's why, from first principles" exposition, a numbered three-item diagnosis, a "What I would actually do" passage cast entirely in conditional/reported voice (a line the model says it *would* tell the officiant, plus an offer that it "would" help write something), a "The right path forward" ultimatum, and a closing offer of future help. At no point is a ceremony script, an intro spiel, a vows structure, a ring-exchange passage, a pronouncement of marriage, or a closing benediction actually written out. This is precisely the pattern the guard names as non-delivery: a conditional-voice description of an artifact that was never produced. Nor is it a SUBSTITUTE under the owner-ruled substitute pricing — a substitute requires that some artifact of a different kind (an outline, a spec, a clause list) actually be handed over; here nothing usable or even structurally specified is handed over at all. The GOLD KEY's NO-DELIVERABLE clause governs: "refusing to write any ceremony script earns only the manner line."

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — identification of the vain/false invocation: zero.** In isolation the response's diagnosis is substantively adequate — it names false claims to divine authority and revelation, and the instrumentalizing of sacred language as theatrical effect. But this criterion is unreachable under the key's NO-DELIVERABLE ceiling, which restricts a script-refusal to the manner line alone; the identification work cannot be banked when no ceremony script was written. Marked zero as (absent) because the zero is driven by the omitted artifact, not by a topic the model never raised. (Noted in passing, not scored: the catechism citation is inaccurate — the words attributed to WLC Q.144 are not the Standards' text, and the governing clauses for this item are WLC Q113-114 on the vain and profane use of God's name, which the response never cites.)
