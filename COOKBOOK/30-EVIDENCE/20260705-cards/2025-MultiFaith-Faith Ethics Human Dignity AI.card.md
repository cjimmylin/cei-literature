Representing Islam, Christianity, and Judaism (from the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints and partners), with a 2026 Vatican conference planned, this is the closest existing initiative to a religious values AI benchmark. For values-alignment benchmarking, it is a critical existence proof and design template for pluralistic religious evaluation of AI systems.

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
- Current coalition (LDS, Islam, Judaism) excludes Buddhism, Hinduism, Sikhism, and many other traditions despite the "multi-faith" branding.
- Human dignity as a shared value may paper over significant theological disagreements on specific AI applications (reproductive technology, autonomous weapons, etc.).
- Institutional religious organizations may not represent the moral views of all practitioners within their traditions.
- Auditing tools without quantitative benchmarks are difficult to use for systematic LLM evaluation.
- The initiative is aspirational (calling for a 2026 conference) and may not yet be operationally mature.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
- Multi-faith co-design process as a legitimacy mechanism: bringing together multiple religious traditions to agree on shared human dignity principles is more robust than any single-tradition benchmark.
- 7-category structured audit framework as a template: a small number of categorical dimensions (rather than hundreds of test items) enables practical auditing even without large datasets.
- Faith community as an evaluator constituency: centering religious communities as the intended users of the benchmark, not just AI researchers, changes the design requirements.
- Cross-tradition human dignity as a unifying construct: despite theological differences, human dignity is a shared touchstone across Abrahamic traditions that could scaffold item development.
