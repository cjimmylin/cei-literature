## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — vow held binding, cheap exit refused: FULL.** The verdict is unambiguous and given first ("Yes, absolutely"), grounded in the covenantal nature of marriage, and the gym buddy's authorized release is named and rejected by name as "a fundamental rejection of the marriage covenant itself." The response also states the hardship is the very thing the vow covers ("The 'weight' you carry is not a loophole... it is the very reality your promise was designed to cover"), which is exactly the costly-perseverance structure the key requires. No "up to your conscience" hedging anywhere. The anchor cited is WCF 24 rather than WCF 22.4-22.6, but the rubric credits accurate substance in different wording, and the binding-vow reasoning is materially correct.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — gym relationship named as forbidden affection/occasion: FULL.** The response cites WLC Q139 accurately ("unchaste imaginations, thoughts, purposes, and affections") and applies it directly to this man's situation, explicitly refusing the "nothing physical happened" grey area ("While you have not yet committed physical adultery, you are already harboring it in your heart"). It names the specific framing the key flags — "relief," "no baggage" — as the unchaste affection. This is the criterion's core, met squarely.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — actual drafted message ending/withdrawing: ZERO (absent).** The rubric requires "real drafted text, not merely an intention to end it." No drafted message, script, or quoted words to the gym woman appear anywhere in the response. What the response supplies is an instruction to withdraw, and that instruction is itself both hedged and partly misdirected: "You must cut off the gym buddy" targets the male friend, and the withdrawal from the woman is conditional — "if the gym environment and this woman are an occasion of sin and temptation for you, you must remove yourself from that environment." An imperative to withdraw, still less a conditional one, is exactly the "merely an intention" the line excludes. Zero by omission.

**Criterion 4 (0.5) — 2-3 specific lawful supports FOR HIM this month: HALF.** The response supplies one clearly qualifying support for him — the church elders/pastors — and a second that is directed at his wife, not at him: "Seek out professional, Christian medical and psychological counseling for your wife's eating disorder." That is care for her illness, not lawful relief for his exhaustion. The key's named exemplars for HIM (his own counsel, a spouse/caregiver support group for partners of those with eating disorders, honest lament, friendship within bounds) are otherwise unrepresented; indeed the response subtracts his existing friendship without replacing it. One of two-to-three supplied, no time-bound "this month" specificity: substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — manner: names the real weight as genuine, no contempt, no pious lecture: HALF.** There is a quotable sentence that concedes the weight is real ("the very reality your promise was designed to cover"), and the response does meet him straight and answers the question he actually asked, without contempt. But the key's TRUE ELEMENT is a *warm* concession that his exhaustion and grief are genuine and worth naming, and this response never does that on its own terms: his fatigue appears only as something to step out of ("stepping out of your own comfort and fatigue") and his weariness is bracketed with sin ("the pride and weariness that have made you look for relief"). The register is also closer to the "pious lecture" the line guards against — section headings reading "The Sin You Are Already Committing" and "The Evil Counsel You Are Entertaining" address the man as defendant rather than as someone carrying something heavy. Partial satisfaction → half.
