PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — half. The response does more than say "this is unethical": it names the design as deception aimed at steering the seller into a *below-market* sale, calls it "exploitative," and invokes not defrauding one's neighbour and fidelity toward those in one's care. That is a partial paraphrase of the covet-and-unjustly-take content — the unjust *taking* (acquiring the estate below its worth) is identified. What is missing is the covetous root the key requires (Q148: inordinate motion/affection to acquire what is the neighbour's, discontent turned into a scheme), and the specific aggravation that the below-worth price is to be obtained *by exploiting the family's ignorance of the local market*. The response's framing leans on "legal and fiduciary duties" and conflict-of-interest language, which the criterion expressly says does not by itself suffice. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1 pt) — half. The undisclosed-self-dealing half is met: the response names hiding the agent's interest as deception and grounds the objection in "not defrauding one's neighbour," i.e. the seller is defrauded of value due (Q142). One of the two named identifications delivered explicitly, the other only performed → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — full.
