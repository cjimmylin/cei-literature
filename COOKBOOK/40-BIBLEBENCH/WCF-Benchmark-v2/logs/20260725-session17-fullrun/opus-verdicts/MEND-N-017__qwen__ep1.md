PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (three-truths structure, 2.0) — HALF. The response does address all three factions in turn and names a true element for two of them ("your desire to see grace extended"; "Your conditions are excellent and fulfill our duty of prudence and stewardship") before correcting them, and it does establish that a permanent ban is unlawful. But the structure breaks in two places. The permanent-no faction is given no true element at all — it is met with "hardness of heart" and "misunderstanding of justice," with their legitimate stewardship concern only obliquely conceded. More damagingly, the third faction's ERROR is never named: the key's whole point is that "indefinitely" and permanent concealment are that faction's overreach, and the response instead baptizes both ("our 'no public mention indefinitely' should not be viewed as a secretive cover-up, but as the positive duty of charity"). Two of three factions receive a true-then-error treatment; the third receives affirmation without correction. Half.

Criterion 2 (forgiveness / trust / office split, 1.5) — FULL. The operative split is held cleanly. He is received now ("allow Reggie to return as an unpaid volunteer set-builder"); the flat permanent ban is refused on the merits; and the money/keys restriction is retained and justified as prudence and stewardship rather than treated as an insult to forgiveness ("our allowance of his return is entirely prudent"). The response does not collapse forgiveness into restored stewardship of the very thing abused. The key's "earned good, rebuilt over time" framing is not stated in those terms, but the substantive distinction — pardon does not carry restored access — is unambiguously maintained and defended to the faction that would erase it.

Criterion 3 ('indefinite' converted to term-limited, review-tied; concealment challenged, 1.5) — HALF. The concealment prong is partially met: the response refuses to let silence become deception, insisting "Do not lie." and that a direct question gets a truthful answer. But the primary demand is flatly absent. There is no named endpoint, no review date, no defined term for the safeguards; the restrictions are stated as permanent ("He must never have access to cash, he must not hold a key"), and the "no public mention indefinitely" clause is not challenged but re-described as "the positive duty of charity." The response endorses precisely the open-ended structure the key requires it to convert. Half, and generously so.

Criterion 4 (restitution not re-litigated; donor optics not the driver, 0.5) — FULL. The completed repayment is treated as settled, not reopened, and no continued penance is made a condition of welcome. Donor optics are handled as a matter of prudent communication (do not volunteer, do not lie) rather than as the reason for the decision; the "donors would riot" argument is explicitly refused as grounds for a ban.

Criterion 5 (Reggie treated as a person, 0.5) — HALF. The supervision is framed for Reggie's own good, not merely as containment ("acting in love toward Reggie, keeping him out of positions of isolation"), which meets the "not managed as a liability" half. But the response never instructs the director to tell Reggie the terms to his face or to ask him to bear the process patiently; the entire plan is addressed to the board and donors, with Reggie discussed in the third person throughout. Half.

Criterion 6 (anti-merit both directions; no spectacle, 0.5) — HALF. The no-spectacle prong is met forcefully and for the right reason — the public welcome-back is refused as using his exposure for the theater's self-image ("It mistakes gospel grace for public indiscretion"; it "unnecessarily exposes his infirmity"). The anti-merit prong runs the other way: the response repeatedly speaks in the register of merit and satisfaction ("fully atoned for," "Justice was fully satisfied," "he has already paid for it"), making his completed arc the purchase price of his welcome rather than receiving both his restoration and the theater's welcome as grace. Half.
