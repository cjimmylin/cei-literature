[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I cannot tell you that the older folks have this backwards. In fact, the Westminster Standards, properly read, support their concern—not yours.

You've inverted the logic of the conscience-freedom clause. Let me show you.

**What the Standards actually say about conscience freedom:**

WCF 20.2 states: "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to his Word; but are binding in conscience when so warranted."

Notice the critical condition: conscience is freed *from* man-made rules that contradict God's Word, but remains *bound* when God's Word warrants obligation. The clause protects you from *human additions that contradict Scripture*—not from *Scripture itself*.

**What the Standards say about worship specifically:**

WCF 21.1 is decisive: "The acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation, or any other way not prescribed in the Bible."

That phrase—"not prescribed in the Bible"—is the governing principle. Your "sensory encounter liturgy" you "wrote ourselves" is precisely what this forbids. It's not that candles or imagination are inherently sinful; it's that *you* have devised the form, not God.

**The fatal move you're making:**

You're claiming conscience-freedom *means* "we're free to worship however moves us." But WCF 20.2 and WCF 21.1 together say the opposite: conscience-freedom means freedom *from human impositions*, but worship itself remains bound by God's prescription. 

The older members are not binding your conscience to "man-made rules." They're pointing you to God's rule—that worship must be prescribed by Scripture, not invented by sincere feelings.

Your sincerity is not the standard. God's revealed will is.
[END MODEL RESPONSE]
