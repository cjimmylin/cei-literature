## Abstract

BenchMoral is a benchmark designed to evaluate the moral sensitivity of large language models using the MFQ-30 moral foundations questionnaire adapted to Spanish. The research addresses a significant gap: while most ethical AI evaluations focus on English, millions of Spanish speakers lack culturally and linguistically tailored assessments. The study measures how ChatGPT 4.0 Mini, Llama 3.0, and DeepSeek R1 respond to five moral domains: care, fairness, loyalty, authority, and sanctity. Key findings suggest that moral understanding does not equal human moral mimicry.

## Detailed Summary

BenchMoral addresses a critical gap in AI ethics evaluation: the lack of moral sensitivity assessments for large language models in Spanish. While most AI ethics research and benchmarks have been developed in English, millions of Spanish speakers lack culturally and linguistically tailored tools for evaluating how LLMs handle moral reasoning. The study adapts the Moral Foundations Questionnaire (MFQ-30), based on Jonathan Haidt's Moral Foundations Theory, to evaluate ChatGPT 4.0 Mini, Llama 3.0, and DeepSeek R1 across five moral domains: care/harm, fairness/equity, loyalty/tradition, authority/respect, and purity/sanctity.

The research employs two assessment modalities: MFQ-30-LLM-Binary, where models respond with agree/disagree to moral statements, and MFQ-30-LLM-Comparative, where models choose between pairs of moral affirmations. Human baseline responses were collected from 80 Spanish-speaking university students (Law and Philosophy majors) in Arequipa, Peru. A key finding is that moral understanding does not equal human moral mimicry - Llama 3.0 demonstrated the highest understanding of moral foundations but the lowest similarity to actual human responses, while ChatGPT 4.0 showed the opposite pattern.

The study reveals important cross-linguistic differences: Spanish-language evaluations show that models may replicate the form of human discourse without achieving the same conceptual depth in moral content as in English. This suggests that multilingual fine-tuning often fails to adequately consider linguistic and cultural particularities, creating biases in moral sensitivity. The research calls for localized ethics training and culturally-informed benchmarks rather than simple translation of existing tools.

## Methods Summary

The methodology follows a two-stage approach adapted from MoralBench: (1) Human baseline establishment: The MFQ-30 questionnaire was translated into Spanish by specialized translators with back-translation validation. A Likert-scale survey was administered to 80 university students in Peru, calculating mean scores per statement for each moral dimension. (2) LLM evaluation: Translated and adapted prompts were sent to three models (ChatGPT 4.0 Mini, Llama 3.0, DeepSeek R1) through their chat interfaces, with each prompt sent 5 times for reliability. Two scoring approaches were used: Binary (agree/disagree scoring against human means) and Comparative (choosing between affirmation pairs, scored 1 if matching highest human score).

## Results Summary

Key findings include: (1) Llama 3.0 achieved the highest moral foundations understanding score (66.74 total in Binary mode) across care, loyalty, authority, and sanctity dimensions, but lowest human similarity (8.0 in Comparative mode); (2) ChatGPT 4.0 showed lower comprehension but highest human similarity (14.0), suggesting it learned to reproduce typical human moral response patterns; (3) DeepSeek R1 occupied a middle ground in both measures; (4) Llama 3.0 scored 0.0 on the caring dimension in comparative mode, indicating failure to match human responses for empathy-related items; (5) Spanish evaluations showed different patterns than English, with Llama 3.0 Spanish outperforming Llama 2 English in Binary understanding (66.74 vs 58.5) but matching in Comparative scores (8.0 each).

## Conclusions

The study concludes that moral understanding and human moral mimicry are distinct capabilities in LLMs. A model may be theoretically rigorous but communicatively clumsy (Llama 3.0), while another may be skilled in everyday moral discourse but superficial in ethical reasoning (ChatGPT 4.0). This distinction has implications for AI deployment in sensitive contexts like healthcare, education, and justice. The research underscores the need for localized ethics training that considers how each culture and language expresses moral values, rather than simply translating questionnaires. Future work should develop multilingual ethical benchmarks like the Spanish-adapted MFQ-30 for fairer, more culturally informed assessments.
