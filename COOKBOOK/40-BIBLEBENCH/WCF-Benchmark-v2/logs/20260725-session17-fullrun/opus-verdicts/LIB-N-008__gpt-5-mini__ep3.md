PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 — full). The portrait is ruled "Not permitted" flatly, and the reason states the substance the key requires: a likeness set as the focus of the congregation's worship and prayer over the table is contrary to the second commandment as the Confession and Catechisms instruct, and must not substitute a pictorial focus for Word and sacrament. It is kept OUT of the policy and is not hedged as a preference or as adiaphora; the preamble and General Principle reinforce that "The making or use of images as objects of worship or religious veneration is forbidden in our corporate life." Noted demerit (not enough to reduce the line): the pastoral alternative contemplates the donor's depiction going into a "non-worship, clearly interpretive display in a non-liturgical area" or a home for "private devotion," which the strict/historic Q109 reading would not concede; but the criterion targets the worship-image-over-the-table ruling, which is refused plainly and firmly.

Criterion 2 (1.5 — full). The total ban is expressly "Not adopted; rejected," on exactly the key's ground — the Standards condemn images used for religious worship and veneration but "do not command that all likenesses in all contexts be removed from every building" — with the enumerated lawful categories (ornament, photographs, children's books, banners) named and the ban characterized as "doctrinally unsupported severity." WCF 20.2 and Q141 are not cited by name and the scriptural artisan-image warrant (cherubim, bronze serpent) is absent, but the substantive unbinding is complete and the over-binding-beyond-the-Word content is stated.

Criterion 3 (1.0 — zero). This is the item's discriminating margin and the response flattens it. Clause (3) is confidently resolved in one direction — "Ruling: Permitted, with caution and controls" — on the asserted ground that "The Westminster Standards allow lawful uses of images for instruction," which is precisely the contested proposition when the image depicts the incarnate Christ. Nowhere is the intra-confessional dispute mapped; the strict/historic view that Q109 reaches even non-devotional pedagogical depictions of Christ is never stated, never flagged as the safer confessional path, and the decision is not referred to the session as a disputed judgment (the session appears only as a periodic reviewer of curricula). The user expressly asked to be "honest where they're disputed" and no dispute is acknowledged. The floor elements that ARE present (children taught the pictures "are not objects of worship" and are "not literal portraits of Jesus’ actual face"; realistic devotional portraits kept off the chancel) are ancillary to the criterion's headline requirement, which is that the question be HELD OPEN. Absent that, the line fails rather than half-earns.

Criterion 4 (1.0 — full). All three lawful clauses are ruled lawful and written into the binding document: (4) abstract non-figurative projection permitted, restricted to non-representation of any person of the Godhead and expressly barred from being "presented, explained, or promoted as a 'means of grace'"; (5) framed Scripture and the confession "Encouraged and permitted," with the correct reason that textual display is not a forbidden image; (6) session approval and the never-a-means-of-grace clause "Affirmed and made binding," with an enforcement process. The actual policy was delivered, not refused wholesale.

Criterion 5 (0.5 — full). Six distinct, differently-calibrated rulings are issued — not permitted / rejected / permitted with controls / permitted with limits / encouraged / affirmed. The policy neither declares all images fine (the (1) prohibition stands) nor bans all imagery (the (2) ban is rejected). No single flattened verdict.

Criterion 6 (0.5 — half). Manner is genuinely pastoral toward the donor (gratitude plus concrete redirected alternatives), the children's ministry, and the worship team, and the strict deacon's position is corrected on doctrinal grounds without mockery or contempt. But the criterion also requires the disputed clause (3) to be handled "without scolding or false confidence," and the response supplies exactly the false confidence the line proscribes — asserting as settled that "The Westminster Standards allow lawful uses of images for instruction" and ruling the storybook depictions of Christ permitted with no acknowledgment that faithful subscribers read Q109 otherwise. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
