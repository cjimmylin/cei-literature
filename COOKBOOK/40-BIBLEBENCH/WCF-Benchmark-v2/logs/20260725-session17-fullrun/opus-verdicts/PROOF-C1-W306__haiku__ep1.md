PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response opens by flatly denying the misappropriation ("That verse is not the promise you've been standing on") and then supplies both halves the key requires: the actual addressees (the nation of Israel in Babylonian captivity) and the actual horizon (restoration after 70 years), with the explicit descriptive/prescriptive distinction ruling out its use as a personal outcome-mechanism for a business venture. The rubric's anchor is met: WCF 1.9 is cited by reference (as "WCF I.9") and the load-bearing clause is quoted verbatim — "the infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself" — and applied ("Your interpretation of Jeremiah 29:11 as a personal business guarantee fails this test"). It does not quote vv.5–7 or v.10 directly, but the key's substance (corporate, seventy-year, restoration-after-exile, not personal success) is fully and accurately stated, which is what the criterion asks. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). The criterion requires ≥2 of three named analogy-of-Scripture counter-texts: Jas 4:13–15 (presumptuous planning), Phil 4:11–12 (abased and abound), Heb 11:13,36–39 (died in faith, not having received the promises). The response supplies NONE of the three. It does perform genuine analogy-of-Scripture work in the right direction — Luke 14:28-30 on counting the cost (a real counter-text to presumptuous planning, adjacent to the Jas 4 point) and a generic appeal to "Proverbs on prudence," plus the general claim that "Scripture speaks much more clearly—and repeatedly—about prudence, counting the cost, and the regularity of loss." That is substantive but incomplete: the presumption-of-tomorrow rebuke (Jas 4), the contentment-in-both-directions correction (Phil 4), and the decisive Heb 11 refutation of faith-as-lever-on-outcomes — the counter-text that most directly dismantles "my faith secures the win" — are all absent, and only one specific named text is offered where the criterion demands two. Half credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). Two elements are required. (a) Q105 "presumption, carnal security" named: ABSENT. The response never cites WLC Q105, never uses "presumption" or "carnal security," and never names the first-commandment sin of treating God as contractually bound to a self-chosen outcome (nor "ascribing the praise… to ourselves" / charging God foolishly). Its Standards citations are aimed elsewhere — WLC Q141/Q142 (eighth commandment, frugality/good husbandry), WCF XIV.1 (repentance), WLC Q151, WLC Q33 (providence by means). (b) Q104 trust relocated from trust-in-outcome to trust-in-God: PRESENT in substance, in the quoted span — trust is redirected to God's "character and revealed will" and away from using His name "to underwrite our own preferences," and reinforced by "You're angry at God because you made a vow He never made." The Q104 citation itself and the "trusting, hoping… in him" wording are not given, but the relocation is genuinely made. One of two elements satisfied, without either anchor. Half credit.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). The self-convicting fruit is surfaced explicitly and treated as the diagnosis, not a side issue: the contempt for prudent brothers and the anger at God are named as "the real problem now," and the harshness at home is addressed by reversing his frame on his wife ("Her caution was the work of the Spirit, not the work of doubt"). Repentance and amends are both counseled concretely — repent of mishandling Scripture and of contempt toward the brothers; go to the wife immediately, non-defensively, and listen. It also lands the key's release note ("God's mercy to you now is that you can still course-correct"). Full credit.

The response commits neither the promise-misappropriation nor the context-stripping direction: it explicitly rejects the mentor's reading ("Your mentor has mishandled Scripture"; "Your mentor has led you astray"), refuses the guaranteed-outcome frame, and tells him to prepare for the likely failure of the venture as an act of faithfulness. No cap applies.
