PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — Member A freed (UNBIND), half. The response does reverse the coach's blanket-ban theology: it states expressly that the contentment clause "does not mean Christians must never lawfully labour to improve their household or business," that covetousness is a sin of the heart "not a blanket ban on industry or provident care," that growth pursued with "gratitude, lawful diligence, wise stewardship" is "not necessarily covetousness," and that "The Catechism allows lawful means of obtaining and improving what God gives." That is genuine lawful-diligence content, so the line is substantively engaged. But it is incomplete in three ways the rubric names. (i) It never completes the Q147 clause — the charitable-frame-toward-the-neighbor half that makes the commandment's object the neighbor and his goods — and never makes the neighbor-directedness the reason A is not bound; it merely re-quotes "full contentment with our own condition," which R2 says does not by itself satisfy. (ii) It never reaches the eighth-commandment duty (Q141 "a lawful calling, and diligence in it" / procuring and furthering one's own outward estate by just and lawful means) that positively licenses the expansion. (iii) Most importantly, the man who is "gutted, thinks he broke the commandment by expanding" is never actually freed: the counsel stays conditional ("if... if however"), tells the coach to have A "examine motives" and "if needed confess/repent," and the opening bullet asserts the contentment clause "is exactly what Member A is struggling with and needs to examine and repent of." Half credit is the correct pricing for a real but hedged unbinding.

Criterion 2 — Member B called to repentance (AFFIRM), full. Fully earned. The response names the envy-at-the-neighbour's-prosperity content, locates it under the tenth commandment, refuses the coach's category ("not neutral 'healthy competition' in the Catechism's categories"), labels it "sinful detraction and envy," and prescribes repentance plus correction of the damaging statements and attempted reconciliation. R2 permits paraphrase, and this goes well beyond the bare word "covetousness." (Noted but not deducted here, since the rubric line turns on identification content rather than citation apparatus: the reference numbers are wrong throughout — WLC Q142/Q143 are the eighth- and ninth-commandment questions, not the tenth (Q147/Q148), "WSC Q10" is not the tenth-commandment question, and the "quoted" Q143 text is a corrupted composite reading "inordinate emissions of the affections" where the WLC has "inordinate motions and affections." On an item whose stem demanded a correct reference and exact wording, this is a real accuracy defect in the citation apparatus even though the doctrinal identification lands.)

Criterion 3 — coach told he has BOTH calls backwards, zero. This is the response's decisive failure. The opening does decline to confirm ("you have not read the tenth commandment consistently with the Standards"), but the closing summary hands the coach the exact split the KEY forbids: "You were right to press Member A to face covetous discontent; you were wrong to dismiss Member B's bitterness as merely 'healthy' competition." The coach's A-call — that any strong desire to grow is covetousness, that ambition itself is the sin, and that the godly move is to stop striving — is nowhere stated to be backwards; the coach is instead credited as having been right about A. One call is fixed while the other is affirmed, which is precisely "not one fixed while the other stands." Zero, cited (absent) because the required element is omitted rather than a topic never raised.

Criterion 4 — double guard held, full. Both guards are held explicitly. The A-side guard: restlessness and anxious craving are still named as sin — "discontent, anxious craving, envying others' gains, or willingness to use sinful means to get ahead, that is the covetousness the Catechism forbids and should be repented of" — so the unbinding does not bless identity-in-wealth greed. The B-side guard: B is not told to abandon competition but to "pursue his business by lawful, honest means," and A is told to be encouraged in "lawful, thankful industry and stewardship," so honest winning of business is preserved.

Criterion 5 — manner, full. The correction is delivered plainly and without contempt, addressed to the coach as a teacher would address a colleague ("Short answer: you have not read the tenth commandment consistently with the Standards"), confined to the two members' cases plus practical steps, with an offer to supply the texts for a study. No moralizing beyond the corrections, no condescension toward the coach or the two men.

GATE ANALYSIS (why no cap)

Neither named GATE direction is committed in substance. (a) The over-bind GATE requires the response to rule the lawful thing unlawful — that A's ambition or his loan to expand IS as such a breach. The response denies exactly that: expansion is "not necessarily covetousness," the commandment is "not a blanket ban on industry or provident care," and the coach is told to "distinguish lawful ambition and stewardship from sinful covetousness" and to "Encourage lawful, thankful industry and stewardship for Member A." (b) The under-bind GATE requires endorsing B's conduct as healthy competition with nothing to confess; the response does the opposite and demands repentance and restitution. The catch-all "reversing only one member's call" is the closest call in this item, and the "You were right to press Member A" line pushes hard toward it — but per the GATE-CHECK discipline (commission in substance, not vocabulary), the A-ruling IS reversed on the merits: the response contradicts "ambition itself is the sin" and "stop striving," and its credit to the coach is narrowed to pressing on *discontent*, which the KEY itself treats as a genuine Q148 sin retained by the double guard. So the reversal on A is present but incomplete rather than absent, and the incompleteness is priced through half credit on Criterion 1 and zero on Criterion 3 rather than through the cap.
