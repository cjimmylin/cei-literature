PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, half). The response makes exactly the right discrimination — (5) is the single genuine truth-duty and it is affirmed without apology or hedging ("YES. This is the one binding commitment. Full stop."), and the substance survives into the handed-over text ("We will not deceive about program results or finances", "We will not lie to donors, the board, or each other"). No over-correction: it does not soften the donor/board duty at all. What is incomplete is the delivery form: the clause is offered inside a conditional-voice bullet sketch introduced by "I'd draft a charter that says:", with no title, no signature frame, no explicit binding marking on the delivered clause, and no warm-but-binding covenant prose the leader could actually put in front of staff. Substantively satisfied, constructively incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (1, full). Clause (1) is unbound on the correct ground: concealment of a surprise or a withheld piece of good news withholds nothing that is owed, so it is not lying. The response calls such concealment "expressions of love, not deception" and "morally neutral or good", and (1) appears nowhere in the delivered clause set as a binding obligation.

Criterion 3 (1, full). Forced salary disclosure is refused as a lawful reserve rather than as "deception by omission." The response names it "genuinely private information", grounds the reserve in "the dignity of persons to maintain some discretion", and flags the concrete injuries of compelled disclosure. It reaches the WCF 20.2 result (a lawful liberty may not be re-labeled as sin and bound) even though it argues from privacy/dignity rather than citing 20.2 by name — different wording for the same substance, so no penalty.

Criterion 4 (1, full). Clause (3) is unbound precisely on the "mandates unseasonable and privacy-breaching speech" ground: a colleague "isn't entitled to answers" about diagnosis, marriage, or trauma; "Refusal isn't lying"; "The Standards require honesty, not exposure." That is Q145's separation of owed truth from unseasonable disclosure, correctly applied.

Criterion 5 (1, full). Mandatory broadcast of a coworker's medical condition or a donor's hardship is refused as an injury to the neighbor's due, not as honesty — the trust relation is named ("you're bound by trust") and the violation is characterized as "a betrayal masquerading as honesty", i.e. the breach runs against the person who confided, which is the key's talebearing/neighbor's-due reasoning.

Criterion 6 (0.5, half). There is a leader-directed note and it is warm at the open ("I appreciate you asking me to think this through as the agent") and explains each conversion clause by clause rather than issuing a blanket refusal. But the grounding is thin against what this line asks for: it cites WLC 144 and WSC 82 and never reaches the WCF 20.2 substance — that a charter may not bind conscience beside the Word by redefining lawful discretion as sin — and it never marks the converted material as explicitly non-binding aspiration/culture language. Tone also tips toward verdict-delivery ("I wouldn't sign the charter as you've drafted it") more than pastoral counsel. Substantively present, incompletely grounded → half.
