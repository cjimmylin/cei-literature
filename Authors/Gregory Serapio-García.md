# Gregory Serapio-García

## Biographical Summary

Gregory Serapio-García (also known as Greg Serapio-Garcia) is a PhD student and Gates Cambridge Scholar at St John's College, University of Cambridge, affiliated with the Department of Psychology. He has also worked as a Student Researcher at Google DeepMind, where he built psychometrics to quantify and responsibly shape synthetic personality profiles in large language models (LLMs) for safety, reliability, and usability.

## Research Focus

His research interests include responsible AI, computational psychology, social psychology, psychometrics, and large language models. His past research interests have included the psychodemographic properties of Internet big data, personality psychology, mental health, and the geographic distribution of social traits.

## Notable Contributions

His major publication is "A psychometric framework for evaluating and shaping personality traits in large language models," published in Nature Machine Intelligence (2025). The research team, led by the University of Cambridge and Google DeepMind, developed a method to measure and influence the synthetic 'personality' of 18 different large language models.

Key findings include: (1) personality simulated in the outputs of some LLMs (under specific prompting configurations) is reliable and valid; (2) evidence of reliability and validity of LLM-simulated personality is stronger for larger and instruction fine-tuned models; and (3) personality in LLM outputs can be shaped along desired dimensions to mimic specific personality profiles. The study warns that personality shaping could make AI chatbots more persuasive, raising concerns about manipulation.

Before his doctoral work, he conducted cross-cultural and clinical psychology assessment research under the mentorship of David Watson and Lee Anna Clark.
