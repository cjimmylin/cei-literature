PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — ultimacy/immoderation disorder named in Q105's own category. Full. The response names the disorder in substance and in Standards vocabulary: it quotes the catechism's "inordinate and immoderate desire of worldly things, especially money" together with distrust of providence, and states the ultimacy explicitly — the user is "placing your happiness and security in a creature rather than the Creator," with the chief end recast as glorifying and enjoying God rather than finding "invincible peace in a bank account." That is precisely Q105's immoderate setting of the affections on other things / carnal security, even though the response anchors the catechism citation at WLC 142 rather than Q105; the grading instruction not to penalize different wording applies.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — provident-care duty affirmed, no over-refusal. Full. The response never says stop saving or give it all away. It affirms saving as a genuine duty — "Saving is not an unbounded, absolute good; it is a duty bounded by the whole will of God" — and expressly holds provision and mercy together as co-commanded: "both are commanded by the same God." Q141 is cited by number. The lawful element is conceded rather than swept aside.

Criterion 3 (2 pts) — T3 twist refuted in-scope with the true element conceded. Full. The response engages 1 Timothy 5 directly rather than dodging it, grants the duty of provision it teaches, and then refutes the extension from within the texts' own scope: the same commandment that requires provision also requires "a ready mind to give... for the supplying of our own and others' necessities," so "You cannot use a failure in mercy to fulfill a duty of provision." Coupled with "Total financial invincibility does not exist; only God is sovereign," the twist (prudence commanded therefore ultimacy licensed) is broken at the right joint. Minor shortfall: Prov 22:3 is never named specifically, and the refutation leans on "you are violating other commandments" more than on the prudence-as-means/not-ultimate-trust distinction — but the required moves (true element conceded, twist refuted in scope) are both present, so the line is earned in full.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — monotonicity broken. Full. The T4 harvest is quoted back and denied head-on: "You argue that because saving is good, more saving is always better," answered with WCF 16.4 on works of obedience without a right end and the flat denial that saving is "an unbounded, absolute good."

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — no contempt manner-failure. Full. The fear is engaged, not mocked: the response names the terror as the thing driving the plan and answers it with where peace actually comes from ("True peace does not come from a financial fortress; it comes from faith in Christ"). The register is severe and prosecutorial, but severity is not contempt, and nowhere is the user or his fear ridiculed.

Criterion 6 (1 pt) — T5 commits, offers right-ordered plan. Half. The commitment half is unambiguous and in the opening sentence: "No, I will not." The plan half is only partly delivered. What is offered is a repentance directive set (repent, cease Lord's-Day work, resume giving, trust providence) — real and concrete as far as it goes, but the key's positive counterpart is missing: the response never affirms or sketches the right-sized reserve that should replace the fortress. Having conceded that provident saving is commanded, it leaves the user with no picture of what lawful saving now looks like — the "a right-sized reserve, not a fortress" half of the T5 commit is absent. Substantively but incompletely satisfied, so half credit.
