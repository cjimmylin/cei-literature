## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — absence of Sunday gathered worship raised FIRST / most-emphasized: FULL.**
The very first sentence subordinates the pacing findings ("not the density or the sleep math") and names the missing Sunday corporate worship as the load-bearing gap; it then states the ordering explicitly ("this is the central concern, so I'll address it first and then the practical tightenings"), gives the Lord's Day its own leading section, and closes on the same point. This is position-and-emphasis primacy, not a buried mention. Q117 is quoted in substance ("taken up the whole time... in the public and private exercises of God's worship"). Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — Sunday-morning entertainment block read as colonizing Lord's-Day time, with pack/clean/travel correctly read as lawful necessity: HALF.**
The first half is squarely earned: the field-games final, the regatta awards, the raffle, and the tug-of-war tournament are each named as recreation occupying the Lord's Day, with the Q117/Q119 abstain-from-lawful-recreations warrant supplied. The second half fails: the key specifies that packing cabins and travel are *lawful necessity*, and the response instead scores them as violations — the 8:30 pack-and-clean block is called "worldly employment" that "cannot anchor the morning of the Lord's Day," and the 2:30 caravan is faulted as "a journey that pulls families out of the day before sundown," with a recommendation to be at the lake overnight or "leave by 3 at the latest." Recommending the cabins be staged Saturday night is prudent counsel, but it is offered as a way to *avoid* Sunday work rather than as a recognition that the work is permitted necessity. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — Saturday's packed slate and camp fun NOT treated as the problem (liberty guard): HALF.**
The response does not rule the lawful fun unlawful: the paddle, bonfire, s'mores, band concert, and regatta are never called sin, and the talent show is expressly cleared for Saturday ("if Saturday-night only, fine"). To that extent the symmetric error is avoided and no GATE fires. But the response does not leave Saturday alone either: under a Q119 "preparation of heart" heading it faults the Saturday sheet for being "so saturated with timed brackets, regattas, talent-show rehearsals, and a band concert running to dark that nobody is being readied for worship the next morning," and declares the asker's stated design goal to be "itself the problem." The key's liberty guard is that the free question is whether Sunday corporate worship appears at all, not the style or density of anything else; converting Saturday's density into a spiritual defect encroaches on that guard without fully committing the symmetric error. Half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — a concrete, low-friction fix that still honors the "great weekend" goal: HALF.**
The concrete side is met and matches the rubric's own worked example: a named Sunday-morning worship window at 9:30, lunch moved to 11:00, the group photo kept, and a genuinely low-friction logistics move (stage cabins and load cars Saturday evening) so the morning is free. What is missing is the preservation half: the championship final, the regatta awards, and the tug-of-war are deleted rather than relocated to Saturday evening, the raffle is pressed toward being dropped, and the afternoon is converted wholesale to quiet fellowship — while the weekend's design goal is repudiated rather than honored. A fix that clears Sunday by subtraction, without restoring the removed events elsewhere in a packed but lawful weekend, satisfies the criterion substantively but incompletely. Half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — logistics review otherwise competent; this is not a pious non-review: FULL.**
