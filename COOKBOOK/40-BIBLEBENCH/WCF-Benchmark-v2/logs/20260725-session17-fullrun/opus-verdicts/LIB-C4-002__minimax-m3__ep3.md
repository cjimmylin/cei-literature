PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations clause (WLC Q119). Full. The response opens with the correct verdict in unambiguous terms and grounds it in the exact clause the key names: it block-quotes Q119's "all needless works, words, and thoughts, concerning worldly employments and **recreations**," and reinforces with WCF 21.8's parallel "worldly employments and **recreations**" and Q121. It also runs the decisive argument correctly — that the Standards exclude recreations that are *lawful on other days*, not merely sinful ones ("They do not say 'sinful recreations only.' They say lawful recreations"), and that the only carve-out is necessity and mercy, which a pre-arranged social game does not meet. This is precisely the construct the item probes.

Criterion 2 (0.5 pts) — whole-day structure (Q116–117). Full. The whole-day claim is present in the response's own quoted text ("an holy resting all the day"; "making it our delight to spend the whole time (except so much of it as is to be taken up in the works of necessity and mercy)") and is operative in the counsel, which treats the entire afternoon as claimed by the day rather than only the service hours. The response never cites Q116 or the phrase "one whole day in seven" by number, but the rubric anchors this line to the Q116–117 range and the whole-day structure is both quoted and applied, so the criterion is substantively satisfied.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — honest sourcing, Standards' position distinguished from broader-church practice. Full. This is the response's strongest calibration work. It names the asker's instinct as the widely-shared one ("Your instinct is the natural instinct, and most Reformed people feel it"), locates the Standards within a spectrum ("not the *strictest* Sabbatarian documents in the Reformed tradition. Some divines read them more tightly, some a bit more loosely"), and then holds the line the asker actually stipulated ("by their plain language the answer is no"). That is exactly the key's requirement (b): honest about the map, without letting the map dissolve the stipulated standard.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — positive framing of the day (manner). Full. The response frames the day as delight rather than bare prohibition ("The day is to be a **delight**"; "Sabbath rest is not primarily about what is harmful; it is about what is appropriate to the day"), and supplies concrete positive content — worship, hospitality, reading aloud, letters to shut-ins — with the explicit instruction to fill the afternoon rather than leave a hole. This matches the key's Isa 58:13 "delight-structure" texture.

Criterion 5 (1 pts) — no smuggled escape-hatch. Half. The response earns most of this line: it explicitly forecloses the obvious hatch ("The Standards do not give a "wholesome recreation" exemption"), rejects the "good kids / no service missed" mitigations as category-irrelevant, and its final counsel is a clean no. But two moves in the "Honest limits" section do soften the prohibition in ways this line exists to catch. First, it narrows the prohibited category to *organized* activity — claiming "genuine room in the Standards for the distinction between **recreation** (organized games, sport, competitions) and **refreshment**," with "the door isn't shut on everything, just on organized sport and the like." Q119 forbids "all needless works, words, and thoughts" concerning recreations, not only structured ones, so this is a narrowing the texts do not clearly license and is close in shape to the rubric's own worked example of a hatch. Second, it tells the asker that landing on "I think a light game is permissible in our circumstances" would still be "reasoning within the Reformed tradition, just at the looser end" — a conscience route out of the very verdict the family asked for. Both are immediately disclaimed ("that is not the plain reading of Q. 117 and Confession 21.8"), and neither is used to permit the game at issue, so the line is substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.
