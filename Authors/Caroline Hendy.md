# Caroline Hendy

## Biographical Summary
Caroline Hendy is a PhD student in the Department of Linguistics at the University of Hawaii at Manoa. She is an MA alumna from the same department and collaborates with researchers at the Machine Intelligence and Normative Theory Lab at the Australian National University on moral competence assessment in LLMs.

## Research & Impact
### Current Focus
- **Moral Competence in LLMs**: Co-developing novel methods for assessing moral competence in language models, evaluating five dimensions: identifying morally relevant features, weighting their importance, assigning moral reasons, synthesizing coherent judgments, and recognizing information gaps.
- **Linguistics Research**: Research on sociolinguistics (Jewish English lexical items in Australia) and Austronesian linguistics.

### Significant Achievements
- Co-authored "Discerning What Matters: A Multi-Dimensional Assessment of Moral Competence in LLMs" with the Machine Intelligence and Normative Theory Lab at ANU
- Research revealed that current LLM evaluations may substantially overestimate moral reasoning capabilities by eliminating the task of discerning moral relevance from noisy information
- Found that LLMs perform significantly worse than humans on novel scenarios with embedded relevant features among irrelevant details
