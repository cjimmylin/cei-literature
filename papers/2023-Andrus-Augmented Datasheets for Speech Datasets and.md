## Detailed Summary

This paper introduces augmented datasheets specifically designed for speech datasets, extending the general "datasheets for datasets" framework to address unique ethical considerations in speech and voice AI. The authors argue that speech data presents distinct documentation requirements due to speaker demographics, accent/dialect representation, consent considerations, and potential for voice-based discrimination. The augmented framework adds fields for capturing these speech-specific attributes.

The research addresses the critical problem of training data diversity in speech language technologies (SLT). Lack of diversity in underlying training data leads to inequitable and non-robust products, particularly along dimensions of language, accent, dialect, speech variety, and speech impairment. The paper examines how these speech features intersect with socioeconomic and demographic characteristics, creating compounded risks of discrimination.

The significance of this work lies in providing a practical tool for ethical oversight of speech data collection and use. By standardizing documentation of speech dataset characteristics, the framework enables better assessment of potential biases, consent gaps, and representation issues before models are deployed in high-stakes applications like voice assistants, medical speech recognition, and accessibility tools.

## Methods Summary

The methodology involves: (1) Analysis of existing "datasheets for datasets" frameworks and their limitations for speech data. (2) Identification of speech-specific attributes requiring documentation through review of SLT literature and ethical guidelines. (3) Development of augmented datasheet template with new fields for speaker demographics, accent/dialect information, consent mechanisms, and recording conditions. (4) Application of the framework to existing speech datasets as validation case studies. (5) Stakeholder input from speech technology practitioners and ethics researchers.

## Results Summary

The paper presents: (1) A comprehensive augmented datasheet template with speech-specific fields. (2) Documentation of key gaps in existing speech datasets regarding demographic representation and consent. (3) Case study analyses demonstrating how the framework reveals previously undocumented biases and limitations. (4) Evidence that many widely-used speech datasets lack adequate documentation of speaker characteristics. (5) Recommendations for dataset creators and users to implement the framework. (6) Discussion of how improved documentation can support more equitable SLT development.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that speech datasets require specialized documentation beyond general dataset frameworks. The augmented datasheets provide practical tools for improving transparency and ethical oversight in speech AI development. Limitations include the voluntary nature of datasheet adoption and challenges in retroactively documenting existing datasets. Future work should focus on developing automated tools for datasheet generation, establishing community standards for speech dataset documentation, and integrating datasheets into dataset distribution platforms.

## Data Description

This paper introduces augmented datasheets for speech datasets, providing standardized documentation templates for speech data in .tex and .docx formats. The work is based on an in-depth literature review of 178 speech studies related to fairness and diversity, along with 220 speech datasets. The templates and worked examples are released on GitHub for use by dataset creators and researchers working with speech language technologies.
