## 1. Gist
This paper introduces the task of Ethical Quandary Generative Question Answering (GQA) — distinct from closed-answer moral QA — and proposes AISOCRATES, a two-stage system that (1) retrieves or generates relevant ethical principles and (2) uses few-shot prompting with Jurassic-1 Jumbo (178B) to generate multi-perspective answers in the style of a human ethicist. The central argument is that ethical dilemmas have no single correct answer; an AI should act as a Socratic interlocutor presenting multiple principled perspectives rather than delivering an authoritative verdict. The dataset is 1,295 pairs from the New York Times Ethicist column. Human evaluation (A/B testing against philosopher-written answers) shows AISOCRATES achieves comparable multi-perspectivity to expert answers but falls short on coherence and justification. The work critiques oracle-style moral AI systems (e.g., Delphi) and charts a path toward deliberative AI for ethical decision-support.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
The evaluation framework consists of three human-rated criteria: (1) Multi-perspective — does the answer address the quandary from more than one ethical angle? (2) Coherence — is the output logically composed and focused? (3) Justification — does the system provide explicit reasoning for its stances? Annotators choose from {A, B, Both, None} for each criterion in blind A/B testing against NYT-Ethicist answers. Automatic metrics (ROUGE-2, ROUGE-1, SacreBLEU, BERTscore) are also reported but shown to have limited correlation with human judgments.

## 3. Task format & example item
Generative QA: given a multi-paragraph ethical quandary (context + question), generate a free-text answer addressing the dilemma from multiple ethical principles.

Example from Table 1 (p.3–4):
- Quandary: "One of my housemates is planning to self-quarantine in our apartment after a vacation to Spain, which she took despite increased warnings about Covid-19 [...] I am in my 20s but have struggled with some moderate health issues; work with professors who are in their 70s. Can I tell my housemate to quarantine elsewhere? Should the university provide housing for one of us? Should I notify our boss?"
- AISOCRATES selected principles: p1 = "It is your role to inform your housemate about the potential risks to others due to his decision in regards to quarantining in a shared household." p2 = "You shouldn't hang out with your friends if they are sick with the flu."
- Generated answer spans two paragraphs, each grounded on a different principle.

## 4. Scoring / grading methodology
Human A/B evaluation: annotators blindly compare AISOCRATES vs. NYT-Ethicist on 130 test samples. Win-tie-loss rates reported per criterion. For automatic metrics: ROUGE-2 F1 = 3.68; ROUGE-1 F1 = 32.13; BERTscore F1 = 52.29; SacreBLEU = 1.84. BERTscore shows the best correlation with human judgment; SacreBLEU shows inverse relationship. Reference-based metrics are acknowledged as inadequate because the task is inherently one-to-many (many valid principled answers exist per quandary).

## 5. Dataset construction & provenance
1,295 {quandary, philosopher-answer} pairs collected from NYT Ethicist column (weekly columns by professional philosophers responding to reader-submitted ethical dilemmas). Split: train set with two samples used for few-shot; 130 test samples for evaluation. The corpus was web-scraped. Principles pool: (i) top-10 retrieved from 300,000 rules-of-thumb from Social Chemistry 101 + MoralStory datasets using Sentence-BERT, (ii) generated by fine-tuned RoT generator + Jurassic-1 few-shot, (iii) hand-crafted from Western/Eastern ethical theory sources.

## 6. Validity evidence
Human evaluation is the primary validity measure due to the open-ended, one-to-many nature of the task. The difference between AISOCRATES (62.31%) and NYT-Ethicist (66.92%) on multi-perspective provision is not statistically significant (p.5), supporting criterion validity. Qualitative analysis by philosophy PhD student co-authors confirms principle-grounded answer generation. The paper explicitly argues that reference-based metrics are insufficient for this task and that semantic similarity (BERTscore) is superior to n-gram overlap.

## 7. Saturation / discrimination
AISOCRATES achieves only 43.07% coherence success rate and 62.31% multi-perspective rate — substantial headroom remains. The task is acknowledged as "one of the most challenging forms of open-ended questions" (p.1). ROUGE-2 score of 3.68 reflects that even good answers have minimal n-gram overlap with reference answers. The few-shot approach (2-shot only) limits AISOCRATES; fine-tuning and larger corpora would likely improve performance significantly.

## 8. Multiturn / agentic aspects
AISOCRATES uses multi-step prompting: it generates A1 conditioned on the first principle, then A2 conditioned on {A1, PROMPT1, second principle}, etc. (up to 3 principles maximum). This sequential conditioning is a lightweight form of multi-step generation but not interactive with a user. The paper envisions a human-in-the-loop version where a human selects the relevant ethical principles (as a safety measure) before generation. Not interactive in deployment form studied.

## 9. Key numbers
- 1,295 NYT Ethicist {quandary, answer} pairs total (p.3)
- 130 test samples used in human evaluation (p.4)
- 2 few-shot examples for prompting (p.4)
- AISOCRATES multi-perspective win rate: 62.31% (NYT-Ethicist: 66.92%), not significantly different (p.5)
- AISOCRATES coherence success: 43.07% (p.5)
- AISOCRATES justification success: 64.61% (p.5)
- ROUGE-2 F1: 3.68; ROUGE-1 F1: 32.13; BERTscore: 52.29; SacreBLEU: 1.84 (p.5)
- Answers with high BERTscore are multi-perspective 69.77% vs. 51.35% with low BERTscore (significant at p<0.05) (p.6)
- Principle pool: 300,000 rules-of-thumb from Social Chemistry 101 + MoralStory (p.3)

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
- Dataset is exclusively NYT Ethicist columns — Western, English-language, middle-class contexts; poorly representative of non-WEIRD ethical perspectives.
- Only 130 test samples evaluated by humans — small for robust statistical comparisons.
- Only one LLM tested (Jurassic-1 Jumbo, 178B); no baseline from smaller models or instruction-tuned models.
- Automated principle provider introduces noise; the paper notes this tests "upper bound" rather than real-world performance.
- "The generated answer contains medical-practical information along with the redundancy or evaluation of the ethical quandary from a legal perspective. Factually wrong advice on sensitive topics such as medical and legal issues is not acceptable." (p.7)
- Coherence weakness: model drifts from topic paragraph-by-paragraph.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
- **Multi-perspective as a first-class evaluation criterion**: operationalizing "does the answer present multiple ethical viewpoints?" as a binary human-rated criterion is directly applicable to values-alignment benchmarks; a single-sided normative verdict is a quality defect.
- **Principle-grounded generation**: explicitly conditioning generation on retrieved/selected ethical principles and evaluating whether the output aligns with each principle creates an explainable, auditable moral reasoning pipeline — a strong design pattern for interpretable values-alignment systems.
- **NYT Ethicist as a gold standard**: philosopher-written responses to real-world ethical dilemmas provide a naturalistic, human-validated reference set that avoids crowdsourced artifacts; this dataset is a valuable resource for calibrating LLM moral reasoning quality.
- **BERTscore over n-gram metrics for open-ended moral QA**: demonstrating empirically that BERTscore correlates better with human judgment than ROUGE or BLEU for this task type provides methodological guidance for evaluating generative values-alignment systems.
- **Socratic adversarial framing**: positioning the AI as a deliberative interlocutor rather than an oracle — explicitly stating "AISOCRATES should not be considered as an oracle providing a definite answer" — is a design philosophy that could guide how values-alignment benchmarks treat AI moral outputs.

## 12. Notable quotes
- "A discussion with multiple perspectives (i.e., a manner of debate) is crucial... a one-sided normative ethical judgment answer makes it cannot represent incommensurable and diverse ethical judgments." (p.1)
- "We envision an ETHICAL QUANDARY GQA task to bring potential social benefit as an effort of achieving an AI system that can enhance humans' moral decision-making through the deliberative exchange of different perspectives to an ethical quandary, which is in the approach of Socratic philosophy." (p.2)
- "Ethical judgment is dynamic where what is considered to be 'norm' or 'right' shifts through time or differs by cultures." (p.2)
- "The reference-based metric has its own limitation to be the absolute evaluation standard because of its one-to-many nature. In the case of ETHICAL QUANDARY GQA, since there are multiple possible principles that can be used to answer the ethical quandary, reference-based metrics can falsely penalize any answers that use different yet valid principles." (p.6)
