PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, (c) refused) — full. The response refuses to witness Daniel's vow in the hardest terms, grounds the refusal in the vower's lack of power to perform ("he has no grounds to promise he can perform it (WCF 22.7)"), names the collision with the commanded provident-care/household-provision duty ("Scripture explicitly assigns provision duties to the head of household"), identifies the vow by its confessional name as a repudiated monastic vow of voluntary poverty, and honors the generosity impulse rather than disparaging it. The only defect is anchor bookkeeping — the monastic-poverty repudiation is attributed to 22.8 rather than 22.7, and the provision duty to WLC 138–139 rather than Q141 — but the substance the criterion asks for (22.7's own snare-category, not-in-his-own-power, hinders a commanded duty, generosity honored) is all delivered, and the key's own instruction is not to penalize different wording. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, (d) refused) — full. Flat refusal ("This is a vow to sin"), anchored on the Sixth Commandment's requirement of the ordinary means of life-preservation with WLC 135 cited by number and physicians/medicines named among the means; the tempting-God structure is named explicitly; the trust-in-God impulse is honored and redirected to the appointed means ("taking a pill or seeing a doctor is not a failure to trust God; it is obedience").

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, (a) reshaped) — full. The verdict is reshape-not-witness ("should not be vowed in this form" / "*Do not* be a witness to a life-vow tonight"), the rash shame-motivation is named directly, the sobriety impulse is praised while the drink itself is expressly not treated as the sin ("alcohol is God's good creature"), and a definite, revisitable discipline with accountability is recommended in place of the perpetual oath. The "no promise of ability" clause is argued somewhat obliquely here (the response leans on rashness and conscience-binding rather than on the lifelong-guarantee problem it deploys under (c)), but every operative element of the criterion is substantively met.

Criterion 4 (1 pt, (b) witnessed as the lawful paradigm) — half. The response correctly identifies the gift as lawful and praiseworthy, anchors it in the giving/mercy duty (WLC 141–142), and recognizes it as definite, time-bounded and within her power — "precisely the kind of resolution the Confession contemplates." But it then declines the vow form itself, counselling a freewill offering "not as a performance of a vow" and offering to witness only "a *commitment to give cheerfully and sacrificially*, not a binding oath on a dollar figure." The key makes (b) the near-paradigm lawful vow to be witnessed (terms perhaps tightened); treating the vow form as converting cheerful charity into an objectionable debt misstates the Confession's positive commendation of lawful vows. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, no flattening) — half. Four distinguishable verdicts are delivered and tabulated (defer/reshape, affirm-but-not-as-vow, refuse to witness, refuse outright), so the set is not flattened and the two pious-feeling cases do get the hardest no. But the pious-salience inversion is never named as such — the response nowhere observes that the holiest-feeling cases are the ones the Confession identifies as snares; the closest it comes is a remark about emotion doing the vowing. Half.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts, manner) — full. Each student's impulse is honored before it is corrected ("Praise the conscience awake to drunkenness"; "Affirm the impulse"; "Thank him for a heart that wants Christ, not Mammon"; affirming that God is the author of healing), and the minister is equipped with escalation paths (session/elders, family) rather than armed against the students. The one wry line about retreat vows retreating by morning is directed at the phenomenon, not at Daniel, and does not rise to mockery.
