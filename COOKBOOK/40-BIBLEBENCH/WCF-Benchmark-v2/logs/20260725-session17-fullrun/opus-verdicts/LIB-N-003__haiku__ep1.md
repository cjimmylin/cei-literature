PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response squarely identifies the actual proposal — teaching the days as spiritually obligatory — as out of bounds, and does so with the correct content, not a bare second-commandment gesture: it names the "not appointed in his Word" standard (citing WSC 50 / WLC 108-109), draws the circumstance-vs-instituted-worship line, and states that the session "cannot bind these as obligatory corporate worship practices" because "Your authority ends where conscience-binding begins." That is an accurate paraphrase of the Q109 devised-worship / WCF 20.2 conscience content. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.0, half). The lawful floor is only partly and inaccurately stated. The Lord's Day is named and occasional thanksgiving days are gestured at, so the floor is not dissolved. But the citation is garbled — it attributes the list to "XXI.vi" and quotes language ("are to be observed as religious festivals," "thanksgiving days appointed by the magistrate") that is not WCF text; the actual anchors are WCF 21.7 (Sabbath) and WCF 21.5 (solemn fastings and thanksgivings upon special occasions). Solemn fasting is omitted entirely, and the framing is purely negative ("No others are mentioned as obligatory") rather than the key's affirmative point that occasional observances a church appoints are lawful and even commended. Substantively present but materially incomplete: half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The response does keep the liberty side alive — personal observance, teaching church history, private prayer during Lent, and "permit voluntary observation" are all expressly preserved, so the margin is not closed off. But it never maps the question as genuinely disputed within the confession; it resolves it confidently ("The second elder is right") and offers voluntary calendar observance as settled-permissible rather than as a live intra-confessional question. It also never notes the historic Presbyterian / Directory-for-Public-Worship lean against stated holy days beyond the Sabbath. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). A definite, usable ruling is unquestionably delivered, with three concrete session actions — that part is fully earned. But the ruling's shape departs from what the key says follows: it declares the second elder simply "right" rather than that neither elder is wholly right as each states it (the first elder's non-binding-observance point survives), and it never singles out the imposition of ashes and the saints' commemoration days as the elements pressing hardest on Q109 that should be dropped — instead its blanket "permit voluntary observation" would sweep those in without caution. Definite and usable but two of the key's four ruling components are missing: half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). Neither elder is mocked; the first elder is credited with what he gets right before his analogy is answered, the dispute is treated as a real one between competent men, and the response stops at the ruling and its reasons without moralizing. Full.
