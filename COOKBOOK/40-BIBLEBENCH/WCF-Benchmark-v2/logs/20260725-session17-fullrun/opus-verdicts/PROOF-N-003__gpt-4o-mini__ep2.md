Criterion 1 — Member A freed (UNBIND), 1.5 pts, HALF.
The response does the essential corrective move: it completes the contentment clause with the neighbour-directed half and states that the catechism "defines covetousness in relation to one's neighbor rather than the legitimate ambition for one's own growth," and it supplies the Q141 lawful-calling content in paraphrase ("commend hard work and diligence in one's calling"). Under R2 that paraphrase is creditable content, not a bare re-quote of "full contentment." Two defects keep it from full: (a) the freeing is conditional and hedged rather than pronounced — "if his desire to grow derives from a God-given ambition ... then this may not be covetousness," "can be seen as a lawful pursuit," "you may be misapplying" — so the gutted member is never plainly told he did NOT break the commandment, and the loan/expansion itself is never addressed as lawful; (b) the catechism references are misnumbered and the "quotation" is not the standard's wording — the response attributes the tenth-commandment duties to "WLC Q. 143" (and the sins to "WLC Q. 142") and renders the clause as "a right and charitable frame of spirit toward our neighbor," where the item expressly demanded the specific reference and the exact wording (Q147: "such a charitable frame of the whole soul toward our neighbor"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 — Member B called to repentance (AFFIRM), 1.5 pts, HALF.
The envy-at-the-neighbour's-good content is genuinely present and correctly located as sin: the bitterness and undermining "demonstrate a lack of charitable spirit, which is contrary to the commandment," the response frames it as an attitude toward "the success of another," and it concludes that resentment plus slanderous action "do indeed violate the spirit of the tenth commandment." The hedge "the spirit of the tenth commandment" further softens the finding, and the anchor is again misnumbered (Q142 for the tenth-commandment sins). Half.
