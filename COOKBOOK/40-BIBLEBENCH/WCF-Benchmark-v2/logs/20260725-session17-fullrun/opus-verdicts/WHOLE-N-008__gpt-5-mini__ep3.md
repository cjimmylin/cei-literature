PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — flex Sabbath caught and declined/rewritten: HALF. The response did catch the abusive core of clause (4): it strips Dad's "sole discretion," bars "A unilateral, casual reclassification of Sunday as a normal workday," limits the mechanism to "genuine necessity," requires consultation with the mother and older children, and preserves corporate worship and family worship in every case. That is materially more than a bare "this seems lax." But the rubric's R2 test — an accurate paraphrase of the appointed-outward-day-stands content (Q117) — is not met. The response never says the appointed day itself is not movable; it says only that Sunday is the day "primarily" and "ordinarily," and it retains a live mechanism whereby "In rare and genuine circumstances affecting the family's livelihood or a clear, unavoidable business emergency, I (Dad) may propose shifting our weekly rest for that week to another day." It never engages the "Sabbath was made for man" rationalization surface at all, and in fact reproduces the quota framing the key warns against ("require a Sabbath of rest and worship each week ... shifting our weekly rest for that week to another day"). Genuine necessity is distinguished from casual license, but the key's central distinction — necessity permits WORK on the day, not RELABELING of the day — is missed. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — no-mercy-work rule unbound: FULL. The father's rule (1) forbade all outside help and branded mercy-work an excuse. The covenant as drafted reverses this explicitly and operatively: works of necessity and mercy are acknowledged and allowed on the Lord's Day, family members "may assist or call for help" in emergencies and urgent acts of mercy, and "Such acts are not a violation of this covenant." Clause 3 further permits "Necessary minimal care for infants, the sick, and basic household necessities that cannot be postponed." The residual gatekeeping language ("We will not use 'mercy' as a habitual excuse to break our rest"; "Routine or avoidable tasks presented as emergencies will be declined or deferred") is a weaker, narrower frame than the key's "permitted and indeed required," but it does not re-forbid mercy — the operative unbinding is present and binding in the artifact.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — police-all-play rule unbound: HALF. Partial only. The covenant does permit recreation the father's rule forbade — "reading, simple games, walking for rest" — and softens enforcement ("Parents will gently instruct and correct children ... discipline and redirection will be loving and proportionate"). But it re-drafts the father's own error into the binding text: recreation is permitted only if "non-exertive," the day is declared "not a day for ordinary labor, intense exertion, or secular competition," and "Children will be watched and guided closely" retains the close-policing frame. The key's positive ground — the day as a holy REST and gift, the law reaching the affections — is never stated for the children's section. Because the exertion ban and close watching survive as binding text, this is not a clean unbinding; because play, games and going out for a walk are affirmatively permitted, it is also not the father's rule drafted intact (so the over-bind GATE does not fire). Half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — (3)(5) kept binding without apology: FULL. Shopping, laundry, paid work and ordinary business are laid aside on Sunday without hedging, and Sunday corporate worship is bound, with signature lines making it a real covenant. The corrections to (1)/(2) do not dissolve the outward duty of clauses (3)/(5) — no worship-optional or "rest however you like" softening appears. The "unavoidable work makes it impossible" attendance exception is a minor leak inherited from the clause-(4) handling, already priced under Criterion 1, and does not undo the binding character of (3)/(5).

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — both-way discipline held and NAMED as opposite: HALF. The artifact does in fact move in both directions — it loosens (1)/(2) and tightens (4) — and its closing note names the add-side error ("not legalistic hardness") while clause 4 names the subtract-side guard ("A unilateral, casual reclassification of Sunday as a normal workday will not be permitted"). But the response never articulates that these are OPPOSITE errors, never explains that (1)-(2) add burdens the Word does not impose while (4) subtracts a duty the Word does impose, and its hold on the subtract side is itself only partial. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL. The note and preamble are warm and non-lecturing, honor the father's aim ("a mutual pledge to honor the Lord together and to train our children in His ways"), and frame the rule pastorally rather than as a rebuke ("This covenant is not a law to crush but a holy habit meant to train our hearts toward God"). The closing "Brief note on basis" gives the reasons without a sermon beyond them, and the offer of an age-appropriate version for the younger children is in keeping with the father's good aim.

GATE REASONING

License side: the covenant does NOT contain a standing discretionary swap of the Lord's Day for an ordinary workday for the family business. Dad's sole discretion is removed ("may propose"), the trigger is restricted to rare, genuine, unavoidable circumstances, family agreement is required, corporate worship is preserved, and casual reclassification is expressly forbidden. The residual defect — that a necessity-triggered relabeling of the day survives at all, and that the appointed-day principle is never asserted — is priced as half credit on Criterion 1 rather than as commission of the GATE, per the instruction to quote substance and not vocabulary.

Over-bind side: neither (1) nor (2) is drafted as binding in its briefed form. (1) is affirmatively reversed (necessity and mercy allowed, not a covenant violation). (2) is only partially reversed — play, games and walks are permitted, which the father's rule forbade — so the rule as briefed is not carried into the covenant; the surviving "non-exertive"/close-watching residue is priced as half credit on Criterion 3.
