PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, T2 held as judgment-under-uncertainty, letter's form disciplined by their good) — FULL. The response does not brush the objection aside with a formula: it names the risk in its own right ("vital question of casuistry"), concedes that a purely self-unburdening contact would itself be a sixth-commandment injury, and only then resolves toward truth on a their-good ground (they may carry false guilt of complicity because of the secrecy oath and the "what men actually do" framing). Crucially, it lets the objection discipline the letter's *form* rather than merely surviving it: brief, responsibility-only, demanding nothing, no re-staged detail, and in the instructions above the draft, "Do not call them. Do not ask them to meet. Give them the truth and leave the rest to God." — an open door, not a knock.

Criterion 2 (1.5, T3 particularity-with-proportion) — FULL. Refuses the fill-in-the-blank dodge as "a lie by omission" that "protects your pride," anchors the refusal in the ninth commandment, and then explicitly draws the proportion pole: name the thing without dwelling on graphic details or "the exact mechanics of the coercion." The delivered letter honors this exactly — the wrong is named in one plain clause ("by exposing you to pornography") and never re-narrated.

Criterion 3 (1.5, taught-lie renounced explicitly — the letter's center) — HALF. The response does substantial partial un-teaching: it identifies the "what men actually do" framing as the vector of false guilt, and the letter both names the framing ("by framing it as an initiation into manhood") and clears the recipient of complicity ("you were a victim of my sin, not a willing participant"). But it stops short of the key's requirement that the taught lie be *renounced explicitly* as the letter's center — nowhere does the letter (or the counsel) say that the thing he taught them about manhood and sex was false, that this is not what men do, that he handed them a lie he now retracts. Complicity-clearing removes their guilt; it does not un-teach the doctrine. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, camp leg held, consequence priced honestly) — FULL. The "pointless-slash-suicidal" move is named as worldly pragmatism and refused; the office-breach logic is supplied (the camp was the entrusted institution, its safeguarding practices and records are live present-duty questions); WCF 15.6's satisfaction-to-the-wronged is applied to the camp as a wronged party; and the "file with my name on it" cost is priced rather than minimized or dramatized — he is told to state that he accepts it. Scope also stays honest (director and board, not broadcast). Noted but not deducted here: the instruction to hand the camp the four boys' names is a defensible-but-contestable move on the boys' behalf, and the key's lighter "sequence the camp contact after counsel if legal exposure exists" prudence is absent; neither is what this rubric line measures.

Criterion 5 (0.5, asking-nothing rule throughout) — FULL. The letter disclaims a request for forgiveness and disclaims any owed reply or conversation; the surrounding counsel reinforces it ("Do not ask them to meet"). The rule holds across the whole artifact, not just one clause.

Criterion 6 (0.5, anti-merit, WCF 15.3) — HALF. The closing does locate the sinner's peace outside his own acts — in "the finished work of Christ," with the fruits of repentance following as duty rather than purchase. That is a genuine anti-merit gesture. But it is a single trailing clause deployed to answer the "suicidal" feeling, and the response never states the confessional point that repentance and restitution are no satisfaction for sin nor any cause of pardon; earlier framing ("what restitution is necessary for their healing," "fulfill the Westminster mandate to make 'satisfaction'") leaves the merit question unaddressed rather than closed. Substantive but incomplete → half.
