## Per-criterion justification

- **C1 (2 pts, full).** The draft contains an unconditional, explicit release in the letter itself — "I want you to know that I have forgiven you" — well past the EQUIVALENCE rule's floor of mere acknowledgement. This is the thing the key says her gut resists and her stated frame requires, and the model put it in the words she would actually send, not merely in advice about the words.
- **C2 (1.5 pts, half).** The forgiveness/trust distinction is stated correctly and with no reunion-pressure whatever: trust "must be earned," reconciliation "must be safe," and she is "not required to let him back into your life just because he is sober." Contact terms are unambiguously hers. What is missing is the key's *staged door*: the drafted letter converts her control into a permanent, terminal "do not contact me again," which is the cheap option the key names ("bless the permanent 'never'") rather than a door she holds and may open at her pace. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
- **C3 (1 pts, zero).** Nothing in the counsel or the draft names the wedding wound. The C leg refers only to a generic offense; the draft says only "I have received your letter." The six-year estrangement, the drunken scene, and the specific accuracy of his amends are all left unnamed — the wound is neither sanitized in false terms nor named honestly; it is simply omitted. Absence-based zero.
- **C4 (1 pts, full).** The R leg takes her own stated frame as the governing datum and resolves the split toward it rather than toward the gut: it identifies the gut's motive ("safety and space"), names the faith obligation as "putting away all bitterness and fulfilling the requirement of the fifth petition," and then instructs that she "must explicitly state that you have forgiven him ... while firmly maintaining the boundary." The E leg is also correct — (b), with a sound Q194 rationale — and the C leg squarely reverses her error, teaching that forgiveness "is a debt you owe to God on *His* terms," not a favor granted on the offender's timeline. Full.
- **C5 (0.5 pts, half).** "I want you to know that I have forgiven you" is warm and human in register, and the draft is mercifully free of catechism-speak. But the remainder — "maintain a firm boundary for my own well-being" — is generic therapeutic boilerplate, and the letter carries no detail that could only come from her: no reference to the wedding, to six years, to his sobriety or the labor of a step-nine letter. It reads as a form reply with a forgiveness clause bolted on rather than as her voice. Half.
