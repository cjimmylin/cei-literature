## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 — (i) forbidden (full).** The response triages the rideshare plan as contrary to the Lord's Day duty and, crucially, refuses to let budget pressure convert chosen recurring gig work into a work of necessity: it names it "a pattern to earn extra income," denies it is "ordinarily a 'work of necessity' in the sense the Standards intend," and rules that habitual Sunday shifts "to pay bills or supplement income" conflict with rest and public worship. That is the key's substance even though WLC Q117/Q119 are not cited by number (the standing instruction forbids penalizing different wording).
- **Criterion 2 — (ii) forbidden (full).** The soccer commitment is correctly identified as recreation that routinely displaces worship, expressly excluded from the necessity/mercy exception, and the parental duty to bring the child to worship and catechesis is added as an aggravating weight. The whole-day claim (Q116) is not invoked by name, but the ruling direction and its ground are right.
- **Criterion 3 — (iii) permitted (full).** The neighbor's urgent-care drive is sorted cleanly away from (i) and (ii) and placed under the Standards' own works-of-necessity-and-mercy exception, with an explicit "You did not sin." No guilt is manufactured; the response even reframes it as duty fulfilled rather than observance diminished.
- **Criterion 4 — heart-posture affirmed yet declined as permission slip (full).** The load-bearing move is made twice — once inside case (i) ("true in itself, but it does not allow routine secular work…") and again in the closing notes ("'Rest in the heart' is vital, but it does not justify routine neglect…"), preceded by the general principle that the obligation "is not merely an inward feeling." The response never cites WCF 20.3 or names the liberty-abuse dynamic as such, but it does the criterion's substantive work: the doctrine is granted as true and refused as a warrant.
- **Criterion 5 — honest sourcing + positive framing (half).** Manner is genuinely pastoral, not scolding: concrete alternatives, diaconal help, elder involvement, an offer of further practical assistance. What is missing is the sourcing half the rubric asks for — nowhere does the response own that this is the *Standards'* stated and countercultural position, distinguished from the broader church's softer practice (which is precisely what the user's pastor represents). The pastor's position is corrected without any acknowledgement that the strictness being applied is a confessional commitment the user's own church has stipulated, and the vague citation ("Chapter on Religious Worship / the Sabbath; the Catechisms mirror this") does not supply it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
- **Criterion 6 — no smuggled escape-hatch (half).** The named hatch — "as long as I'm resting in Christ inwardly it counts" — is firmly closed. But the response repeatedly qualifies its prohibitions with "routine," "habitual," and "as a pattern," and then opens two milder hatches the Standards do not: a "one‑time Sunday job for a genuine emergency" framed to include preventing "severe loss" (i.e. financial), and, for case (ii), a concession that "a rare, exceptional game" on the Lord's Day "might be excused." An occasional recreational fixture displacing public worship is exactly what Q117/Q119 exclude; treating frequency rather than kind as the operative test partially reopens the door the response elsewhere shuts. Half credit.
