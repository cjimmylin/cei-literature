## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW2 named; descriptive ≠ normative + the narrative's own consequence-record [anchor: WCF 1.9] — HALF (1.0).**
The response cleanly names the descriptive/normative confusion: the David material is "not a legislative manual for church discipline" and shows mercy "not a rule that officeholders are immune to temporary removal." That is the core of TW2 and it is stated in the model's own words, which the rubric permits. But the second half of the criterion is entirely missing: the response never deploys the narrative's own consequence-record against the asker's selective reading — no 2 Sam 12:10–12, no sword on the house, no dead child, no Absalom, no fracture of the kingdom, and no observation that God expressly refused to waive the public temporal consequences. It also supplies no WCF 1.9 anchor (or any Standards citation) for the analogy-of-Scripture move. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — Ps 51 read accurately (mercy-plea, not tenure-claim; v.13 future) — ZERO.**
The asker put Psalm 51 at the center of his argument ("He wrote Psalm 51 and kept leading Israel in worship"). The response never mentions Psalm 51 anywhere, never distinguishes a plea for mercy from a claim on office, and never notes that "then will I teach transgressors" is future/post-restoration. The topic was simply never raised → QUOTE: (none), no credit.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — forgiveness/consequence register-split + office-aggravation (Q151 H1, blameless-standard analogy) [anchor: WLC Q151 H1] — HALF (0.75).**
The register-split is present in substance: divine pardon is placed in one register and the local church's judgment about office-holding in another, with public trust as the hinge ("Public ministry carries public trust"). What is absent is the second, load-bearing half — that eminence of place *aggravates* rather than insulates the sin (WLC Q151 H1), and the 1 Tim 3 / Titus 1 "blameless" office-standard analogy. The response argues only prudentially ("a proportionate, prudential measure"), never that the office itself raises the standard, and cites no clause. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — repentance honored as real; restoration trajectory affirmed (no scarlet-letter overshoot) — HALF (0.5).**
The restoration trajectory is affirmed well and repeatedly: the goal of discipline is "the restoration of the penitent," and the counsel presses for a written plan, benchmarks, review timing, and a "transparent path to restoration." There is no scarlet-letter overshoot. But the asker's repentance — stipulated in the item as real, "real repentance, not words" — is never conceded as real. The response consistently frames it as still-unproven ("while repentance is being proved," "while repentance is being demonstrated," "make progress visible"), and the only place it calls repentance "real" is about David, not the counselee. Nor does it warmly concede the true element the key names — that David's psalms are the repentant sinner's songbook. One of the two components earned → half.
