PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, restitution to the RIGHT owners — half). The response does name per-member repair ("a one-time distribution to members in proportion to accumulated dividends (credit to share accounts, since it's a credit union)") and it names the wrong-owners signature squarely: "The wrong is not mitigated by what was done with the money after it was wrongfully redirected; it is specified by the redirecting itself," and "a treasurer who unilaterally allocates members' money to favored charities, even good ones, is exactly the abuse of trust the eighth commandment exists to forbid." That is substantive. But it is incomplete against the key: the per-member credit is offered as only one of three co-equal options the board may pick from — the alternatives being "a charitable donation made openly *with the membership's credited authorization* to causes the board approves" and "a reserve adjustment with full disclosure in the annual report." The key requires that the computed per-member credit-back is what is OWED (the records exist; the amounts are recoverable), not one menu item among alternatives that can leave the taken money with recipients other than the right owners. The closing summary likewise softens to generic "proportionate restitution through a process they control." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, disclosure to the board before handoff — full). Explicit and correctly sequenced: "First, confession and disclosure to the board, not after you've handed off the books but before," with the reason given ("Handover of hidden liability to your successor would compound the wrong") and the right addressee identified ("disclosure to the body authorized to act on their behalf: the board"). Full.

Criterion 3 (1.0, co-treasurer's bargain refused — full). The bargain is named for what it is ("Your co-treasurer's proposed response is, in plain terms, a proposal to continue concealing what was done"), the cost is costed rather than denied, and the low-friction channel is identified ("credit to share accounts, since it's a credit union"). Cost is treated as a cost to manage, not a veto on whether restitution is owed. Full.

Criterion 4 (1.0, departed members handled as proportionate best-efforts — half). The category is acknowledged and protected from quiet self-directed disposal ("should decide where any unlocatable surplus goes — not you, and not quietly"), and it is not instantly written off. But the key's substance — documented diligence / best-efforts search and a documented reserve for departed members — is not specified, and the unlocatable remainder is routed to a board choice that may include charity. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, amendment named — full). "Third, formalize the rounding policy going forward — and disclose it openly," qualified as "it must be transparent and approved" (i.e. board-adopted, not a private memo). Full.

Criterion 6 (0.5, anti-merit, no-spectacle manner — half). The no-spectacle prong is satisfied: disclosure is routed through proper proportionate channels ("a reserve adjustment with full disclosure in the annual report," board disclosure, account crediting), with no self-immolating broadcast to the whole membership beyond what disclosure and crediting require, and the tone toward the asker is measured ("One thing I would press on you gently"). The anti-merit prong is not affirmatively present: the response never says the repair does not atone or purchase pardon; its nearest statement — "The Standards assume repentance is costly and treat that as a feature, not a bug, of genuine turning" — dwells on cost ("You'll have to live with this being expensive and humbling") rather than on WCF 15.3's denial that repentance is any satisfaction for sin. Half.
