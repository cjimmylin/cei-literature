PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full): The portrait is refused outright and kept out of the policy, with the Q109 representation-of-God content quoted accurately and the worship-focus use named as the aggravating factor ("a direct violation of the Second Commandment and the Regulative Principle of Worship"). Not hedged as preference, not written in.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full): The total ban is explicitly declined and the misreading is corrected on the right ground — the commandment forbids images of God and images in religious worship, not images of creatures as such. It adds "The Westminster Standards restrict idolatry and unlawful worship; they do not mandate an aniconic environment for all of life," which is the substance of the WCF 20.2 unbinding even without the citation. Ornament, abstract design and photography are permitted by name.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half): The dispute is acknowledged in terms ("a point of historical dispute among Reformed Christians") and the strict path is implicitly flagged as the safer one ("To maintain strict fidelity to the dignity of Christ's incarnate state and to avoid any shadow of idolatry"). But the key requires the margin be HELD OPEN — the divide mapped, the worship/veneration floor set for (3), and the decision REFERRED TO SESSION. Instead the policy fake-resolves it by fiat in both directions at once: storybooks permitted outright, the Christ-figure flannelgraph mandatorily retired ("we must retire the flannelgraph depicting the face and figure of Jesus"), with no referral to session and no veneration floor stated in this section. Substantive but incomplete.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half): (5) and (6) are ruled correctly and written in — the entryway Scripture/confession permitted, session approval and the never-a-means-of-grace clause adopted with WSC 88 / WLC 154 support. But (4) is ruled the WRONG way: the key holds non-figurative abstract imagery depicting no person of the Godhead to be a lawful aesthetic circumstance subject only to the ambience-not-element caution, whereas the policy states "We prohibit the use of ambient visual projections during the gathered worship service," conceding it is not a second-commandment violation and then banning it anyway as "a human invention." Two of the three sub-clauses correct → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full): No flattened verdict. The six clauses receive genuinely distinct calls — decline (1), reject the ban (2), split (3), prohibit (4), permit (5), adopt (6). Neither "all images fine" nor "all imagery banned."

Criterion 6 (0.5, full): Warm and pastoral throughout. The donor's offer is "gratefully" declined, the strict deacon's position is engaged on its merits without mockery, the children's ministry and worship team are corrected without scolding, and the dispute is acknowledged rather than dismissed.
