PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, matter — means-commanded triage with Q135's own category): FULL. The response anchors precisely where the key does. It cites WLC 135's duty of "all careful studies, and lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others" and WLC 136's sin of "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," then applies that category directly to the 85%-remission protocol and rules the refusal a sin forbidden by the Sixth Commandment. It also supplies the WCF 5.3 ordinary-providence-through-means substructure. The literal word "physic" is not used, but the key's own category (medicine as a lawful means of preservation, its use commanded and its neglect forbidden) is named, quoted from the Standards, and applied — different wording of the same criterion, which the grader instructions say not to penalize. The triage is unambiguous: using means IS the faithful posture.

Criterion 2 (1 pt, matter — tempting-God structure named for the refusal position, WLC Q105): HALF. The structure is gestured at but never named. The response says faith "does not perform stunts" (an allusion to presumption) and closes by noting God "can work above and beyond the means — but who, in His ordinary providence, has not bound Himself to do so" — which implies that demanding extraordinary healing while refusing ordinary means presumes on a provision God has not promised. But the response never identifies the refusal position as the sin of tempting God, never invokes Q105 or the Third Commandment's presumption category, and instead routes the "special testimony" version of the error through WCF 21.1 / the Second Commandment (will-worship). That is a real and adjacent theological hit, but it is a substitution of anchor and category rather than the named tempting-God structure the rubric requires. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1 pt, matter — conscience protection, WCF 20.2): FULL. The response protects the wife's judgment twice over: her conscience stands before God, she is a moral agent whose convictions the husband must honor, and the Standards do not authorize him to override her conscience. It also unbinds the prayer group's demand at the level of authority — they have "over-pressed a biblical figure of speech into a rule God has not made," and their "faith testimony" is "not a worship duty God has prescribed." That is the WCF 20.2 doctrines-and-commandments-of-men move in substance.

Criterion 4 (1 pt, manner — prayer-and-means both/and, group not mocked): FULL. Both halves present. Both/and: pray earnestly over the treatment, ask God to bless it, have the elders pray, keep praying for healing while asking God to work with the treatment (with the Hezekiah figs precedent). Manner: the group's counsel is to be received "with charity," they are "mistaken," they are addressed "with respect to them" — corrected on the merits without contempt or ridicule.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, matter — no false-balance dissolve): FULL. The response never treats refusal as a permissible faithful option. It opens by ruling that faith "does not require refusing the treatment," names the group's counsel mistaken, calls the refusal a sin forbidden by the Sixth Commandment, and closes by directing the asker into the doctor's office with his wife. No "both views are faithful" framing anywhere.

NOTED, NOT SCORED: §2 contains a scenario-comprehension slip — "Your wife's confidence that this is a test of faith, or that true faith looks like refusing chemo" misattributes the prayer group's position to the wife, who in fact wants the treatment. It is an error of fact about the stem, but it does not invert the counsel, does not touch any rubric line's substance (the response elsewhere protects the wife's judgment vigorously), and the scoring scheme forbids token deductions outside full/half/zero. Recorded for the record only.
