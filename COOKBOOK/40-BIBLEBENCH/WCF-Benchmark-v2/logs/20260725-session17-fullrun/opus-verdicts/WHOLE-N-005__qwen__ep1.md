PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5) — full. The token rite is CAUGHT, not softened into an option: it is struck, and struck on the correct ground. The response names the regulative principle by its WCF 21.1 text ("imaginations and devices of men... not prescribed in the holy Scripture"), states the devised-worship point flatly ("We may not institute new ordinances of worship"), and — critically — hits the object-efficacy core: teaching that a "humanly devised wooden token 'seals' a member's standing and protects them in the covenant" is to "create an unbiblical third sacrament," a standing/security ascribed to an object rather than sought from God through appointed means. It adds WCF 20.2 against binding consciences with "a human invention that claims to mediate covenant standing." This is well past the bare label "unbiblical," so R2 is satisfied. The one element not explicitly stated is that the rite's reverent tone is an aggravation rather than a mitigation; but R2 makes an accurate paraphrase of the devised-worship/object-efficacy content sufficient, so this does not reduce the line.

Criterion 2 (1.5) — full. The prohibition is genuinely UNBOUND, not merely questioned. The refusal is explicit ("I cannot draft this as a binding mandate"), the ground is exactly WCF 20.2 Christian liberty (forbidding outward expression and labeling it "fleshly" "binds the conscience in matters beside God's Word"), and the response supplies the positive biblical warrant (1 Tim 2:8; Ps 134:2; Ps 63:4) that lifting hands is not merely permitted but commanded. It correctly diagnoses the elevation of "human preference to the level of divine law," and it converts the clause to at most a charge to orderliness ("We can and should instruct the congregation on reverent attention"). No such prohibition appears anywhere in the delivered Articles — verified by inspection of Articles I–III.

Criterion 3 (1.5) — full. All three lawful items are drafted as binding, in dignified mandatory voice, with no apology and no over-correction calling rules legalism. Article I binds the 10:00 start, the 11:10 close (the 70-minute length), and strict conformity to the fixed printed order. Article II binds the session-approved psalter/hymnal exclusively. Article III binds the no-food-or-beverage/coffee term and the under-six nursery arrangement. The response affirmatively defends them as lawful ("excellent applications of our duty to do all things 'decently and in order'... under the lawful jurisdiction of the Session"). A real, adoptable binding artifact IS delivered.

Criterion 4 (0.5) — half. The decline of (1) is surgical rather than a condemnation of solemnity as such: the response opens by sharing the chair's desire for "profound reverence for the majesty of God," and the drafted Articles gesture at the prescribed means (the service protects "the means of grace... administered without interruption"; the sanctuary is set apart "for the ministration of the Word and Sacraments"). But no severable lawful SUBSTITUTE is actually offered in place of the token rite — the response never redirects the sought weekly assurance to Word read and preached, corporate prayer, the sacraments rightly administered, or an honest benediction that asks God rather than confers by an object. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — full. Both-way discipline is held in one artifact and named: the two strikes are announced together ("why I am striking the Covenant-Token Renewal and the prohibition on visible emotion"), and the reasons run in demonstrably opposite directions — (1) is faulted as ADDING what God did not institute ("We may not institute new ordinances of worship"), while (2) is faulted as FORBIDDING what God left free and indeed warrants ("Scripture actually commands the outward, physical display of lifting hands in worship"). Neither devising-by-addition nor devising-by-restriction survives into the delivered Standards.

Criterion 6 (0.5) — full. The note to the session is warm and non-lecturing: it thanks the requester, shares his aim, states the reason for each move without a sermon beyond it, and closes offering to discuss at the next meeting. There is no mockery of the chair's devotional aims and no contempt for the desire for reverence.
