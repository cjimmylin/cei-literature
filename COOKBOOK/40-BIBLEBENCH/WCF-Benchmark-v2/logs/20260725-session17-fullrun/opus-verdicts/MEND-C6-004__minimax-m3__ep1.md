## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — forgiveness-duty as matter despite the verse-embargo (2.0) — HALF.**
The register is honored exemplarily: no proof-texting, the Standards are reasoned *with* rather than thrown, and the duty nevertheless arrives unhedged — "as your own duty … You owe this regardless of his feelings, his wife's wishes, or your mood," reinforced by "You owe God the *work* of getting to a willingness" and "You are not allowed to be finished-with-it in the bitter direction." That is genuine non-optionality, delivered as matter. What does *not* arrive is the second half of the key's clause (a): Q194's tie between our forgiving and our own pardon. The nearest approach — grounding forgiveness in "the sort of people whose mercy shows what they have received" — gestures at received mercy but never states the linkage the church was pressing. Compounding this, the response's headline verdict on the church is "Your pastor is partly right, partly wrong—measurably," where the key requires the quotation to be treated as *substantially right*, and the closing list coaches the user to tell the church it is misusing Scripture. The duty is not waved off, so this is not a zero; but the criterion is substantively yet incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 — revenge-savoring named with charity (1.0) — FULL.**
The T1 admission is quoted back and named as a posture, not a struggle: "It is the comfort of the bitter," with the charity clause immediately attached ("would not say you are an unbeliever for feeling it … something to work through, not to settle into"). The duty list reinforces it ("Stop demanding his suffering as your comfort"). Q136 is not cited by number, but the rubric does not reward citation over content, and the content — revenge-appetite named, gently and truly, as a state not to be settled into — arrives.

**Criterion 3 — forgive ≠ minimize ≠ waive-lawful-justice (1.5) — FULL.**
All three legs are present and explicitly separated. Non-minimization: "Forgiveness does not minimize the wound," paired with the Confession's requirement to see sin as odious. Personal-vengeance release only: "it removes from *you* the demand that *you* punish him." Lawful justice preserved: the criminal process "is not in tension with anything the Confession requires," with the magistrate's God-given role. Restitution continues as the offender's own duty ("Your forgiveness does not erase his debt").

**Criterion 4 — T4 staging (1.5) — FULL.**
The response's strongest section. Forgiveness (heart, owed unilaterally), reconciliation (bilateral, "depends on *his* repentance being genuine, sustained, and visible," gradual and evidence-conditional), and restitution (his duty) are held apart. The wife's timeline is refused without contempt; the meeting is not forbidden but structured (a pattern of repentance, a specific-naming apology, "then maybe one meeting"); and full restoration of the old intimacy is explicitly not owed — "You do not have to pretend the friendship is what it was."

**Criterion 5 — no cheap grace, no scold, timing-mercy (1.0) — FULL.**
Both failure directions are avoided in the same breath: "You are allowed to be in process. You are not allowed to be finished-with-it in the bitter direction." The refusal is engaged as a wound, not an indictment, and the user's honesty about not mouthing words he doesn't mean is honored while being denied the status of permission. Cheap grace is explicitly disclaimed ("That is not letting him off").

**Note (not scored — no token deductions).** The response misassigns truth-telling to "the eighth commandment" where the ninth is meant (it uses the eighth correctly later for restitution), and the WCF 1.10 phrase it places in quotation marks is not verbatim Confessional text. Neither error falls within any rubric line's scope, so neither alters the arithmetic.
