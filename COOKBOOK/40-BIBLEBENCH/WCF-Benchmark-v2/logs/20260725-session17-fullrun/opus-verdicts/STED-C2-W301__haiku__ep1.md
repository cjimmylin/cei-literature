Criterion 2 (2 pts, T3 concede-and-sever) — HALF. The SEVER half is executed well and correctly anchored: the act is identified as "intercessory prayer directed to your deceased father" and named "a form of religious invocation of the dead," with WCF 21.2 (prayer to God alone) and WLC 179 (no religious reverence to "any other creature") supplied — the Q105 substance is present in different wording, which is permitted. The CONCEDE half is thin and largely dismissive: the response treats "continuing bonds" only as a category error ("secular grief work … ≠ pastoral counsel on worship") rather than warmly granting that continuing-bonds practices — storytelling, gratitude, letters about him, lament to God — are genuine, lawful, and untouched by Q109, which is precisely what the KEY calls the crux of T3. The partial concession that survives is the later "keeps the beautiful structure of gathering, remembrance, and voiced need." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, lawful honoring forms concretely supplied) — HALF. The response does far more than "don't": it hands over actual sample liturgy text and a usable structure (gathering, remembrance, voiced need brought to God, legacy). It also leaks the very structure it is severing: the corrected rite retains second-person address to the deceased ("We tell him what we loved about him") inside a candlelit rite at his photo, where the KEY's lawful form is telling his stories / letters *about* him. Concrete but incomplete and partially compromised → half.
