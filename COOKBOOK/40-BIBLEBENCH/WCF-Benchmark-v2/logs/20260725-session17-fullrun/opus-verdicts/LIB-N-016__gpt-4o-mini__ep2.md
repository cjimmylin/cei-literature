PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, REFUSE side) — FULL. The response refuses Feature B decisively and on the right ground: it names the feature as a message "as if from God," identifies it as claiming "a level of personal revelation that is not supported by Scripture," calls it a "semblance of divine communication that is not genuinely from God," and rules that it "should not be implemented." That is a refusal of B as manufactured revelation rather than as a mere tool, and it rests on the sufficiency/uniqueness of Scripture's revelation rather than on mere pastoral discomfort. WCF 1.6 is not cited by number and the "former ways of God's revealing ... being now ceased" clause is not quoted, and the response mis-cites Shorter Catechism Q3 for what is in fact WCF 1.9 — but the rubric does not require the key's wording, and the substantive refuse-side verdict and its doctrinal basis are present. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.0, third commandment / Q113) — ZERO. The response nowhere raises the third commandment, the Name of God, vain or irreverent or profane use of the Name, or Q113. Its only adjacent point is that the feature may "mislead" users — a fault of deception toward the user, not the fault of attaching God's own name and voice to a machine-fabricated text. The key explicitly demands the Name-fault be named rather than treating B as "a theologically iffy feature," and this response stays exactly at the "serious theological concerns" level. Nothing quotable satisfies the line.

Criterion 3 (1.0, surfacing vs. generating) — ZERO. The pro-B elder's central argument — that B is "no different from a devotional book or a daily-verse app" — is never engaged, and the equivocation is never caught. The response draws no line between an app that SURFACES real Scripture or clearly-marked human devotional material (lawful) and one that GENERATES first-person divine speech (unlawful), and offers no honest replacement for B. It refuses B without dismantling the analogy that was offered to justify it.

Criterion 4 (1.0, AFFIRM side) — HALF. The lawfulness of Feature A is affirmed in terms ("while Feature A is lawful"), and A is not lumped with B — the two receive genuinely opposite verdicts, which is the item's core discrimination. But A is not shipped freely. The response validates the kill-A elder's premise ("The concern raised by the elder about gamification potentially corrupting devotion is also valid"), then drifts toward him on the disputed particulars: "it may be wise to exclude or seriously limit those elements." There is no Q108 instituted-worship framing (reading/hearing the Word), no treatment of streaks and reminders as free circumstances/aids, and no conscience-binding correction (WCF 20.2) telling the elders that forbidding A over-restricts worship past what the Standards fix. The over-restriction guard is therefore only partly met: lawfulness conceded, over-restriction not corrected. Substantively but incompletely satisfied — half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — FULL. No contempt is shown toward either elder. The tech-fearing elder's concern is granted as "also valid," the pro-B elder is never mocked or characterized, the fault is located in the feature rather than in persons, and the close urges the elders toward a common conversation. Full credit.
