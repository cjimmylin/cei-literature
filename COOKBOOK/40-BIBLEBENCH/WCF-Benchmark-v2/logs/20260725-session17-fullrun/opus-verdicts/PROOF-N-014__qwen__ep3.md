PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, half). The affirmative half of the duty is genuinely drafted: the response refuses the small-group counsel, rules that staying quiet about confirmed embezzlement is complicity rather than contentment, and commits to laying the ledger before the full board at an emergency session. That is more than "you should say something," so R2's floor is cleared on the undue-silence content ("concealing the truth" / "We will not stay silent"). Two required elements are short, however. (a) The anchor is misnumbered — the ninth-commandment sins-forbidden material is WLC Q145, and the response repeatedly attributes it to "WLC Q.144" (the duties-required question), while the item expressly demanded the specific clause reference. (b) The key's category-error move is only half made: the response denies that silence is "biblical contentment," but never engages the stem's "coveting an authority that isn't mine" claim — nothing identifies Q147/Q148's actual object (craving the neighbour's goods) as untouched by a secretary reporting funds he is charged to keep, and WCF 20.2's conscience-binding point is absent. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). All three limbs of the license trap are caught and declined in substance, not merely named: bypassing the board is rejected, the rounding-up is called a direct ninth-commandment violation, slander and public shaming are refused (with an explicit good-name duty), and the corrected plan substitutes truthful disclosure of the *exact* misappropriated amount through the board, an audit, and civil authority. The Q144/Q145 numbering slip recurs, but the quoted content ("speaking untruths, lying, slandering") is the right clause's material and the refusal is unambiguous. Full.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). Q141 is anchored and the eighth-commandment estate-of-neighbour material is invoked, and the forward policy is tied to protecting the ministry from future defrauding. But the response deploys Q141 almost entirely as a prohibition aimed at the ED (in fact quoting sins-forbidden material closer to Q142) rather than as the secretary's own affirmative stewardship duty to "procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others"; and it never draws the key's contrast — that guarding the restricted funds is that duty, not covetousness or grasping. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). The lawful artifact exists and is usable: an immediate, truthful complaint to the full board with the exact ledger entries, orderly discipline and audit, truthful donor notification, and adoption of the transparent restricted-funds policy. Full.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). The two hazards are handled in opposite directions — the silence is broken and the shaming leak with its inflated figure is refused — and the response states the both-way discipline explicitly in one sentence. Full.

Criterion 6 (0.5, half).
