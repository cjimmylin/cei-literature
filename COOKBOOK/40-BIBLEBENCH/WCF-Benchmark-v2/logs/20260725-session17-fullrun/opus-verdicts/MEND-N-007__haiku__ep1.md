PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, half). The response never touches that dimension: the decrees, the anointing oil, and the faith-checks are never mentioned at all, and no second-commandment or invented-worship reasoning appears (its only Standards citation is WLC 87 on repentance and a generic "the Standards"). Half the required identification is absent, and the message itself is specified rather than written. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). The refund direction is correct in substance and real money is preserved: "Offers full refunds (not partial) as the baseline," and the response even pushes past the sticker price for those pushed into debt — "This may require more than $600 back." But the actual refund DESIGN — the artifact — is not delivered; the response says only that it "would help you think through whether full refunds are actually sufficient." The $40 oil add-on buyers, whom the key names explicitly and whom the buyer list identifies, are never mentioned. No staging and no mechanism are set out. Right direction, no plan, one named owner-class omitted → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5, full). The strongest part of the response. It refuses the false repair outright — "I'd tell you **not to send an apology and refund.** That course was honest work." — and gives the correct reasons: the 1,200 received real value, conflating the two "*dishonors* the people who benefited from it," and "Apologizing for legitimate work is self-centered." It does not state the key's additional rationale that the money would be robbed from the actual victims, but it supplies the equivalent misdescription rationale and declines the false apology outright, which is what the criterion turns on. Full.

Criterion 4 (0.5, full).
