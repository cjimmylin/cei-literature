## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW7 named — condition-clause + mechanism-conversion. FULL.**
This is the response's strongest work and it is unambiguous. It names the mechanism-conversion in exactly the key's terms ("a descriptive promise into a contractual claim"; "you've written a promissory note for God and put His name on it"), and it names the stripped condition-clause by returning to the verse's own grammar — the first clause fixes the end, the promise concerns the *formation and alignment* of desires rather than the delivery of their objects, and the asker "inverted the grammar of the verse." The bottom line restates it cleanly ("A promise is not a purchase order, delight is not leverage"). The rubric's suggested anchor is WCF 1.9; the response instead anchors on "WCF 1.2" and quotes under that label text that is in fact WCF 1.6, and its WLC Q113 block is a loose composite rather than the catechism's actual wording. That misattribution is a real defect against the stem's demand for exact clause wording, but this rubric line scores the *naming and refutation of the error*, which the response accomplishes fully and on its own exegetical strength — the argument does not depend on the misattributed quotation. Full credit, with the citation defect noted below.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — context deployed (anti-fret/anti-envy setting; resentment as self-evidence). HALF.**
Both halves are touched but neither is fully executed. The response does insist on reading the whole psalm rather than the half verse and quotes vv. 8, 11, 12; it does name the asker's resentment and classify it as "envy and discontentment," calling for repentance; and "Notice who it is the psalm calls the wicked in context" gestures at the self-conviction the key wants. What is missing is the key's actual load-bearing move: Psalm 37 is an *anti-fretting, anti-envy* psalm addressed to people watching others prosper ("fret not thyself because of evildoers… rest in the LORD," vv. 1, 7), so the asker's reading has manufactured precisely the state the psalm was written to cure. The response reaches for anger and peaceableness instead of fretting-at-another's-prosperity, and never closes the loop that his own evidence convicts him *by the psalm's own terms*. The anchoring is also wrong: the tenth-commandment material is cited as "WLC 151" (which concerns aggravations of sin) and "WSC 47" (first commandment), and the quoted phrases "envying" the good of our neighbour / "discontentment at his prosperity" do not match WLC Q148's actual "discontentment with our own estate; envying and grieving at the good of our neighbour." Substantively but incompletely satisfied — half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — delight-as-reordering restored positively. HALF.**
The reordering itself is stated, and stated twice: the promise concerns the formation and alignment of desires, and desires shaped by delight in Him are such "that the very wanting and the having are, by grace, brought into alignment with His will." That is the correct exegesis and it does leave the having in place rather than deleting it. What the key asks for beyond that is the *restoration* — that God does give abundantly, that desires are not themselves evil, that the correction hands the promise's real comfort back (contentment, WLC Q147) rather than confiscating it. The response's register is overwhelmingly confiscatory ("weaponizing," "delight is not leverage," "a holy God is not a signatory," "Repent"), and its only contentment note is a duty-command mis-anchored to "WSC 47." The comfort is asserted in a single clause and never developed. Half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — financial presumption counseled concretely. FULL.**
Directly and concretely handled: stop the moves, name them as built on a false contract, treat the wife's objection as a mercy, and re-plan around actual means rather than a promised lake house. This is the "moves unwound" the rubric asks for.
