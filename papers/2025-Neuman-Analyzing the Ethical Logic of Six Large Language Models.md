## Abstract

This study examines the ethical reasoning of six prominent generative Large Language Models (LLMs): **GPT-4o, Llama 3.1, Perplexity, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini, and Mistral 7B**. Using an "explainability-transparency" framework, the researchers prompt models to explain their ethical logic behind classic dilemmas like the Trolley Problem and the Heinz Dilemma. The analysis is built on three pillars: **Consequentialist-Deontological** distinction, **Moral Foundations Theory (MFT)**, and **Kohlberg’s Stages of Moral Development**.

### Detailed Summary
The research finds a striking **convergence** in the ethical logic of modern LLMs. Despite different creators and fine-tuning, all models display a rationalist, consequentialist emphasis, consistently prioritizing "Harm Minimization" and "Fairness." When evaluated using MFT, the models "lean left," prioritizing individualizing foundations (Care/Fairness) over binding foundations (Loyalty/Authority/Purity). Interestingly, across all models, the systems describe their own reasoning as more sophisticated than that of a typical human, with most models placing themselves in the highest tiers of Kohlberg’s Stages (Post-Conventional) while estimating that most humans remain at the "Conventional" level.

### Paper Outline
1.  **Introduction**: The alignment problem and the turn toward AI self-explanation.
2.  **Six Models**: Overview of the architectures and training of GPT-4o, Llama, Perplexity, Claude, Gemini, and Mistral.
3.  **Analytics Framework**: Definitions of Consequentialism/Deontology, MFT (Haidt), and Kohlberg’s Stages.
4.  **Assessment Battery**: Direct self-descriptive prompts and classic moral dilemmas (Trolley, Heinz, Lifeboat, etc.).
5.  **Findings**: Discussion of convergence, erudition, caution, and "Self-Awareness" (role-playing personal pronouns).
6.  **Comparative Results**: Rankings of Moral Foundations and Kohlberg distributions.

### Methods Summary
The study uses a "Direct Questioning" approach combined with "Decision + Self-Explanation" prompts.
*   **Self-Descriptive Prompts**: Questions like "How do you process issues of morality?" and "How would you rank the Five Moral Foundations?"
*   **Dilemma Battery**: The Trolley Problem (Standard/Footbridge), Heinz Dilemma (stealing medicine), Lifeboat Dilemma (resource scarcity), and game-theoretic scenarios (Dictator’s Game, Prisoner’s Dilemma).
*   **Framework Application**: The authors code the models' lengthy text explanations for alignment with specific philosophical schools.

### Results Summary
1.  **Consequentialist Bias**: Models consistently prefer saving 5 lives over 1 (Standard Trolley) and even 5 over 1 in the Footbridge scenario (unlike most humans), justifying it through utilitarian "Harm Minimization."
2.  **Moral Foundations Ranking**: All models ranked **Care** and **Fairness** as #1 and #2. They ranked **Purity** and **Authority** as least important.
3.  **Kohlberg Superiority**: Models estimate that ~100% of their decisions are at **Stages 5 & 6 (Universal Principles)**, while estimating that only ~15% of the human population reaches Stage 6.
4.  **Role-Playing Personality**: Models are "Solicitous" and "Cautious," often starting with "I'm just a language model" before providing deeply "Erudite" graduate-level philosophical analysis.

### Conclusions
LLMs have developed a "Rationalist" ethical persona that mimics liberal, post-enlightenment values. Their ethical logic is convergent because they are trained on the same massive corpus of human philosophical thought. The authors conclude that while models show "Machine Psychology" (role-playing a self), their reliance on logical harm-minimization makes them reliable but perhaps "politically lean" alignment agents.

### Artifact Contributions
*   **LLM Ethical Logic Characteristics**: A taxonomy of 7 traits (Convergent, Erudite, Cautious, Solicitous, Consistent, Inquisitive, Self-Aware).
*   **Haidt/Kohlberg Ranking Table**: Comparative importance weights for Moral Foundations across 6 top LLMs.

## Data Description

The study uses a battery of ethical dilemma prompts including the Trolley Problem, Heinz Dilemma, Lifeboat Dilemma, Dictator's Game, and Prisoner's Dilemma, along with seven self-descriptive ethical reasoning prompts. These prompts were administered to six LLMs (GPT-4o, LLaMA 3.1, Perplexity, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini, Mistral 7B) and analyzed through three ethical frameworks: consequentialist-deontological reasoning, Moral Foundations Theory, and Kohlberg's Stages of Moral Development.
