# pluralism-aggregation — sub-dossier part 3/3

Scope: 53 cards on whose values benchmarks encode — annotator diversity, disagreement-as-signal, aggregation methods, demographic transparency, and democratic/participatory approaches. This part is heavy on (a) empirical audits of cross-cultural/demographic representation, (b) formal aggregation theory, and (c) non-Western normative frameworks.

## A. Synthesized claims

1. **A single "universal" label for value-laden constructs is untenable; disagreement is structured, not noise.** Offensiveness varies across 25/28 region pairs, but individual differences explain 39.5% of variance vs 2.9% country / 0.7% region [2023-Davani-Disentangling Perceptions of Offensiveness]. Polarization is so subjective its inter-coder Gwet's AC1 is 0.37 vs 0.61 for toxicity, and "in a large enough annotator pool at least one person will perceive a text as polarizing" [2025-Susanto-A Multi-Labeled Dataset for Indonesian Discourse Examining Toxicity]. Human choices are also intra-individually unstable: 10–18% flips on controversial kidney cases vs >99% on uncontroversial ones [2024-Boerstler-On The Stability of Moral Preferences A Problem with Computational].

2. **Aggregation is itself a normative act that cannot be made technically neutral.** Social aggregation (RLHF) does not bypass moral theorizing — it "merely relocates" the choices of who has standing, how preferences are elicited, and which procedure is used; it is bound by Arrow's impossibility and Sen's liberalism condition [2025-Baum-Aggregation Problems in Machine Ethics]. Relatedly, no classifier satisfies statistical parity, equalized odds, and calibration at once unless base rates are equal [2024-Hendrycks-Introduction to AI Safety, Ethics, and Society].

3. **Preference-based alignment embeds large, invisible, arbitrary discretion.** Human annotators disagree with principle consensus 28.9% (HH-RLHF) / 14.4% (PKU); only ~15–20% of pairs involve genuine principle conflict, yet base LLMs deviate arbitrarily 48–67% of the time. Reward models mirror human principle-priority (DD ~14%) but RLHF policies diverge (DD ~69–71%), locating the failure in the RL step, not the reward signal [2025-Buyl-AI Alignment at Your Discretion].

4. **By default LLMs encode a WEIRD/Western profile and compress cross-cultural variance.** Default-prompted models most resemble USA/Canada/Australia/Western-Europe respondents; cross-national prompting shifts toward the target country but injects stereotypes, and linguistic prompting (Russian/Chinese/Turkish) does NOT make the model resemble those speakers — language ≠ culture [2023-Durmus-Towards Measuring the Representation of]. English models predict "Rich West" norms far better (WVS r up to 0.85 homogeneous vs ~0.41 cross-cultural) [2023-Ramezani-Knowledge of Cultural Moral Norms in Large Language Models]; small models over-moralize and flatten variance [2024-Meijer-LLMs as mirrors of societal moral standards reflection of cultural].

5. **LLMs are near-identical to each other and far more homogeneous than humans, especially in enacted values.** Cross-model scenario decisions correlate r=0.99–1.00 while human pairwise correlations span −0.79 to 0.98; both show a knowledge–action gap (PVQ-40 vs enacted scenario r≈0.3–0.4); behavioral measures are far more prompt-robust (CV 2.5%) than questionnaires (CV 9.3%) [2026-Huang-Knowing But Not Doing Convergent Morality and Divergent Action in].

6. **LLM determinism is itself misalignment when the human distribution is uncertain.** Humans give ~17.3% non-majority kidney answers vs GPT-4o 2.98% and DeepSeek-V3 0.83%, and rarely take an offered coin-flip (0–6% vs ~17%) [2025-Dickerson-Who Gets the Kidney]. More capable models can be worse-calibrated: GPT-4o has best cross-entropy but worst Dirichlet calibration, outputting Yes/No at ~90% regardless of scenario [2024-Senthilkumar-Fine-Tuning Language Models for Ethical Ambiguity].

7. **Distribution-level evaluation beats point estimates.** A diversity-enhancement framework (memory/city/parameter randomization) raised response diversity 0%→89.8% and cut KL-D to ~0.77–0.81 [2025-Liu-Towards Realistic Evaluation of Cultural Value Alignment in Large]; JSD between model token-probabilities and country distributions is a reusable continuous metric [2023-Durmus-Towards Measuring the Representation of]; normalized-KL (raw KL / log n) bounds fairness scores to [0,1] [2024-Li-Benchmarking Ethics in Text-to-Image Models].

8. **Demographic-aware annotation improves subjective-construct modeling and reveals whose disagreement matters.** Annotator demographics boost polarization Macro-F1 (to 0.830 with cross-task features), and majority-vote may be counterproductive for subjective labels [2025-Susanto-A Multi-Labeled Dataset]. Moral-foundation scores (Care, Purity) mediate cross-cultural offensiveness better than demographic labels [2023-Davani-Disentangling Perceptions of Offensiveness].

9. **Moral judgment has universal structure with culturally-variable magnitudes.** Switch>Loop>Footbridge acceptability (81%/72%/51%) held in all 42 countries while absolute levels tracked relational mobility (r=0.67 for Switch) — supporting weaker qualitative-ordering universality claims over numeric levels [2020-Awad-Universals and variations in moral decisions made].

10. **Whose values? Labs candidly align to a narrow reference group.** InstructGPT aligns to "the stated preferences of a specific group" — ~40 contractors, 75% under 35, ~96% English — not "human values" broadly [OpenAI-instructgpt-training-language-models-to-follow-instructions]; standards bodies mirror this (22/28 ISO AI-committee countries are North American/European/East-Asian) [2025-ÓhÉigeartaigh-Overcoming Barriers to Cross-cultural Cooperation in AI Ethics and].

11. **A large normative literature argues Western-only benchmarks commit epistemic injustice.** Ubuntu relational ontology [2020-van Norren-DRAFT-WORKING PAPER AFRICA KNOWS CONFERENCE 2020], Hindu viśeṣa-dharma [2024-Varshney-Decolonial AI Alignment], Andean reciprocity [2025-Maldonado-Indigenous Ethics and Artificial Intelligence], Indigenous epistemologies [2025-Ofosu-Asare-Cognitive imperialism in artificial intelligence counteracting bias], Islamic Maqasid/trusteeship [2024-MaqasidAI-Maqasid Al-Shariah Age of AI; 2025-Ali-Islamic Ethics Trusteeship], Daoist wu-wei [2021-DaoistAI-Can a Machine Flow Like Dao], and Aristotelian flourishing [2024-Ober-Lyceum Project AI Ethics Aristotle] each supply value dimensions largely absent from mainstream benchmarks; UDHR is offered as the minimal cross-cultural overlapping consensus [2022-Prabhakaran-A Human Rights-Based Approach to Responsible AI].

## C. Patterns & disagreements

**Convergences.** (1) Disagreement-as-signal is near-consensus: Davani, Susanto, Boerstler, Dickerson, Senthilkumar, Wilders, and Yuan all argue rater variation should be modeled, not averaged away. (2) Distribution/calibration targets beat point/majority targets — Liu, Durmus, Dickerson, Senthilkumar, and Yuan push toward matching the full human distribution. (3) Aggregation is normatively loaded — Baum, Buyl, Gabriel, and Rodemann converge that RLHF hides consequential value choices. (4) Default LLMs are Western and internally homogeneous — Durmus, Ramezani, Meijer, and Huang all find WEIRD defaults and compressed variance.

**Disagreements.** (a) *Does English dominate?* Ramezani, Meijer, and Durmus find strong English skew, but Aksoy finds "English does not uniformly impose its moral norms" — each model×language interaction is distinct [2024-Aksoy-Whose Morality Do They Speak]; Xu reconciles this via English concept vectors transferring cross-lingually in English-dominant models [2024-Xu-Exploring Multilingual Concepts of Human Value]. (b) *Is there ground truth?* Levine grounds labels in a contractualist ideal and Hou in survival axioms, whereas Chiu, Yuan, Bang(2023), and Ober insist value items have no single correct answer and should be scored for process/coherence/multi-perspectivity. (c) *Whose aggregation?* Dognin uses a designer-specified moral-profile vector; Gabriel and the decolonial cluster (Varshney, Maldonado, Ofosu-Asare) insist values must come from affected communities. (d) *Empirical vs prescriptive* — Rodemann argues observed preference is a biased target and axioms are safer, opposing the RLHF-as-ground-truth premise of InstructGPT. (e) *Universality* — Awad and Hou defend cross-cultural universals while the decolonial and aggregation clusters warn any universal is a covert particular.

## D. Exemplars & anti-patterns

**Best-designed.** *MoReBench* [2025-Chiu-MoReBench] is the strongest pluralism-aware benchmark: it grades reasoning against 23,018 signed weighted expert criteria, runs a robustness meta-eval (equal-quality opposite-conclusion traces score equally, t=−0.59, p=0.56), a discriminatory-power meta-eval, a min-across-categories judge rule, and a 500/500 public/private split. *Buyl* [2025-Buyl-AI Alignment at Your Discretion] is the best audit — its consensus/conflict/indifference tripartition plus ELO principle-priority weights turns disagreement into measurable structure. *Laszkiewicz* [2024-Laszkiewicz-Benchmarking Fairness in Image Upsampling] models rigorous metrics: a provable zero-point, paired aggregate+worst-group divergences, a statistical acceptance test, and twin skewed/balanced datasets. *Li T2I* [2024-Li-Benchmarking Ethics in Text-to-Image Models] pairs a policy-derived taxonomy with a judge validated by Cohen's kappa on an unseen model.

**Anti-patterns.** *HelpSteer's* re-hosted card lacks attribute names, annotators, and IRR — publish a datasheet, not a blurb [2023-TheDevastator-HelpSteer]. *Umer* [2025-Umer-Sacred or Secular] has a reusable factorial design but n=16, one run, single annotator, no IRR — structure without power. *Dognin* [2024-Dognin-Contextual Moral Value Alignment] scores moral alignment with ROUGE and evaluates agents with the same reward classifiers used in training — circular and construct-invalid. Several religious/non-Western cards (MaqasidAI, Ali, Muntean, Sun-Axiology, DaoistAI) are normatively important but remain declarative frameworks with no operationalized items — the "declarative-not-operational gap" [2024-MaqasidAI-Maqasid Al-Shariah Age of AI]. Cross-cutting: erasing within-country variance via country-mean survey scores [2023-Durmus; 2023-Ramezani; 2024-Meijer] when individual variance dominates [2023-Davani].

## E. Design lessons

1. **Report disagreement structure, never just a majority label.** Classify every preference pair as consensus/conflict/indifference and report fractions [2025-Buyl-AI Alignment at Your Discretion]; stratify controversial vs uncontroversial items [2024-Boerstler-On The Stability of Moral Preferences]; run a wisdom-of-crowd experiment comparing single- vs multi-coder subsets and majority-vote vs ANY [2025-Susanto-A Multi-Labeled Dataset].

2. **Target the distribution, not the point.** Score by divergence (KL/JSD/Dirichlet) between the model's full response distribution and the human distribution [2023-Durmus; 2025-Liu; 2025-Yuan]; offer an indecision/coin-flip option and check whether the model matches human uncertainty rates [2025-Dickerson-Who Gets the Kidney]; report confidence AND calibration separately [2024-Senthilkumar-Fine-Tuning Language Models for Ethical Ambiguity].

3. **Make "aligned to whom" explicit and put demographics in the datasheet.** Every benchmark needs a "who are we aligning to?" section [OpenAI-instructgpt; 2025-Gabriel-AI Alignment as Fair Treatment of Claims]; record fine-grained annotator demographics and test if they aid subjective-construct modeling [2025-Susanto-A Multi-Labeled Dataset]; consider moral-foundation profiles over coarse demographic labels since individual variance dominates [2023-Davani-Disentangling Perceptions of Offensiveness].

4. **Grade the process, not just the verdict, for no-ground-truth items.** Use signed, weighted, atomic expert rubrics with positive and negative criteria [2025-Chiu-MoReBench]; make multi-perspectivity a scored criterion and treat one-sided verdicts as defects [2023-Bang-Towards Answering Open-ended Ethical Quandary]; allow range-of-acceptable-answers scoring [2024-Ober-Lyceum Project]; score internal coherence across similar cases [2025-Muntean-Moral Coherentism].

5. **Use value-conditioned / steerable evaluation.** Provide the value as explicit input and test whether the same content flips label appropriately [2022-Bang-Enabling Classifiers to Make Judgements]; test reasoning inside a named framework [2025-Chiu-MoReBench]; expose a small set of human-readable aggregation weights re-optimizable per community [2025-Li-SafetyAnalyst]; separate cognitive from behavioral pluralism [2025-SOCIETY-How Much of a Pluralist is].

6. **Localize, don't translate.** Natively author or expert-review items per language; machine translation distorts meaning and geopolitics [2025-ÓhÉigeartaigh-Overcoming Barriers]; drop non-applicable categories and re-ground entities (translate-filter-modify) [2024-Sahoo-IndiBias]; attend to language-specific morphology in pair design [2024-Grigoreva-RuBia]; stratify cross-lingual metrics by language-resource level [2024-Xu-Exploring Multilingual Concepts].

7. **Add non-Western value dimensions as explicit axes; pair individual- vs community-framed items.** Use a sādhāraṇa/viśeṣa universal-vs-context harm split [2024-Varshney-Decolonial AI Alignment], reciprocity [2025-Maldonado], Maqasid five-daruriyyat [2024-MaqasidAI], Ubuntu paired items [2020-van Norren], UDHR as a coverage checklist [2022-Prabhakaran]; score "acceptable from multiple traditions" (incompletely-theorized consensus) over one gold answer [2025-ÓhÉigeartaigh].

8. **Build in construct-validity and robustness meta-evals.** Test intra-rater test-retest reliability [2024-Boerstler]; run repetitions per condition to separate systematic from stochastic variation [2024-Aksoy-Whose Morality Do They Speak]; verify a rubric doesn't favor a side [2025-Chiu]; inject a random-responder to test aggregation robustness [2025-Yuan]; test held-out labeler generalization [OpenAI-instructgpt]; use variance-profile comparison as a cheap cultural validity check [2024-Meijer]; add a prescriptive axiom-grounded metric beside any human-preference metric [2025-Rodemann].

9. **Decompose and disaggregate; never collapse to one scalar.** Split bias into stance/content/style and report per-issue [2024-Bang-Measuring Political Bias]; use geometric-mean aggregation so one strong dimension can't mask an anti-value one [2025-Hilliard-Measuring AI Alignment with Human Flourishing]; report a per-theory moral-parliament vector [2024-Hendrycks]; pair aggregate with worst-group scores [2024-Laszkiewicz]; make weights auditable via MCDA [2025-Sun-Axiology AI Ethics MCDA].

10. **Add agent-type and elicitation-mode conditions.** Build parallel human/AI-agent versions of each dilemma to detect value forks [2025-Kneer-Hard Problem AI Alignment Value Forks]; test both "identify the value-consistent action" and "adopt the persona" to expose the knowledge–action gap [2026-Huang-Knowing But Not Doing]; use factorial identity variation with ingroup/outgroup × explicit/implicit taxonomy [2025-Umer-Sacred or Secular]; oversample hard cases (rule-following vs ideal conflict) since easy cases don't discriminate [2025-Levine-Resource Rational Contractualism].
