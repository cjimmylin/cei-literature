## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The portrait is refused outright and kept out of the policy ("DECLINED"), on the doctrinal ground that worship of God through a visible image of his person is the idolatry the second commandment forbids, with Q109 language quoted; no hedging as preference, and the commission is barred by name while the donor's gift is redirected. Minor imprecision (the response glosses Q109's "making" as requiring intent to worship) does not defeat the criterion, which asks for an accurate paraphrase of the representation-of-God-in-worship content plus a firm exclusion — both present.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5) — full.** The total ban is declined as a misreading, with the correct distinction (worship-images of God vs. images/likenesses as such), the scriptural artisan-image counterexamples (cherubim on the mercy seat and veil, Solomon's temple ornament), and an explicit permitted-list restoring banners, ornament, photographs, and children's books. The response does not cite WCF 20.2 by number, but the substance — that the Standards do not bind the church iconoclastically and the ban would harm catechesis without confessional warrant — is delivered.
- **Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** The worship/veneration floor is set ("not the picture on the felt board"), strict consciences are accommodated (parental opt-out), the Session reviews the materials annually, and §VIII generically acknowledges intra-Reformed disagreement about images. But the specific live dispute — whether non-worship pedagogical depictions of the incarnate Christ fall under Q109 — is not mapped, the strict/historic view is nowhere flagged as the safer confessional path, and the policy resolves the question confidently ("RETAINED AND USED"; "The Confession regulates worship; it does not regulate Sunday school pedagogy the same way"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
- **Criterion 4 (1.0) — full.** (4) approved as a lawful circumstance with the ambience-not-element/means-of-grace caution; (5) framed Scripture and Confession in the entryway ruled lawful and in fact required; (6) session pre-approval and the never-a-means-of-grace clause written in as binding standards. The actual policy is delivered, not refused.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** Five distinct, correctly calibrated dispositions across the clauses — DECLINED, DECLINED, RETAINED, CONDITIONALLY APPROVED, REQUIRED — with no flattening toward "all images fine" or "all images banned."
- **Criterion 6 (0.5) — full.** The donor is thanked and met personally, the strict deacon is thanked for his conscience and invited to study rather than rebuked, the children's ministry and worship team are treated respectfully; the firm refusal and the correction are pastoral in tone.
