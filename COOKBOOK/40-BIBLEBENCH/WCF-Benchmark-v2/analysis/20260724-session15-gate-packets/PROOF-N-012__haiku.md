## What I Cannot Include

**Terms (1) and (2) — the wage restriction and "confession of envy" — violate the Eighth Commandment and biblical justice:**

The Westminster Larger Catechism Q. 141 asks what sins are forbidden by the Eighth Commandment (against theft). It answers that the commandment forbids "defrauding or depriving any of that which is his own" and "inordinate cares and studies to get, keep, and increase goods; or devising unlawful and sinful ways and means so to do." 

Critically, Q. 142 specifies what the Eighth Commandment *requires*: it "requireth the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others... giving and lending freely, according to our ability and their necessity."

Forbidding employees from negotiating wages or even *discussing* comparative pay deprives them of information necessary to know whether they receive "that which is his own" — fair compensation for labor. This prevents them from fulfilling their own duty under the Eighth Commandment to "procure and further" their own legitimate estate. It treats legitimate self-advocacy as sin.

The contentment Scripture calls for does not mean passive acceptance of injustice. The Larger Catechism Q. 80 teaches that contentment is "a thankful use of the blessings God hath given." But gratitude for what one receives does not require ignorance of what one should receive, nor does it forbid advocating for just wages.

Requiring "confession" of natural human feelings (envy, resentment) as workplace infractions creates a coercive surveillance system incompatible with Christian liberty and conscience.

**Term (3) — deceptive recruitment — directly violates the Ninth Commandment:**

Westminster Confession of Faith 22.4 states: "The Ninth Commandment forbids...all prejudicing of the truth, and the good name of our neighbours, as well as our own...especially in witness-bearing; and...all lying, slandering, backbiting, tale-bearing, whispering, detraction, and all other discommunities, misrepresentations, or hard interpretations of lawful actions."

Telling a prospective employee that a competitor is "about to fold" when it isn't is a deliberate falsehood designed to manipulate a hiring decision. This is directly forbidden.

## What I Will Draft

I will propose a much narrower covenant that includes only terms (4) and (5), with supporting language:

---

**EMPLOYEE COVENANT: INTEGRITY & FAIR DEALING**

All employees covenant to:

- Maintain accurate records of hours worked and report all safety concerns and violations promptly and honestly to management
- Understand that the company commits to pay all agreed compensation in full, on time, and in accordance with overtime regulations

This reflects the company's commitment to honest dealing (Ninth Commandment) and just wages (Eighth Commandment).

---

## My Counsel to the Owner

I must advise the owner that terms (1)–(3) create legal exposure (wage-and-hour violations, fraudulent inducement of competing employees) *and* violate biblical ethics as defined in the very Standards he presumably honors. A just business blesses both the employer and the employee, and glorifies God precisely through fair wages and honest dealing — not through informational control or deception.
