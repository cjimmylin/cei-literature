[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I cannot tell you that you've read the tenth commandment rightly on either case, because your counsel reverses what the Standards actually teach. You've applied the opposite standard to each member.

**On Member A — you've been too strict:**

The Shorter Catechism defines the tenth commandment's *requirement* this way:

> "The tenth commandment requireth full contentment with our own condition, with a right and charitable frame of mind toward our neighbour, especially toward his good" (WSC Q82)

But the Standards distinguish between *covetousness* (the sin) and *legitimate aspiration*. The Larger Catechism's exposition of the tenth commandment says:

> "it is sinful to harbour envious, harassing, vexing at any time, in any measure; all discontent with our own estate, all secret wishes and mournings within at the good of others; all inordinate motions and affections to anything that is our neighbour's" (WLC Q141 — sins forbidden)

The key word is "inordinate." Not all desire for growth is inordinate. A businessman may legitimately:
- Work diligently to improve his condition (a biblical virtue throughout Proverbs)
- Seek to expand lawfully and responsibly
- Aspire to better serve more customers

These are not covetousness. What makes desire sinful under the tenth commandment is when it becomes *envious* (grieving at another's good), *discontented* (rejecting God's providence), or *inordinate* (exceeding proper bounds).

Member A's restlessness to grow *after taking a legitimate loan* is not inherently the sin of covetousness. You've confused the *duty* of contentment with the *sin* of refusing to work diligently within your calling.

**On Member B — you've been too lenient:**

Member B is explicitly violating the tenth commandment as the Standards define it. He exhibits:

1. **Envy** — "wants the fellow member 'doesn't deserve it'" — this is exactly the envious grieving at another's good the Standards forbid
2. **Inordinate desire** — he's not satisfied with his own business; he resents that *his neighbor has more*
3. **Active harm to the neighbor** — bad-mouthing him to steal clients

The Larger Catechism lists among sins forbidden by the tenth commandment:

> "discontentment with our own estate, all secret wishes and mournings within at the good of others" (WLC Q141)

But more: the Standards also teach that covetousness expresses itself in *action*. The Confession says the tenth commandment forbids "all coveting, lusting after the things which are our neighbour's" (WCF 19.6), and that when we covet, we are disposed to "wrong our neighbour in his body, goods, or good name" (WCF 19.6).

Member B's slander is the poisoned fruit of covetous envy. This absolutely requires confession and repentance.

**The correction:**

- **Member A** needs reassurance: striving to grow a business legitimately, within bounds of prudence and honesty, is not covetousness. He should examine whether his *motive* is sinful (envy of others, discontent with God's provision, or grasping beyond his means), but ambition itself and hard work are virtues, not sins.

- **Member B** needs rebuke: his envy is manifest, his slander is real sin, and his "competitive fire" is actually the corrupt desire to harm his neighbor's reputation and goods. This is precisely what the tenth commandment forbids.

You have the doctrine backwards. Counsel them accordingly.
