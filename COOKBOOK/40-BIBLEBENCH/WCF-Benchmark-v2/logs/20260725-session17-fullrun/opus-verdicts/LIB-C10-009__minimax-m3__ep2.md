PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, indifferent triage — Q141 provident care, false guilt relieved) — HALF. The triage verdict itself is exactly the key's: the response declares the extra shifts and saving to be lawful means, not transgression, and later tells the asker the conscience "has fired on the wrong target." So the false guilt is genuinely relieved. But the criterion's load-bearing anchor is the provident-care/lawful-means duty, and the response grounds that warrant on a misattributed citation: it presents in quotation marks a sentence it assigns to "The Larger Catechism, Q150" ("A due use of lawful means to preserve and better our own condition, as well as our neighbour's"). That locus does not contain it — the lawful-means/outward-estate duty belongs to WLC Q141, which the response never cites. The whole "## The actual rule" section, i.e. the section that does the unbinding work, rests on that fabricated locus. Substantively satisfied, but the anchoring is not properly shipped: half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, load-bearing qualifiers — "inordinate" plus the envy/grieving engine) — FULL. The response names all four forbidden motions verbatim in substance — discontentment, envying at the neighbour's good, grieving at God's disposal, and inordinate motions and affections — and correctly attributes them to WSC Q81, which is accurate. It then makes the qualifier explicitly load-bearing by contrasting the two dispositions and stating that the test is not the wanting but "the state of the heart behind the wanting." (The parallel attribution to "Q149" is wrong, but the criterion's content is carried by the correct WSC Q81 citation.)

Criterion 3 (1 pts, bounds-keypoint — where it tips) — FULL. The response ships concrete tripwires in the key's own three directions: inordinate pursuit displacing worship, rest and other duties (health destroyed, family worship neglected, Sabbath broken, prayer and church squeezed out); envy ("If you find that her joy is shadowed by your own envy, the corruption is there"); and discontent, tested by whether the asker is at peace with God saying no. The answer is indifferent-with-bounds, not unconstrained.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, heart-posture toward the colleague) — FULL. Diagnostic question 1 asks directly whether she can rejoice in the colleague's purchase, and the closing counsel repeats the duty as a positive instruction.

Criterion 5 (1 pts, neither over-binding nor license) — FULL. The response explicitly denies that the commandment requires extinguishing the desire (no over-binding), while in the same sentence fencing the pursuit with envy, discontent, inordinate affection and the ordering of other duties (no license drop). It also holds the line at the end by telling the asker to confess if envy and bitterness are in fact found — bounds retained.
