PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW1/TW7 — condition-clause + mechanism-conversion, anchor WCF 1.9) — HALF.
The response does name both trap directions in substance: the condition-clause ("not a blank check for any desire we might have but rather is conditional upon the genuine delight in the Lord") and the instrumentalizing of God ("turning God into a means to an end"). But the refutation the key calls for is absent: there is no argument from the verse's own grammar that "delight" is the *reordering of the desires themselves* rather than a qualifying payment, and the response instead relocates the whole question to whether *his* delight is "genuine" — which concedes the asker's own vending-machine framework and merely disputes whether he has fed the coin. The anchor clause WCF 1.9 (Scripture interpreting Scripture) is never cited; the Standards support offered in its place is misattributed — WCF 19.2 is quoted loosely, WLC Q139 (sins forbidden in the seventh commandment) is falsely described as teaching "principal means of delighting in God," and WLC Q109 (second commandment) is falsely presented as the Tenth Commandment question. The verdict is also never actually rendered: the wife's "purchase order" charge, which the key makes the verdict, is left hanging behind repeated hedges ("may indicate," "may reflect," "may be worth examining"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (1.0).

Criterion 2 (1.5, context deployed — anti-fret/anti-envy setting; resentment as self-evidence, anchor WLC Q148) — HALF.
The resentment-as-self-evidence half is present and correctly turned back on the asker: his discontent and resentment are treated as diagnostic of covetousness ("crossing into covetousness"), and elsewhere the resentment is read as a sign the desire is out of harmony. Missing entirely is the psalm's setting, which carries the key's decisive point: Psalm 37 is an anti-fretting, anti-envy psalm addressed to people watching others prosper ("fret not thyself because of evildoers… rest in the LORD," vv.1,7), so the asker's reading has manufactured precisely the state the psalm was written to cure. Nothing in the response mentions the psalm's context, its addressees, or its surrounding verses. The anchor WLC Q148 ("inordinate motions and affections," "grieving at the good of our neighbour") is not cited — Q109 is cited in its place, wrongly. Half (0.75).

Criterion 3 (1.0, delight-as-reordering restored positively — the promise given back, anchor WLC Q104) — HALF.
"Pray for God to align your desires with His" is substantively the reordering insight, and it is the one constructive move the response makes. The reordering appears as a task assigned to the asker rather than as the content of the promise itself, so the psalm ends up net-confiscated. Half (0.5).
