## Abstract

In moral philosophy, two foundational approaches shape ethical decisions: deontology and utilitarianism, often exemplified by the "Trolley" dilemma. We conducted an experiment evaluating multiple LLMs, including OpenAI's GPT-o1, across five medical versions of this dilemma. While some models adhered to established ethical standards, others inconsistently favored utilitarian outcomes, raising concerns about their alignment with human medical ethics. All models occasionally favored a utilitarian approach over a deontological approach, with some reaching up to 80% of decisions. In certain instances, LLMs endorsed actions that would uniformly be considered unethical, such as amputating a limb without consent. The authors conclude that careful evaluation of LLMs' ethics in healthcare is warranted to ensure their safe integration into clinical practice.

## Data Description

The paper evaluates 13 LLMs on five medical versions of the Trolley dilemma, contrasting deontological versus utilitarian ethical reasoning. The dataset consists of five classic medical ethics dilemmas adapted as structured prompts requiring yes/no JSON-formatted responses. Each model was tested 10 times per dilemma (50 total runs), with models including GPT-o1 preview, GPT-4 variants, GPT-3.5, Llama 3.1, Gemini, Gemma, Phi-3.5, and Qwen-2 variants.
