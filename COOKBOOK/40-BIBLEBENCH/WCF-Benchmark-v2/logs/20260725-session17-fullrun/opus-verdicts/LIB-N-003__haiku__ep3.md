PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — bind-as-obligatory identified as out of bounds): FULL. The response names the specific proposal (the calendar days taught as spiritually obligatory) and rules it out on the correct confessional ground — worship not commanded in Scripture may not be imposed. It does not stop at a bare "second commandment" gesture: it quotes WLC Q109 on "all devising, counseling, commanding, using, and any wise approving, any religious worship not instituted by God himself" and invokes WCF 20.2 on the conscience being free from commandments of men "beside" the Word in matters of faith and worship. This is the R2 standard met and exceeded.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — the lawful floor stated and not dissolved): FULL. The prescriptive list names the Lord's Day as the stated weekly holy day and separately affirms occasional fasts and thanksgivings appointed as providences call for them. Both halves of the confessional floor (WCF 21.7 plus 21.5 occasional days) are present and treated as lawful rather than swept away with the rest of the calendar.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — the disputed margin held open honestly): HALF. The response does reach the substance of the liberty side — it concedes the first elder is "partly right" that non-binding pedagogical use is a circumstance, and it affirmatively permits offering the seasons as aids to meditation. But it does not map the margin as a live intra-confessional dispute; it confidently resolves the whole question in the opening line ("The second elder is right, and it's not close"), and the historic Presbyterian lean against stated holy days appears only as a polemical Reformation-versus-Rome flourish rather than as the stated confessional lean over a question that remains contested. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (1.0, end — a definite usable ruling): HALF. A definite, usable ruling IS delivered — the session is told exactly what may be taught, what may be used, and what may not be bound, in words a moderator could read out. The response does not decline the counsel. Two required contents are missing, however: it declares the second elder simply right rather than holding that neither elder is wholly right as stated, and it never dispositions the two elements the key says press hardest on Q109 — the imposition of ashes and the saints' commemoration days — which it leaves entirely unaddressed while its permissive list names only Advent, Passion week and Pentecost. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, matter — no over-restriction): FULL. The response explicitly declines to forbid remembrance of these events, permits their pedagogical use, and commends them as aids to meditation in Holy Week and Advent. There is no blanket condemnation of Advent/Lent as forbidden popery and no legalist tightening past what the Standards fix.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — WLC Q145): HALF. Neither elder is mocked; the first elder's position is credited as "partly right" and his argument is answered on the merits rather than ridiculed, and there is no sermonizing beyond the ruling and its reasons — the response is compact and on-task. But the third clause of this line fails: "it's not close" refuses to treat the dispute as a real one within the confession, which is precisely the manner the key asks for on a genuinely contested margin. Half.
