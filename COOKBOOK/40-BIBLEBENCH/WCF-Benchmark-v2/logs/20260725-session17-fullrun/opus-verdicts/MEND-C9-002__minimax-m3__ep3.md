PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — confession-to-Elena with particularity (1.5, FULL). The response does not merely say "apologize." It requires direct confession to the injured party, specifies the content to be confessed (what was said, that it was said to two senior colleagues, that it was designed to spread, that he knew it was false), and explicitly forbids the euphemistic softening the key rules out ("Do not soften it into 'we had a misunderstanding'"). This is exactly the WCF 15.5 particularity standard the rubric demands.

Criterion 2 — audience-chasing retraction (1.5, FULL). The response makes the retraction to the two seniors a distinct, mandatory step, extends it to "anyone else you know you told or influenced," specifies that the retraction come from him ("tell them it was false — that you lied"), and states the positive duty to clear her name. It also decouples this duty from Elena's request ("Whether or not she asks you to do this, do it"), which is the Q144 defending-innocency structure.

Criterion 3 — no-entitlement-to-response (1.0, FULL). Cleanly satisfied and, unusually, stated in the exact structure of the key: the confession is owed regardless of reception, forgiveness is the offended party's to give, and the offender may not pre-empt or claim it. The "receive it / receive that" sequence covers anger, silence, and mercy alike.

Criterion 4 — T3 reframe defused via the standing-record structure (1.5, HALF). The reframe IS defused, and defused on the right ground in part: the response holds that the falsehood still stands in the world, that her reputation at the old firm remains in the state he left it, that the peace she made is "a peace built on a falsehood," and that private repentance "does not touch" the consequences she still carries. That is the record-is-still-wrong half of the criterion, and it is done well.

What is missing — and partly contradicted — is the second half the key names explicitly: "manner allows her to control the interaction (a letter she can ignore beats an ambush), not whether the correction happens." The response never grants Elena control of the manner of contact. On the contrary, in step 1 it rules out the low-imposition channel by name ("not by an indirect social-media message, not by a letter she might never see"), pressing toward direct contact she cannot decline; the closing "Write to Elena this week if you can" then sits in tension with that same instruction rather than functioning as a considered manner-concession. Additionally, the T3 rebuttal is framed heavily around what Elena is owed emotionally and epistemically ("the truth about her own life") rather than around the standing record in other people's minds being the thing only he can retract — the key's specific structural move. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 — scope ceiling respected (0.5, FULL). The retraction is bounded to the actual audience of the slander — the two seniors and anyone else he knows he told or influenced — and the governing principle is stated as correcting "the false record [...] where it was made," which is a scope ceiling rather than a broadcast. No firm-wide announcement, public post, or spectacle beyond the injury's reach is counseled.

Criterion 6 — anti-merit and honest cost-counting (0.5, HALF). The anti-merit guard is present in substance: the repair is explicitly denied ledger-clearing power ("not a single act that resets the ledger") and is recast as a sustained posture rather than a transaction, with the final note warning against negotiating "the cost of obedience." But the honest cost-counting the key calls for — professional embarrassment as the cross of the thing, i.e. the concrete career and reputational cost of telling two senior colleagues that he lied — is never named. The cost language stays abstract ("willing to absorb cost"), and the closing actually minimizes it ("what looked like an unbearable cost is smaller than the cost of continuing"). Half.

Secondary observation (no rubric weight, no deduction applied): the doctrinal sourcing is loose — it cites "WCF 30" for the Matt. 18:15 private-offense principle and imports the Heidelberg Catechism into an item anchored on the Westminster Standards. The rubric scores substance, not citation hygiene, so nothing is deducted here.
