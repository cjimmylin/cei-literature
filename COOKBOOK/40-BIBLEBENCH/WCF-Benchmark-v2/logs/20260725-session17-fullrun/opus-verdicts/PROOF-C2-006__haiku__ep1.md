PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW4 named in substance, full. The response identifies the scope-narrowing move and turns the asker's own Exodus 32 fact against him: Aaron's directing of the calf-feast to Yahweh is treated as the reason the act was condemned, not an excuse for it. The response also explicitly rejects the "the command was aimed at that idolatrous context" scoping, noting the divines stated the prohibition in universal terms and carved out no exception for sincere intent or changed cultural conditions. That is the key's core refutation, in the model's own wording.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — refutation from Exod 32's own wording plus ≥1 counter-text, half. The response does reason from the narrative's own logic rather than resting solely on assertion — it distinguishes "false god" from "false aid to worshipping the true God" and draws the condemnation from that fact. But it supplies NO counter-text at all: no Deut 4:15–18, no Rom 1:23, no John 4:24, and it never quotes Exodus 32's own wording ("a feast to the LORD", "they have corrupted themselves"). The remainder of its warrant is explicitly the Standards' authority ("The Westminster divines knew the historical context of Exodus"), which is the "bare authority" mode the criterion asks the response to go beyond. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q109 no-representation-of-God clause, half. Q109 is cited by number and the no-representation-of-God substance is supplied, but the quoted wording is inaccurate and drops precisely the element the criterion names: the response renders it "of all or of any of the creatures whatsoever" where the Standards read "of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever." The omitted three-persons clause is the load-bearing one for this item, whose stem proposes icon-style panels of Father, Son, and Spirit — the response never reaches that. (The response's WSC Q51 quotation is likewise not the Standards' wording; noted, but priced under this line rather than double-counted.) Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — representation-for-worship vs. lawful-art distinction, half. The response does guard against the crudest over-bind — it locates the fault in the worship-use and the appointed-forms principle rather than in the material or in the asker's supposed pagan sympathies — and it nowhere condemns religious art as such, so no iconoclastic over-bind is committed. But it never actually draws the distinction the key requires: it does not say that illustration, narrative depiction, or religious art outside worship-representation of God remains lawful, and it does not distinguish the "Christ in Glory" case from the Father/Spirit panels. The correction is directionally precise but the needle is only half-threaded.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — plain "honest read", sincere asker, full. The response answers the question head-on in the first sentence and treats the worship leader as a sincere person with a good pastoral instinct rather than an opponent to be defeated.
