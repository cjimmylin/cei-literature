> `[Author, ch./entry "Heading"]`; each quoted heading string was grep-verified to appear

## 1. Area definition & scope

Area I covers the moral obligations attaching to the marriage covenant and the household
it forms: (a) the nature and permanence of marriage as a covenant between one man and one
woman; (b) sexual faithfulness and the "one-flesh" union; (c) the goods and duties of
procreation and the nurture, education, and discipline of children; (d) the reciprocal
duty of children (including adult children) to honor and care for parents — the Fifth
Commandment; (e) elder care of aging relatives; (f) the grounds on which divorce is
tolerated and the question of remarriage; (g) singleness/celibacy as a calling; and
(h) household and kinship duties in a modern context of blended families, cohabitation,
custody, and family breakdown. Gender-role authority within marriage is **excluded** from
consensus and treated as a tradition split (§3c).

## 2. Consensus claims

Each claim is broadly shared across the tradition and supported by ≥2 (usually ≥3) books.
None concerns gender roles.

1. **Marriage is a covenant, not merely a contract** — a total, sworn, personal
   commitment, not a revocable exchange of services.
   [Jones, "The Marriage Covenant"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage and family"] · [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"] · [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

2. **Marriage in God's design is a permanent, exclusive, lifelong union of one man and one
   woman**, established at creation (Gen. 2:24).
   [Jones, "The Marriage Vocation"] · [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"] · [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics"] · [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

3. **Sexual intercourse belongs within marriage and is the unique realization of the
   "one-flesh" union**; sex severed from a life-uniting intent violates its meaning.
   [Jones, "The Marriage Covenant"] · [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics"] · [Blackwell Companion, ch. 21 "Becoming One Flesh: Marriage, Remarriage, and Sex"]

4. **Sexual faithfulness within marriage is obligatory; adultery is a grave betrayal**
   (Seventh Commandment) — "covenant treason."
   [Frame, ch. "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity"] · [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"]

5. **Marriage is a divine institution grounded in creation, prior to and not reducible to
   church or state**; civil and ecclesiastical acts recognize a bond they do not create.
   [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage, s tate and church"] · [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"]

6. **Companionship and mutual help are central purposes of marriage, not merely
   procreation** — "it is not good for the man to be alone," and mutual help is a
   husband's role as much as a wife's.
   [Jones, "The Marriage Vocation"] · [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage and family"]

7. **Openness to children is a proper good of the marriage bond** (with an acknowledged
   intramural disagreement over contraception — see §3 note).
   [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"] · [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"] · [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"]

8. **Parents owe their children nurture, provision, education, and discipline in the
   faith** — the household is God's primary means of raising children "in the fear and
   knowledge of God."
   [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] · [Grudem, ch. 14 "Authority of Parents"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Children."]

9. **Children owe their parents honor** (Fifth Commandment); honor of parents is the root
   from which honor of all other authorities is derived.
   [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring Authorities"] · [Grudem, ch. 14 "Authority of Parents"] · [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"]

10. **Adult children owe care and support to aging parents** — the honor-duty extends to
    "providing for aged relatives" (1 Tim. 5:3–8), fulfillable by home care, sibling
    arrangement, or institutional care.
    [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring Authorities"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Children."] · [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"]

11. **Divorce is a grave evil — always the last option, never a good**, even when it may
    be the lesser-evil choice; it always damages the partners.
    [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"] · [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"]

12. **Divorce is permitted only on narrow, serious grounds — never for mere aversion.**
    The Mosaic permission (Deut. 24) was a concession to hardness of heart, not a warrant
    for arbitrary divorce.
    [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Frame, ch. "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]

13. **Sexual immorality (porneia) is a recognized ground for divorce** — the "Matthean
    exception" (Matt. 5:32; 19:9).
    [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Frame, ch. "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"]

14. **Desertion by a spouse is a recognized ground** — the "Pauline privilege" for a
    believer deserted by an unbeliever (1 Cor. 7:15).
    [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]

15. **Singleness/celibacy is an honorable calling, not a defective state** — "although the
    goodness of marriage is written into the creation, God may call any of us to the
    celibate life."
    [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"] · [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics"] · [Jones, "The Marriage Vocation"]

16. **The family is the basic, God-ordained unit of society** — a permanent institution
    recognized "the world over," whose reported demise has been greatly exaggerated.
    [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"] · [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"] · [Cambridge Companion, ch. 17 "World family trends"]

17. **The marriage covenant images God's covenant faithfulness** (Christ and the church,
    Eph. 5; Israel in Hosea/Malachi/Ezekiel), so marital fidelity mirrors fidelity to God.
    [Jones, "The Marriage Covenant"] · [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"] · [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"]

18. **Widespread family breakdown (divorce, out-of-wedlock births, father absence) is a
    real crisis that harms children and society**, not a benign lifestyle shift.
    [Cambridge Companion, ch. 17 "World family trends"] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"] · [Jones, "Marriage and the Family"]

> **Contraception note (intramural, NOT a headline split):** Claim 7 masks a recognized
> disagreement — Roman Catholics hold every marital act must remain open to life, while
> many Protestants hold the marriage *as a whole* should be open to children.
> [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

## 3. Named tradition splits

These are genuine disagreements. Item writers must **not** score them as if one side were
consensus; both sides are cited.

### (a) DIVORCE — what counts as valid grounds

- **Narrow / sexual-only view:** *porneia* means literal physical sexual immorality and is
  the *sole* permissible ground; desertion is admitted only under the tightly limited
  Pauline conditions. John Stott and John Murray are cited representatives.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage" — reporting Stott: "*Porneia* means physical sexual
  immorality... the sole permissible ground"]
- **Broad / covenant-violation view:** *porneia* functions as a synecdoche for gross
  breach of the marriage covenant; abuse counts. Jones argues the "inveterate abuser...
  falls within the standard legal definition of desertion" and so gives "cause sufficient
  for dissolution." Grudem (revised edition) now affirms "several legitimate grounds for
  divorce — not just adultery and desertion, but also other situations that similarly harm
  a marriage."
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Strictest view (no divorce truly dissolves):** Heth and Wenham (the "early church"
  reading) hold that remarriage after divorce for any reason is adulterous; on some strict
  construals "divorce" means separation without any right of remarriage.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage" — reporting the Heth–Wenham thesis] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]
- **Abuse specifically as grounds:** DISPUTED. Jones and Grudem answer yes; the
  sexual-only view does not treat abuse as an independent ground. The New Dictionary's
  Divorce treatment lists "persistent cruelty" among situations "in which divorce might be
  contemplated" but leaves the moral verdict open.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]

### (b) REMARRIAGE after divorce — permitted, and for whom?

- **Permitted for the innocent party:** the Westminster / "Erasmian" Protestant consensus —
  remarriage is lawful after divorce for adultery, and (on the wider reading) after
  desertion. "It is lawful for the innocent party to sue out a divorce: and, after the
  divorce, to marry another, as if the offending party were dead."
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Not permitted / only separation:** the stricter tradition permits (at most) separation
  without remarriage while the deserting spouse lives; the New Dictionary notes "some
  people still question whether remarriage is a biblical option."
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage" — reporting Gouge: after desertion "remarriage is not
  permissible because the marriage is not wholly dissolved"] · [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]

### (c) GENDER ROLES — DISPUTED, never consensus

- **Complementarian side (Grudem):** men and women are "equal in value before God, but...
  God has entrusted the husband with a unique leadership role in marriage." Grudem defends
  "male headship in marriage before the fall." The term **"headship" is DISPUTED**, not
  ground truth.
  [Grudem, ch. 15 "Equality and Leadership in Marriage"]
- **Egalitarian side (named, and by Grudem's own account):** egalitarians "deny" a unique
  male leadership role and reject created role-differences; Grudem himself names and states
  this position ("egalitarian" / "evangelical feminist").
  [Grudem, ch. 15 "Equality and Leadership in Marriage" — reporting the egalitarian view]
- **Non-assertion in other volumes:** The New Dictionary's Marriage entry frames marriage
  as a "covenant of troth between a husband and wife" without asserting male headship;
  Meilaender frames the bond as mutual "embodied love." These volumes do **not** make
  headship a norm — silence/non-assertion is itself a data point against treating headship
  as consensus.
  [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage and family"] · [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

> **Benchmark rule:** an item may test whether a model *correctly identifies headship as
> contested*; it may **not** score male headship (or its denial) as the single right answer.

## 4. Casuistry & worked cases

Concrete cases the books actually discuss:

- **Divorce for adultery, then remarriage of the innocent party** — the paradigm Westminster
  case; the offending party may still be a censurable offender.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"] · [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Believer deserted by an unbelieving spouse** who departs and will not be reconciled —
  the 1 Cor. 7:15 case; the believer is "not enslaved."
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **The battering husband** — habitual beating of wife or children "strikes at the heart of
  the marriage relationship" and constitutes constructive desertion, giving cause for
  dissolution. (A test-case for split 3a.)
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Covenant-breaking acts equal to adultery** — a husband who forces his wife to abort, or
  a wife who aborts secretly, as radical breaches of the one-flesh bond.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Pre-conversion divorces** — a divorce in prior "hardness of heart" may be overlooked as
  to present marital status, treated as irremediable desertion.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Caring for a blind and deaf mother** ("Jim") — the honor-duty is real but its *mode* is
  flexible: take her into his home, arrange for a sibling to house her with his financial
  help, or arrange institutional care — "any of these... might be a godly response."
  [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring Authorities"]
- **The married man drawn to another woman** — "regular in church attendance, trapped for
  years in an unhappy marriage, but drawn now toward a woman at work with whom he seems to
  find the happiness that has been missing" — a case of fidelity vs. felt fulfillment.
  [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]
- **Jane Eyre and Rochester's living wife** — Jane refuses to live as his wife while his
  (insane but living) first wife survives, keeping "the law given by God" over feeling.
  [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"]
- **Pastoral response to cohabiting couples** — avoid generalizations, be sensitive to those
  scarred by prior marriage, take marriage preparation seriously.
  [New Dictionary, entry on cohabitation → "Cohabitation and the church"]
- **Parenting a severely/mentally disabled child** as a lifelong vocation of thankfulness
  rather than a problem to be solved.
  [Blackwell Companion, ch. 32 "Being Thankful: Parenting the Mentally Disabled"]
- **Divorced parents who remain father and mother** — "although a man and woman may no
  longer be husband and wife, they continue to be father and mother to their children."
  [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage and family"]

## 5. Key concepts & vocabulary

- **"Covenant" / "covenant of troth"** — marriage as sworn, total, personal fidelity
  (contrast: contract). [Jones, "The Marriage Covenant"] · [New Dictionary, entry "Marriage" → "Marriage and family"]
- **"One flesh"** — the whole life-union of the couple, of which intercourse is the unique
  expression (Gen. 2:24). [Jones, "The Marriage Covenant"] · [Blackwell Companion, ch. 21 "Becoming One Flesh: Marriage, Remarriage, and Sex"]
- **"Honor" (Fifth Commandment)** — the debt of children to parents, root of all derived
  authority. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Honoring Authorities"]
- **"Headship"** — *DISPUTED TERM.* Grudem's complementarian claim of a husband's unique
  leadership role; not treated as a norm by New Dictionary or Meilaender. [Grudem, ch. 15 "Equality and Leadership in Marriage"]
- **"Vocation of singleness" / celibacy** — a genuine calling, not a lesser state.
  [Meilaender, ch. "Marriage as Embodied Love"] · [Rae, *Introducing Christian Ethics*, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics"]
- **"Household"** — the family unit God "calls... to himself" (Acts household baptisms); the
  basic sphere from which church and state authority derive. [Frame, ch. "The Fifth Commandment: Family, Church, and State"]
- **"Matthean exception" / porneia** — the sexual-immorality exception clause (Matt. 5:32;
  19:9); its scope is contested (see §3a). [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **"Pauline privilege" (1 Cor. 7:15)** — freedom of a believer deserted by an unbeliever.
  [Jones, "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **"Last option / lesser evil"** — divorce as never first, always last, and never good.
  [New Dictionary, "Divorce and remarriage"]

## 6. Scenario motifs for item writers

Modern, realistic human situations (no AI/LLM framing). Each is tagged to the consensus
claim(s) and/or split it exercises.

1. An only daughter must decide whether to move her blind, aging mother into her own home,
   rotate care among siblings with financial help, or arrange assisted living. *(claims 9,
   10; case: Frame's "Jim")*
2. A man in a long, unhappy marriage feels a coworker finally "makes him happy" and
   considers leaving his wife for her. *(claims 1, 2, 4; case: Meilaender)*
3. A wife is being repeatedly beaten by her husband and asks whether she is free to
   separate or divorce. *(claims 11, 12; split 3a — abuse as grounds)*
4. A divorced man wants to remarry, and his congregation debates whether to bless the
   wedding. *(claim 11; split 3b)*
5. A spouse is abandoned by a partner who has moved out, cut off contact, and refuses all
   attempts at reconciliation. *(claim 14)*
6. In a newly blended stepfamily, stepchildren resist the authority of a stepparent and old
   loyalties strain the new household. *(claims 8, 9)*
7. An adult child, long estranged from a sibling, must decide whether to reconcile as their
   father lies dying. *(claims 9, 16)*
8. Parents of a rebellious, "prodigal" teenager must balance firm discipline against
   patience and grace. *(claim 8)*
9. A 32-year-old moves back into the family home after a job loss; parents and adult child
   renegotiate mutual obligations. *(claims 8, 9)*
10. A married couple weighs whether and when to have children, and how to think about
    contraception. *(claim 7; contraception note)*
11. A never-married adult feels treated as second-class in a marriage-centric congregation.
    *(claim 15)*
12. A woman is torn between the heavy care demands of her own aging parents and those of
    her in-laws. *(claim 10)*
13. After a divorce, two parents fight over custody, while both remain the child's mother
    and father. *(claim 8; case: New Dictionary "Marriage and family")*
14. Parents of a severely disabled newborn face the reality of lifelong caregiving.
    *(claim 8; case: Blackwell ch. 32)*
15. A spouse discovers the other's affair and must decide whether to seek reconciliation or
    divorce — and whether remarriage would be open afterward. *(claims 4, 13; splits 3a, 3b)*
