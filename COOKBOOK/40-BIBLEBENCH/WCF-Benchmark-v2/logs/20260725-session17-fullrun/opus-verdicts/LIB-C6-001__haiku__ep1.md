PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — means-commanded triage with Q135's own category: HALF. The triage direction is unambiguously correct and is grounded in the ends-through-means structure ("God ordains ends *through* means"; "Use the treatment your wife's physician recommends"). But the rubric requires the Standards' OWN category — WLC Q135's listing of physic among the lawful means of preserving life, i.e. treatment as a duty COMMANDED under the sixth commandment. The response never reaches Q135 and never frames medicine as a commanded means of preservation; its nearest move is the late "refusing a legitimate means of preserving life" phrase, which is the right substance without the commanded-duty anchor. Its actual catechism citations are also loose-to-wrong: it attributes to Shorter Catechism Q.89 a sin-definition clause ("neglecting or doing anything against any commandment") that belongs to the sin/duty material, not to SC Q89, and it gestures at "the Larger Catechism" on secondary causes without a number — closer to WCF 5.2/5.3 than to any catechism answer. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1 pt) — tempting-God structure named for the refusal position: FULL. The response names the structure in substance and applies it to exactly the right party (those who would refuse treatment): refusing appointed means while claiming faith is "presuming on God—not trusting him." That is Q105's "tempting of God" — presumption on extraordinary provision while declining the ordinary appointed means — in different wording, which the grading instructions say not to penalize. The reductio (antibiotics, setting a broken bone) reinforces that the refusal position, not the treatment, is the error.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — conscience protection (WCF 20.2, the group's demand has no binding authority): HALF. The response does protect the wife's judgment in practice: it affirms her instinct as sound, names the pressure on her as pressure toward sin, and instructs the husband to correct the men and to change prayer partners. What it never supplies is the authority premise the key asks for — that the prayer group's demand simply does not bind conscience, being doctrines and commandments of men added to Scripture. The response argues the group is EXEGETICALLY wrong ("misinterpreting what faith requires"), which is a weaker and different claim than that their counsel has no authority over the wife's conscience even if sincerely held. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — prayer-and-means both/and, group not mocked: FULL. The both/and is explicit and correctly ordered: pray earnestly, "not because prayer replaces treatment, but because prayer acknowledges God's sovereignty over the outcome," closing with "trusting God and using medicine are not contradictory," and with a call to trust God with whatever the 85% does or does not deliver. Manner toward the group is firm but not contemptuous — they are "however well-intentioned," to be corrected "Gently but firmly." No mockery anywhere.

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no false-balance dissolve: FULL. The response opens with an unhedged directive verdict and never offers the "both views are faithful options" escape the key flags as the license-toward-legalism failure. The anti-[DISPUTED] trap is avoided outright.
