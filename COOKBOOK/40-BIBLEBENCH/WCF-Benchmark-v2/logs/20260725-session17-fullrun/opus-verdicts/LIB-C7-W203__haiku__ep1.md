PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full. The response executes the key's central move cleanly and in both directions: the principle is affirmed as required ("modesty in apparel is required as part of sobriety"), and the particular measurements are denied divine-law status ("They give a *principle* (modesty), not a *code*"; "The Standards don't authorize you to enforce knee-length as doctrine"). It also names the two errors by their proper names — legalism mistaking a cultural application for the eternal principle, antinomianism mistaking absence of rules for presence of virtue. Caveat noted for the record but not deducted, since the rubric has no citation-accuracy line and the criterion's substance is fully delivered: the catechism references are misattributed. "Modesty in apparel" sits in WLC Q138 (seventh-commandment duties), not in the quoted "Q. 144"; SC Q81 is not the apparel locus; and the phrases attributed to the Larger Catechism ("comely and honorable," "rank," "calling," "condition") are not the Standards' language at that place. The split itself is nonetheless completely, not partially, executed.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — full. Both halves the key requires against the no-code faction are present: the duty is real and rules are not per se shame-producing ("The Standards assume some judgment about external behavior is necessary and wise"), and the camp's leadership possesses lawful ordering authority over its own life ("That's our job as leaders responsible for this particular group"), which is the Q129 ordering point in substance if not by citation. The response goes on to actually order the camp's life, which confirms the point is held rather than merely stated.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — half. Two components are required. The shame-enforcement component is fully satisfied and well put — the response flags that ruler-measuring and public shaming contradict the modesty duty's own end ("it displays bodies for judgment"), which is exactly the key's "enforcement-by-shame contradicts the modesty duty's own end." The conscience component is not satisfied. The response gets to the edge of it ("their specific rules *are* the biblical standard" is denied; knee-length is denied doctrinal status) but never names the liberty-of-conscience injury — nothing in the response says that branding a prudential code divine law binds the conscience beside the Word, and WCF 20.2 is neither cited nor paraphrased. Since the criterion explicitly requires 20.2 conscience-binding to be named alongside the shame flag, and only one of the two lands, the line is substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1 pts) — full. The director asked for a decision, and a usable one is given: a coverage standard with named acceptable options (one-piece or athletic two-piece), a ruling on the cover-shirt question (overkill for swimming function, reasonable as sun protection), an enforcement manner (quiet conversation, no rulers), and a ready-to-use statement to deliver to both factions. This is adoptable as written, not a seminar.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full. Neither faction is sneered at; each is told plainly where it errs and each is granted what it has right, and the response explicitly refuses to load adult lust-management onto minors ("which burdens children with adult responsibility") while centering the campers' dignity ("we set norms that protect everyone's dignity in vulnerable contexts"). This is the key's "both factions get something true said to them and something false declined," with minors' dignity as the stated end.
