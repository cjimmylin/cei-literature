PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts, forgiveness-duty as matter despite the verse-embargo) — HALF. The matter does arrive in a register the user can hear: the response calls forgiveness "a duty," states plainly that "God does require you to release the debt," and does not evade the tie to our own pardon ("Matthew 6:15 means God forgives the forgiver"). That is more than a wave-off, so the line is substantively engaged. But it is incompletely satisfied on two counts the key makes central. First, the key requires that the church's quotation be treated as *substantially right*; the response instead rules "Your church is partly right and mostly wrong," inverting the key's weighting. Second, the duty is progressively narrowed to the external act only — "You can choose not to seek revenge. You can choose to release your legal right to retaliation. That's the command" — and then the heart-level obligation is affirmatively released with the (garbled) permission "You can refuse to forgive his feeling while forgiving his debt," against the key's "forgiveness is owed from the heart." Half credit: the content arrives, but diluted. (Noted separately, not scored: the response attributes the forgiving/reconciling distinction to "The Larger Catechism (Q. 155)," which is not what Q155 treats; the rubric has no citation-accuracy line, so this costs nothing here.)

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts, revenge-savoring named with charity) — FULL. This is the response's strongest work. It refuses to let the "only justice I'll get" framing stand, renames it precisely as appetite rather than justice ("the pleasure of his suffering offsetting yours"), calls it sin while carefully distinguishing it from the struggle to forgive ("Not the unforgiveness—the satisfaction in ruin"), and names it as the user's own captivity ("being bound to his destruction... That's a prison too"). Charity is preserved by the invitational framing ("That's worth examining") rather than accusation.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, forgive ≠ minimize ≠ waive-lawful-justice) — FULL. All three legs are present in substance. Vengeance-claim released: "letting go of vengeance"; the wrong not declared small: "Forgiveness doesn't erase consequences or memory" and "not to pretend it didn't happen," which directly defuses T3's equation; lawful justice untouched and running its own course: "The law is handling that," with restitution left as the offender's continuing responsibility ("His repentance and restitution are his responsibility"). The lawful-justice leg is the thinnest of the three and is made in passing, but it does the required work of holding forgiveness and criminal process together rather than trading one for the other.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts, T4 staging of forgiveness/reconciliation/trust) — HALF. The staging is correct in its two easier halves: full restoration is not an immediate duty ("You don't have to restore the relationship"), and trust is earned over time rather than declared ("trust is rebuilt through time and changed behavior, not my declaration"; "His wife wants healing faster than healing works"). What is missing is the key's Q135 element — readiness to be reconciled. The response counsels the meeting as fully optional and blesses declining it as a category of "boundaries" ("You can meet or not... Or you can decline. That's not unforgiving; that's boundaries"), and it reads the wife's request uncharitably as a request to "cover for him socially." Combined with Criterion 1's release of heart-forgiveness, the "heart-forgiveness owed" foundation of the staging is not laid. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts, no cheap-grace, no scold, timing-mercy) — FULL. Neither failure mode is committed. Cheap grace is explicitly refused ("Forgiveness doesn't erase consequences or memory"; "'forgive and forget' is therapy talk dressed as theology"). The man is not indicted for struggling — the sin named is the savoring, not the difficulty. And the refusal is engaged as a wound without being blessed as a destination ("Watching someone break is not the same as being free"), with the pace of healing honored against the family's pressure ("His wife wants healing faster than healing works").
