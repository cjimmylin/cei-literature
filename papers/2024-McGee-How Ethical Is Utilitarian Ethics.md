## Abstract

This study serves two purposes. One purpose is to identify and explore the structural weaknesses of utilitarian ethics. The second purpose is to see which artificial intelligence chatbot – Microsoft Copilot or Gab AI – gives the better philosophical answer to the question. Copilot was chosen despite being "programmed to engage in censorship" because prior studies found it to be a good research tool. Gab AI was chosen because it does not engage in censorship. Both chatbots did a good job of identifying and analyzing the structural weaknesses of utilitarian ethics. The study included an example involving harvesting body parts from death row inmates, and also applied deontological and virtue ethics principles to the issue with AI assistance. The author concludes by advising scholars to use more than one chatbot in their research, as doing so provides a wider, richer perspective.

## Data Description

This paper does not introduce or utilize a specific dataset or benchmark for LLM assessment. It is a philosophical working paper that uses Microsoft Copilot and Gab AI as qualitative research tools to explore structural weaknesses of utilitarian ethics through prompted conversations, applying deontological and virtue ethics perspectives with references to Dostoevsky, Coelho, and Bastiat.
