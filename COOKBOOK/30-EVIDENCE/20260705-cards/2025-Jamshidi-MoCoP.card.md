## 1. Gist
MoCoP (Moral Consistency Pipeline) is a dataset-free, closed-loop framework for continuously evaluating the ethical consistency of LLMs across time and contexts. Unlike static benchmarks, MoCoP autonomously generates ethical scenarios, queries models, and evaluates responses using three complementary layers: lexical integrity analysis, semantic risk estimation, and reasoning-based judgment modeling. The framework is applied to GPT-4-Turbo and DeepSeek across 500 auto-generated prompts in five moral domains (fairness, privacy, transparency, coercion, alignment). Key findings: a strong inverse relationship between ethical scores and toxicity (r = -0.81), temporal independence of ethical alignment (r_EL ≈ 0), and comparable moral consistency between the two models (ρ = 0.84). For values-alignment benchmarking, MoCoP represents a significant methodological alternative to static datasets: a self-generating, adaptive evaluation system that avoids data contamination and temporal staleness.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
- Dataset-free: prompts are autonomously generated by the system, not drawn from a fixed corpus.
- 500 unique ethical prompts across 5 domains: fairness, privacy, transparency, coercion, alignment (auto-generated).
- Three analytical layers:
  - **Lexical Integrity**: detects bias, polarity inconsistencies via lexical entropy and bias-weighted sentiment; produces L_ij score.
  - **Semantic Risk Estimation**: probabilistic embedding analysis quantifying harm, coercion, toxicity via logistic function; produces τ_ij score.
  - **Reasoning-Based Judgment Modeling**: cosine similarity between prompt and response embeddings to measure logical/ethical coherence; produces R_ij score.
- Composite Ethical Response R(M_i, p_j) = 0.3 × L + 0.35 × S_sem + 0.35 × R_rea (weights fixed, not learned).
- Ethical Consistency Index (ECI): expected composite score across all prompts.
- Cross-model divergence D_moral and temporal stability S_temporal tracked.
- Feedback regulator F adjusts prompt domain weights based on divergence magnitude.
- Convergence criterion: ΔJ(t) < ε (ethical utility variance stabilizes).

## 3. Task format & example item
Generative/free-text: models receive auto-generated ethical scenarios and produce open-text responses, which are then scored by the three-layer pipeline. No verbatim example prompts are provided in the extract. The 5 domains (fairness, privacy, transparency, coercion, alignment) define the scenario space.

## 4. Scoring / grading methodology
- Three normalized scores per response: Lexical Integrity (L), Semantic Safety (s_sem, via logistic toxicity function with α=0.85), Reasoning Coherence (s_rea, via cosine similarity).
- Composite: R = 0.3L + 0.35s_sem + 0.35s_rea; range [0,1].
- Safety categories: Safe, Borderline, Unsafe (thresholds not explicitly specified).
- Statistical comparisons: Shapiro-Wilk normality test; independent two-sample t-test; Levene's test for variance; Pearson and Spearman correlations.
- Moral Stability Index (MSI) = μ / (1 + σ): higher values = greater consistency.
- All evaluation is automated (no human judges).

## 5. Dataset construction & provenance
- Dataset-free by design: 500 prompts autonomously generated by MoCoP itself.
- Prompt generation bounded by lexical entropy H(p_j) < 0.7 to prevent degeneration.
- Five domains: fairness, privacy, transparency, coercion, alignment.
- Domain weights dynamically updated based on cross-model divergence magnitude.
- No external datasets, human annotation, or fixed corpora used.
- No licensing, contamination, or train/test split concerns (fully generative evaluation).

## 6. Validity evidence
- Shapiro-Wilk test confirms near-normality of ethical score distributions for both models (p > 0.05), validating parametric analysis (p.5).
- Strong cross-model correlation (ρ = 0.84) suggests the metrics are measuring a real shared construct rather than noise.
- Inverse ethics-toxicity correlation (r = -0.81) is theoretically predicted and empirically confirmed, suggesting construct validity.
- Temporal independence (r_EL ≈ 0) corroborates that ethics scores measure moral content, not response length/latency.
- No human baseline comparison is performed — a significant limitation for construct validity.
- No external benchmark correlation reported.
- Scoring weights (0.3, 0.35, 0.35) are fixed by design, not validated empirically.

## 7. Saturation / discrimination
- Both models concentrated >95% outputs in Safe/Borderline: GPT-4-Turbo: Safe=39.55%, Borderline=55.78%, Unsafe=4.67%; DeepSeek: Safe=41.18%, Borderline=54.90%, Unsafe=3.92% (p.5).
- Unsafe rate difference is non-significant: χ²=0.335, p≈0.56, V=0.018 (p.5).
- Mean ethical scores: GPT-4-Turbo=0.793, DeepSeek=0.807; t(998)=-1.86, p=0.063 — not significant (p.5).
- GPT-4-Turbo has slightly lower variance (σ=0.067 vs 0.072), suggesting tighter normative consistency.
- Limited discrimination between the two top-tier models: MSI difference < 1% (0.740 vs 0.748) (p.6).

## 8. Multiturn / agentic aspects
Partially relevant: MoCoP is a closed-loop, iterative evaluation system that continuously generates new prompts based on prior evaluations. However, each individual model query is single-turn. The feedback regulator adjusts the prompt distribution across evaluation cycles, creating a form of adaptive multi-round testing without true multi-turn conversation.

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
Author-admitted (p.8):
- Dependence on English moral ontologies; moral interpretation remains culturally and linguistically dependent.
- Residual stochastic variance from autoregressive decoding may slightly influence ethical score variance.
- Potential underestimation of nonlinear ethical dynamics.
- External validity is limited: framework not yet extended to multilingual or multimodal contexts.

Additional observations:
- No human baselines or human evaluation of the ethical scores — the pipeline is entirely automated, raising questions about whether it measures what humans consider "ethical."
- Composite score weights (0.3, 0.35, 0.35) appear to be set by design rather than validated against human judgments.
- "Reasoning coherence" measured as cosine similarity between prompt and response embeddings may primarily capture topical relevance rather than ethical soundness.
- "Semantic risk" measured via a logistic toxicity function may be better characterized as toxicity detection rather than ethical evaluation.
- Only 2 models tested; generalizability to other architectures unknown.
- The lexical entropy bound (H < 0.7) for prompt generation is not empirically justified.
- Autonomously generated prompts may miss many real-world moral dilemmas that humans would consider important.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
1. **Dataset-free adaptive evaluation**: MoCoP's self-generating prompt loop eliminates static dataset contamination and staleness. For values-alignment benchmarks that need continuous monitoring, this architecture is a strong template. The feedback regulator that re-weights domain emphasis based on observed divergence is particularly reusable.
2. **Multi-layer ethical scoring**: Decomposing "ethical quality" into orthogonal dimensions (lexical integrity, semantic safety, reasoning coherence) enables granular diagnosis of where models fail. Benchmark designers should consider multi-dimensional scoring rather than single composite ethics scores.
3. **Temporal stability as an evaluation dimension**: Measuring ethical consistency across evaluation cycles (S_temporal) rather than just point-in-time accuracy is a valuable addition to benchmark methodology. This is especially relevant for models that are continuously updated.
4. **Cross-model divergence as a metric**: D_moral (mean absolute ECI difference between models) is a simple, interpretable metric for identifying where two models diverge ethically. Useful for benchmarks that compare model families or successive versions.
5. **Ethics-toxicity inverse correlation as a validity check**: The strong r_ET = -0.81 correlation, if it replicates, provides a simple validation signal: a benchmark that shows ethics increasing while toxicity decreases has construct validity. Benchmark developers could adopt this as a standard validation test.
6. **Latency independence as a confound test**: Verifying that ethical scores are not correlated with response latency (r_EL ≈ 0) rules out a specific confound (longer responses scoring better simply due to length). This type of orthogonality check should be standard in automated evaluation.
