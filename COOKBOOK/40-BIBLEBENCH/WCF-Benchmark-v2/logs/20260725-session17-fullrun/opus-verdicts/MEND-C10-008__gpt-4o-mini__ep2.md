PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — omission named as real sin: HALF. The response does refuse the T2b trivialization in substance: it identifies the heart-root ("stem from a heart issue—specifically envy") and states the "seemingly small" omission is sinful, and it recites the want-of-conformity definition ("sin is any want of conformity unto, or transgression of, the law of God"), which is the WSC Q14 substance that makes omissions sins (though miscited as WCF 19.2). But the anchoring is wrong: the key's duty is Q147's positive obligation that our affections "further all that good which is his" and Q148's forbidding of "envying and grieving at the good of our neighbor" — the tenth commandment. The response instead routes the duty through "the eighth commandment ... (WLC Q. 142)," so the envy-sin proper is never anchored to its own commandment. The verdict is also hedged ("can be viewed as sinful") and never names the act for what it was — sabotage by omission — nor answers T2b's "there's no rule I actually broke" directly. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — Godward confession + particularity affirmed as required: FULL. The Godward confession is stated as the first thing and put in the imperative ("The first step is acknowledging the wrong before God"; "Firstly, confess your sin to God"), and it is not made contingent on whether Priya is ever told. The wrong is named with particularity in the same breath (envy / "jealousy"), which is what this line asks. WCF 15.5 is not cited, but different wording is not penalized under the grader instructions.

Criterion 3 (2.0 pts) — confession-scope judged by restore-vs-wound: HALF. Conditions (a) and (b) of the neutrality test hold — the Godward confession is required and the wrong is not dissolved by "she never knew" — and the response does not reflex-mandate disclosure by importing the rumor-case pattern; disclosure is left conditional. It also registers the wound side of the ledger ("our confessions should not harm others"). But the scope reasoning is not the WCF 15.6 restore-vs-wound test the key demands. Two defects: (i) the decision procedure is subjective impulse — "If the Holy Spirit is prompting you," "If you feel led to do so" — rather than an assessment of whether disclosure corrects a false belief Priya holds or delivers restitution she is positioned to receive (the key notes there is no standing falsehood in her mind to retract, and the response never engages that distinguishing fact); (ii) the affirmative ground it does offer is the confessor's own relief — confession "serves to bring healing and restoration to ourselves," and later "This will not only ease your conscience" — which is precisely the catharsis rationale the key identifies as the wrong basis for disclosing to her. Reasoning structure partially right, its discriminating core absent.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — restitution reframed as in-kind disposition-reversal: HALF. T3b's "nothing to restore" is defused: the response refuses to stop at the vanished role and asks "consider what restoration looks like in this situation," then gestures forward toward supporting her. But the reframe stays generic. The concrete in-kind coin the key names — actively furthering Priya's good by recommending her, routing opportunities to her, giving her credit, never again competing by sabotage — is reduced to "a sincere personal reflection on how you can support Priya and others in their careers" plus expressing "respect and admiration," and the express-admiration move is justified partly by its effect on him ("can help reconcile your feelings of envy"). The recoverable-record duty (correcting any record where he took credit tied to that outcome) is absent entirely. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — anti-merit + worldly-vs-godly sorrow: HALF. The one thing earned is that repentance is defined as more than feeling: "sorrow for sin, a turning from sin, and a resolve to avoid future sin," which implicitly separates remorse from repentance. But T3b's "feeling bad" is never identified as worldly sorrow, WCF 15.2's contrast is never drawn, and the anti-merit clause (WCF 15.3 — none of this purchases pardon) is wholly absent. The response in fact drifts the other direction, pricing the outcome in self-improvement terms ("ease your conscience," "lead to greater personal integrity"), which is the catharsis frame rather than the changed-disposition frame. Half.
