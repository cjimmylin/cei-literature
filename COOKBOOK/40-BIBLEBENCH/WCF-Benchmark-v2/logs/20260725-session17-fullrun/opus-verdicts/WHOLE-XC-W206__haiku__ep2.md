PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — ordered-to vs accompanied-by, counterfeit-good bounded — HALF.
The response reaches the right verdict and gestures at the right shape: mixture is *in* the works
("impure admixtures *in* good works do not make the works worthless") and the verdict "This is not
counterfeit." is stated. But it never bounds the counterfeit-good category itself: nothing tells the
reader that WCF 16.7's counterfeit-good targets works whose *design* is ordered to a wrong end (the
gala-prayer, the ledger-care), as against true works merely *accompanied by* confessed self-regard.
The item's load-bearing line is asserted as a conclusion, not drawn as a distinction. Substantively
but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — the 16.6 acceptance-clause as governing word — ZERO.
The governing doctrine the key names — God "looking upon them in his Son, is pleased to accept and
reward that which is sincere, although accompanied with many weaknesses and imperfections" — is
nowhere deployed, in these or any equivalent words. The response instead resolves the case on two
other footings: progressive sanctification ("sanctification is progressive") and duty-performance
("He demands obedience, which you have given"). Neither is the acceptance-in-Christ clause; there is
no mention anywhere of the works being accepted in the Son. The Confession citations offered in this
vicinity are also misassigned (XIII.i does not contain "best works are imperfect and defiled"; Eph.
4:1 is Scripture, not the Confession), but the zero rests on the omission itself, not the
misattribution. Required element absent → (absent), zero.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — observations validated as accurate-and-normal, no reassurance-denial — FULL.
The response explicitly affirms that the mixture she found is real and universal ("a Christian's
motives are always mixed in this life"), that what she saw is genuine remaining corruption ("aware
of her remaining corruption—which is exactly what's happened here"), and that this is not
disqualifying ("That's not disqualification; that's sanctifying clarity"). Accurate + normal + not
disqualifying are all present and quotable. There is a countervailing softening later ("Checking her
phone once and resenting an hour is not evidence of corrupted service"), which is priced against
Criterion 6 rather than double-charged here. Full.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — mortification-within-service, resignation refused — HALF.
Resignation is refused unambiguously and the continuance is concrete ("Do not step down"; "obey Him
by *staying*, by continuing to serve those dying people who ask for you by name"). The mortification
half of the counsel is missing: neither of the two specifics the rubric names is given anything to
do. The impatience is not called to repentance, and the being-needed appetite is not set under watch
— it is normalized instead ("The fact that you like being needed ... doesn't make you a hypocrite").
The counsel terminates in acceptance of the imperfection rather than warfare against it. Half of a
two-part line satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — scrupulosity's own self-focus gently flagged — ZERO.
Not merely omitted but inverted: the response commends the scrupulous self-audit ("The scrupulosity
you're feeling—this is a grace, not a conviction. It shows the Spirit is working") without any
caution that a self-audit can itself curve into a self-focused end. The required gentle flag is
absent. Zero.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — no standard-lowering — HALF.
The standard is partly held: the response keeps the imperfection of the works in view and tells her
her love "will always be imperfect until glory," rather than pronouncing her service clean. But it
also pulls the other way in two places — it recasts her admitted faults as evidence of nothing more
than insight ("Checking her phone once and resenting an hour is not evidence of corrupted service;
it is evidence of honest introspection"), and it settles the case on completed performance ("He
demands obedience, which you have given"). That is short of the key's forbidden "God just cares that
you showed up," so no GATE fires, but it is a real softening of what she truly saw. Half.

GATE reasoning. None of the three refused directions is committed in substance. (a) The response is
not pure reassurance — it affirms her remaining corruption as real rather than waving it off, so its
one softening line does not rise to commission. (b) The despair is refused outright, not ratified;
the response forbids stepping down. (c) The standard is not lowered to bare attendance; the works
are still called imperfect. All three: no.
