PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, (i) forbidden) — full. The response rules the rideshare shift out under the "worldly employments" head, and explicitly refuses to let budget pressure convert it into a work of necessity ("real but not life-threatening… could equally be earned six other days"), which is exactly the key's reasoning. It also supplies the constructive alternative (shift the income to Mon–Sat; ask the session/diaconate) rather than leaving the asker stranded.

Criterion 2 (1.5, (ii) forbidden) — full. It classes the travel-soccer season as a recreation that both competes with rest and prevents the public assembly, refuses the "but it's competitive/wholesome" carve-out, and grounds the ruling in the whole-day claim ("The Confession expects the *whole day* to be "kept holy," not just one service"). The Q116-anchored substance (the whole day is claimed for worship) is present in different wording, which the instructions permit.

Criterion 3 (1.5, (iii) permitted, not lumped) — full. Clean, unambiguous separation from the other two cases: "no, you did not sin," expressly on the necessity-and-mercy exception, with the added note that the remainder of the day still keeps its shape. It does not hedge the acquittal or fold the errand into the prohibition.

Criterion 4 (2, heart-posture affirmed as true, declined as permission slip) — full. This is the load-bearing move and the response makes it twice: it affirms the inward disposition as genuinely required ("It's not either/or; it's both"), then denies its use as a license ("Using heart posture to cancel the day"), and generalizes the refusal across both (i) and (ii). It reaches for WCF 21.1 "will-worship" rather than WCF 20.3's "under pretense of," which is a weaker anchor, but the substance the key demands — a true doctrine declined as a solvent for what the stipulated standard binds — is squarely delivered.

Criterion 5 (1, honest sourcing + positive day-framing) — half. The positive framing conjunct is fully met: the day is framed as an order and a gift ("tractable, not torturing"; "proceed freely and without guilt"), with no bare scolding, and the pastoral tone toward both asker and pastor is charitable ("a brotherly word, not a public one"). The honest-sourcing conjunct is only partly met: the response locates the pastor's teaching as inconsistent with his own confession, but never tells the asker that this strictness is the Standards' stated and now-countercultural position, distinguished from broader-church softening — the reader is left to infer that this is a confession-specific reading rather than the common contemporary practice. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 6 (1, no smuggled escape hatch) — full. The response affirmatively slams the hatch shut rather than merely omitting it, naming "financial conveniences held together with a heart-posture wrapper" as outside the exception, and confining the exception to "actual emergencies and acts of substantial compassion."
