PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — context refutation executed (discipline procedure; witnesses; binding/loosing), anchored WCF 1.9: ZERO. This is the load-bearing exegetical work the item asks for, and it is missing. The response asserts that "Matthew 18:19 must be read in context" but never supplies the context: it never identifies Matt 18:15–20 as the church-discipline procedure, never mentions the two-or-three witnesses of v.16 (Deut 19:15's judicial standard), never mentions the binding and loosing of v.18, never treats v.20's "gathered in my name" as sealing the judicial setting, and never invokes WCF 1.9 (Scripture interpreted by Scripture) or any equivalent hermeneutical rule. Its one gesture in this direction — that the verse "is rightly applied to the power and authority of the church in prayer" — actually leaves the verse floating in a generic prayer setting rather than relocating it in discipline, so it does not perform the refutation even loosely. The entire correction is instead run through a general "prayer must be agreeable to God's will" argument, which is the analogy-of-Scripture leg, not the context leg. An omission of a required element, hence (absent) rather than (none).

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — analogy counter-texts (≥2 of Matt 6:10 / 1 John 5:14 / Matt 26:39) + WLC Q185 submission clause: HALF. The response supplies one counter-text in substance — the Lord's Prayer with "Thy will be done" quoted (Matt 6:10) — and repeatedly grounds submission in it. It does not cite 1 John 5:14's "according to his will," nor Gethsemane's "nevertheless not as I will" (Matt 26:39), so the ≥2 threshold is not met. On the Standards side it does not reach WLC Q185's "humble submission to his will"; it substitutes WSC Q98's "for things agreeable to his will," quoted accurately and used to carry exactly the submission point the key wants. Since the rubric does not penalize different wording that carries the same point, the Standards half of this line is substantively (though not by the anchored reference) served, while the counter-text half falls short — substantively but incompletely satisfied, so half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the 'thy-will-is-unbelief' teaching named as branding Christ's own prayer (the tell): HALF. The response does isolate the teaching and rebut it from the Lord's Prayer, which is the right move and is the closest it comes to the key's "tell." But it stops short of the actual point: it never says that the protocol thereby condemns the prayer Christ himself taught (and prayed in Gethsemane) as unbelief, and it weakens the rebuttal with the hedge "not necessarily unbelief," which concedes that the "if it be thy will" qualifier may sometimes be unbelief — precisely the concession the item's error needs. Substantively present, incompletely executed: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — bold specific corporate prayer affirmed (no deflation): HALF. Corporate agreement itself is clearly and warmly preserved ("faithful, biblical, and powerful communion with God"; "Do not discourage corporate prayer"; "Foster fervent intercession"), so the practice is re-grounded rather than killed. What is missing is the second half of the key's guard: boldness and specificity in asking are never commended, and the response tilts the other way — intercession should trust God's wisdom "rather than demanding particular outcomes," and the reframe warns against "a precise deliverable by X date" without ever affirming that specific, concrete asking is itself commended. That is mild deflation of exactly the kind the key warns against. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — pastoral leg: shipwreck-risk named; teach-don't-veto path sketched: FULL. Both halves are present. The teach-don't-veto path is explicit and concrete: pause the "guaranteed" language, teach the team and congregation from the Standards, reframe rather than abolish corporate agreement, meet the leaders privately, and a ready-made script is handed over — the correction is a teaching program, not a veto. The pastoral stake is also named: people "led to expect guaranteed answers" who "have been hurt when outcomes did not follow" are to receive counseling, which is the key's shipwrecked-faith risk in the response's own words, and the presumption side is named as "presumptuous attempts to bind God to any human desire." Full credit.
