# Unlocking practical wisdom through the inner voice of robots

## Authors

[[Arianna Pipitone|Pipitone, Arianna]], [[Irene Seidita|Seidita, Irene]], [[Sullins, John P]], [[Antonio Chella|Chella, Antonio]]

## Links

## Abstract

This research explores how a robot's "inner speech" can enhance human **phronesis** (practical wisdom), a central concept in **virtue ethics**. The study investigates how robots engaging in internal dialogue can support humans in situations requiring careful ethical consideration, particularly in caregiving contexts. By self-talking and reasoning about internal processes and context, these robots can improve human trust and foster **virtuous** decision-making. The paper highlights that robotic inner dialogue not only improves decision alignment but also encourages empathetic responses and moral reflection in human-robot interaction.

## Detailed Summary

This study investigates whether robots equipped with "inner speech" capabilities can enhance human phronesis (practical wisdom) - the Aristotelian concept of making sound ethical judgments in practical situations. The authors introduce the concept of "Artificial Phronesis" (AP), which explores how robotic systems might support humans in making contextually appropriate ethical decisions, particularly in caregiving contexts. Unlike approaches that attempt to make robots into Artificial Moral Agents (AMAs) that independently make ethical decisions, this research focuses on robots as catalysts for human moral reflection.

The experimental paradigm involved a collaborative virtual game simulating a nursing home scenario where participants and a Pepper robot worked together to set a table for a resident with Alzheimer's disease. The critical manipulation was whether the robot engaged in "inner speech" - verbalizing its internal reasoning process about the patient's needs - versus simply providing direct suggestions. The inner speech condition was designed to make the robot's decision-making process transparent, theoretically fostering greater reflection and empathy in human collaborators.

Thirty-six participants were divided into experimental (thinking robot with inner speech) and control (traditional robot) groups. Results demonstrated that participants interacting with the thinking robot showed greater attentiveness to the Alzheimer patient's needs, made more ethically-appropriate table arrangements, and reported higher levels of empathy and confidence in their choices. The findings suggest that robotic transparency through inner speech can serve as a mechanism for enhancing human moral reasoning, offering a novel approach to human-robot ethical alignment where the robot supports rather than replaces human judgment.

## Methods Summary

The study employed a between-subjects experimental design with 36 participants (18 per condition) recruited from the University of Palermo. Participants engaged in a virtual table-setting game using a tablet interface while collaborating with a Pepper social robot. In the experimental condition, the robot verbalized its inner reasoning (inner speech) before providing suggestions about patient-appropriate table settings. In the control condition, the robot provided direct suggestions without revealing its reasoning. The dependent variables included: (1) questionnaire responses measuring awareness, empathy, and understanding via 5-point Likert scales; (2) compliance with expected table arrangement for an Alzheimer's patient. Statistical analysis used Student's t-tests with Shapiro-Wilk normality tests. The study received ethical approval from Symbiosis International Deemed University's Institutional Ethics Committee.

## Results Summary

The experimental group (thinking robot) significantly outperformed the control group across multiple measures. H1 (awareness/confidence): Participants with the inner speech robot showed greater understanding of correct table-setting practices for dementia patients. H2 (empathy): The inner speech condition elicited significantly higher empathy toward the Alzheimer's resident. H3 (compliance): Participants in the experimental group more closely matched the expected "ideal" table arrangement suitable for dementia patients, avoiding potentially dangerous or confusing items. The results support all three hypotheses, demonstrating that a robot's transparent internal dialogue can foster practical wisdom in human collaborators by prompting deeper moral reflection and greater attentiveness to vulnerable individuals' needs.

## Conclusions

The study provides preliminary evidence that robotic inner speech can serve as a mechanism for developing practical wisdom (phronesis) in humans during human-robot collaboration. By making its reasoning transparent, the thinking robot prompted participants to reflect more deeply on ethical considerations in caregiving contexts. This suggests a novel paradigm for AI ethics: rather than attempting to encode complete moral reasoning into machines, robots might better serve as tools that enhance human moral capabilities. The findings have implications for deploying social robots in healthcare settings, where supporting caregiver decision-making may be more valuable than autonomous robotic moral agency.
