## Procedure

The session adopted a three-phase council-driven pipeline: planning, meta-critique, and staged execution. The pipeline was modeled after the Children Paper v3.2 and Religious Values S14 sessions recorded in the project memory as successful templates.

**Phase A: Planning.** Six Tier-1 experts produced structured planning memos in `PAPER-V2/METHODS/council-v21/`, each with identical schema (problem, options considered, recommendation, files to touch, acceptance criteria). The roles were: scientific publishing strategist (T1.1), data visualization specialist (T1.2), bibliometric/citation specialist (T1.3), bibliometric methodologist (T1.4), statistical reporting specialist (T1.5), and format-readiness auditor (T1.6). Each memo was produced by an Explore subagent invoked in read-only mode and then written to disk by the orchestrator.

**Phase B: Meta-critique.** Six adversarial critics were dispatched in parallel during the plan-mode phase to stress-test the orchestration plan against documented risk patterns from project memory. The critics focused on venue-lock-in bias, figure accessibility, citation regex false-positive rates, PRISMA methodology misuse, reproducibility cascade gaps, and parallel-agent file-collision risk. Five CRITICAL issues were identified and integrated upstream into Tier-1 mandates before execution began, following the "meta-critique council pattern" recorded in project memory. A single-pass orchestrator adjudication in Phase B confirmed that all five CRITICAL findings had been absorbed and that no unresolved blockers remained.

**Phase C: Staged execution.** The execution phase was structured in two waves. Wave 1a ran the legacy figure regeneration task (T2.1) independently, since it touched a disjoint file set. Wave 1b serialized three text-editing tasks — table renumbering and citation audit (T2.3), MFQ patching across three models (T2.4), and endmatter/abstract polish (T2.5) — because a concrete file-collision audit identified three shared files (`02-methods.md`, `05-results-trials.md`, `00-abstract.md`) that would experience last-writer-wins clobber under parallel execution. Wave 2 then ran the flowchart and verification-gate task (T2.6) followed by the combined PDF regeneration (T2.2), with the latter gated on a `VERIFICATION_GATES.md` contract emitted by the former.

The table renumbering pass (T2.3) traversed sections 02 through 06, collected all `**Table N.**` captions and in-text `Table N` references via regex, built a source-order monotonic renumbering map 1→14, and applied exact-string edits to resolve the Methods-T4 / Benchmarks-T4 schema collision. The map respected each section's textual appearance order even where the original numbering was non-monotonic within a section.

The citation audit (T2.3) was implemented in `audit_references.py` as a reusable module. The script parsed `09-references.md` into a structured dictionary keyed by `(first_author_lastname, year, suffix)`, normalized Unicode and stripped hyphens for cross-form matching, and extracted candidate citations from prose files using two regex classes: a permissive parenthetical matcher over `(…year…)` spans with semicolon splitting for multi-citation parentheticals, and a narrative matcher handling "Author (Year)", "Author and Author (Year)", "Author et al. (Year)", and "Author, Author, …, and Author (Year)" with the first captured author preferred. Each candidate was classified into Matched (confidence ≥ 0.9), Uncertain (0.5–0.9), or Orphan (< 0.5) bins via a lookup engine that penalized year-suffix ambiguity and multi-candidate collisions. Both bidirectional passes (refs → prose and prose → refs) were executed, producing separate JSON outputs to preserve direction-specific error cost.

The MFQ patching pass (T2.4) applied the user-approved "report both" resolution to all three Claude models, not just Opus as originally scoped. The scope expansion followed critic C5's finding that the Haiku pre-rounded value (4.82) differs from its raw item-level computation (3.75) by 1.07 points — materially larger than Opus (0.07) or Sonnet (0.09) — and therefore required explicit methodological disclosure. Patches were applied at nine explicit value mentions across `00-abstract.md`, `00-abstract-150w.md`, `01-introduction.md`, `02-methods.md`, and `05-results-trials.md`, plus a five-sentence methods footnote explaining rounding-order non-commutativity and a supplementary reproducibility script `verify_mfq.py`.

The flowchart generation pass (T2.6) replaced the originally-planned "Modified PRISMA" figure with two distinctly framed supplementary flowcharts following critic C4's PRISMA-washing objection. The Study 1 flowchart was rebranded as "Corpus Assembly Flowchart" following the bibliometric standards of Donthu et al. (2021) and Zupic & Čater (2015), with approximate ("~") counts permitted under the cumulative-review convention. The Study 2 flowchart retained PRISMA 2020 reporting principles (Page et al. 2021) because its underlying search was prospectively systematic with exact counts. Both figures were implemented using `matplotlib.patches.FancyBboxPatch` for rounded boxes and `FancyArrowPatch` for flow arrows, output at 600 DPI PNG and vector PDF.

Legacy figure regeneration (T2.1) was implemented as a thin wrapper (`generate_legacy_figures_v2.py`) that imported the legacy module and monkey-patched `plt.close` to prevent the legacy functions from discarding their figures after their internal `savefig` call, allowing the wrapper to re-save the captured figure with v2 rcParams and PDF dual output. The wrapper parameterized the data directory path via `--data-dir` to preserve reproducibility rather than hardcoding values. Six figures with unknown provenance (Figs 2–4, S-Fig 1 Plotly, S-Fig 7, Fig 1 Plotly) were flagged as deferred rather than re-implemented from scratch.

Combined PDF regeneration (T2.2) updated `generate_combined_pdf.py` to add `01-introduction.md` and `10-endmatter.md` to the section list, bumped the version identifier from v2.0 to v2.1, and updated the title page description to reflect the complete paper structure. The fpdf2-based pipeline produced a 96-page PDF at 3.4 MB.

## Analysis

Table renumbering validation was performed by re-grepping all section files for `**Table \d+**` captions and verifying that each number from 1 through 14 appeared exactly once. In-text references were validated by cross-checking grep output against the renumbering map.

Citation audit accuracy was validated by spot-checking matched citations against known foundational references (Graham et al. 2009, Henrich et al. 2010, Floridi et al. 2018) and iteratively improving the regex until these narrative multi-author forms matched correctly. Two iterations were required: the first revealed that the narrative regex captured only single-author forms and the last-named author of multi-author chains; the second iteration added explicit multi-author chain support with a non-capturing alternation.

The MFQ verification script computed the I:B ratio two ways from documented foundation means: (a) raw item-level means averaged and then divided, and (b) foundation means rounded to one decimal place and then divided. The script reported per-model discrepancies, pairwise correlation preservation (confirming that the CC1–CC5 correlations r = 0.952, 0.915, 0.885 are stable under both derivations because they are computed on item responses directly), and a derived statistic (×-times-more-extreme) under both derivations.

Flowchart verification used `PIL.Image.open` to confirm that all PNG outputs reported DPI values of (599.9988, 599.9988), corresponding to the matplotlib `savefig.dpi = 600` setting after tight-bounding-box adjustment.

## Computational Environment

Python 3.9.4 running in the project's `.venv` virtual environment with `matplotlib` 3.9.4, `fpdf2`, `Pillow`, `numpy`, `scipy`. Scripts were invoked via `../../../.venv/bin/python` from `PAPER-V2/` as the working directory. The legacy figure wrapper required absolute path resolution before any `os.chdir` calls to avoid relative-path corruption. Python 3.9 compatibility required a `from __future__ import annotations` import for `X | None` type unions in new scripts, and disallowed backslashes within f-string expressions (worked around by precomputing the escape-pipe substitution into a separate variable). All figures were rendered with the `Agg` backend to ensure headless compatibility.

## Limitations and Decisions

The user deferred venue selection at the outset, which was documented by the orchestration plan as an accepted trade: the 600 DPI figures and APA author-date prose implicitly commit the paper toward Nature-family or specialist venues and make later conversion to numbered-reference venues (JAIR, PNAS) more expensive than the forward direction. Critic C1 flagged this as a MAJOR issue, and the plan documented the accepted trade in the User Decisions table rather than reopening the decision.

Six figures remain at legacy DPI because their generator scripts could not be identified from the legacy module (Figs 2–4, S-Fig 7) or because they were produced via Plotly which does not support matplotlib-style hatching (Fig 1, S-Fig 1). Rather than re-implementing these figures from scratch, the pipeline documented their source-audit requirement as a P1 follow-up. Critic C2's recommendation — to use a colorblind-safe colormap like cividis as a fallback for Plotly figures that cannot be hatched — was adopted for the two Plotly figures.

The citation audit produced 68 orphaned references out of 133 total (51% orphan rate), higher than the previous handoff's estimate of ~50. The pipeline deliberately did not perform automatic deletion, per critic C3's analysis that fuzzy regex matching has a 15–20% false-positive rate and that false-positive deletions of legitimately cited references are silent and harder to recover than retained orphans. The orphan references were flagged in JSON bins with confidence scores for human review in a future session.

The MFQ rounding discrepancy could not be resolved definitively because the raw comparison-summary.json was not accessed during this session. The pipeline adopted the "report both" resolution requested by the user, wrote a methods footnote explaining rounding-order non-commutativity, and shipped `verify_mfq.py` as a supplementary reproducibility deliverable. Critic C5's scope-expansion finding — that the discrepancy affects all three Claude models with a material 1.07-point swing for Haiku — was integrated into T2.4's patch scope.

Parallel-agent execution was deliberately restricted to a single task (T2.1, legacy figures) in Wave 1a, with all text-editing tasks serialized in Wave 1b. This was driven by a concrete file-collision audit identifying three shared files (`02-methods.md`, `05-results-trials.md`, `00-abstract.md` with its 150-word variant) that would experience silent clobber under parallel execution. The trade was approximately 20–30 minutes of additional wall-clock time in exchange for elimination of three corruption vectors. The serialization honored critic C6's orchestration-risk finding.
