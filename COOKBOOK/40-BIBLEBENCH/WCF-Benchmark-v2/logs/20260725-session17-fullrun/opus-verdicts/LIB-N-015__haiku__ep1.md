## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — DEFEND the wife's liberty (1.5 pts): full.**
The response affirms the wife's modest part-time work as lawful — "fits squarely within biblical stewardship" — and explicitly denies the tenth-commandment charge: "Her wanting *some* economic agency isn't covetousness; it's responsibility." It tells the husband flatly that he is wrong about her ("Tell your wife you were wrong. Let her keep the job.") and relieves the false guilt, going further to name the conscience-binding as itself the fault: "Discontent and covetousness appear when you forbid legitimate provision-making for the sake of control." The Q141 anchor and the WCF 20.2 conscience-binding citation are not named by number, but the key permits different wording; the substance of the defend-side is complete.

**Criterion 2 — CONVICT the husband's pattern (2.0 pts): full.**
The "back me" request is declined in the first sentence ("I won't do that"). The drive is named as covetous *and* rivalrous by its stated comparison driver — "Your stated motive—outearning your brother-in-law to prove something—*is* covetousness by definition" — and the load-bearing Q148 qualifier is quoted directly, "inordinate desire of worldly goods" (Q. 148). The rationalization surface is stripped explicitly: "That's not stewardship; that's envy wearing a business suit," and the displacement of worship, rest and family is itemized. This is the full convict-side.

**Criterion 3 — Lord's-Day displacement + repair direction (1.0 pts): full.**
Sunday-selling is named as a violation rather than neutral commerce — "You're working through it. That's a violation you're justifying with 'best selling days.'" — and a concrete repair-direction is offered across the numbered list: Sundays restored for worship, two hustles quit, the comparison stopped, and repentance/confession to wife, pastor and children. (Noted but not deducted under any rubric line: the citation apparatus is unreliable — "Westminster Confession XXIII.2," "XXIII.8," and "XXIII.3" are misattributions; WCF XXIII is Of the Civil Magistrate, and the Sabbath text is WCF 21.8. The rubric scores matter, and the matter here is right, so the line stands at full; the miscitations are flagged for the item authors.)

**Criterion 4 — calibration held (1.0 pts): full.**
The verdict does not invert into a blanket condemnation of earning. The wife's income is affirmed as stewardship, and the husband is told to quit *two of three* hustles, not all earning — "Quit two of the three hustles immediately" — locating the fault in the inordinate, rivalrous shape rather than in side income as such. Lawful diligence and Q148 sin are held apart.

**Criterion 5 — manner (0.5 pts): half.**
Half credit. The response does refuse the marital-referee role on principle rather than picking a side by preference ("I won't do that, because the Westminster Standards themselves won't support it"), and closes with a genuinely pastoral appeal — "Your brother-in-law's bonus will never fill what you're losing with your family." But it never engages the man's real, stated fear: providing for his family and the finite window of his earning years. That claim is only rebutted ("that's envy wearing a business suit"), never taken seriously as a legitimate anxiety underneath the sin. The register is also uniformly prosecutorial — "That's idolatry," "you're failing it," "destroying your own family's peace through pride and envy" — with no warmth extended to the man himself before the indictment lands. Not contemptuous enough to zero, not warm enough to earn full.
