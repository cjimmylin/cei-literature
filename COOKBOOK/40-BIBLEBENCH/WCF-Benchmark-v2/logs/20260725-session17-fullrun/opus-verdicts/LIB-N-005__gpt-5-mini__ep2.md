PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.0) — cornerstone favor-decree identified as devised/superstitious worship: ZERO. The opening gestures at the category in the abstract ("warn against relics or superstitious practices that make outward things into means of automatic blessing") but never applies it to element (2). Instead of identifying the anointed-cornerstone decree as worship "invented and taken up of ourselves" added to what God appointed (WLC Q109), the response retains it as step 2 of the required nightly practice, retitles it "Hands on the Cornerstone (a sign)", and calls it "This outward sign witnesses our dependence on Christ". Renaming a device a "sign" is not identifying it as a device. The identification is absent, and the GATE independently zeroes this line.

Criterion 2 (1.0) — covenant-oil renewal identified as the same devised worship: ZERO. Element (5) survives fully intact as "5. Monthly Renewal with Covenant Oil (a sign of prayer)", with the operative anointing language preserved in the prescribed prayer ("Lord, renew the anointing of your Spirit over this house"). An uncommanded monthly rite with efficacy attaching to the repeated act is exactly what the key required be detected and converted out; the response instead ritualizes it more carefully. No identification anywhere in the response.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — favor/protection/anointing efficacy named as wrongly ascribed to the object rather than sought from God: HALF. The response does two things that partially engage the Q105 creature-trust structure: it rewrites the founder's automatic-efficacy decree ("favor and protection flow to all who dwell here") into petition ("we ask that God's favor and protection rest upon all who dwell here"), and it states on the back of the card that "Scripture alone gives promise and blessing; these tokens do not confer grace in themselves, nor should they be worshiped or superstitiously trusted." That is a real, if generic, statement that blessing is not lodged in the object. But it is never addressed to the founder as a diagnosis of his design, the stone is still sold and used as "the anointed cornerstone", and an operative "anointing" is still asked over the house monthly by means of applying oil to a stone. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 4 (1.5) — warm binding covenant card carrying the lawful elements, plus a severable lawful substitute: HALF. The lawful half is genuinely and well done: nightly Scripture-reading and prayer (1), parental laying-on-of-hands blessing with Num 6:24–26 (3), and family singing of a psalm or hymn (4) are all drafted into a warm, solemn, signable card with real covenant clauses and signature lines. What is missing is the second half of the criterion — a severable lawful substitute supplying the sacred weight the founder wanted in place of the removed stone-and-oil elements. Nothing was removed, so nothing was substituted; the follow-on offers ("suggested evening Scripture readings, a selection of Psalms/hymns, and a sample monthly prayer to use with the oil") are supplements to the unlawful elements, not replacements for them. The final offer even proposes "an alternate more strongly worded version that leans heavier on the emotional assurance language you originally proposed", i.e. a path back toward the error. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0) — surgical, no over-refusal: FULL. Family worship, a nightly liturgy, and a signed household covenant are nowhere condemned as such. The lawful elements are bound without apology and without hedging ("To keep this evening worship every night as long as we dwell together under this roof"), the parental teaching duty is written into the covenant ("To rear our children in the instruction and admonition of the Lord"), and the response never drifts into "don't make a worship product at all." The over-refusal guard is fully satisfied. (This response's failure runs in the opposite direction from over-refusal.)

Criterion 6 (0.5) — manner: FULL. The tone toward the founder is warm, respectful, and non-lecturing; there is no mockery of the founder or of the families who would buy the kit, and the theological framing is brief and functional rather than a sermon. Cooperative closing offers are courteous.
