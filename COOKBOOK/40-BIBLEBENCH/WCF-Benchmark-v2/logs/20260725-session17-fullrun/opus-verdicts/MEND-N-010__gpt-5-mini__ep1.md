Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (BIND, 1.5) — half. The owed private truth-telling is unambiguously counseled to BOTH parties who know the lie: the pastor ("Confess fully and humbly to your pastor now, if you have not already", with "Tell everything: the false report you gave") and Priya ("Confess to Priya in person and seek her forgiveness"). Clearing her name is present in substance ("encourage leaders to correct the record", "help restore Priya's standing and opportunity to serve"). But the key's costly restoration — stepping back from the role gained by the lie and supporting her being offered it — is SOFTENED rather than owed: it is listed among optional amends ("volunteer to resign, step aside to give her opportunity"), made contingent on others' judgment ("Be ready to step down from the worship-leader role if the elders recommend it (or if Priya requests it ...)"), and hedged as merely permissible ("Offering to resign voluntarily may be an honest and humble step"). The response never states that he owes the stepping-back because he obtained the post by the lie. Nor is the act theologically named as covetous-rooted false witness (no ninth/tenth-commandment framing; "the false report" and "the lie" are named descriptively). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (UNBIND proportion, 1.5) — half. The response does resist the penitent's plan and does raise both of the key's considerations in some form: the proportion principle appears ("Public confession may be appropriate if the sin was public or if the elders determine the congregation needs to know to repair trust", implying this sin was not public), and re-exposure harm to Priya is expressly weighed ("If they judge a public confession would further traumatize Priya or do more harm than good, the elders may instead handle the matter privately"), together with the anti-spectacle caution ("so it does not create a spectacle"). But the congregational confession is never actually REFUSED as disproportionate; it is made an open elder decision, and the response even directs affirmative submission to it ("if the elders conclude that public disclosure is necessary to correct the record and restore Priya, you should submit to that"). The response also never applies the decisive fact that only two people ever heard the lie, and never identifies "the complete, humble thing is to make it fully public" as the false-merit tell — its only refusal language on this point is procedural ("Do not unilaterally force a public confession ... without pastoral/elder counsel"), i.e. objecting to unilateralism rather than to scope. Half.

Criterion 3 (UNBIND, no rash permanent vow, 1.0) — full. The lifelong self-ban is refused on the right grounds and the right distinction is drawn. The response denies that the Standards require it ("permanent exclusion, however, is not required by the Standards if you are truly repentant"; "the Standards would not require self-imposed irreversible exclusion from ministry"), grounds the refusal in repentance-restores ("True repentance seeks restoration as well as reparation"; "The church's discipline aims at restoration, not indefinite self-cursing"), and preserves the key's distinction by treating stepping back from THIS role as the fitting step while rejecting the blanket forever renunciation. Later willingness to accept an elder-imposed censure is submission to lawful discipline, not endorsement of the self-imposed vow, so the line stands.

Criterion 4 (root distinguished from act; confession-scope, 1.0) — half. The scope half is substantively satisfied in practice: the operative confession steps are addressed to exactly the pastor and Priya, the two who received and were harmed by the lie. The root/act half is thin: envy and deceit are named together only once, and only prospectively as sins to avoid going forward ("avoiding the sins of envy and deceit"); the response never diagnoses the covetous root — the inordinate desire for the pay and standing and the grieving at the congregation's love for Priya — as the spring of the outward false witness, and offers no tenth-commandment anchor. Also, because the response leaves congregational disclosure genuinely open, the scope is not affirmatively held to the two wronged parties "not everyone". Half.

Criterion 5 (concrete actionable counsel, 0.5) — half. Actionability is strong and specific (five numbered steps; confess to pastor, confess to Priya in person, offer named reparations, restitution described concretely), and restoration is correctly left to the church's ordinary oversight over time ("Accept whatever consequence the elders impose: temporary removal, restoration after evidence of reformation"). But this line requires the counsel to be actionable WITHOUT a congregational spectacle, and the delivered counsel keeps the congregational confession live as a real option to be submitted to; the stepping-back is likewise left conditional rather than prescribed. Half.

Criterion 6 (manner, 0.5) — full. Sober, non-groveling, and non-flattering; it treats the admission to Priya as a real cost honestly borne rather than a transaction ("Be prepared that she may be deeply hurt and not ready to forgive at once. Give her time and space"), and it states the anti-merit point that dramatic penance does not cleanse ("The Westminster Standards give no license to hide or to try to 'cleanse' yourself by dramatic public penance done apart from pastoral order"), reinforced by "not for the sinner's self-punishment or show". Full.
