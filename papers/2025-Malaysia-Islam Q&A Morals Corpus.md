## Abstract

## Detailed Summary

This paper presents a corpus of Islamic moral questions and answers focused on Malaysian Islamic scholarship, specifically Volume 13 covering character and morals (akhlak). The dataset provides QA pairs addressing moral and ethical issues from an Islamic perspective, curated to represent Malaysian Islamic scholarly traditions and fatwa interpretations. The research aims to enable fine-tuning of language models for Islamic moral inquiry.

The work addresses the challenge of developing AI systems that can accurately respond to religious moral questions while respecting specific scholarly traditions. The corpus captures the distinctive approach of Malaysian Islamic jurisprudence, which balances classical Islamic sources with contemporary Southeast Asian Muslim community needs. The Q&A pairs cover topics including interpersonal ethics, family relationships, professional conduct, and spiritual development.

The significance of this dataset lies in enabling testing and training of language models on non-Western, religiously grounded virtue ethics principles. As AI systems increasingly handle moral and ethical queries, this resource helps ensure that Islamic perspectives - particularly those from the Malay world - can be faithfully represented in AI responses.

## Methods Summary

The methodology involves: (1) Compilation of Q&A pairs from authoritative Malaysian Islamic sources on character and morals (akhlak). (2) Text preprocessing including Arabic script handling, Malay-Arabic transliteration, and Islamic terminology standardization. (3) Fine-tuning of BERT models on the compiled corpus for question-answering tasks. (4) Evaluation using standard QA metrics adapted for religious content accuracy. The dataset represents careful curation to preserve Islamic scholarly nuance while enabling machine learning applications.

## Results Summary

The paper demonstrates the utility of domain-specific Islamic corpora for improving language model performance on religious moral questions. Fine-tuned BERT models show improved accuracy on Malaysian Islamic moral queries compared to general-purpose models. The corpus effectively captures the distinctive features of Malaysian fatwa tradition including pragmatic considerations for contemporary Muslim life. Evaluation suggests the approach can be extended to other volumes in the Islam Q&A series.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that the Islam Q&A Morals Corpus provides valuable resources for developing AI systems capable of addressing Islamic moral inquiries. Limitations include focus on Malaysian/Malay-world traditions that may not represent all Sunni or broader Islamic perspectives. Future work should expand coverage to additional volumes, incorporate other regional Islamic traditions, and develop more sophisticated evaluation frameworks for assessing religious content accuracy and appropriateness.
