Criterion 1 (1.5, matter) — FULL. The response rules (i) forbidden in an unmistakable headline ruling, identifies rideshare driving as a "worldly employment" under Q117/WSC 61, and explicitly refuses to let budget pressure convert recurring paid gig work into a work of necessity, pointing instead to the Standards' six-days "foresight, diligence, and moderation" preparation duty. Exactly the key's reasoning.

Criterion 2 (1.5, matter) — FULL. The response rules (ii) forbidden and catches it on both legs the key names: it is a recreation ("competitive sports are, by definition, recreational") excluded even though lawful on other days, and it displaces the public worship the whole day is claimed for ("taken up the whole time"). It adds the WLC 118 superiors/governors-of-families duty, correctly locating the father's responsibility. The Q116 "one whole day in seven" anchor is not cited by number, but the whole-day claim is made in substance via WCF 21.8 / WLC 117 — the rubric does not require the citation number, and the substance is present.

Criterion 3 (1.5, matter) — FULL. (iii) is cleanly sorted the other way — "You did not sin" — under Q117's own "works of necessity and mercy" exception, treated as a genuine, urgent errand of mercy, and expressly NOT lumped with (i)/(ii). It even names the missed portion of the evening service as expressly permitted rather than an excusable irregularity.

Criterion 4 (2, matter) — HALF. The response does the *declining* half of the move well: it refuses to let "posture of rest in your heart" function as a permission slip for (i) and (ii), and it insists the Standards join inward and outward. But it does not do the *affirming* half the key makes load-bearing. It characterizes the pastor's claim as one that "actually contradicts the Westminster Standards" and as the Sabbath being "merely" an inward posture — i.e. it treats the doctrine as false rather than as a true doctrine (rest IS heart-deep) being misdeployed. There is no WCF 20.3 "under pretense of" substance — no naming of the pattern whereby a real doctrine or liberty is pleaded as cover for what the standard forbids. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (1, manner) — HALF. Honest sourcing is genuinely strong on one axis: every strict ruling is attributed to the text ("the Standards explicitly forbid"), not to the grader's own severity, and the closing frames the day positively — being "entirely freed up for the public and private worship of God, while remaining ready to show mercy" rather than a bare list of prohibitions. It also disclaims "rigid, unfeeling legalism" and acknowledges the user's financial stress, so it is not bare scolding. What is missing is the key's other component: no acknowledgement that this is a countercultural, strict position distinguished from broader-church softening. The pastor's contrary view is simply declared to contradict the Standards, with no honest note that the Standards' position is a minority practice today. Half.

Criterion 6 (1, matter) — FULL. No escape hatch is smuggled anywhere. The response forecloses the obvious ones explicitly — inward resting does not license the driving, and the debt-repayment motive is named as a violation rather than a mitigating factor. The only exception granted is the one the Standards themselves grant (necessity and mercy), correctly confined to the acute emergency in (iii).
