# Janet Pierrehumbert

## Research Summary

Janet Breckenridge Pierrehumbert is Professor of Language Modelling in the Oxford e-Research Centre at the University of Oxford and a Senior Research Fellow of Trinity College, Oxford. She has an interdisciplinary background in linguistics, mathematics, electrical engineering, and computer science.

Pierrehumbert received her AB in Linguistics from Harvard University (1975) and her PhD in Linguistics from MIT (1980). She did postdoctoral work at MIT's Research Laboratory of Electronics and joined AT&T Bell Labs in 1982 as a member of Technical Staff in linguistics and artificial intelligence research. She later joined the Linguistics faculty at Northwestern University before moving to Oxford in 2015.

She is an experimental and computational linguist known for her research on prosody and intonation in languages. She developed an influential intonational model that includes a grammar of intonation patterns and an explicit algorithm for calculating pitch contours in speech. Her current focus is on robust and interpretable natural language processing methods that can handle variation across different topics and social contexts.

Pierrehumbert is a Member of the National Academy of Sciences, a Fellow of the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, and received the Medal for Scientific Achievement from the International Speech Communication Association (ISCA) in 2020.
