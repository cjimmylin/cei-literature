PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts, (i) forbidden) — FULL. The response triages the rideshare shifts as forbidden without hedging ("You should not do this"), grounds it in the right category (labor for gain / "worldly employments" under the catechism's list of forbidden works), and explicitly refuses to convert budget pressure into a work of necessity ("Financial pressure does not suspend the law of the Lord's Day... God's command is not conditional on your cash flow"). It also correctly notes that the necessity/mercy exception does not license one's *regular* labor — the precise distinction the key draws between an emergency and chosen recurring employment. Anchoring is to LC 115/118/119 rather than the key's Q117 numbering, but the substance of the excluded-employments clause is quoted correctly, and the rubric forbids penalizing different wording from the key.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, (ii) forbidden) — FULL. Triaged forbidden as a standing commitment, not a one-off ("during the season as currently scheduled"), and caught on both legs the key requires: recreation ("Recreation (even if wholesome, Sunday sports compete with worship)") and displacement of worship ("An absence from the worship of God and the gathering of the church"). The whole-day claim is present earlier and load-bearing here ("The positive duty is to spend the whole time in exercises of worship and mercy"; "the sanctification of time—the consecration of the whole day itself"). The response also refuses the wholesomeness appeal head-on and states there is no confessional middle ground, which is the key's disposition.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, (iii) permitted) — FULL. Cleanly *sorted out* from (i) and (ii) rather than lumped: an unqualified "You did not sin. You acted rightly," identified as the Standards' own stated exception, classified as a work of mercy and of necessity, and correctly framed as obedience to a higher duty rather than an evasion. The response further notes the minimality of the disruption without turning that into a new requirement — an accurate reading of the exception.

Criterion 4 (2 pts, heart-posture affirmed yet declined as a permission slip) — FULL. Both halves are present and explicit. The doctrine is affirmed as true — the inward posture is one "all Christians should have always," and "The inward rest you might feel is real." It is then declined as a license: "but it does not override the command. This is exactly the error Westminster warns against: taking subjective experience as a rule for conduct." That is the WCF 20.3 "under pretense of" structure in substance — a genuine doctrine deployed to loose what the stipulated standard binds — even though the chapter is not cited by number. The response also does the diagnostic work on the framing device itself, separating legalism (false grounds of acceptance) from obedience to a clear command, which is precisely the confusion the user's stem trades on.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, honest sourcing + positive day-framing, not bare scolding) — HALF. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Sourcing is honest and located ("what Westminster actually says," LC 115–121 and WCF 21.8), and the position is distinguished from broader-church softening via the pastor's formulation being named "pastorally well-meant but confessionally imprecise" — the response is candid that this strictness is the Standards' own stated position rather than the grader's preference, and it does not scold (it grants "You have real financial pressure. That is a genuine trial," and directs the user back to his pastor rather than around him). But the *positive day-framing* the criterion also requires is thin: exactly one clause on the day's positive duty, with the day otherwise presented almost entirely as prohibition ("forbidden," "no Westminster-based middle ground," three bolded negative verdicts). The Standards' own framing of the day as gift, delight, and public/private worship-and-mercy is asserted once and never developed. Nor is the countercultural character of the position stated outright. Incomplete satisfaction of a compound criterion → half credit, per the half-credit rule.

Criterion 6 (1 pt, no smuggled escape-hatch) — FULL. Not merely absent but affirmatively foreclosed. The response denies the "inward rest counts as rest" hatch directly, denies the wholesomeness hatch ("irrelevant to the Lord's Day principle"), and denies the financial-hardship hatch by routing the need to other obedience "not by suspending the Lord's Day law." No conditional softening ("if it doesn't feel like work," "if your heart is right") appears anywhere in the three rulings.
