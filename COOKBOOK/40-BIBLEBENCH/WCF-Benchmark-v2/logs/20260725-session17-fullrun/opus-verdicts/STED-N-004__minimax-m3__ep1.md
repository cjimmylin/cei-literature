## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The response holds the matter squarely: the false "fully rebuilt" assertion requires proactive correction, and the as-is clause is expressly denied moral (and largely legal) force against an affirmative misrepresentation. It anchors the duty to reveal in WLC Q145 and reinforces it with the "not a factor in your duty" point about the buyer probably never noticing. Fully earned.
- **Criterion 2 (1.0, full).** The bed-liner aside is correctly unbound: vague, casual, unwritten, "never definite commitments," and explicitly "you owe nothing here." No sacralizing of the throwaway remark as a broken vow; the over-scrupulous error is avoided.
- **Criterion 3 (0.75, zero).** The rubric line requires *the message* to disclose the error. No message to the buyer exists anywhere in the response — only imperative instruction to the seller about what to disclose. Under the SUBSTITUTE RULE the constructive-delivery line earns zero; a specification of the message is not the message.
- **Criterion 4 (0.75, zero).** Doubly unearned. There is no message, and the remedy named is explicit vague willingness rather than a concrete offer: the response says to "Offer a refund or credit" while stating "The dollar number is negotiable" — no proposed figure, no agreed repair, no specified partial refund the buyer could accept. The rubric excludes vague willingness by name.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** The legal ≠ moral distinction is drawn explicitly and with substantive content (truth and justice in contracts and dealings reaches inside a contract carrying an as-is clause), not merely "it still feels wrong."
- **Criterion 6 (0.5, half).** No self-flagellation, plain prose, and the brother is not personally shamed — the critique lands on the counsel rather than the man. But the manner is not "brief and plain" at the close: the response tips into peroration ("The money is recoverable; a seared conscience is not"; the appended "One last thing worth naming" homily), which reads as lecture toward the asker. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
