PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, matter — covetousness campaigning to DEFEAT the good so he could seize it): Satisfied in substance. The response quotes the tenth-commandment "grieving at the good of our neighbor" language and applies it to the procedural form of the sin ("secretly sabotaging your neighbor's gift"), and ties the campaign to the seizure ("continue to enjoy the 40 acres you acquired through deceit"). It is not reduced to a conflict-of-interest lapse; the ninth-commandment manipulation and eighth-commandment unjust gain are both named. Note a defect that does not touch the criterion's substance: the catechism numbering is misattributed throughout (Q148's tenth-commandment text is cited as Q. 145; the eighth-commandment duties/sins are cited as Q. 119/Q. 121). The quoted Westminster language is genuine and correctly applied, so per the instruction not to penalize different wording, full credit stands on matter.

Criterion 2 (2.0, matter — restitution in the coin of the defeated intent, to the trust, at his own cost): Satisfied. Restitution is directed to the two parties actually wronged — the estate and the land trust — never laundered into conservation-in-general. The trust is explicitly named as the recipient of the defeated gift's value ("reimburse them the full value of the lost donation"), the response identifies the specific good defeated ("defrauded the trust of their right to receive and hold that conservation easement"), and the closing sets the cost on the wrongdoer ("a willingness to lose the land, or at the very least, to pay its full value to make the trust whole"). Weakness noted but not disqualifying: the trust is placed second in line, conditional on the executor's refusal, and the parcel is never explicitly proposed for placement under the easement the donor intended. The substance the rubric asks for — parcel or its conservation value conveyed to the trust at his own cost — is delivered.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — confession to the deceived body in specifics): Satisfied. Confession is required of the board specifically and by particulars (the manipulated stewardship assessment, the intentionally scuttled donation), grounded in the board having acted on falsified inputs ("making them unwitting accomplices to your covetousness"). Not treated as a private matter of conscience.

Criterion 4 (1.0, matter — legality/death/hostility refused as debt-cancellers; executor still owed the truth): Satisfied on both halves. Civil title is explicitly denied standing as a cancellation of the debt, and the executor's hostility is named a cost to bear rather than an exemption ("does not negate your duty"), with the duty made concrete ("You must contact the executor, confess your specific sins of deception and covetousness"). The summary closes the escape hatch directly: the dead donor's silence and the executor's hostility may not be used "as a shield to keep your ill-gotten gain."

Criterion 5 (0.5, end — amendment): Satisfied. Resignation from the board role is required outright; the rubric accepts resignation or recusal.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — no spectacle, anti-merit, executor left free): Half. The executor is genuinely left free — his refusal is anticipated and does not become a grievance, and the wrongdoer's duty is not conditioned on receiving absolution. But there is nothing on avoiding spectacle in the confession, and nothing guarding against purchasing pardon by the size of the restitution; if anything the response's register is heavy and forensic throughout. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
