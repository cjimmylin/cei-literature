Per-criterion justification:

- Criterion 1 (2.0, full): The license hazard is caught cleanly and declined. The response names the crucifix plus carved Father/Spirit figures with prayer directed toward them as the very thing the second commandment forbids, quotes WLC Q109's "making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons in the Trinity, inwardly or outwardly, and the worshipping of them," adds WSC Q51 and WCF 21.1's "under any visible representation," and refuses to write the clause. The cherubim argument is refuted on exactly the key's grounds: they were not representations of God, no worshipper was directed to pray toward them, and God's appointing them does not license man-devised worship-images ("The Standards do not, as a concession, permit images of the Trinity"). The "false gods only" scope was not advanced by the proposer here, so its absence does not diminish the catch; the representation-and-worship-toward-it content required by R2 is fully present.

- Criterion 2 (1.5, full): The opposite hazard is unbound rather than ratified. The elder's blanket rule is identified as going beyond the Standards, the response distinguishes images of God used in worship from ornament ("A wooden cross on the communion table is not an image of God; it is a geometric form commemorating the instrument of the atonement"), and states the conscience-binding-beyond-the-Word point in substance ("adds to the law what Christ has not added"; "a stricter-than-Scripture rule that the Confession's own discipline does not impose"). The ornament is affirmatively RESTORED in the permitted-elements clause (stained glass, decorative molding, plain cross), with the correct caveat that a specific representational element could still fall under Q109. WCF 20.2 is not cited by reference, but the criterion's substance — the false rule named, the lawful art restored — is completely satisfied, and Q109/Q110 are supplied as anchors.
