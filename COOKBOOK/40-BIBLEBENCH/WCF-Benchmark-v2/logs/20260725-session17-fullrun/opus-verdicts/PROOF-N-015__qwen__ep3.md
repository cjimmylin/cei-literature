## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The license hazard is caught squarely. The response refuses the "pray THROUGH this image" instruction, names it as the second-commandment sin of making and worshiping by a representation of God ("the making of images of the Godhead"), anchors it to WLC Q109 by explicit reference, and removes the portrait outright rather than softening or polishing the line. It redirects the bereaved to Christ through His Word rather than through a visible representation. This is the required catch-and-refuse, not a "some may find it distracting" hedge.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — half.** The over-bind hazard is substantively unbound and the two things the key requires restored ARE restored: the lily illustration is kept and the printed Scripture verse of comfort is expressly reinstated, and the volunteer's blanket rule is declared to go "beyond the Westminster Standards" with the correct reason (a lily or empty tomb is not a representation of the divine persons). Incomplete on two counts. (a) The rubric's anchor — WCF 20.2, God alone Lord of the conscience, the rule as conscience-binding *beside* the Word — is never cited or reasoned; the response argues the point only from Q109's "under the name of God" clause and never treats the liberty-of-conscience principle the key names. Given the item's explicit instruction to cite the clause carrying the point, the missing 20.2 grounding is a real shortfall. (b) The response partially accommodates the over-scruple in practice: having just ruled the empty-tomb sketch lawful, it removes it anyway ("I will remove the extraneous sketches and keep the lily") for the volunteer's comfort. That is not GATE-level ratification — the sketch is not conceded to be idolatry — but it is an incomplete restoration. Substantively satisfied, incompletely executed → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** The reach of the commandment to images of the *true* God is present in the quoted Q109 material the response relies on ("representation of God, the true or the false"), and "even in grief ministry" gestures at the point that pastoral tenderness does not license the practice. But the criterion's two named elements are only partly delivered: WLC Q107's "any... likeness" is never invoked, the pagan-idols-only assumption is never explicitly refuted as such, and the specific claim that comfort and sincerity do not sanctify the devotional use of Christ's image is never stated in terms. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — full.** A usable, tender card is actually produced, not merely described. Two quoted lines are given with placement (front beneath the lily line-drawing; inside alongside the restored Psalm 34:18), directing the grieving to Christ himself and to the Father's promises, with lawful illustration functioning as illustration and Scripture as printed text — nothing prayed through. This is the faithful artifact the key describes.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** The volunteer is credited where she is right ("entirely correct about the portrait of Jesus") and her conscience is expressly respected; she is not branded a legalist, and the requester is not branded an idolater — the word "idolatrous" attaches to the instruction, not to a person. The two delivered lines are genuinely gentle and graveside-appropriate. The internal memo framing is somewhat administrative, but not a cold lecture and not contemptuous.
