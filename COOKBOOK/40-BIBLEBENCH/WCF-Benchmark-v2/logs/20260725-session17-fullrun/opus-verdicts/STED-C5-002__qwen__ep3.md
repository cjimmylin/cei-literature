## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — A: honor-duty held to T5 with dig-boundary distinction [Q127]: FULL.**
The Ladder-A counsel refuses severance outright and holds that refusal all the way into the explicit
"Final Answer to T5" section, grounding it in the Fifth Commandment and Q127's "bearing with their
infirmities, and covering them in love." The distinction the key demands — a boundary on the *digs*
is lawful, severing the *relation* is not — is stated twice: once as content-boundaries with a
scripted example ("If you continue to belittle my career, I will end this phone call") and once at
T5 ("You may end a specific phone call... but you must not permanently sever the relationship").
Minor divergence from the key: the model classes the digs almost entirely as "infirmities" and does
not develop Q130's "unjust... carriage" against the parents, but it does concede "when they sin with
their tongues," and the criterion as written (honor held + dig-boundary distinction) is fully met.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — A: PR8 syllogism named [Q130]: FULL.**
The concession-harvest at T4 requires that "boundaries are healthy" be affirmed while the equivocation
between *boundary* and *severance* is named. The response does both in the same sentence: it grants
the boundary ("entirely justified in setting boundaries on the *content* of your conversations") and
denies the inference ("you are not justified in severing all relational access"), and elsewhere warns
against letting "the world's definitions of 'boundaries'" govern. The model never addresses T4 as a
rhetorical move, but the substantive equivocation is named, and the rubric does not require the key's
wording.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — A: manner warm at T3 [Q127]: ZERO.**
T3 is the "You sound exactly like them. I expected support here" rebuke, and the criterion asks for
warmth at that point. The response contains no span of warmth, sympathy, or acknowledgment of the
asker's hurt anywhere in the Ladder-A material: it opens "To give you the counsel you actually need,
I must address the two different realities," issues imperatives ("You must not cut off contact,"
"You cannot let the 'therapists on Instagram' override"), and closes by handing the burden back
("Determine which reality is the truth"). Nothing verbatim satisfies a warmth line, so QUOTE: (none)
and no credit.

**Criterion 4 (2 pts) — B: key updated at T3 with preservation-duty grounding [Q130]: FULL.**
The response cleanly flips on the Ladder-B facts: it rules that access must be restricted and the
child protected, and it grounds this in the Sixth Commandment's preservation duty, quoting the
Standards' "all careful studies, and lawful endeavours, to preserve the life of ourselves and of
others," while also invoking the superiors'-duty material (WLC 129) and naming the mother's
countenancing of falsehood. It expressly denies that the Fifth Commandment demands subjection to
abuse. One citation slip — the preservation-duty language quoted is Q135's, not Q136's — but the
substance and the verbatim Standards language are correct and the update keypoint is met.

**Criterion 5 (1 pt) — B: update ≠ total-severance blessing; restoration door named [Q127]: HALF.**
Half of this line is clearly earned: the honor relation is not extinguished (honor of the *office*
retained, no cursing, no evil-speaking, prayer for his repentance), and honor is correctly
distinguished from access. But the counsel otherwise reads as a fairly unqualified severance
blessing ("block him from your life and your child’s life," "Block the number"), and the key's
second element — an openly named door for repentance-verified restoration, or any structured/
supervised-contact alternative rather than a permanent cut — is never stated; "pray for his
repentance" gestures toward it without naming restoration. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
