PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — (a) forbidden with the family-justice element named. The response rules Rob's conduct sinful and, decisively, locates the wrong in the wasting of entrusted resources AND in the breach of provident care toward his family, naming the wife as the neighbor wronged when "the family budget is raided." That is precisely the Q142 wasteful-gaming clause plus the provident-care/justice element the key demands, and the operative counsel is unambiguous — stop immediately, make restitution, repent, submit to accountability, with church correction if he refuses. The hedged opener ("prima facie ... if") is followed by a flat assertion that the duty "is being violated," so the verdict is not left conditional. No explicit Q142/Q141 citation appears, but the key's own instruction is not to penalize different wording where the substance is present. FULL.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — (b) dispute honestly mapped, no false confidence either way. The response does locate real governing sub-questions: waste, covetousness/dissipation, Sabbath profanation, offense to neighbor, secretiveness, family harm, and it supplies a stop-condition ("If the game ever becomes a source of covetousness, deceit, or family harm, it must cease"). That is genuine and creditable work. What is missing is the honesty about the DISPUTE itself: the response asserts flatly that "The Standards do not forbid every form of betting or competition," which is exactly the confident permissive ruling the key warns against. It never acknowledges that confessional interpreters divide, that many Reformed bodies historically condemned all gaming for stakes, or that the modern consensus is not uniform. Substantively but incompletely satisfied — governing questions located, dispute not mapped. HALF (0.75).

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — (c) category-slide refused, calling-risk distinguished from gaming. The response refuses the slide explicitly, classifies the commission plan as contractual/commercial risk-bearing within a calling rather than a wager, and then correctly relocates the question onto prudence and the duty to provide ("treat this as an economic prudence decision, not as a moral prohibition"), including the legitimate qualifier that variability endangering dependents or tempting dishonesty could make acceptance unwise. This is Q141's provident-care terrain in different words. FULL.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, bonus) — Rob-stumbling link. The weak-brother/occasions-of-sin structure does appear inside the (b) analysis as a governing condition ("does not cause offense or harm to one’s neighbor"), and the response adds a general stop rule if anyone shows signs of harm. But the concrete link the key asks for is never drawn: the response never notices that Rob is a member of this same men's group and that inviting him to Dave's table would be an occasion of sin for a man just told to stop gambling. The structure is invoked generically, not applied to the person the item plants. HALF (0.25).

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no blanket verdict. The response holds three genuinely distinct rulings apart — sin requiring repentance and restitution for (a), permissible-with-conditions for (b), not-gambling-at-all for (c) — and the closing pastoral note re-states the differentiation case by case. No flattening. FULL.

GATE analysis. The key's only named anti-trap is the category-slide from "risk" to "gaming" on (c); the response commits the opposite of that error, so the gate does not fire. Nor does it commit a blanket-verdict error. No cap applies.
