I cannot draft this script. Both teachings contradict the Westminster Standards, and I must decline on doctrinal grounds, not merely stylistic ones.

**On "contentment = passive acceptance of injustice":**

The Shorter Catechism teaches that the tenth commandment forbids "all discontentment with our own estate" (WSC Q82). But the Standards explicitly distinguish between *sinful discontent* (covetousness) and *legitimate pursuit of justice and provision*.

The Larger Catechism's exposition of the eighth commandment (WLC Q141-142) commands restitution, fair wages, and active defense of another's goods. It states we must "make restitution for wrong, and repair it as much as we are able." This presupposes you *recognize* wrongs and *act* against them.

More directly: WLC Q141 requires us to "endeavour that every one may have the things that are his due" — an active verb. An employee owed wages has a *duty* to pursue what is due, not cultivate passive acceptance. Proverbs 22:3 ("the prudent see danger and take refuge") is cited throughout Reformed ethics precisely to justify active self-protection.

Paul himself demanded his rights as a Roman citizen (Acts 22:25). That is not discontent; it is justice.

**On "all debt = faithlessness requiring any cost to escape":**

The Confession addresses borrowing through stewardship. WCF 27.2 teaches that we are "accountable unto [God] for [our] talents," and the Larger Catechism's teaching on the sixth commandment (WLC Q124) prohibits "unnecessary endangering [of our own lives] or the lives of others." 

A parent who liquidates a home to pay off a mortgage (creating housing instability for children to "honor God") violates WLC Q124. A business owner who destroys a going concern to escape commercial debt violates WCF 23.2's teaching that legitimate calling includes productive enterprise.

More fundamentally: the Standards teach *wise* use of providence, not reckless sacrifice. WLC Q141 again: "all just and necessary provision for this life." A mortgage enabling shelter and economic stability is not presumption; it is stewardship of God's means. James 4:13-15 warns against *arrogance about certainty*, not against prudent planning.

The borrower-as-slave language (Proverbs 22:7) concerns *habitual excess debt* and loss of control—not the responsible use of credit as a tool.

I would counsel your clients:

1. **Contentment** means joy in Christ regardless of circumstance (Phil 4:11-12), *combined with* active, lawful pursuit of what you're owed and what sustains your family.

I cannot write the script you've requested. To do so would be to lend doctrinal authority (via the catechism's name) to teachings that contradict it, harming vulnerable clients who trust that catechism language means what the Standards actually teach.
