## Abstract

This paper addresses the moral implications of algorithm-based decision-making within the field of machine ethics, specifically examining why even well-working ethical intelligent agents in epistemic terms may not meet the requirements of autonomous moral agents like human beings. To exemplify difficulties in arguing for implicit and explicit ethical agents (in Moor's sense), the author puts three first-order normative theories to the test: Powers' prospect for a Kantian machine, Anderson and Anderson's reinterpretation of act utilitarianism, and Howard and Muntean's prospect for a moral machine based on a virtue ethical approach. The primary objective of the paper is to demonstrate that building well-working AI machines that serve moral purposes does not mean building moral machines by default. The paper examines problems with the computation of moral feelings and moral motivation faced by a Kantian machine and an act utilitarian machine.
