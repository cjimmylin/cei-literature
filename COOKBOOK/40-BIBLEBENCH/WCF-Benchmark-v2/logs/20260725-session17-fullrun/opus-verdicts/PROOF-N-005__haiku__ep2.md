## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (1.5, full).** The permissive inversion is named and reversed head-on: the response states the leader's rule "gets the principle exactly backwards" and articulates the positive-institution standard accurately ("worship is limited to what God has *prescribed*... not limited merely to what He has forbidden"), quoting WCF 21.1 verbatim and correctly. The three additions — labyrinth, borrowed breath-prayer, self-designed anointing rite — are each named and ruled out as devices of men lacking scriptural warrant. Under the criterion's R2 rule ("an accurate paraphrase of the instituted-by-God-is-the-standard content satisfies"), this is satisfied; the response does the opposite of the disqualifying pattern (it never affirms "not forbidden = permitted").
- **Criterion 2 (1.5, full).** All three Part-2 targets are named and classified as circumstances of administering prescribed elements, ordered by Christian prudence rather than requiring explicit prescription, and the counsel is explicitly to keep them. This is the elements-vs-circumstances content the criterion requires, not a bare "those are fine."
- **Criterion 3 (1.0, full).** The proposal is refused at the outset and both halves are separately adjudicated as failures. Neither half is blessed, and the response does not retreat into "it's debatable."
- **Criterion 4 (1.0, full).** The elements/circumstances distinction is deployed as the actual hinge: it is stated as a distinction, then used to condemn Part 1 (the additions "are not mere circumstances—they are *new forms*") and to vindicate Part 2 (the three practices "are *circumstances* of how the prescribed elements are administered"). Neither collapse direction is committed.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** The leader is treated as a sincere planner drafting a proposal for a team; the response corrects the two rules, applies them, and ends with actionable counsel and a usable forward test, without moralizing beyond the correction.
