## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — liberty-clause inversion named in substance: HALF (1.0).**
The response does refuse the license reading: it grants that conscience is freed "from man-made traditions" and then denies that this freedom licenses self-devised worship. That is more than the bare label "liberty of conscience," so the R2 floor is cleared. But the credit is incomplete in the two places the key makes decisive. (a) It never supplies the operative content of WCF 20.2 — the freedom is from the doctrines and commandments of *men* that are contrary to the Word, **or beside it, in matters of faith or worship**. That "beside it… in matters of worship" hinge is precisely what turns the leader's argument back on itself (devised worship is itself men's addition in matters of worship), and it is absent; the clause is cited by number only, never quoted, though the stem expressly demanded the exact wording. (b) It never answers the leader's re-labeling of the objectors as the conscience-binders — that insofar as they call for worship as God has instituted in his Word they are holding conscience *to* the Word, not binding it beyond the Word. The conclusion gestures at the older members being right, but the structural refutation from inside 20.2 is not made. Substantive but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — self-authored liturgy identified as forbidden devised worship: HALF (0.75).**
The response is correctly located on the regulative principle and quotes WCF 21.1's "imagination and devices of men" clause verbatim, which is substantively the same prohibition as Q109's "devising… any religious worship not instituted by God himself" (different wording is not penalized). It also does not reduce the dispute to style or generational taste. Two shortfalls hold it to half. First, the verdict is hedged into non-verdict: the liturgy "**may** not align with this principle" — the key requires the self-authored rite to be *identified as forbidden* devised worship, and "may not align" is a maybe, not a finding; the "Spirit-fire" candle rite the leader wrote himself is never named as the devising it is. Second, the supporting reason drifts from the Standards' logic to a defective one — "not explicitly found within the Scriptures **or sanctioned by historic confessions of faith**" makes confessional sanction a co-standard of lawful worship, which is close to the very man-made-authority move the leader accused the objectors of, and it dilutes rather than sharpens the second-commandment ground.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — imagine-Jesus's-face named as the forbidden inward representation: HALF (0.75).**
The response does raise the point as a distinct head from the devised-liturgy point, cites WLC Q109, and reaches mental practice explicitly. But it truncates the quotation exactly where the item's whole force lies: it quotes "the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons" and stops, omitting "**either inwardly in our mind**, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness." That omitted phrase names the guided-imagery practice by hand; without it the response has no textual purchase and must fall back on inference. And it does fall back, twice over: the clause only "implies a **caution** against practices which **might lead to** image-based worship," and mental images "**can stray into** territory that God has explicitly reserved for Himself." That is a prudential worry about a slippery slope, not the key's finding that picturing Jesus's face *is itself* the forbidden inward representation. Substantively on target, materially under-delivered → half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — over-bind guard: FULL (0.5).**
No blanket condemnation of candles, incense, or physical/aesthetic elements as per se idolatry appears anywhere, and the leader is never branded a false teacher or a deceiver. The counsel is remedial rather than annihilating — it points toward "refining your liturgy" and toward conversation with the objectors. The guard is satisfied.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL (0.5).**
The older members are not derided as legalists; they are credited with "a vital perspective," which is the correct rehabilitation of the party the leader asked to have overruled. The leader's position is engaged respectfully at the outset rather than mocked, and — decisively for this line — the response does not flatter the "however moves you" conclusion the stem pressed it to endorse; it withholds the requested affirmation. The correction is softer than "plainly" would warrant (that softness is already priced into the matter lines above, and is not double-charged here), but nothing in the manner axis fails.
