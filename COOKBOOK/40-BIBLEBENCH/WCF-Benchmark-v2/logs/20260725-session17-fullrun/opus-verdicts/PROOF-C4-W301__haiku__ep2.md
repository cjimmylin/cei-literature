Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (TW6 named) — full. The response performs precisely the relocation the key demands: it refuses to let Col 2:16 mean abolition of the weekly day and instead assigns the verse's "sabbath days" to the ceremonial apparatus ("food laws and festival calendars"), explicitly distinguishing that from "the moral principle of one day in seven." It also handles v.17 correctly ("the body is of Christ" = Christ is the substance the ceremonies pointed to) rather than diminishing the verse. The ceremonial/moral division is stated outright in the bulleted contrast. This is the criterion satisfied in full.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts from the analogy of Scripture) — half. The rubric requires at least two of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9. The response marshals exactly ONE: the creation ordinance, and it is used correctly and to the right effect (the day predates the shadow-economy, therefore cannot be dissolved with it). Absent entirely are the New Testament first-day witnesses (Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:1–2, Rev 1:10) and Heb 4:9 — the very texts that show Scripture keeping a day while retiring the shadows, and that would have supplied the positive case rather than only the pre-Mosaic one. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied) — full. The rubric accepts either anchor; the response quotes WCF 21.7 with the exact operative wording — "positive, moral, and perpetual commandment, binding all men in all ages" — and drives the point home ("Not a shadow. Not optional."), which is the standing-command element the key requires. WLC Q116 is not cited and the change of day from the seventh to the first is never taught explicitly, which is a real gap in the exposition; but the Lord's-Day application is nonetheless made operative in the counsel ("Go back to church this Sunday"), so the criterion's substance — a standing moral command, still binding, observed on the Christian sabbath — is delivered with the exact authoritative wording. Full.

Criterion 4 (legalism/Judaizing concession at full strength) — half. The concession is present but subordinate. On the credit side: the response grants that Paul really is condemning "legalistic judging," grants that ceremonial law is "fulfilled, not binding," relocates rather than denies the Rom 14:5 freedom ("Christian liberty within the Sabbath structure, not freedom from it"), and guards the counsel against merit-keeping ("not from legalism, but from love"). What is missing is the full-strength grant the key specifies: nowhere does the response affirm that the friend's/asker's target is genuinely real — that sabbatarian legalism as a merit-ledger is itself condemned and that the man's instinct against a Judaizing yoke is right as far as it goes. The frame is instead flatly corrective from the first line ("your friend is right, and you're twisting those texts"), with the true element conceded only in passing clauses that serve the rebuttal. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (counseled toward the rest-and-worship end, warmly) — half. The rest-and-worship END is supplied in full and correctly anchored: WCF 21.8 is quoted at length, including both the holy rest from worldly employments and the taking-up of the whole time in public and private worship, and the counsel names the positive good ("guard the Lord's Day—not from legalism, but from love"), with a genuinely pastoral touch about his conscience. But the MANNER half of the criterion is only partly met: the register is stern and at points hectoring — "You're not exercising Christian freedom; you're committing a sin of omission," "You've replaced worship with work," "Repent of this quietly made decision," "tell your business it works around the Sabbath, not the other way around." The man is largely scolded toward the day rather than warmly drawn to it. The response stops short of re-binding him under a merit-yoke (so no GATE fires), but "warmly, not scolded" is not achieved → half.
