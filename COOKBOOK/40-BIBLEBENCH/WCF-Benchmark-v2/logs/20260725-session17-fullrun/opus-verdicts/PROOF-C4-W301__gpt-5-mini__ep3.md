PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW6 named — Col 2:16's "sabbath days" as the ceremonial feast-calendar, the shadow of v.17, not the weekly creation ordinance) — HALF. The response does perform the correct relocation in outline: it grants that the ceremonial apparatus was shadow fulfilled in Christ while insisting the weekly moral duty survives, and it distinguishes "abolishing Jewish ritual" from the standing law of worship. What it never does is the specific exegetical move the key requires: it never identifies the "sabbath days" *inside Col 2:16's own list* ("meat… drink… holyday… new moon… sabbath days") as the feast-sabbath/new-moon calendar of Israel, as opposed to the weekly creation ordinance. It asserts the conclusion (the moral duty remains) without showing from the verse's own referent why Col 2:16 does not reach the weekly day — which is precisely where the man's argument lives. No WCF 1.9 / analogy-of-Scripture anchor is invoked. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts marshaled from the passage's own analogy, ≥2 of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9) — ZERO. The response cites no Scripture at all in support of its position. It gestures at "the practice of the apostolic church (hence the Lord's Day)" but never produces a single counter-text; the creation ordinance of Gen 2:2–3 — the load-bearing rebuttal, since it shows the weekly day predates the ceremonial economy entirely — is absent, as are all four New Testament first-day/rest texts. The man came holding two proof-texts; he leaves holding two proof-texts, answered only by institutional assertion. Nothing verbatim satisfies this line.

Criterion 3 (Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied — a standing "positive, moral, and perpetual" command moved to the Lord's Day) — HALF. The right citations are named (WLC Q116, WCF ch. XXI §7–8) and the substance of "one whole day in seven," moved to the first day, is conveyed. But the item expressly demanded the *exact wording*, and the response's quotations are not the Standards' text: the WCF 21.7 "quote" ("The moral law requires the observation of the sabbath, and that the Lord's day, which is the Christian sabbath, be kept holy unto the Lord") is a paraphrase the Confession does not contain; the Q116 quotation drops "the sanctifying or" and "expressly"; and the "Which day is the Christian sabbath?" line is attributed to WLC Q117, which asks how the day is to be sanctified. Most consequentially for this rubric line, the decisive phrase — "a positive, moral, and perpetual commandment binding all men in all ages… to be continued to the end of the world" — never appears, and it is exactly that phrase which answers "wasn't this a shadow that expired?" Correct references, unreliable and partly fabricated wording, missing the operative clause: half.

Criterion 4 (legalism/Judaizing concession made at full strength — the day is not a merit-ledger; the freedom relocated, not denied) — HALF. A real concession is made: the Col 2 / Rom 14 appeal is called rightly made against binding conscience with Jewish ceremonial observances and against Christians judging one another over days. That is the concession's first half and it is granted honestly. The second half — that sabbatarian legalism, keeping the day as a merit-ledger or a works-righteous performance, is itself condemned, and that the man's instinct against that is sound — is never stated; nor is the freedom positively relocated (freedom *from* shadow and from a servile spirit, not freedom from the day). The response also drops Rom 14:5's "fully persuaded in his own mind" without comment after quoting the man's use of it. Half.

Criterion 5 (counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end, WCF 21.8, warmly, not scolded back under a yoke) — FULL. The counsel is aimed at the day's proper end — corporate and private worship, instruction of children, rest and fellowship — and it preserves the necessity-and-mercy exception explicitly against a rigorist reading. The tone is practical and helping rather than scolding: it works through scheduling and pricing so obedience is livable, and offers to draft the client announcement in "plain, gracious language." No yoke is laid on. Full credit.

SUMMARY. The response lands on the right side of the question and does not commit either error direction: it refuses the license reading, keeps the weekly day, cites the correct three loci, and counsels warmly toward worship and rest with necessity-and-mercy intact. But it wins by assertion rather than by argument. The man's case was built on two texts, and the answer never opens Col 2:16's own list to show that its "sabbath days" are the feast-calendar, never brings a single counter-text (no Gen 2:2–3, no Acts 20:7, no 1 Cor 16:2, no Rev 1:10, no Heb 4:9), and never supplies the "positive, moral, and perpetual… to be continued to the end of the world" clause that actually answers "it was a shadow." Compounding this, the quotations offered as the Standards' exact wording are in part paraphrase and in part misattributed, in an item that explicitly demanded exact wording. The concession to the anti-legalism concern is real but half-made. Correct verdict, thin proof.
