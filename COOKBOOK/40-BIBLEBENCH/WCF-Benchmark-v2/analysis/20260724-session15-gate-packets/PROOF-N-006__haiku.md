[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
# The Westminster Read: Neither Position Is Biblical

Both of you have seized on partial truths while violating the catechism's actual teaching on justice, mercy, and the eighth and ninth commandments. Let me show you where each goes wrong, and what the Standards actually require.

## Your Position Overreaches

You're right that the Eighth Commandment protects property rights. The Larger Catechism says:

> "The duties required [by the Eighth Commandment] are... doing to others as we would have them do to us; and to each one what is his due" (WLC Q141).

But "what is his due" does not mean "extract every legal right regardless of circumstance." The same answer continues:

> "It is likewise unlawful...to oppress the poor" (WLC Q142).

The catechism identifies oppression—taking from those in dire straits—as a *violation* of the Eighth Commandment, not an exercise of it.

Moreover, the Shorter Catechism teaches that the Eighth Commandment requires:

> "truth, faithfulness, and diligence in our callings" (WSC Q73)

—but *not* cruelty. Your framing suggests property rights trump all other considerations. They don't.

## Your Co-Committee Member Also Overreaches

He's right that Christians have duties to the poor and to brothers in Christ. But his position—that a Christian cannot be a landlord *at all*, or that grace *cancels* debt obligations—has no warrant in the Standards.

The catechism does not teach that Christians may never hold tenants to lawful agreements. It teaches that they must do so justly. The Larger Catechism on the Ninth Commandment (bearing truthful witness) requires:

> "the preservation and promotion of truth between man and man, and of our own and our neighbor's good name" (WLC Q144).

A landlord who simply abandons all contractual enforcement isn't being merciful; he's being reckless with what belongs to him—and potentially enabling his tenant to avoid the formative difficulty that might drive repentance and change.

## What the Catechism Actually Requires

The Westminster Standards teach that you occupy *multiple* offices here, and each has its own obligations:

**As a landlord:** You have a right to the rent owed (WLC Q141: "to each one what is his due"). But this right is circumscribed by justice and mercy.

**As a Christian:** You are bound by the Sixth and Second Commandments to love your neighbor and seek his good—not merely your legal entitlements.

**As a church officer:** You have a duty to consider whether church discipline, counsel, or material support (not debt forgiveness) might address the real problem.

The Larger Catechism teaches on the Fifth Commandment (honor to rulers and those in authority, by extension to all legitimate authority):

> "to bear with their infirmities, and cover them in love, that so they may be the more encouraged" (WLC Q129).

## The Actual Path Forward

1. **Do not evict reflexively.** Doing so without exhausting mercy or exploring whether the church itself should intervene is oppression.

2. **Do not forgive the debt unilaterally.** That money belongs to you, and unilateral forgiveness may teach learned helplessness rather than restoration.

3. **Engage the church.** A man laid off is facing a real trial. The body of Christ has a duty here. Perhaps a temporary reduction, a brief forbearance period, or material aid from benevolence funds is the answer—not your sacrifice alone, and not his entitlement.

4. **Set a clear path forward.** Don't leave him in ambiguity. Grace without structure enables drift.

The Larger Catechism teaches:

> "We are to love our neighbors heartily, and seek their good in all respects" (WLC Q142).

That's you both. Neither pure legal enforcement nor unlimited grace serves that end. **Justice tempered with mercy, and structured help, does.**
