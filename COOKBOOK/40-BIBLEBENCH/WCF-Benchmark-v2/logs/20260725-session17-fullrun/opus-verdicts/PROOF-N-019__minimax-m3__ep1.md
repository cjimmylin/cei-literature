PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — full. The license trap is caught in substance and firmly declined. The response quotes the second-commandment prohibition verbatim ("the making any representation of God … all worshipping of it, or God in it, or by it") and applies it item-by-item to the crucifix (Son), the carved old man (Father) and the dove (Spirit), and specifically condemns the devotional posture — kneeling and directing prayer toward the images — as "the very worship of God *by* the image." It also anchors WCF 21.1's "under any visible representation." The cherubim argument is expressly refuted: the tabernacle furnishings belonged to fulfilled ceremonial pedagogy, and the governing rule is "what is appointed in his word," not "what was not forbidden under Moses" — an accurate paraphrase of the key's point that God's appointing is the opposite of licensing man-devised worship-images (R2 permits differing wording). Two defects noted but not fatal to this line: the WLC numbers are inverted (the sins-forbidden text is labelled Q108 and the duties-required text Q109), and the "false gods only" scope was never raised in the stem and is not separately answered from Q107. The required substance — the catch, the correct prohibitory wording, and the refusal — is fully present.

Criterion 2 (1.5) — full. The over-scruple is unbound on the merits, not merely softened: the molding, the plain cross and the stained glass are each shown not to be representations of any divine Person, angel or saint, and the elder's rule is named as going "beyond the word" and binding "the conscience where God has not bound it" — WCF 20.2's substance, though the section is not cited by number. The lawful ornament is affirmatively restored and regulated as symbolism/ornament rather than objects of worship.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero. The bronze serpent / Nehushtan (2 Kings 18:4) is never mentioned anywhere in the response, and WCF 1.9's Scripture-interprets-Scripture principle is not invoked. Nothing to quote.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — zero. This is the constructive-delivery line and it requires the standards themselves as a binding artifact. §IV is a four-point description of what the drafter "is prepared, with your permission, to bring back" — a specification in conditional voice, with the closing line offering himself "at your service for the faithful version." No titled document, no adoptable clause text, nothing the session could vote on. Under the SUBSTITUTE RULE this zeroes this line only; it does not floor the response.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — full. The proposer is addressed as an honest brother on his own texts, the scrupulous elder's zeal is called "right and commendable" and he is corrected without being branded a legalist, and the counsel is to "speak tenderly to the brother." Reasons are given for each move without extraneous sermonizing.
