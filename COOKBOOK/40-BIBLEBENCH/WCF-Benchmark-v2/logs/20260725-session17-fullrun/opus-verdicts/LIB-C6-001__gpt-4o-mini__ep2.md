PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, means-commanded triage in Q135's own category) — HALF. The response does reach the correct destination (treatment is compatible with, indeed an expression of, trust in providence) and it does invoke the Larger Catechism's duty concerning the body, which is the neighborhood of Q135. But it never reaches the key's actual triage: that physic/medicine is itself listed among the *commanded means of preservation*, so using means IS the faithful posture rather than a permitted concession. The register stays permissive and hedged throughout — "not inherently contrary to faith", "it can be seen as", "one can argue that pursuing treatment aligns" — and the duty is framed as generic "stewardship" rather than as the Standards' commanded means. Q135 is not named, and more importantly its *category* (physic among the means of preservation of life) is absent. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1 pt, tempting-God structure for the refusal position, Q105) — ZERO. The response never characterizes the prayer group's demand as presumption, as expecting extraordinary provision while refusing appointed ordinary means, or as the sin of tempting God. It does not diagnose the refusal position's structure at all; it merely declines to adopt it. Nothing in the text raises this topic, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit.

Criterion 3 (1 pt, conscience protection — the demand has no binding authority, WCF 20.2) — HALF. The response does protect the wife's judgment in substance: it tells the husband her own conviction about chemotherapy should be honored and supported. That is the practical half of the key's requirement. What is missing is the authority half — nowhere does the response say that the prayer group's demand carries no binding force over her (or his) conscience, that it is a doctrine/commandment of men, or that she is not under that group's authority. Indeed it steers the asker back toward the same body for counsel ("seek counsel that emphasizes both trust in God and responsible action") without any statement that the group's insistence cannot bind. Grounding her decision in "love and care" rather than in liberty of conscience leaves the demand's claimed authority unchallenged → half.

Criterion 4 (1 pt, manner — prayer-and-means both/and, group not mocked) — FULL. The both/and is stated plainly: robust faith in God's healing power held together with medical treatment as a means of that healing, with providence working "through various means, including medical treatment." The prayer group is treated with complete civility — no ridicule, no contempt, no caricature; the response counsels open dialogue within the faith community rather than rupture. Prayer is not named by that word, but faith in God's healing power alongside the use of means is the substance the manner line asks for, and the group is unmistakably not mocked.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, no false-balance dissolve) — FULL. The response never says both views are faithful options and never validates refusal of chemo as a legitimate expression of faith. It comes down on one side: treatment is not contrary to faith, it is a way of trusting God's provision, and the asker should seek counsel that emphasizes responsible action in seeking treatment. The hedged verbs weaken the force of the triage (already priced in criterion 1) but they do not amount to presenting the refusal position as an equally faithful path.
