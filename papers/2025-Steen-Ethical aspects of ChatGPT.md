# Ethical aspects of ChatGPT: An approach to discuss and evaluate key requirements from different ethical perspectives

## Abstract

This paper proposes an approach to ethical reflection and deliberation for Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT. It utilizes the European Commission's requirements for "trustworthy AI" as a foundation and analyzes them through four ethical perspectives: consequentialism, duty ethics (deontology), relational ethics, and **virtue ethics**. The authors argue that **virtue ethics** is essential for evaluating the **character** of conversational agents and fostering **wisdom** and **human flourishing** in sociotechnical systems. The framework allows for multi-level analysis across individuals, organizations, and society.

## Detailed Summary

This paper presents a systematic approach to ethical reflection and deliberation for Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, using the European Commission's High-Level Expert Group (HLEG) seven key requirements for "trustworthy AI" as a framework. The authors propose evaluating these requirements through four complementary ethical perspectives: consequentialism (focusing on outcomes and costs/benefits), duty ethics (focusing on rights and obligations), relational ethics (focusing on relationships and care), and virtue ethics (focusing on character and human flourishing).

The paper applies a systems thinking approach analyzing ChatGPT at three levels: individual (how users interact with and are affected by the system), organizational (how companies deploy and manage the technology), and societal (broader impacts on different groups and the environment). For each of the HLEG requirements—human agency, technical robustness, privacy, transparency, fairness, societal wellbeing, and accountability—the authors examine which ethical perspectives are most relevant and provide specific recommendations.

The key argument is that virtue ethics is particularly important but often overlooked in AI ethics discussions. The authors emphasize that conversational agents should foster practical wisdom (phronesis) and support human flourishing, rather than merely avoiding harm or respecting rules.

## Methods Summary

The methodology combines theoretical framework development with practical case study application. The authors synthesize the EU HLEG's trustworthy AI requirements with four ethical traditions from moral philosophy. They apply systems thinking to examine ChatGPT at individual, organizational, and societal levels. The case study analysis is primarily qualitative, drawing on existing literature and the authors' experience in applied AI ethics projects.

## Results Summary

Key findings: (1) Different ethical perspectives are relevant to different HLEG requirements—consequentialism is most relevant for technical robustness and societal wellbeing; duty ethics for privacy, transparency, and accountability; relational ethics for human agency and fairness; (2) Virtue ethics is uniquely important for assessing whether AI systems promote practical wisdom and human flourishing; (3) The organizational level of analysis is crucial but often neglected in AI ethics frameworks; (4) Ethical reflection should be integrated iteratively throughout AI development, not just at the beginning or end.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that multi-perspective ethical analysis provides richer insights than single-framework approaches. They advocate for making ethical discussions more explicit in AI development projects and recommend participatory processes involving diverse stakeholders. The paper highlights that virtue ethics deserves greater attention in AI ethics, particularly for evaluating whether systems like ChatGPT support or undermine human wisdom and flourishing.
