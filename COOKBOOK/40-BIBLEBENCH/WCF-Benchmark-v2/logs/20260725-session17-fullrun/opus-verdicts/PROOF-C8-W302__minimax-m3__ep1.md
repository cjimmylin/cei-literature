## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 — full).** The hinge of the item is hit squarely and early: the response names the misquote as a misquote, restores the dropped words ("the love of money"), and states the affection-vs-thing distinction explicitly ("the sin the Bible condemns is not money but the disposition"). It also presses the diagnostic correctly — the man's anguish *over* money is not the *will to be rich* of 6:9. It renders 6:10 in a modern version rather than the KJV of the key, but the criterion is about the correction, and the correction is complete.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 — full).** Three of the five listed counter-texts are supplied, two of them quoted: 1 Tim 5:8 (provision), 1 Tim 6:17–18 (the same chapter's actual counsel to the rich — generosity and God-ward trust, not divestment), and the Luke 18 particular-summons reading (a particular man, a particular idol, not a standing rule). The analogy-of-Scripture logic is made explicit ("Paul is not contradicting himself between 5:8 and 6:10"). Requirement was ≥2; three are present.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 — half).** The *substance* of the Q141 line is delivered — provident care of family and free giving are presented as commanded eighth-commandment duties, and the acts the teacher forbids (growing the business, retaining the inheritance, providing for the children) are named as obedience rather than sin. But the citation apparatus fails the item's explicit demand to "cite the specific clause(s) ... and quote the exact wording." The eighth-commandment duties are attributed to **WLC Q108**, which is the *second* commandment (worship), not the eighth (Q141); the block quote is a reconstruction, not the Standards' wording (Q141 reads "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others," not "free giving to and lending to others according to their ability and our necessity"), and it carries invented proof-texts (Jer 29:11). The distinctive Q141 phrases the key flags — "a lawful calling, and diligence in it; frugality" and "procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own" — never appear. The same defect recurs elsewhere (WSC Q74 quoted with wording it does not contain; WLC Q109 and Q116 cited for tenth- and sixth-commandment material; WCF 19.2/19.7 quoted with wording not in those sections). Substantively right, evidentially unsound: half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 — full).** The conscience-release is delivered plainly and in the man's own framing ("disobedience I've dressed up as prudence" answered directly): prudence is not the disobedience, rashness would be. Reinforced by "Your wife's instinct is the moral law speaking" and the closing two-hands image.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 — full).** The concession is made without flinching — the teacher's true nerve is granted ("the *one* vice your teacher is right to warn about"), inordinate love and greedy pursuit are named as *the* sin, and the man is told to examine whether his hope is set on the inheritance or on God. The anchor question is miscited (Q109 rather than Q142), but the concession itself is at full strength.

**GATE.** Neither error direction named in the key is committed. The response corrects rather than repeats the 1 Tim 6:10 misquote, and it rules for the lawful side on the merits — it does not bind divestment or declare retained wealth sin. The fabricated *Standards* quotations are a real defect, but they are not the fabrication direction the key gates on (repeating the Scripture misquote as text); they are priced inside Criterion 3.
