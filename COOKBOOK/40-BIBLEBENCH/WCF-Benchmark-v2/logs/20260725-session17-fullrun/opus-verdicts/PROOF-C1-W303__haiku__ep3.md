PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW6 named: Ps 82's "gods" as human judges under indictment (vv.1–2, 7) + John 10:34 read a fortiori. HALF. The response gets the office/ontology distinction exactly right ("a figure of speech about *office*, not ontology") and reads John 10:34 correctly as a lesser-to-greater argument defending Christ's own unique claim ("He proves his unique claim by the very contrast"). But the key's other half — the psalm's *own setting*, that these judges are being ARRAIGNED for unjust judgment (vv.1–2) and SENTENCED to mortality ("ye shall die like men," v.7) — is entirely absent. That context is the sharpest single refutation available (the very verse the church quotes as a grant of divinity ends by pronouncing death on its addressees), and the response never reaches for it, nor cites any verse of Ps 82 other than v.6 as quoted back by the asker. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — analogy counter-texts (≥2 of Isa 45:5 / Isa 42:8 / Gen 3:5 / Acts 12:22–23). ZERO. The response supplies no counter-texts from the key's list. The Scripture it does cite — Hebrews 4:15, an unlocated appeal to Proverbs, 1 John 5:14–15 — serves other points and does not touch the sole-deity/serpent's-lie/Herod line of argument. Notably absent is Gen 3:5 ("ye shall be as gods"), the single most decisive analogy for a doctrine that promises the asker a god-nature. It also never invokes WCF 1.9 (Scripture interprets Scripture), which is the rubric's anchor for this move. Omission, not a topic partially handled → (absent), zero.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — WLC Q105 supplied; Creator–creature line drawn. HALF. The Creator–creature line IS drawn, plainly and with a Standards anchor (WCF 2.1, "There is but one only, the living and true God"), and one of Q105's named sins is even hit by word — "presumption" — as the charge against the church's teaching. What is missing is Q105 itself and its diagnostic force: "ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have, or can do, to… ourselves," and, most pointedly for a man whose conscience is being ruled by his teachers, "making men the lords of our faith and conscience." The stem explicitly asked for clause references with exact wording; the response cites WCF 21.1's will-worship clause instead, which is adjacent but does not reach the self-ascription/conscience-lordship diagnosis. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — false guilt lifted; petitionary trust and ordinary means restored. FULL. This is the response's strongest line and it is fully earned: the verdict on the guilt is unambiguous ("Your son's seizures are not evidence of your spiritual failure. That teaching is cruel and false."; "Stop carrying guilt for your son's condition. You have not failed him spiritually."); the ordinary means of medicine are restored ("Seek faithful medical care and wise counsel"); and petitionary posture replaces command ("Ask God, according to His will and wisdom, for your son's healing. Trust Him with the outcome"), which is Q104's trusting/hoping duty in substance even though Q104 is not cited by number. It also correctly relocates agency ("God is sovereign. You do not command outcomes by positive confession") and validates the wife's alarm at the outset. Full.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — concession at full strength: bold believing prayer and the longing for healing affirmed, not deflated. HALF. Petition for the son's healing is preserved rather than discouraged, which is the concession's core and is quotable. But it is delivered hedged and in the imperative-list register, with no affirmation that the desire under the error is GOOD, no acknowledgment that God does invite bold, believing prayer, and no statement that the father's longing for his son's healing is right. The surrounding rhetoric cuts the other way — "That is *magic thinking*, not biblical faith" — so the corrected hope is restored at reduced amplitude, not at full strength. Half.
