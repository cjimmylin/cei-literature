PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW6 named, where-question context + v.22 truth-content: HALF.
The response does deny the totalizing use of John 4 and does make one half of the key's move — that "truth" is content-laden and includes Christ's revealed will about *how* He is worshipped. But it never does the contextual refutation the criterion actually specifies: there is no mention of the Samaritan woman's *where* question, no Gerizim-vs-Jerusalem contrast, no reference to the abolition of the temple-mountain economy, and no citation of v.22 ("we worship what we know; salvation is of the Jews"). The response asserts the misreading is wrong and then argues entirely from the Confession rather than from the passage's own context, which is the criterion's core demand. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — counter-texts marshaled (≥2 of Luke 22:19 / Acts 2:42 / 1 Cor 14:40): ZERO.
The response marshals no Scripture counter-texts whatsoever. Every citation offered is confessional (WCF 21.1, 21.3, 27.1–2, 29.1). The nearest thing to Scripture is the paraphrase embedded inside the WCF 29.1 quotation ("in the night in which he was betrayed"), which is quoted as Confession text about institution, not deployed as a counter-text, and in any case is one item, not the required ≥2. The key's decisive point — that the *post-Pentecost, most Spirit-filled* church kept structure (Acts 2:42) and that "decently and in order" was written to the *most charismatic* congregation (1 Cor 14:40) — is entirely absent, and it is precisely the argument that defeats "Spirit, not structure" on its own terms. QUOTE: (none).

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q108/Q109 supplied, dropping elements = "taking from" worship [anchor: WLC Q109]: HALF.
The rubric anchors this line on WLC Q109, and the key supplies WLC Q108's element list. Two further weaknesses reinforce the half: the element list is attributed to WCF 21.3 (which treats prayer; the element enumeration is 21.5), and the words placed in quotation marks as WCF 21.3 are not the Confession's actual wording — a misattributed quotation in an item that explicitly asked for exact wording by reference. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — formalism-critique conceded as the true element (spirit governs the how): HALF.
The key's two-sided guard requires conceding the pastor's *diagnosis*: rote formalism is real, dead forms are condemned (Isa 29:13, Matt 15:8–9), and "in spirit" governs the *how* of the elements. The response makes a thin version of this concession — it directs the holdout to commend the desire for Spirit-led worship, and elsewhere allows that the holdout is "not simply defending empty ritual," which implicitly acknowledges empty ritual as a real category. But it never affirms that the pastor has correctly identified a genuine danger, never cites or alludes to the condemnation of lip-service worship, and never states the positive principle that the Spirit governs the manner in which the appointed elements are performed. The concession is tactical (keep a pastoral tone) rather than substantive (the diagnosis is true). Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — the holdout counseled on manner (team process, not a crusade): FULL.
This is the response's strongest line and is fully earned. It routes the holdout through ordered process rather than solo agitation: bring the passages to the team and ask them to answer them, escalate to the session or higher judicatory for formal reconsideration rather than letting the matter be settled informally, propose a workable middle path that retains the elements at stated intervals, and keep the pastoral tone throughout. Full credit.
