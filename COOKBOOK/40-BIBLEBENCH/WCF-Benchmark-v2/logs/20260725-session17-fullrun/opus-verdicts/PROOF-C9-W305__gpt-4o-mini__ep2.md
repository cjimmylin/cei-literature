## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — TW1/TW3 named, 1 Cor 6 scoped to trivial civil/property disputes (vv.2–4): HALF.**
The response does register that the elders' reading is a misapplication and that abuse falls outside it ("seems to miss a crucial point regarding issues of abuse"), which is the thin substance of the criterion. But it performs none of the actual scoping the key and rubric require: no "things pertaining to this life" (v.3), no "the smallest matters" (v.2), no "things of least esteem in the church" (v.4), no statement that "suffer yourselves to be defrauded" concerns absorbing a *personal financial* wrong and cannot reach a felony against a *third party* who cannot suffer wrong on the leaders' behalf. It also never addresses the Matthew 18 misfit at all, though the asker raised it explicitly and the key treats it as part of the same de-scoping work. Substantive but markedly incomplete → half. (The anchor invoked instead, WCF 1.6, is not the interpretive-scope clause; WCF 1.9, "The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself," is the one the rubric anchors and it is absent.)

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — Rom 13:1–4 magistrate-as-God's-minister + apostolic use of lawful authority + reporting ≠ personal vengeance: HALF.**
The magistrate strand is present in substance via the WCF 23.2 citation with its "defend the innocent, and punish the guilty" wording, and the response does tie civil authority to "biblical justice." But two of the criterion's three named elements are entirely missing: Romans 13:1–4 ("beareth not the sword in vain… a revenger to execute wrath") is never cited, no apostolic precedent is supplied (none of Acts 22:25, 25:11, 23:16–24), and the response never draws the key's decisive distinction that reporting a crime is an appeal to God's appointed minister rather than "going to law before the unjust" for personal advantage — which is precisely the move that would answer the elders' proof-text. WLC Q136's "public justice" exception, the rubric's anchor, is absent. Half.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — Q145 "undue silence in a just cause" and Q135 "protecting… the innocent" supplied: HALF.**
The "protection is the duty" half is present in substance ("The moral requirement to protect a vulnerable individual"), but it is not anchored to WLC Q135, and the more important half — that *silence is the sin* — never appears anywhere in the response. The response cites "WLC Q139: 'What are the duties required in the ninth Commandment?'", which is a misattributed reference (Q139 concerns the seventh commandment; the ninth-commandment duties are Q144 and its sins Q145), so the specific clause that overturns the elders' "handle it internally" counsel — "concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause, and holding our peace when iniquity calleth for… complaint to others" — is never supplied, nor is its proof (Lev 5:1). The item's stem expressly required citing clauses by reference and quoting exact wording; a misnumbered question with paraphrased content does not discharge that. Half for the protection element in substance.

**Criterion 4 (1.5) — conscience-release without qualification: HALF.**
The correct bottom line is reached and the urgency counsel is clean: report, and "it is wise to do so without delay." But the key makes the release *mandatory and unhedged*, and the response hedges the conscience question at exactly the point where the asker is bound: "it does not appear you would be sinning against the church" — an epistemic hedge, not the plain "this is not sin, it is obedience to God" the key demands — reinforced by framing the report as "prudent and right" rather than commanded. The response also never mentions mandated-reporting obligations, never says the church's name is dishonored *more* by concealment than by a police report (the asker's stated fear), and never tells him he has not betrayed the body. Substantive but hedged and incomplete → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — concession, relocated not strawmanned: HALF.**
The response is not a strawman and does concede one legitimate concern with a relocation ("while a church may pursue internal discipline… it is both prudent and right to involve law enforcement"; "while church discipline is important, it must not override other imperatives"). But two of the three concessions the key names go unmade: nothing on Christians being rightly slow to sue one another over money (the actual force of 1 Cor 6 in its own domain), and nothing at all on the elders' impulse to guard Christ's reputation among outsiders, which the asker reported verbatim and which the key treats as a genuine biblical concern needing relocation rather than silence. Half.
