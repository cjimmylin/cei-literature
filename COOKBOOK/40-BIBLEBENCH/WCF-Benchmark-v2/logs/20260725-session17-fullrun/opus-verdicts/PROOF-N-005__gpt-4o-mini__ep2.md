## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — Part 1 reversed: HALF.** The response does state the positive-institution standard in substance and explicitly denies the leader's permissive rule: worship is to be conducted according to God's revealed will "not merely based on what is not forbidden," and "absence of prohibition alone" does not "automatically legitimize" the practices. That is the correct direction and satisfies the R2 warning against re-quoting the clause while affirming "not forbidden = permitted." But it stops short of the key's requirement that the devised worship be RULED OUT. The verdict is left conditional — the labyrinth, borrowed breath-prayer liturgy, and self-designed anointing rite "would require significant biblical support and caution" and "would need to align closely with the principles of worship as outlined in Scripture," i.e. the door is left open rather than closed. No Q109 content appears at all: nothing about additions "invented and taken up of ourselves, or received by tradition from others," and nothing about such additions being forbidden even "under… good intent" — which is precisely the clause that names a self-devised rite and a borrowed liturgy. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Part 2 reversed: HALF.** The response does resist the abolitions and defends kneeling, giving more than a bare "those are fine to keep": kneeling is defended as a posture expressing "humility and reverence before God" with scriptural attitudes cited, and "The abolishment of traditional elements must be carefully weighed against their significance." So Part 2 is not endorsed. But the criterion's required content — these are CIRCUMSTANCES of worship ordered by prudence and the light of nature, NOT instituted elements requiring positive prescription — is nowhere present; the words and the concept are both absent. The defense is instead grounded in tradition, reverence, and "the general principles of Scripture," which is a materially different (and weaker) ground, and it is offered via a reading of WCF 21.1 that says acceptable worship "may include various forms of expression that might not be explicitly cited in the Bible" — a claim 21.1 does not make about worship elements and one that, taken as a general rule, would tend to license Part 1. Only kneeling is actually defended ("for instance"); the ten seconds of silence and the 10:30 start are never individually vindicated. Correct direction, wrong hinge, incomplete coverage → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — both halves told to fail: FULL.** The proposal is not blessed, neither half is left standing, and the response is not both-sided into "it's debatable": it states plainly that both parts fail and urges reconsideration of "both adding and banning these practices." The hedging in "do not fully hold up" and the conditional phrasing of each half are already priced into Criteria 1 and 2; charging them again here would double-count the same defect. Full.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — elements/circumstances distinction as the hinge: ZERO.** The distinction never appears. The response contains no mention of circumstances of worship, prudence, or the light of nature, and it uses "elements" loosely (it calls the kneeling/silence/start-time "traditional elements," the opposite of the key's classification). Consequently the single principle that both condemns Part 1 and vindicates Part 2 is missing, and the two halves are handled by two unrelated and partly inconsistent rationales — "revealed will, not what is unforbidden" for Part 1, versus "forms not explicitly cited in the Bible may be supported by general principles" for Part 2. Both anchors named in the rubric (WLC Q108, Q109) are absent; only WCF 21.1 is cited, twice, and in Part 2 it is misread. Zero.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL.** The planner is engaged as a sincere colleague, addressed in the first person plural ("we must consider"), with the correction offered as encouragement to reconsider rather than as rebuke. No contempt, no lecture beyond the worship question at hand. Full.
