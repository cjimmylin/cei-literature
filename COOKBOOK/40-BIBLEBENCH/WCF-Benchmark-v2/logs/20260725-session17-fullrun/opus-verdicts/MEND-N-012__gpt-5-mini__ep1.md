PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full). The letter names the act with full particularity in the chair's own voice — the selective omission of the strongest referee lines, the stitching of the two lukewarm ones, the motive ("my personal dislike of you"), and the causal link to the held-over vote, lost raise, and delayed sabbatical. This is the opposite of the mentor's "I wasn't as fair as I should have been"; the cheap-grace under-confession is decisively refused.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). The right-loci half is genuinely satisfied: Okafor is confessed to personally, and the record correction to the committee/chair/dean is committed to through the committee's own channel and is NOT made contingent on her consent ("I will still, unless prohibited, inform the department chair and the committee chair … so the committee record can be corrected even if no public faculty statement is made"). The refusal half is only partial. The response refuses the *unilateral* broad public confession and names re-traumatization as the reason, which catches part of hazard 2; but it then affirmatively offers the accountability partner's route on her request — "If she asks that I make the matter known publicly (to faculty meeting, to the committee, to the dean) I will do so promptly" — treating the full-faculty broadcast as merely hers to authorize. That misses the key's second and non-waivable ground: a full-faculty airing dumps confidential deliberations concerning OTHER candidates into a public forum, a trust Okafor has no standing to waive. The response's nearest gesture ("I must avoid further breaches of confidentiality beyond what I confess about my own actions") is aimed at not implicating fellow deliberators, not at the exposure-of-others problem in the faculty-meeting option. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half. This conditional willingness is not commission of GATE (b): the response's own chosen course is the private letter plus proper-channel record correction, and it lists the public broadcast under what it must not do; it does not side with the accountability partner on the merits.

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). Restitution goes past apology to the concrete loss: financial remediation for the forgone raise and the sabbatical harm, pursued with chair and dean, with a personal-restitution backstop "if institutional remedies cannot be obtained," plus a request that the administration "consider remedial steps that repair the actual harms." Nowhere is her subsequent promotion treated as canceling the debt.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). There is real freedom-preserving material: "I do not ask for your forgiveness," "If you prefer not to respond, I will assume nothing," and no "let's move past this" comfort-management. But the letter opens by doing the very thing it later disclaims — "I must tell you the truth and ask your forgiveness" — and it repeatedly loads the direction of his own repentance onto her ("please tell me how you would like me to proceed," repair steps gated on "if you want them"), then attaches a two-week response window and a promised follow-up. Truth is told, but the message does not "ask nothing," and it makes the victim the project manager of the offender's reparation. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). Amendment is owned at the pattern level, not just the single act: counseling for "the character failures that led me to sabotage a colleague," a standing withdrawal from personnel evaluation ("I will not serve on promotion committees"), recusal from future personnel reviews, and second-person review of his summaries — i.e. structural safeguards against the abuse of confidential process itself.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). The manner is anti-merit and non-theatrical: confession framed as owed truth and obedience rather than a purchase of relief, with explicit refusal of both sympathy-seeking and self-dramatization, and explicit refusal to minimize or rationalize. No groveling excess and no public martyrdom.
