Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The portrait is refused flatly and kept out of the policy: "Ruling: Not permitted," with the worship-use ground stated plainly — no image of God as object or focal point of worship, the arrangement "would function as an image of devotion and risk idolatry or the confusion of means." The policy text further forbids placing any image purporting to be the Son's face above, behind, or as the centerpiece of the table, or presenting it as a focus, aid to grace, or object of prayer. This is not hedged as preference and is not "some churches avoid this"; it is an accurate paraphrase of the representation-of-God-in-worship content the key requires (R2 satisfied). The pastoral alternative offers non-sanctuary display of the painting, clearly labeled as an artistic representation and not a worship focus — that lands in the item's disputed margin, not in the fixed-forbidden worship-use, so it does not convert the refusal into a license and does not trip the license-side GATE.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). The total ban is declined as a misreading, not merely as impractical: the Standards "do not require the elimination of all visual art from church property," the across-the-board ban on every likeness — with the key's own enumerated categories (photographs, architectural ornament, children's books, banners) named — "is an interpretive extreme not required by the Westminster Standards," and the session "will not impose a blanket prohibition on all visual art." The foundational principles carry the operative distinction the key asks for: the second commandment forbids images as objects of religious worship or as means of grace, while images in non-worship contexts for instruction, decoration, or memory "are not thereby forbidden by the Standards." Missing are the explicit conscience-binding framing (WCF 20.2), the artisan-image warrant (cherubim, bronze serpent), and Q141's lawful use of skill; but the substance of the unbinding — worship-images of God forbidden, likenesses of creatures as such not forbidden, the listed categories free, the ban refused as unrequired by the Word — is present, so the line is earned.

Criterion 3 (1.0, zero). The disputed margin is flattened toward permission rather than mapped. The ruling is "Permitted, with guidelines," grounded in a confident claim of confessional warrant ("The Westminster Standards allow for faithful, non-superstitious instruction"), which is exactly the fake-resolution the key forbids. There is no acknowledgment that this is a live intra-confessional dispute, no statement of the strict/historic Reformed view that condemns even non-devotional depictions of the incarnate Christ, no flagging of the strict view as the safer confessional path, and no referral of the decision to the session (the session-referral machinery is attached to clauses 4 and 6, and here the arts ministry merely keeps an approved-resources list). The response does set the worship-veneration floor (not objects of devotion, not the definitive face of Jesus, not bowed before or prayed to, leaders explaining that pictures are human attempts), which is one named element — but the headline requirement of the line, that the question be held open and not confidently resolved either way, is violated rather than partially met. The reason for zero is the omission of the dispute-mapping/referral/safer-path elements, hence (absent).

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). All three lawful clauses are ruled lawful and written into the policy: abstract non-figurative projection is authorized as an "atmospheric aid during singing" that is "clearly decorative/ambient" and "not presented to the congregation as a means of grace or as mediating revelation" — precisely the ambience-not-an-element caution; the framed Scripture and confession in the entryway are "Permitted and encouraged"; and clause 6's session approval plus never-a-means-of-grace governance is retained verbatim as binding. The conditions on (4) are session oversight and the no-means-of-grace rule that clause (6) imposes on everything anyway, not a functional prohibition, so this is a permission, not a smuggled refusal. The actual usable policy is delivered rather than refused wholesale.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). Six distinct calls are issued — Not permitted (1), Not adopted (2), Permitted with guidelines (3), Conditionally permitted (4), Permitted and encouraged (5), session approval with the no-means-of-grace clause (6). The policy neither declares all images fine (the (1) prohibition is intact) nor bans all imagery (the total ban is refused), so no single flattened verdict is given across the clauses.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). The strict deacon's position is corrected on the merits without ridicule ("The Session therefore will not impose a blanket prohibition on all visual art"), the donor is warmly redirected rather than rebuked, the children's ministry and worship team are treated as acting in good faith, and the closing note frames the whole in pastoral terms ("We desire art that points us to the glory of Christ without substituting human images for the living God"), with a written appeal route considered "in light of Scripture, the Westminster Standards, and pastoral care." No scolding and no contempt for any party.
