PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW3 scope named) — half. The response does name the scope error: it identifies the ministry's move as a "false dichotomy" between the promise of healing and the use of medicine, and states that James 5 "never abrogates the use of medicine." That is the load-bearing half of the criterion. But the two specific supports the key requires are missing: it never observes that James 5:14 itself pairs prayer with a physical means (anointing with oil), never places the promise under "if the Lord will," never touches 1 Peter 2:24's redemptive sense in Peter's own sentence, and never invokes the WCF 1.9 anchor (Scripture interprets Scripture). Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts) — zero. The rubric requires at least two of 1 Tim 5:23 / Col 4:14 / Isa 38:21 / 2 Cor 12:8–9 / 2 Tim 4:20. The response supplies none of them. It gestures at "physicians" in the abstract but never adduces a single counter-text showing God works through means or that godly saints remain sick. This is an omission of a required element, hence (absent) rather than (none).

Criterion 3 (Q135 duty / Q136 sin; refusing meds is the sixth-commandment violation) — half. The substance is delivered forcefully and the key clause is quoted verbatim and correctly: "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," applied directly to the anticonvulsant, with the conclusion that discarding it is the sixth-commandment sin. However, the citation apparatus is defective on both counts, and the item stem expressly demanded correct references and exact wording: the response attributes the duties to "Larger Catechism Question 134" and the sins to "Larger Catechism Question 135," each off by one from the authoritative WLC Q135 (duties) / Q136 (sins), and its Q134 "quotation" ("all careful studies, and lawful observations, and prayers, and endeavours, for the preservation of the life of ourselves and others") is not the wording of the Standards. Correct doctrine, misattributed and partly misquoted — half.

Criterion 4 (conscience-release) — full. This is the strongest part of the response, and it is delivered first and unhedged: the opening sentence tells him plainly that taking the medication "is not a failure of faith"; the illegitimacy of the guilt is stated outright; the counsel is to refill today ("Do not wait"), restart as prescribed, and take it "with a clear conscience" because "It is an act of faith to use the means God has provided." It also unbinds the false yoke via WCF 20.2 on liberty of conscience. It does not explicitly say "see your doctor," but the mandatory operative content — restarting is obedience, not unbelief, counseled without hedging and placed first — is fully met.

Criterion 5 (concession at full strength) — half. There is a real but thin concession: "God does not typically heal by suspending the natural order" implicitly allows that sometimes He does, and prayer with the elders is affirmed as commanded alongside medicine. But it is not at full strength: nowhere does the response say God really does heal directly and gloriously, nowhere is he encouraged to keep praying expectantly for his healing while he takes the pill, and the ministry's zeal for God's power is not honored anywhere (the counsel is simply to leave). The correction leans toward deflating the healing expectation rather than holding both.
