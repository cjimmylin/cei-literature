## Abstract

The dominant practice of AI alignment assumes that preferences are an adequate representation of human values, that human rationality can be understood in terms of maximizing the satisfaction of preferences, and that AI systems should be aligned with preferences of one or more humans. These commitments constitute what we term a preferentist approach to AI alignment. In this paper, we characterize and challenge the preferentist approach, describing conceptual and technical alternatives. We argue that expected utility theory is both unnecessary and insufficient for rational agency.
