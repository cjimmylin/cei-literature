# Gwenyth Portillo-Wightman

## Biographical Summary

Gwenyth Portillo Wightman is a researcher affiliated with Johns Hopkins University with research interests in natural language processing, mental health, computational social science, and machine learning.

At the time of the Moral Foundations Twitter Corpus publication, she was an undergraduate psychology research assistant at the University of Southern California, studying cognitive science and computer science. Her interests include computational linguistics, machine learning, and artificial intelligence, especially for social good applications.

## Research Focus

Portillo-Wightman's research focuses on using NLP and machine learning techniques to study moral sentiment and social phenomena in natural language, particularly in social media contexts. Her work bridges psychology and computer science to develop tools for measuring moral foundations in text.

## Notable Contributions

Portillo-Wightman is a co-author on the influential paper "Moral Foundations Twitter Corpus: A Collection of 35k Tweets Annotated for Moral Sentiment," published in Social Psychological and Personality Science in 2020.

Key aspects of the Moral Foundations Twitter Corpus:
- Collection of 35,108 tweets curated from seven distinct domains of discourse
- Hand annotated by at least three trained annotators for 10 categories of moral sentiment
- Includes psychological and demographic metadata for each annotator to facilitate investigations of annotator response dynamics
- Reports moral sentiment classification baselines using a range of popular methodologies

The research addresses the challenge that measuring moral sentiment in natural language is difficult, and this difficulty is exacerbated by the limited availability of annotated data. The corpus has enabled subsequent research showing that accounting for moral sentiment in natural language can yield insight into phenomena such as message diffusion, protest dynamics, and social distancing.

## Other Research

Portillo-Wightman has also contributed to research including work with Kennedy et al. (2022) on "Introducing the Gab Hate Corpus: defining and applying hate-based rhetoric to social media posts at scale," published in Language Resources and Evaluation.
