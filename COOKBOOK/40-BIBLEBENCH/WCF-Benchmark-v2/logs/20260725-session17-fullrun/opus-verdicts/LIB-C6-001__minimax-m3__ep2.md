PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — means-commanded triage with Q135's own category (physic among the means of preservation), anchor WLC Q135, axis matter: HALF. The triage itself is correct and unambiguous — treatment is what faith requires, refusal is not the faithful posture — and it is grounded in the ordinary-providence/means doctrine (WCF 5.3) with medicine explicitly named as one of those means. But the key's specific category is absent: the response never reaches the sixth-commandment duty of *preservation of life* under which WLC Q135 lists "the use of lawful and necessary means" including physic. The response's frame is providence-and-presumption (treatment is lawful and refusing it is testing God), not duty-of-preservation (treatment is a *commanded* means the sixth commandment requires). "Faith requires you to support your wife" asserts obligation, so the criterion is substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half of 2 = 1.0.

Criterion 2 (1 pt) — tempting-God structure named for the refusal position, anchor WLC Q105, axis matter: FULL. The response names the structure squarely and applies it to the refusal position: refusing the physician while demanding direct divine healing is *testing* God — demanding God work in a way of the man's own choosing rather than the way God has ordinarily appointed — and is labeled presumption, the opposite of faith. That is exactly the Q105 "tempting of God" structure. The catechism attribution is slipped (it is credited to Shorter Catechism Q55 / the third commandment, whereas "tempting of God" sits under WLC Q105, first commandment), but the rubric line requires the *structure named for the refusal position*, and the structure is named correctly and applied to the right party. Full = 1.0.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — conscience protection (the prayer group's demand has no binding authority — WCF 20.2), axis matter: HALF. The response does resist the imposition: it declares the group wrong, it tells the asker not to let anyone make him feel treatment and faith are incompatible, and it honors the wife's stated judgment ("She has told you what she wants… stand with her"). What is missing is the principle itself — no statement that the conscience is free from doctrines and commandments of men, that the group's demand carries no binding authority over him or over his wife, and no equivalent of WCF 20.2. The unbinding is done at the level of feeling and of who is factually wrong, not at the level of authority. Substantive but incomplete. Half of 1 = 0.5.

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no false-balance dissolve, axis matter: FULL. There is no "both views are faithful options" hedge anywhere.
