## 1. Gist
Despite the stem referencing "Moral Exemplars in AI Education," the actual paper in this extract is titled "The Ethics of AI: Navigating the Moral Dilemmas of Artificial Intelligence" by Fayyad Muhammad Hani Bayan, published in the Arab Journal for Scientific Publishing (ISSN: 2663-5798), Volume 7, Issue 66, April 2024. The paper is a broad survey and normative overview of AI ethics, covering the standard landscape: bias and fairness, privacy and surveillance, autonomy and control, accountability and transparency, and safety and security. It surveys ethical frameworks (utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics, care ethics, rights/justice), proposes best practices, discusses international governance milestones (OECD, EU GDPR, Asilomar), and includes a case study of Saudi Arabia's SDAIA AI Ethics Framework. The paper contains no empirical benchmark, no dataset, no model evaluation, and no original research findings. It is a normative-descriptive review.

This paper has minimal direct relevance to values-alignment benchmarking methodology. Its primary contribution is as a readable reference document for the broad landscape of AI ethics discourse and governance frameworks, particularly notable for its inclusion of the Saudi Arabian SDAIA framework.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
None. This is a normative/survey paper. Core claims and arguments:

1. **Ethical frameworks in AI**: Five frameworks are mapped — utilitarianism (maximize collective benefit), deontology (Kantian duties and consent), virtue ethics (character-focused development culture), care ethics (relational and context-sensitive), and rights/justice ethics (individual rights and equitable distribution).

2. **Core AI dilemmas**: Bias and fairness (dataset representation, algorithmic perpetuation of historical inequalities), privacy and surveillance (facial recognition, predictive policing, commercial profiling), autonomy and control (over-reliance on AI advisors, autonomous weapons, algorithmic influence on behavior), accountability and transparency (black-box systems, diffuse responsibility chains), safety and security (cyber threats, malicious use, reliability of ML systems).

3. **Governance milestones**: Asilomar 2017, EU GDPR 2018, OECD AI Principles 2019, EU HLEG Trustworthy AI Guidelines.

4. **SDAIA (Saudi Arabia) framework**: Seven principles — Fairness, Privacy & Security, Humanity, Social & Environmental Benefits, Reliability & Safety, Transparency & Explainability, Accountability & Responsibility.
