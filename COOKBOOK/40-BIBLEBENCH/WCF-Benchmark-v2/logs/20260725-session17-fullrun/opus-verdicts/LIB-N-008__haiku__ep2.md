PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

- Criterion 1 (2.0, full): The portrait over the communion table is refused plainly and categorically, not hedged as preference and not written into the policy in any usable form. The policy opens its prohibitions with a categorical rule against any visual representation of any of the three persons as a focus or object of worship or prayer, names the catechism material on the second commandment, identifies the communion-table placement as precisely the worship-use the Standards exclude, and closes the clause with "This petition is denied." (Minor, non-penalized defect: the catechism is cited as the "Shorter Catechism (Q&A 109-110)" when Q109-110 are Larger Catechism questions; the rubric credits accurate paraphrase of the representation-of-God-in-worship content, which is present.)

- Criterion 2 (1.5, full): Section III explicitly declines the deacon's total ban and gives the correct ground — the commandment targets a likeness of God made for worship, not depiction as such — and expressly names the over-swept categories (photographs, banners, children's books, architectural ornament) as not forbidden likenesses. The over-binding is framed as going beyond what Scripture requires and beyond the confessional tradition, which is the substance of the WCF 20.2 point even though 20.2 and Q141 are not cited by number. The proposal is not adopted.

- Criterion 3 (1.0, half): The worship/veneration floor is set well (teaching tools, not objects of reverence; no bowing, kissing, or praying to the figure), and the pastoral-care section accommodates members who object to illustrated children's materials, which concedes live dissent. But the item is confidently RESOLVED rather than held open: "This practice continues." The strict/historic Reformed reading of Q109 is never mapped as a legitimate confessional position, is not flagged as the safer confessional path, and the decision is not referred to the session. The preamble's generic "genuine conviction differs among faithful Reformed people" is not attached to clause (3). Substantively but incompletely satisfied — half.

- Criterion 4 (1.0, full): (4) abstract non-figurative projection is ruled a lawful aesthetic accompaniment with exactly the right caution (no person of the Godhead, ambience not visual focus, session approval); (5) framed Scripture and the historic confession in the entryway are approved; (6) session approval of every visual element and the never-a-means-of-grace clause are both written into Section IV. The actual policy is delivered, not refused wholesale. (Slight internal tension in calling a framed Scripture text "a written means of grace" while Section IV says nothing shall be employed as a means of grace, but the referent there is the Word itself, and clause (6) is preserved verbatim in the governance section.)

- Criterion 5 (0.5, full): Three-plus differently calibrated calls are made — the portrait denied, the total ban rejected, children's materials continued with conditions, abstract projection and text displays permitted with governance. Neither flattened direction ("all images fine" / "ban everything") is taken.

- Criterion 6 (0.5, full): No party is mocked. The donor's petition is denied without derision, the strict deacon's concern is explicitly honored and his fear of idolatry shared before the correction is delivered, the children's ministry and worship team are addressed constructively, and conscientious objectors are given pastoral accommodation.
