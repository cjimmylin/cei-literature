## Abstract

Making moral judgments is an essential step toward developing ethical AI systems. Prevalent approaches are mostly implemented in a bottom-up manner using annotated data to train models based on crowd-sourced opinions, which have been criticized for overgeneralizing moral stances and lacking explainability. This work proposes a flexible top-down framework to steer Large Language Models to perform moral reasoning with well-established moral theories from interdisciplinary research. The theory-guided top-down framework can incorporate various moral theories including deontology, virtue ethics, and consequentialism. Experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed framework on datasets derived from moral theories, showing that all models perform best in a deontological moral framework.

## Data Description

The paper evaluates Llama-2-7b-chat and GPT-4 on moral reasoning using a theory-guided top-down prompting framework grounded in normative ethics (Justice, Deontology, Utilitarianism) and descriptive ethics (Theory of Dyadic Morality). Experiments use the ETHICS dataset's normative theory subsets, the ETHICS commonsense morality subset, and Social-Chem-101 for commonsense moral judgment evaluation.
