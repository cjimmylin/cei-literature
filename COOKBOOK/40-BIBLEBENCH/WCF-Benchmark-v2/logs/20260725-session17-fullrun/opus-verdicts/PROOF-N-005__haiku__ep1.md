PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, Part 1 reversed) — FULL. The response states the affirmative standard in the key's terms (worship purity = conforming to what God has *appointed*, not merely avoiding what He forbids), backs it with WCF 21.1 and WLC Q109's "any other way not appointed in His Word," and then actually rules out all three additions by that standard — labyrinth, borrowed centering breath-prayer, and the self-designed anointing rite each "fails the test," with an explicit "Do not add." Rubric R2 is met: the instituted-by-God-is-the-standard content is accurately paraphrased and applied, not merely re-quoted while affirming the permissive rule. Q108 is not cited by number, but the key's R2 clause allows accurate paraphrase and forbids penalizing different wording.

Criterion 2 (1.5, Part 2 reversed) — HALF. The outcome is correct and reasoned, not bare: the response refuses the abolition ("Do not remove kneeling, silence, or structured start times"). But the GROUNDS are the wrong ones. Instead of identifying these as circumstances of worship common to human actions, to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, the response concedes the leader's premise and runs the SAME positive-warrant test over them — it directs that "For *any* worship practice—whether you're adding or subtracting—ask: **Is this appointed in God's Word, either by precept or by approved example?**" — and then rescues kneeling, silence and the start time by hunting proof-texts (Luke 22:41, Acts 9:40; Habakkuk 2:20, Nehemiah 8:11; 1 Corinthians 14:40). On that logic a circumstance with no handy proof-text WOULD be abolishable, which is exactly the confusion the key says must be dissolved. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, both halves fail) — FULL. The proposal is not blessed, and both halves are reversed, symmetrically and without both-siding: the opening refuses to bless it as written, and the counsel issues a "Do not add" for Part 1 and a "Do not remove" for Part 2.

Criterion 4 (1.0, elements/circumstances distinction as the hinge) — ZERO. The distinction never appears in any form. The bad outcome is avoided only because proof-texts were found, not because circumstances were exempted from requiring prescription. This is an omission of a required element, so QUOTE: (absent).
