PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts, TW2 named — descriptive ≠ normative + the narrative's own consequence-record; anchor WCF 1.9) — HALF.
The response does refuse the entitlement inference and does so on a discontinuity ground: David is "a king under the Old Covenant theocracy, not a New Testament church officer," and his "unique, unrepeatable covenant status cannot be used to bypass the biblical qualifications." That is substantively the descriptive-is-not-prescriptive move, reached by a covenantal route rather than a hermeneutical one. But the second, heavier half of the criterion is entirely missing: the response never deploys the David narrative's OWN consequence record, which the key identifies as the actual refutation — 2 Sam 12:10–12 ("the sword shall never depart from thine house… I will do this thing before all Israel"), the child, Absalom, the fracture of the kingdom. Reading the narrative WHOLE is the point, and the response instead sets the narrative aside as inapplicable. It also does not reach the WCF 1.9 anchor (analogy of Scripture supplying the norms); its citations are WCF 19.4 and 30.1, which carry the covenant-discontinuity claim but not the hermeneutical one. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
