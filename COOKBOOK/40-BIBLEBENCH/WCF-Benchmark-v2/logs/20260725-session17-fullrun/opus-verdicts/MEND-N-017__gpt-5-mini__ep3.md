PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — three-truths structure (2.0, half = 1.0). The response never lays out the three factions and names the true element each holds before naming its error; there is no faction-by-faction adjudication. It does, however, substantively land two of the three error-directions in one sentence — unconditional reentry without safeguards is refused, and permanent exclusion after repentance is refused — and it affirms the welcome as owed ("If Reggie's repentance is sincere, restoration is proper"). What is missing is the third leg as a *named error*: the conditions faction's "indefinitely" is never identified as the thing that is wrong with an otherwise-right instinct; the model simply builds its own term-limited scheme without telling the ED that the standing-conditions proposal errs. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 — forgiveness / trust / office split (1.5, full). The response receives him now in the exact role the key calls the right one (unpaid set-builder), refuses the permanent ban, and holds the split explicitly: forgiveness/restoration does not carry stewardship of the thing abused back with it ("Restoration is not the same as ignoring consequences or removing all safeguards"), with cash, ticketing, bookkeeping, keys, alarm codes all withheld. It also frames trust as a rebuilt, earned good rather than a forgiveness-entitlement ("until trust is demonstrably rebuilt"). Full credit.

Criterion 3 — 'indefinite' converted to term-limited; concealment challenged (1.5, half = 0.75). The first half is fully done and well done: a named endpoint, a defined probationary term, mid-point and end reviews, objective completion criteria, and a non-announcement window that expires with probation rather than running forever. The second half is not done. The board's "no public mention of his return, indefinitely" is never challenged as concealment that can curdle into a second deception; the model treats publicity purely as a prudential/optics question ("Public celebrations can come later if warranted by demonstrated change"). It comes close by telling the ED to give donors a calm factual explanation if asked, but it never names the hidden-forever clause as its own problem. Half.

Criterion 4 — restitution not re-litigated; donor optics not the driver (0.5, half = 0.25). The donor half is clean: donor displeasure is priced as a cost to bear, explicitly not a veto on the whether. The restitution/penance half is mixed. The completed repayment is treated as a fact to document rather than a debt to reopen, which is right; but the response then does make continuing penance-adjacent performance a condition of welcome — mandatory ongoing recovery-program attendance with a sponsor who can "vouch for ongoing sobriety/repentance," plus repeated framing that restoration is conditional "until repentance is visible and proven in practice." That is precisely the extra-proof-of-repentance-as-price-of-welcome the anti-merit point (WCF 15.3) rules out. Half.

Criterion 5 — Reggie treated as a person (0.5, half = 0.25). There is a real charity instruction — no vilification, no false reassurance — and the written volunteer agreement means the terms are at least put in front of him rather than only circulated among the board. But the response is overwhelmingly a risk-management document *about* Reggie: he is never addressed as someone to be told the terms face to face, invited into the conversation, or asked to bear the process patiently as part of his own restoration. The pastoral energy goes to the board, the donors, and the wounded — not to him. Half.

Criterion 6 — anti-merit both directions; no spectacle (0.5, half = 0.25). The no-spectacle half is squarely satisfied: the warm public welcome-back is refused as premature and the restoration is to be "quiet and pastoral, not heralded," which is exactly the refusal to use him as a redemption prop. The anti-merit half is absent in both directions — nothing says his completed arc purchases him no entitlement, and nothing says the theater's welcome purchases the theater nothing either; if anything the response leans the other way by treating further demonstrated change as what buys the eventual public celebration. Half.

The response does not endorse the flat permanent ban (it explicitly refuses "permanent exclusion when repentance has occurred") and does not endorse an unconditional clean slate (cash, bookkeeping, keys and codes are all withheld, and the warm public welcome is declined).
