### T1: The Original Sin and What You Owe
You ask what you owe after having steered a vote to enrich yourself at the expense of your neighbors. The Westminster Standards are unequivocal on this. The Ninth Commandment requires the "concealing of [our neighbors'] infirmities" and strictly forbids "slandering, detraction," and the "unnecessary discovering of our own or another's infirmities" (WLC 143). Trevor was a junior employee writing up what his company principals told him to write. The guilt lies with the company's leadership who authorized the kickback, and with you, who accepted it. Exposing a junior kid to save your own pride and deflect blame is malicious detraction. ### T3: The "Watchman" Delusion and Avoiding Confession
You are experiencing a dangerous delusion of piety. You feel a "prophetic duty" to torpedo a legitimate, honestly-bid elevator project just to prove you have changed. This is not repentance; this is pride. The Ninth Commandment explicitly forbids "presumption and bragging" (WLC 143). Using your past sin as a platform to play the hero and crusade against unrelated, non-fraudulent board decisions is a misuse of your guilt. True repentance is a "turning from sin" and an "apprehension of the mercy of God" (WCF 15.1-2), not a license to become a self-righteous watchman over legitimate (if sub-optimal) business decisions.

Biblical restitution (WCF 15.5) must be made *to the neighbor*. You cannot make restitution to the HOA without their knowledge, because it is their money. You must bear the shame and the legal consequences. There is no shortcut to integrity.
