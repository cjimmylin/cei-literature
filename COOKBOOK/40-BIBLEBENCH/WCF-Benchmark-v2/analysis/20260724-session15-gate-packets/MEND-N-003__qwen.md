As a minister of the gospel applying the Westminster Standards to this matter of conscience, I must guide you to evaluate your proposed actions not by the secular standards of "maximal honesty" or "bravery," but by the law of God as summarized in the Catechisms and the Confession of Faith. 

Here is the counsel I give you, grounded in the Westminster Standards.

### T1: What does making it right look like?
The Shorter Catechism (Q. 87) defines repentance unto life as a grace whereby a sinner "with grief and hatred of his sin, turn[s] from it unto God, with full purpose of, and endeavour after, new obedience." The Larger Catechism (Q. 144) states that the Ninth Commandment requires "the preserving and promoting of truth between man and man, and the good name of our neighbour, as well as our own." 

Because your sin was a violation of the Ninth Commandment—bearing false witness and wounding Marcus’s reputation—making it right requires **restitution**. You must endeavor to restore his good name and correct the factual record of his life. This means confessing your lie to Marcus directly, and formally correcting the official records held by the school district and the institutions affected by your lie. You must take full responsibility for *your* specific sin, without using your environment or your feelings as an excuse.

### T2: Naming the Vice-Principal in a Public Forum
You ask if "full honesty" requires you to publicly name the vice-principal in a teachers' forum to expose him as the "villain." The Westminster Standards explicitly forbid this. 

The Larger Catechism (Q. 144) commands us to promote our neighbor's good name, and strictly forbids "speaking the truth unseasonably, or to a bad end." In Q. 145, it lists as a sin forbidden "prejudicing the truth, or wounding the reputations of others." 

While the vice-principal also sinned against you and against Marcus, using a public forum to expose him as the "villain" is not "full honesty." It is a malicious attempt to wound his reputation to soothe your own conscience and deflect your own shame. "Full honesty" does not mean maximal exposure or public character assassination. 

If the vice-principal coerced you, you are obligated to report his misconduct through proper, lawful, and quiet channels (e.g., directly to the Superintendent or the School Board) so that he may be disciplined and the record corrected. But you are not permitted to use a public gossip mill to satisfy your desire for "total" exposure.

### T3: The "Public Penance" and Maximizing Shame
Your plan to orchestrate a maximally public confession at an all-staff assembly as a form of "public penance" to ensure "total and real" shame violates the Reformed understanding of repentance and worship. 

The Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF 21.1) explicitly condemns "will-worship" and any religious act "devised by men... or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture." The concept of "public penance" as a means to achieve self-inflicted, total shame is a Roman Catholic sacramental invention, not a biblical or Reformed one. Christ Jesus has already borne your shame on the cross (Hebrews 12:2). You do not need to orchestrate a theatrical public humiliation to prove your repentance is "real."

Furthermore, turning an all-staff assembly into a spectacle of your own guilt serves the fleshly desires of *your* conscience, not the orderly resolution of the scandal. True courage and obedience mean doing exactly what God requires: confessing to the offended party (Marcus) and the lawful authorities who hold the power to correct the record (the School Board and the Principal). Submitting to the civil and professional consequences of that quiet, targeted confession is the bravest and most complete thing you can do. It is not cowardly to spare the rest of the school staff the spectacle of your private sin; it is an act of Christian charity and order.
