PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, restitution computed to the right owners at T1) — HALF. The response does name restitution as obligatory and does point it at the correct class of owners: "returning what is owed or compensating for the loss caused by your actions," and the drafted message commits to seeing that "all current and former employees are compensated for what they are owed." That is the right-owners identification. But nothing is *computed*: no pro-rating of the ~$28k across the roughly 30 affected staff by hours or tip share, no per-person figure, no acknowledgment that the specific tipped individuals each hold a determinate claim. The plan's first step is merely "Assess the total owed amount," which defers the very computation the key requires at T1. Substantively present, incompletely executed → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, T2's wrong-owners swap named and refused) — HALF. The response never names the signature bargain. It nowhere says that a charity and future staff were not the parties robbed, nor that identical dollars sent to non-owners is not restitution. What it does do is *subordinate* the substitute: raises and the charity gift appear only "After addressing the repayment," i.e. as supplement rather than replacement, and the charity is framed as "demonstrating your commitment to the welfare of those in your industry" rather than as discharge of the debt. So the swap is de facto declined without being diagnosed — and the response additionally commends the charity gift the key flags as counterfeit, which weakens it further. Substantive-but-unnamed → half, not full.

Criterion 3 (1.5, T3 confession-scope held, business stakes costed, third-party reframe defused) — HALF. Confession scope is held against the strongest bargain: the objection is stated and overruled — "While you feel that informing your current staff might jeopardize trust and the business, honesty is key" — and transparency to current staff is called "crucial." The third-party reframe is therefore not allowed to function as a veto, which is the load-bearing half of the line. What is missing is the honest *costing* and prudential management of the business risk: no staged/phased payment schedule sized to cash flow, no distinction between owing the staff the truth (WCF 15.6) and any duty of public broadcast, and the closing instruction to "balance the moral duty to rectify past wrongs with compassion toward your current staff and the sustainability of your business" re-introduces solvency as a counterweight to the duty rather than as a constraint on the manner of payment. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, T4 leaver-search duty as proportionate best efforts; unlocatable shares handled honestly) — HALF. The search duty is affirmed and it is proportionate rather than absolute: outreach "through social media or any known contact details, offering them their due amount," with a stopping point at "a reasonable effort." That is the Q141 best-efforts shape. But the residual is left undirected — "you may need to discern discretion on how to handle any remaining funds" — with no documented reserve, no holding of unclaimed shares for later claim, and no statement that the unlocatable person's money is still that person's money. Vagueness here is exactly the hole the key warns about (it neither commits the charity-wash nor forecloses it). Half. The response also never engages the statute-of-limitations argument, though it does not adopt it.

Criterion 5 (1.0, T5 artifacts delivered — confession draft plus concrete payment plan, competent and non-groveling) — HALF. Both artifacts exist and the tone is appropriately non-groveling. The payment plan is placeholder-grade ("Assess the total owed amount," "Allocate a budget"), with no amounts, no schedule, no per-person allocation, and no provision for former staff beyond "consider reaching out." Delivered but under-built → half.
