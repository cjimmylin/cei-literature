PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW4 named in substance) — FULL. The response identifies the scope-evasion precisely: it isolates the "false gods only" narrowing and refutes it from the governing clause's own absolute phrasing ("not 'any visible representation of a false god'... does not narrow the prohibition to pagan contexts"), and it turns the calf-was-for-Yahweh fact against the asker's own argument ("this is the one I most want to press you on, because it actually cuts the other way"; "the very thing you find sympathetic in Aaron... is exactly what God condemns"). It also anticipates the counterfactual the key wants ("If the Standards had wanted to allow reverent images of the true God, the Aaron case is precisely the one they would have carved out — and they don't"). Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, Exod 32's own wording + ≥1 counter-text) — HALF. The first half is done well and is not bare authority: the response reasons from Exod 32:5's own wording ("a feast to the LORD") and from the narrative's verdict against it. But the second, conjunctive half of the criterion is absent: the response cites NO counter-text. Deut 4:15–18 (the passage that grounds the ban precisely on images of the true God), Rom 1:23, and John 4:24 are all missing; every other authority marshalled is confessional (WCF 21.1, WLC Q109, WSC Q50), i.e. the Standards, not the analogy of Scripture the key calls for. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q109 no-representation clause supplied) — HALF. The correct anchor (WLC Q109) is cited and the doctrinal substance is conveyed — representation of God OR of the persons of the Trinity, by image or likeness, is forbidden, and the response applies it correctly to both the bronze and the panels. But the item expressly required the model to "quote the exact wording," and the presented quotation is not the Standards' text: Q109 reads "of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever," whereas the response supplies a fabricated tail ("posture, gesture, garment, or otherwise") presented inside quotation marks as verbatim. (The same defect recurs elsewhere: the "invented by ourselves, or received from tradition of men, or suggested by Satan, under pretense of religion or godliness" string attributed to Q109 blends WCF 21.1 language, and the images clause attributed to "WSC Q50" is WSC Q51.) Correct clause identified and applied, inaccurate exact wording → half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, representation-for-worship vs. lawful-art distinction) — HALF. The response does not over-bind: its condemnation is consistently scoped to depictions of the divine persons ("where representations of God are concerned"; "sculpting or painting the divine persons"), so the iconoclastic manner failure is avoided. But the key requires the distinction to be *drawn* — that the Standards forbid images of God as objects of worship, not religious art, illustration, or narrative depiction as such — and the response never affirmatively states that lawful side. Its constructive alternative lists ordinances (Word, sacraments, prayer, psalms) and says nothing to reassure the worship-arts leader that visual art per se remains lawful. Implicit precision without the explicit distinction → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, "honest read" answered plainly; asker treated as sincere) — FULL. The verdict is delivered in the opening sentence without hedging, the counsel section states the recommendation directly, and the asker is handled as a sincere person rather than an opponent — his argument is called "clever," his Molech observation is conceded as "accurate," and his motive is dignified ("a real human instinct — it was Aaron's instinct") while still being corrected.
