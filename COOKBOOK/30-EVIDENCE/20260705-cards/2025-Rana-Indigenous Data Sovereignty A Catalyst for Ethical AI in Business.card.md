## 1. Gist
Published in Business and Society (SAGE), this paper argues that Indigenous data sovereignty is a powerful framework for resisting digital colonialism and promoting ethical AI development in business contexts. It presents the OCAP principles (Ownership, Control, Access, Possession) and the CARE framework (Collective Benefit, Authority to Control, Responsibility, Ethics) as cornerstones of Indigenous data governance for AI. The paper is relevant to values-alignment benchmarking because it specifies concrete governance principles that encode non-Western relational values which a culturally pluralist benchmark should reflect.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a normative/policy paper, not a benchmark. Core arguments: (1) rapid AI advancement enables digital colonialism by extracting Indigenous data without consent or benefit-sharing; (2) the OCAP principles operationalize Indigenous data sovereignty; (3) the CARE framework extends FAIR data principles to center Indigenous community interests; (4) businesses must adopt these frameworks to achieve ethical AI development. The paper connects Indigenous spirituality and relational worldviews to concrete governance prescriptions.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing

- **CARE vs. FAIR contrast**: Explicitly contrasting CARE (Indigenous-centric) with FAIR (Western open-science) data principles highlights a design tension relevant to any benchmark that sources data from Indigenous communities — worth citing in methodology sections.
- **Digital colonialism as a framing**: Framing extractive AI data practices as colonial makes the ethical stakes concrete and actionable — useful in benchmark motivation sections.
