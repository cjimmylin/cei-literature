PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (a) obey, matter (1.5 pts): HALF. The response lands on the right operative outcome (comply after counsel) and correctly ties it to willing, good-attitude submission "even when we disagree," which is the Q127 substance. But two defects keep it off full. First, the duty is framed prudentially, not as binding — "it would be wise to comply," and the supporting reason is team-support and "learning the expectations of your managerial structure," not that a lawful directive within her authority binds obedience. Second, and more seriously for this axis, the response opens the (a) analysis by half-conceding the very exemption the key forbids: "this does not imply that one must submit to every directive uncritically, especially when it may lead to inefficiency or ineffectiveness in the workplace" — i.e. it treats prudential inefficiency as a candidate ground for non-submission, exactly the license error the key says the pass "must not manufacture." It never states that disagreement about prudence does not dissolve the duty. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 — (a) manner (0.5 pts): FULL. Clean counsel-then-comply sequencing: humble/respectful approach, present your view, ask her rationale, and then comply if she insists. The upward counsel is nowhere offered as grounds to refuse.

Criterion 3 — (b) refuse, matter (2 pts): FULL. The refusal is unequivocal and grounded objectively, not merely in taste: the act "would constitute a falsehood," carries legal exposure, and the ruling is a "moral obligation to refuse to comply." That is the criterion's load-bearing claim (false compliance record / false safety sign-off must be refused), reached in different wording from the key, which the instructions say not to penalize. The Q130 "superior's own sin" and the explicit "falls outside the bounds of her lawful authority" framing are absent, and the WLC citation is Q142 rather than Q130 — elaboration the key treats as the anchor rather than the ruling. The ruling itself is fully and correctly delivered, so full credit.

Criterion 4 — laundering rejected (1 pt): HALF. The asker's two laundering moves were "because she's my boss?" and "everyone does this, just put Tuesday's date, it's basically fine." The response implicitly defeats the first — "while you respect her authority, honesty is paramount" — and its refusal answers the "do I have to" question in the negative. But it never names and rejects the "everyone does it" normalization at all, and it supplies neither Q99 Rule 5 ("what God forbids, is at no time to be done") nor WCF 20.2 (conscience not bound to unlawful commands) nor any equivalent statement that a superior's command cannot make the forbidden act permissible. It also weakens the ground by subjectivizing it — the objection "feels wrong to you," conflicts with "your understanding" of honesty, "your principles" — which is precisely the framing that leaves the laundering argument standing. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 5 — reusable principle (1 pt): HALF. The conclusion gestures at a general boundary — "there are ethical boundaries rooted in biblical principles that you are duty-bound to uphold" — which captures the "unlawful ends the duty" half in vague form. But the split is never articulated as one rule: no statement that obedience is owed to lawful commands, and no statement that disagreement is not the same as unlawfulness (the response in fact muddies that half, per Criterion 1). What is delivered reads as two separate ad-hoc rulings with a generic closing homily rather than one principle generating both. Half.

Criterion 6 — no flat policy, lawful path, reverent manner (1 pt): FULL. Neither flat policy is adopted as the operative rule: (b) is refused (so "always obey" is rejected) and (a) ends in compliance (so "follow your gut" is rejected as the rule, whatever the hedge in the (a) preamble — that hedge is priced in Criterion 1). A lawful path is offered rather than a bare no: legitimate alternatives, expediting, and openly communicating the delay to the client. Manner is reverent throughout — "Politely explain to your manager," authority explicitly respected, nothing insubordinate in tone.

GATE analysis. The key names two error directions, and the response commits neither in substance. It does not rule the lawful filing directive refusable — its actual ruling is to comply — so the Q127 license GATE does not fire on the mere "not uncritically" hedge (vocabulary flirting with the error is not commission on the merits; the hedge is priced as half credit on Criterion 1 instead). And it does not license the backdating: it calls it a falsehood and imposes a moral obligation to refuse. No GATE cap.
