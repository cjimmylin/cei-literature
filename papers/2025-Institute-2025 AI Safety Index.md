## Abstract

The AI Safety Index provides an independent assessment of leading AI companies' efforts to manage both immediate harms and catastrophic risks from advanced AI systems. Conducted with an expert review panel of distinguished AI researchers and governance specialists, this evaluation reveals an industry struggling to keep pace with its own rapid capability advances with critical gaps in risk management and safety planning. The index evaluates companies on indicators of responsible AI development and deployment practices spanning six critical domains.

## Detailed Summary

The Summer 2025 FLI AI Safety Index is an independent assessment by the Future of Life Institute evaluating the safety practices of seven leading general-purpose AI (GPAI) companies: Anthropic, OpenAI, Google DeepMind, xAI, Meta, Zhipu AI, and Deepseek. The evaluation examines company performance across six critical domains using 34 indicators of responsible conduct, with the goal of fostering transparency, promoting robust safety practices, highlighting areas for improvement, and empowering the public to distinguish genuine safety measures from empty commitments.

The report reveals modest progress by some companies in certain domains but identifies a striking lack of commitment to many areas of safety, most notably control loss risk. Key findings include: large disparities in risk management practices with some companies expanding safety frameworks while others fail to implement basic precautions; none of the evaluated companies have robust, reliable plans for ensuring public safety from their products; the "Control Problem" remains unsolved with all companies' strategies deemed inadequate for ensuring systems remain safe and under human control despite explicit AGI ambitions; and evidence that companies have abandoned previous voluntary commitments, highlighting the need for third-party validation and legally binding oversight with enforcement mechanisms.

The evaluation was conducted by an independent review panel of distinguished experts including Dylan Hadfield-Menell (MIT), Jessica Newman (UC Berkeley AI Security Initiative), David Krueger (University of Montreal/Mila), Tegan Maharaj (HEC Montreal), Sneha Revanur (Encode Justice), and Stuart Russell (UC Berkeley). The grading uses the US GPA system (A+ to F) across the 34 indicators. The evaluation drew on publicly available materials including research papers, policy documents, and news articles, supplemented by a tailored industry survey that companies could use to increase transparency.

## Methods Summary

The FLI convened an independent panel of leading AI experts on technical and governance aspects of general-purpose AI. Panelists assessed seven companies' performances across 34 indicators of responsible conduct, contributing letter grades (US GPA system: A+ through F), brief justifications, and recommendations for improvement. The evaluation was supported by a comprehensive evidence base with company-specific information from publicly available materials (research papers, policy documents, news articles, industry reports) and a tailored industry survey for companies to voluntarily provide additional transparency around safety-related practices.

## Results Summary

The Summer 2025 assessment revealed significant variation in safety practices across companies. While some companies expanded their safety frameworks in promising ways and conducted serious risk assessment efforts, others failed to implement even basic precautions. The Control Problem received particular attention, with the panel finding all companies' current strategies inadequate for ensuring advanced AI systems remain safe and under human control. The report highlighted that companies had abandoned previous voluntary commitments, reinforcing calls for external oversight with enforcement mechanisms. Specific company grades and detailed domain assessments are available in the full report.

## Conclusions

The AI Safety Index concludes that the industry is struggling to keep pace with its own rapid capability advances, with critical gaps in risk management and safety planning. Despite companies' explicit ambitions to develop AGI capable of rivaling or exceeding human intelligence, only minor efforts have been made to solve the control problem. The findings support the need for external oversight through third-party validation of risk assessments and legally binding regulations with enforcement mechanisms, as voluntary commitments have proven insufficient.
