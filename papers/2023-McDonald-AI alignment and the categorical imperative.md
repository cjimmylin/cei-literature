# AI, alignment, and the categorical imperative

## Authors

[[McDonald]], [[Fritz J]]

## Links


- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-022-00160-w

## Abstract

This paper responds to the work of Tae Wan Kim, John Hooker, and Thomas Donaldson who attempt to solve the alignment problem—defined as the issue of how to give AI systems moral intelligence. They contend that one might program machines with a version of Kantian ethics cast in deontic modal logic, where machines can be aligned with human values by obeying principles of universalization and autonomy, as well as a deontic utilitarian principle. While programming machines this way might be useful for applications such as autonomous vehicles, McDonald argues that this approach makes demands of machines that go beyond what is currently feasible, and may extend past the limits of what's possible for AI.

## Data Description

This philosophical commentary critiques the proposal by Kim, Hooker, and Donaldson to solve the AI alignment problem using Kantian ethics cast in deontic modal logic combined with connectionist AI. McDonald argues that their approach makes demands beyond current AI feasibility and advocates for greater emphasis on Kant's formula of humanity. No datasets, benchmarks, or AI models are tested.
