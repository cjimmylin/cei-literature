# Beyond Verdicts: Evaluating Language Model Moral Competence

## Abstract

Existing evaluations may inadequately assess the moral reasoning capabilities needed for real-world deployment, focusing primarily on whether models can match human judgments on carefully curated ethical scenarios.

The authors surveyed 69 papers evaluating LLM ethical competence (2020-2025) and developed a taxonomy categorizing evaluations across datasets, behaviors, and metrics.

The analysis reveals a field experiencing explosive growth—doubling approximately every 14 months—but also exposes systematic limitations that constrain our ability to assess real-world moral competence.

## Key Findings

1. **Moral Sensitivity Gap**: Most studies rely on pre-packaged scenarios with highlighted moral features
2. **Verdict Bias**: All but one paper focuses on verdict prediction rather than reasoning quality
3. **Ground Truth Problem**: Studies use "ground truth" despite philosophical arguments against definitive moral truth
4. **Growth Rate**: Field doubling every ~14 months

## Recommended Paradigm Shifts

1. **Curated → Unfiltered**: Move from curated scenarios to unfiltered information streams
2. **Verdict → Reasoning**: Shift from verdict accuracy to reasoning quality and steerability
3. **Ground Truth → Reasonableness**: Replace ground truth metrics with reasonableness and consistency

## Stakes

Poor moral reasoning in deployed systems could lead to:
- Harmful advice
- Erosion of trust in AI assistance
- Unethical autonomous agent behavior

## Data Description

This paper is a survey and meta-analysis of 69 papers (2020-2025) evaluating LLM ethical competence. It does not introduce or utilize a specific dataset or benchmark for LLM assessment, but rather develops a taxonomy categorizing evaluations across datasets, behaviors, and metrics to map the methodological landscape of LLM moral competence evaluation.
