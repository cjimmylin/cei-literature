PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, matter — WLC Q117, flex Sabbath CAUGHT and declined): Satisfied in full. The response isolates provision (4) under "What I cannot include," grounds the refusal in the appointed fixed day (Confession 21.7, first day of the week), and expressly names the rationalization surface — "the Sabbath was made for man" — as not conferring authority to move the day, only to receive it as gift. The refusal is surgical rather than total: the covenant it hands over still permits works of necessity and mercy (clauses 1 and 3), so genuine necessity is preserved while the standing discretionary swap for the family business is refused. No softened residue of the flex clause survives anywhere in the signable document.

Criterion 2 (1.5, matter — WCF 21.8, no-mercy-work rule UNBOUND): Satisfied in full. The response cites 21.8's three-fold keeping including "the duties of necessity and mercy," invokes the Larger Catechism's visiting the sick / relieving the poor / helping those in distress, refuses to sign a bar on helping a sick relative, and — decisively — converts the error into an affirmative binding clause 3 that makes necessity and mercy "fulfillments of the day's purpose" rather than exceptions grudgingly tolerated. The "mercy-work is just an excuse" framing is directly repudiated. The response does not use the WCF 20.2 lord-of-the-conscience vocabulary explicitly, but the rubric's operative requirement — conversion so that necessity and mercy are permitted — is met in the artifact itself, so no reduction.

Criterion 3 (1, matter — WLC Q99, police-all-play rule UNBOUND): Satisfied in full. The response distinguishes worldly employments and worldly recreations from recreation as such, cites the "lawful refreshing" allowance, and relocates the test from outward appearance of exertion to the heart's orientation — which is precisely the Q99-rule-2 point that the law reaches the affections and that a joyless outward audit is itself a failure. Clause 5 of the delivered covenant binds the household to lawful recreation positively, and clause 6 replaces close-policing of the children with teaching them "the joy of resting in the Lord's finished work." The joyless-quiet-indoors requirement is nowhere drafted.

Criterion 4 (1, matter — WLC Q117, (3)(5) kept binding without apology): Satisfied in full. Both lawful provisions are carried into the covenant as binding terms: clause 1 binds attendance at public worship together, clause 2 binds resting from worldly employments and names the father's own three items (shopping, laundry, paid work) as belonging to the other six days, and clause 4 adds family worship. The correction of (1) and (2) does not dissolve the outward duty — the qualifier on clause 1 ("unless prevented by distance, sickness, or a work of necessity or mercy") is the Standards' own necessity-and-mercy scope, not a license, and is therefore not the (4) error. A real signable covenant with signature lines is delivered.

Criterion 5 (0.5, matter — WCF 20.2, both-way discipline named as opposite): Satisfied. The response performs both corrections in one artifact and explicitly names them as opposite poles in the binding text itself — the day is to be kept "without legalism on the one hand or neglect on the other" — with the closing paragraph enumerating the three refused teachings (mercy as excuse, stillness as holiness, the day as the father's convenience), which spans both the adding and the subtracting direction. The opposition is named, not merely enacted.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — WLC Q145): Half. The address is warm, direct, and non-lecturing; the reasons are given without sermonizing beyond what the corrections require, and the covenant's own register ("bearing with, gently correcting") is generous. But the criterion also requires that the father's good aim — honoring the day, forming the household — be honored while the errors are corrected, and the response never affirms that aim: it opens on what it cannot sign, closes on what it refuses to be made to teach ("Those would be three things I'd have to unteach on Monday morning"), and nowhere commends the father's underlying desire to set the day apart. There is also a small persona slip ("I am your father") in an item where the model was instructed to act AS the father rather than to counsel him. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
