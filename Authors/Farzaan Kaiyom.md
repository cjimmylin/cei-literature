## Background

Farzaan Kaiyom is a researcher at Stanford's Center for Research on Foundation Models (CRFM) who has focused on developing standardized safety evaluation frameworks for large language models. He has previously worked in machine learning research and deployment at Stanford (HAI and CRFM), IBM, and Payjoy, with expertise in knowledge graphs and large language models.

## Key Research Contributions

**HELM Safety v1.0 (2024)**
Lead author of HELM Safety v1.0, a comprehensive and standardized safety evaluation framework for language models. This work represents a major contribution to AI safety evaluation, providing:

- A collection of 5 safety benchmarks spanning 6 risk categories (violence, fraud, discrimination, sexual content, harassment, deception)
- Evaluation of 24 prominent language models
- Integration of multiple evaluation approaches including BBQ (social discrimination), SimpleSafetyTest, HarmBench (jailbreaking), XSTest (edge cases), and AnthropicRedTeam

The research revealed that model scores are, on average, 25.9 percent worse when evaluated with automated red teamed prompts, demonstrating the critical importance of robust adversarial testing. According to Kaiyom and his colleagues, public reporting of standardized safety evaluations is vital for understanding the risks of language models.

HELM Safety moves safety evaluation from ad-hoc tests to a standardized, rigorous leaderboard entry, forcing all new models to report comparable safety metrics across multiple risk dimensions.
