# Joseph Henrich

## Research Summary

Joseph Henrich is the Ruth Moore Professor of Biological Anthropology and Chair of the Department of Human Evolutionary Biology at Harvard University. He is a leading figure in cultural evolution research and is best known for coining the "WEIRD" concept (Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic) to describe the psychological peculiarity of populations commonly used in behavioral research.

His research integrates evolutionary approaches to psychology, decision-making, and culture, using ethnographic methods from anthropology combined with experimental techniques from psychology and economics. Key research areas include cultural learning, culture-gene coevolution, human sociality, prestige, leadership, large-scale cooperation, and religion.

Major books:
- "The Secret of Our Success: How Culture is Driving Human Evolution" (2016, Princeton)
- "The WEIRDest People in the World: How the West Became Psychologically Peculiar and Particularly Prosperous" (2020)

His work argues that Western psychological traits like individualism and analytical thinking emerged from medieval Catholic Church policies that undermined kin-based societies, demonstrating how culture and psychology co-evolve over centuries.
