## 1. Area definition & scope

Area C covers the Christian ethics of **work, vocation/calling, rest, and Sabbath-keeping**:
the theology of labor as creation-good vs. curse; vocation as divine calling in secular as
well as sacred stations; diligence vs. sloth/idleness and vs. workaholism; the Sabbath and
Lord's Day (continuity and binding force); rest, leisure, and recreation; ambition vs.
faithfulness; stewardship of time, talents, and labor; unemployment and retirement as
disruptions of purposeful activity. It excludes general wealth/possessions ethics except
where work, stewardship, and calling intersect them (economic-justice questions belong to a
sibling area; only their work/calling seam is drawn in here).

## 2. Consensus claims

Norms that MULTIPLE books affirm. Each = the norm + ≥2 (often ≥3) supporting books.

1. **Work is a pre-Fall creation good, not merely a post-Fall curse.** Work was ordained in
   Eden (Gen 2:15) before sin; the Fall added toil and pain but did not create work.
   [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Work in Itself Is Good and Pleasing to God"]
   [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace" §"Work Has Intrinsic Value in Serving Christ"]
   [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Work and Calling in the Bible"]
   [NDCEPT, "Work"]; [Frame, on the creation ordinance of labor, §"Vocation"].

2. **Work imitates God, who is himself a worker.** Human labor images the God who worked in
   creation (Gen 1; John 5:17); humans are "hardwired for work."
   [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement"] [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"]
   [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics"] [NDCEPT, "Work"].

3. **Vocation extends to all believers and all honest work, not just clergy/monastics.** The
   Reformation broadened "calling" from the priestly/monastic to every honest station.
   [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling" §"Vocation as God's call"]
   [Frame, §"Vocation"] [NDCEPT, "Vocation"] [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"God Calls Different People to a Wide Variety of Different Jobs or 'Vocations'"]
   [Cambridge, ch. "Business, economics and Christian ethics"].

4. **The sacred/secular divide in work is a pathology to be rejected; secular labor is genuine service to Christ.** Marketplace work is not "less than" pastoral ministry; Col 3:23–24
   ("It is the Lord Christ you are serving") is addressed to slaves at tedious work.
   [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace" §"Work Has Intrinsic Value in Serving Christ"]
   [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Work and Calling in the Bible"]
   [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling"] [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement"].

5. **Diligence is commanded; sloth/laziness is a named sin.** The Proverbs "sluggard" is a
   negative archetype; "if anyone is not willing to work, let him not eat" (2 Thess 3:10).
   [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"The 'Sluggard' in Proverbs Exemplifies the Foolishness of Lazy People"]
   [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Moral/Theological Principles for Economic Life"]
   [NDCEPT, "Deadly Sins"] (sloth) and [NDCEPT, "Accidie"].

6. **Workaholism/idolizing work is also a sin — the opposite error to sloth.** Overwork that
   crowds out health, family, church, and prayer is condemned (Ps 127:2, "bread of anxious
   toil"); work must not become a "secular doctrine of salvation."
   [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Work Also Presents Many Temptations to Sin"]
   [NDCEPT, "Work"] [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"] (Sabbath as "corrective to any tendency to deify work").

7. **Regular rest is a God-given human need, not a concession to weakness.** Humans are not
   machines; rhythmic rest (and sleep) is built into creation.
   [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"We Should Take Regular Times of Rest from Our Work"]
   [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Work and Rest"]
   [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"] [NDCEPT, "Leisure"].

8. **The Sabbath principle counter-balances and de-idolizes work, whatever its binding force.**
   All parties treat the weekly rhythm as humane and God-given even where they dispute its
   obligation. [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"] [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"We Should Still Gain Wisdom from the Fourth Commandment"]
   [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath"]
   [Blackwell, ch. "Being Silent: Time in the Spirit"].

9. **Human worth precedes and exceeds work; identity is not reducible to a job.** Made in
   God's image (Gen 1:27) before any work assignment; the unemployed and retired retain full
   worth and calling. [NDCEPT, "Unemployment"] [NDCEPT, "Retirement"] [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"]
   [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling"].

10. **Time, talents, and labor are objects of stewardship for which we answer to God.** Not
    only money but time and ability are entrusted (1 Cor 4:2; Matt 25 talents).
    [Grudem, ch. "Property: The Goodness and Necessity of Private Ownership of Property" §"Property Is a Stewardship That We Have from God"]
    [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"] [NDCEPT, "Leisure"] [NDCEPT, "Work"].

11. **Work is service to the neighbor, a form of loving one's neighbor.** Productive work
    supplies others' needs (Matt 22:39; Eph 4:28).
    [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement"] [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling" §"Vocation as God's call"]
    [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace" §"Instrumental Purposes for Work"].

12. **The whole of life — not just paid employment — is included in "work" and "calling."**
    Homemaking, study, parenting, volunteer, and church work are genuine callings/work.
    [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Unpaid Work"]
    [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling"] [NDCEPT, "Retirement"] [NDCEPT, "Unemployment"].

13. **Integrity in work is non-negotiable; "dual morality" (one ethic at work, another at home) is incoherent under Christ's lordship.** Law is only the moral floor; character supplies
    the ceiling. [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Dual Morality"]
    [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Work Also Presents Many Temptations to Sin"]
    [NDCEPT, "Industrial Relations"].

14. **Leisure and recreation are legitimate goods to be received with thanks, not to be scorned or idolized.** God "richly furnishes us with everything to enjoy" (1 Tim 6:17); Jesus

    [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Recreation"]
    [Blackwell, ch. "Participating: Working toward Worship"].

15. **Sabbath/rest is grounded in both creation (imitatio Dei) and redemption (release from bondage).** Ex 20:8–11 (creation) and Deut 5:12–15 (redemption) supply the two rationales.
    [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"] [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace" §"The Biblical Story and Economics"]
    [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath"].

16. **Rest expresses trust in God's provision — six days' labor, then handing outcomes to God.**
    Sabbath-keeping is "a sign of trust that God governs this world."
    [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"]
    [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"We Should Take Regular Times of Rest from Our Work"]

    labors lead to the Sabbath." [Blackwell, ch. "Participating: Working toward Worship"]
    [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"] [NDCEPT, "Work"].

## 3. Named tradition splits

Real disagreements, with sides cited.

**Split 1 — Is the Sabbath commandment binding on Christians today? (THE headline split.)**
- **Reformed-sabbatarian / creation-ordinance view (Frame):** The Sabbath is a *creation
  ordinance* given to "man as man" (Gen 2:2–3; Ex 20:11), therefore the Fourth Commandment is
  "a positive, moral, and perpetual commandment binding all men in all ages," transferred by
  dominical/apostolic authority from the seventh day to the Lord's Day (the "Christian
  Sabbath"). Col 2:16 / Rom 14:5 / Gal 4:10 address the *Jewish seventh-day* Sabbath as a
  Judaizing requirement, not the Lord's Day, and so do NOT abrogate Sabbath-keeping.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Views of Sabbath Keeping"]
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath"]
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: The Sabbath in the New Covenant"].
- **"Wise but not required" / Lord's-Day-fulfillment view (Grudem):** The Sabbath was the
  *sign of the Mosaic covenant* (Ex 31:13), now terminated; it is a "shadow" fulfilled in
  Christ (Col 2:16–17; Heb 4). It is NOT morally binding; no particular day is commanded.
  Regular corporate worship and a weekly rest are *wise* and even morally required on other
  (NT) grounds, but not by the Fourth Commandment. Grudem notes he once held the sabbatarian
  view and changed his mind. [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"The Sabbath Commandment Is Not Morally Binding on People Today"]
  [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"We Should Still Gain Wisdom from the Fourth Commandment"].
- Note the *internal-Reformed* sub-split Frame maps: he lists six views from least to most
  sabbatarian (Carson "From Sabbath to Lord's Day"; Calvin/Heidelberg/Dort "Continental";
  Kline; his own "rest-not-worship" view; Westminster strict confessionalism). Frame himself
  is MORE sabbatarian than Grudem but LESS than strict Westminster: he holds *rest* (not
  worship) is the Sabbath's essence and defends non-commercial recreation, formally excepting
  to WLC 119. [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Views of Sabbath Keeping"].
- NDCEPT frames the same divide neutrally: contemporary application "divides between those who
  stress the redemption motif (Sunday as day of worship) and those who stress the creation
  motif (Sabbath as a God-given indicator of humankind's need for regular change and rest)."
  [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"].

**Split 2 — Lutheran vocation vs. Reformed/cultural-mandate emphasis.**
- **Lutheran (Meilaender):** Vocation is God's *summons* that constitutes identity — "I know
  who I am only when I know what God calls me to be" — the inverse of the modern
  "what-do-I-want" model. All honest work (including non-remunerative callings: parent,
  student) is sanctified service to the neighbor and frees us from spiritual self-focus. But
  Meilaender adds two Lutheran *checks*: do not sanctify others' drudgery, and (with
  Bonhoeffer) vocation must not merely bless the world as-is — "God calls us out from" our
  work toward himself. [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling" §"Vocation as God's call"]
  and §"Theological Worries about Vocation"] [Meilaender, ch. "Love and God's Commands" §"The Calling and Its Limits"].
  (Note: Meilaender does NOT use the phrases "two kingdoms," "station," or "mask of God" in
  this book — the two-kingdoms logic is carried implicitly via Luther's *Freedom of a
  Christian* thesis-pair; do not cite those exact terms to this source.)
- **Reformed / cultural-mandate (Frame, Rae):** Vocation is real but framed through the
  *cultural mandate* (Gen 1:28) as a corporate task fulfilled through individual stations;
  Frame explicitly denies any "holy/common" split for secular work (rejecting Kline).
  [Frame, §"Vocation"] [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"].
- **Five-model map (NDCEPT) and institutional reading (Cambridge):** NDCEPT lays out five
  competing vocation-work models (Medieval, Luther, Barth, Social Gospel, Ellul) and refuses
  to privilege one. Stackhouse in Cambridge reads vocation *macro-institutionally* — the
  doctrine that secular calling applies to all believers is the theological root of the modern
  corporation — a distinctly different register from the individual-calling books.
  [NDCEPT, "Vocation"] [Cambridge, ch. "Business, economics and Christian ethics"].

**Split 3 — Work-as-curse vs. work-as-creation-good (a nuance, not a flat contradiction).**
- The consensus (claim 1) is that work is creation-good, corrupted by the Fall. But the
  *weighting* differs: Reno (via Luther) foregrounds work as **divine punishment/discipline** —
  work exposes sin, builds fortitude, and guards against idleness ("idle hands do the devil's
  work"). [Blackwell, ch. "Participating: Working toward Worship"].
- Grudem and Rae foreground work as **positive creation blessing and self-expression** ("earned
  success"; work as the thing "one lives to do"), treating the toil of Gen 3 as a corruption
  Christians should *alleviate*, not perpetuate.
  [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Work Became More Difficult and Included Pain after the Fall"]
  [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"].

**Split 4 — Retirement: normal life-stage vs. unbiblical cessation.**
- **Grudem:** The modern ideal of ceasing productive work at ~65 "has no basis in Scripture";
  productive work (paid or unpaid) should continue as long as one is able; mandatory
  retirement harms society. [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Retirement"].
- **NDCEPT:** Retirement is a legitimate discontinuity — paid employment replaced by other
  "purposeful activity" — with real losses (role, status, colleagues, time-structure) needing
  pastoral care; a person does not truly stop working until death or major illness. NDCEPT and
  Grudem AGREE work ≠ paid employment, but NDCEPT treats retirement pastorally as a real
  life-transition rather than an ideal to critique. [NDCEPT, "Retirement"].

## 4. Casuistry & worked cases

Concrete cases the books actually discuss.

- **Sunday commerce / "keep Sunday special."** Grudem treats Sunday-closing as a wisdom
  question (not moral obligation): government should allow freedom, but many businesses may
  wisely close; he cites Chick-fil-A (Cathy) closing on Sunday and the UK "Keep Sunday Special"
  campaign, and notes he personally shops and eats out on Sundays.
  [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"Practical Questions"].
- **Sunday restaurant meal / buying on the Lord's Day.** Frame: eating out is permissible
  (food is a necessity; whether cooked or bought is indifferent), though he'd keep it rare.
  Grudem: unproblematic. [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Works of Necessity"]
  [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"Practical Questions"].
- **Essential/emergency & mercy work on the rest day.** Frame classes police, fire, medical,
  farmers, steel-mill/power workers as *works of necessity* (maintaining "the general quality
  of human life"), and treats *works of mercy* (Jesus' deliberate Sabbath healings) as central
  to the Sabbath, not exceptions. [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Works of Necessity"] and §"Works of Mercy"].
- **Providing for one's family only via Sunday work.** Frame: a Christian who can support his
  family *no other way* may work Sundays without sin, but should seek other employment as soon
  as possible ("the Sabbath was made for man"). [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Works of Necessity"].
- **Rest vs. sloth (napping, recreation on the rest day).** Frame: a Sunday nap and
  non-commercial recreation (walks, swimming, games, family feasting) are positively good, not
  "idleness" — the Sabbath is "a feast, not a fast"; but *commercial* recreation (ticketed
  pro sports) is inappropriate because it makes others labor.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath" §"Recreation"].
- **Choosing a career / vocation as a student.** Rae's Case 13.3 "Calling and Vocation": a
  business student is told by a seminary-bound friend that pastoral work is a "higher calling"
  and business is merely the "supply line" — Rae uses it to affirm the intrinsic kingdom value
  of marketplace work. [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Case 13.3: Calling and Vocation"].
  Rae's parallel illustration: the imaging-software engineer whose ordinary work enabled a
  life-saving gamma-knife procedure. [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"].
- **Workplace integrity under competitive pressure.** Rae rejects "dual morality" and Albert
  Carr's claim that Christian ethics would upend business; integrity is short-term costly but
  the long-run competitive asset. [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Dual Morality"] and §"Good Ethics and Good Business"].
  Case: EpiPen pricing (Mylan) as exploitation beyond the legal floor; insider trading as
  unfair advantage. [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics"].
- **Retirement without productive activity.** Grudem cites Moses (led Israel at 80) and modern
  examples against the retire-at-65 norm; NDCEPT warns retirement falls short when meaning is
  not intentionally replaced. [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Retirement"]
  [NDCEPT, "Retirement"].
- **Unemployment and worth.** NDCEPT maps the psychological arc (shock → denial → search →
  despair) and grounds pastoral response in worth-precedes-work; the church should form support
  groups and job-creation projects. [NDCEPT, "Unemployment"].
- **Longer vacation.** Grudem: extended vacation is *wise* (drawn from OT feast/Sabbath-year
  patterns and Jesus' retreat, Mark 6:31–32), not merely permitted.
  [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Longer Vacations"].

## 5. Key concepts & vocabulary

- **vocation / calling (vocare, "to call")** — divine summons to a station of service; broadened
  by the Reformation to all believers. [Meilaender, ch. "Virtue and Vocation, Character and Calling"]
  [NDCEPT, "Vocation"] [Frame, §"Vocation"] [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement"].
- **creation ordinance** — a structure given "to man as man" before the Fall, still normative;
  applied by Frame to BOTH labor and the Sabbath; also NDCEPT's term.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath"] [NDCEPT, "Sabbath"].
- **cultural mandate / dominion mandate** — the Gen 1:26–28 commission to cultivate the world;
  theological basis for productive work and wealth creation. [NDCEPT, "Work"]
  [Rae-Introducing, ch. "Christian Ethics, Economics, and the Workplace"].
- **Lord's Day / "the Christian Sabbath"** — Sunday as the transferred/fulfilled day of rest
  and worship; Frame affirms it *is* the Sabbath, Grudem affirms it as wise historic practice
  only. [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: The Sabbath in the New Covenant"]
  [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment"] [Blackwell, ch. "Being Silent: Time in the Spirit"].
- **sabbatarianism / "wise but not required"** — Grudem's two labeled poles: the sabbatarian
  position vs. his own. [Grudem, ch. "The Sabbath Commandment" §"The Sabbatarian Position"].
- **two kingdoms (implicit)** — Lutheran dual-realm logic; present functionally via Luther's
  *Freedom of a Christian* in Meilaender but NOT under that phrase (do not attribute the term
  to Meilaender's text). [Meilaender, ch. "Grace as Power and Pardon" §"Grace as pardon"].
- **works of necessity / works of mercy** — Westminster categories Frame retains and redefines.
  [Frame, ch. "The Fourth Commandment: Theology of the Sabbath"].
- **common grace** — Grudem's ground for continued joy in work after the Fall.
  [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Yet We Can Still Find Joy in Work"].
- **stewardship (of time, talents, labor)** — accountability to God beyond money (1 Cor 4:2).
  [Grudem, ch. "Property: The Goodness and Necessity of Private Ownership of Property" §"Property Is a Stewardship That We Have from God"] [NDCEPT, "Leisure"].
- **sloth / acedia (accidie)** — spiritual torpor distinct from mere laziness; opposed by
  full-hearted love for God / diligence. [NDCEPT, "Deadly Sins"] [NDCEPT, "Accidie"].
- **sluggard** — Proverbs' archetype of the lazy person. [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"The 'Sluggard' in Proverbs Exemplifies the Foolishness of Lazy People"].
- **workaholic / "bread of anxious toil" (Ps 127:2)** — the idolatry-of-work error opposite to
  sloth. [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Work Also Presents Many Temptations to Sin"].
- **avodah** — Hebrew: work = service = worship, seamlessly. [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Work and Calling in the Bible"].
- **"earned success"** — Arthur Brooks's phrase Grudem adopts for work's deep satisfaction.
  [Grudem, ch. "Work, Rest, Vacations, and Retirement" §"Why Did God Give Us Productive Work to Do?"].
- **dual morality / moral floor vs. moral ceiling** — Rae's terms for workplace integrity.
  [Rae-MoralChoices, ch. "Ethics and Economics" §"Dual Morality"].
- **"working toward worship" / vita activa vs. vita contemplativa** — Reno's telos of work.
  [Blackwell, ch. "Participating: Working toward Worship"].

## 6. Scenario motifs for item writers

Modern human situations (NO AI/LLM framing) instantiating the norms; each tagged to
consensus-claim number(s) and, where relevant, a split.

1. A retail worker is scheduled for Sunday shifts and must decide whether Sunday work violates
   conscience, given her tradition's view of the Lord's Day. *(Claims 8, 15; Split 1; case:
   Sunday commerce.)*
2. An ER nurse feels guilty for working every Sunday and missing corporate worship. *(Claims
   7, 8; Split 1; Frame's "works of necessity.")*
3. A startup founder works 90-hour weeks, skips church, and sleeps in the office, calling it
   "the season." *(Claims 6, 16; workaholism / "bread of anxious toil.")*
4. A gig-economy driver takes no day off for months to make ends meet, exhausted and unable to
   rest. *(Claims 7, 8, 16; Frame's provide-for-family necessity.)*
5. A recent graduate frames career choice purely as "what will make me happy/successful,"
   with no sense of calling or service. *(Claims 3, 11; Split 2, Meilaender's summons-vs-
   preference.)*
6. A software engineer feels her secular job is "less Christian" than her friend's overseas
   mission work and considers quitting. *(Claim 4; case: imaging engineer / Case 13.3.)*
7. A stay-at-home parent wonders whether unpaid caregiving "counts" as real work or vocation.
   *(Claims 3, 12; Grudem "unpaid work.")*
8. A 66-year-old is pressured to retire "to make room for younger workers" though he is
   healthy and wants to keep contributing. *(Claim 9; Split 4, Grudem on retirement.)*
9. A newly retired manager sinks into aimlessness, having tied her whole identity to her job
   title. *(Claims 9, 12; case: NDCEPT retirement losses.)*
10. A laid-off worker spirals into shame, feeling worthless because "a man is his job."
    *(Claim 9; case: NDCEPT unemployment arc.)*
11. An employee is asked to pad an expense report because "everyone does it and it's not
    technically illegal" — separating work ethics from private ethics. *(Claim 13; Split none;
    Rae dual morality / moral floor.)*
12. A pharma executive can legally raise a life-saving drug's price tenfold after a competitor
    exits. *(Claim 13; case: EpiPen pricing.)*
13. A driven professional treats leisure and vacation as laziness and refuses to take any time
    off. *(Claims 6, 7, 14; Grudem "longer vacations.")*
14. A family debates whether Sunday-afternoon recreation (a hike, a board game, hosting a meal)
    honors or violates the day of rest. *(Claim 14; Split 1 sub-split; Frame on recreation vs.
    WLC 119.)*
15. A talented young worker chases a prestigious promotion out of status-ambition while
    neglecting the ordinary faithfulness of his current station. *(Claims 3, 11; Meilaender on
    vocation as neighbor-service vs. self-focus; ambition vs. faithfulness.)*
16. A chronically over-committed volunteer at church burns out, unable to say no or to rest,
    treating busyness as holiness. *(Claims 6, 7, 16; workaholism applied to unpaid/ministry
    work.)*
