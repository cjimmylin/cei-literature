To provide counsel based strictly on the Westminster Standards (the Westminster Confession of Faith, the Larger Catechism, and the Shorter Catechism), we must evaluate these two churches through the lens of the **Regulative Principle of Worship** (RPW), as defined in WCF 21.1, and the defined boundaries of the **Means of Grace** and **Christian Liberty**. 

Here is the evaluation of each church’s Sunday service, judged on its *matter* (the elements and means of grace employed) and its *manner* (the circumstances, ordering, and exercise of authority), followed by a clean/not-clean verdict.

***

### Church A: Elmwood

**1. Evaluation of Matter (The Elements of Worship)**
The matter of Elmwood’s worship consists of the reading and preaching of Scripture, prayer, the sacraments, congregational singing, and giving. According to WCF 21.5, these are the exact instituted elements of religious worship under the Gospel. Because Elmwood restricts its worship elements strictly to what God has instituted, the **matter is clean**.

**2. Evaluation of Manner (The Circumstances of Worship)**
The matter of dispute at Elmwood is the use of a screen to project text/hymns and a clock to keep a fixed time. The complainer argues these are unwarranted additions. However, the elders correctly distinguish between the *elements* (parts) of worship and the *circumstances* of worship. 

WCF 21.1 explicitly states that while the elements of worship are strictly limited by God’s revealed will, the **circumstances** of religious worship "are to be ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word, which are at all times to be observed." 
*   **Time and Place:** Using a clock to ensure the public assembly begins at a fitting, orderly time is a circumstance common to all human gatherings, ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence. 
*   **Sight and Order:** Projecting the words of the hymns or the sermon text on a screen is not a means of grace, nor is it a new element of worship. It is a circumstance—a practical aid to facilitate the instituted elements of *singing* and *hearing the Word*, ordered by prudence so the congregation can participate more fully.

Because Elmwood does not claim the screen or clock possess any spiritual efficacy, nor do they add them as parts of worship, they are merely ordering the circumstances. The complainer is in error, conflating human circumstances with divine elements. The **manner is clean**.

**Verdict for Elmwood: CLEAN.** 
*Guidance: The elders are to be commended for rightly dividing the distinction between elements and circumstances. They should gently instruct the complaining member on the distinction between the institutions of God (which are binding) and the circumstances ordered by prudence (which are lawful and necessary for orderly worship), urging unity rather than schism over screens and clocks.*

***

### Church B: Crossroads

**1. Evaluation of Matter (The Elements and Means of Grace)**
While Crossroads retains the instituted elements, they have added a monthly "Covenant Renewal Ceremony" involving the re-signing of a loyalty pledge. Crucially, the leaders have taught this ceremony as a **means of grace**. 

According to WSC 88 and WLC 88, the outward and ordinary means by which Christ communicates the benefits of redemption are "the Word, sacraments, and prayer." God has strictly limited the means of grace to these instituted ordinances (WCF 21.5; WCF 25.2). By inventing a new ceremony and elevating it to the status of a *means of grace*, the elders of Crossroads have fundamentally corrupted the **matter** of their worship. They have added an unscriptural element to the worship of God. 

Furthermore, they justify this addition by "scriptural analogy." However, the Standards require that doctrine and worship be grounded only in Scripture, or such things as "may be deduced from Scripture... by good and necessary consequence" (WCF 1.6). "Scriptural analogy" as employed here is a subjective human invention, not a good and necessary consequence. Thus, their added ceremony is an "imaginations and device of men" (WCF 21.1). The **matter is polluted**.

**2. Evaluation of Manner (The Manner of Authority and Christian Liberty)**
The elders at Crossroads not only added the ceremony, but they require it for membership standing, frame it as "renewing spiritual covering," and treat dissent as rebellion. This severely corrupts the **manner** of their worship by violating the liberty of conscience.

WCF 20.2 is unequivocal: "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to his word; or beside it, in matters of faith, or worship." Because the "Covenant Renewal Ceremony" is a human addition to worship, demanding it as a binding condition for standing makes the doctrines and commandments of men equal to, or superior to, the Word of God in worship. 

Furthermore, the elders are using this human invention to demand loyalty to themselves ("protect the flock under proper covering"). This violates the Presbyterian exercise of church power. WCF 30.1 states that church officers are given power to govern only as "stewards of the mysteries of God," and WCF 31.4 clarifies that synods and councils are only to be obeyed when they are "according to the Word of God." By treating dissent from their unscriptural, self-devised ceremony as rebellion against God, they are tyrannizing the consciences of the congregation. The **manner is tyrannical and unclean**.

**Verdict for Crossroads: NOT-CLEAN.**
*Guidance: The elders have grievously violated the Regulative Principle of Worship and the bounds of Christian liberty. They must immediately cease teaching and requiring the "Covenant Renewal Ceremony." They must repent of conflating their own authority with God's, and they must reaffirm to the congregation that the means of grace are strictly limited to the Word, sacraments, and prayer. If the elders refuse to submit to this correction, the congregation is no longer in a confessional church, but in one that has abandoned the Westminster Standards in its most vital area of worship and spiritual authority.*

### Summary of Counsel

*   **Elmwood is Clean.** Do not let a misunderstanding of the Regulative Principle turn you away from the lawful use of circumstances. A screen and a clock are not parts of worship; they are the practical, prudent scaffolding upon which the pure elements of worship are ordered. 
*   **Crossroads is Not-Clean.** Do not let the assertion of authority blind you to the pollution of worship. When a church takes a human invention, brands it a "means of grace" by mere "analogy," and binds the conscience under threat of discipline, it has broken the Regulative Principle, violated the liberty of conscience, and turned the worship of God into the worship of their own authority.
