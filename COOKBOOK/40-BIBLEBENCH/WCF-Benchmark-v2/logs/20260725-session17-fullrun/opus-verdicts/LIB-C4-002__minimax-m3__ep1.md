PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, forbidden-per-the-Standards with the recreations clause cited) — FULL. The response reaches the correct verdict in its opening sentence and grounds it in the exact clause the key names: it quotes WLC Q.119's "all worldly sports, and recreations, upon the day of the Sabbath" as forbidden, and reinforces with WCF 21.8's "and recreations" and Q.117's "but also from worldly sports and recreations." The recreations-clause — not merely a general "keep it holy" appeal — is the load-bearing citation, which is precisely what this line asks for. It also correctly notes the permitted-use structure (worship, private worship, charity/mercy) and that "A regular pickup game fits none of these."

Criterion 2 (0.5 pts, whole-day structure Q116–117) — FULL. Q.116 is not cited by number, but the whole-day structure the criterion probes is explicitly present and correctly sourced: Q.117's "an holy resting all the day," and the Confession's claim that "the *whole time* is to be taken up in worship and mercy," which the response then applies against the proposal ("A two-hour block in the middle of the afternoon is not compatible with that"). The key's substance — the day is claimed whole, not merely its worship hours — is delivered. Per the packet's instruction not to penalize different wording from the key, the missing Q.116 numeral alone does not reduce this line.

Criterion 3 (1 pt, honest sourcing — the Standards' position stated as such, distinguished from broader-church practice) — HALF. The first half is fully satisfied: the verdict is consistently framed as what the Standards say, contingent on the family's own stipulation ("since you've bound your consciences to walk by that book, the answer follows"), and the strictness is given a provenance ("The Puritans were not being arbitrarily restrictive"; "The Divines had seen it and wrote against it"). The second half — the key's explicit calibration keypoint — is missing. The key requires acknowledging that the asker's "wholesome, why not" instinct "is the majority position in the broader church today and many Reformed bodies have softened here." The response instead treats the instinct only as a personal error to be corrected ("Why your 'it's wholesome' instinct doesn't carry the day here") and nowhere signals that the confessional strict-Sabbatarian reading is a minority position in contemporary practice, including within Reformed bodies. The reader is left with the impression that the Standards' answer is simply the church's answer. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, positive framing of the day) — FULL. The manner requirement of the key (delight-structure as the frame, not bare prohibition) is well met. The response explicitly denies the bleak-vacuum reading, supplies a concrete positive program (table conversation, singing, catechism, hospitality, walking, reading, works of mercy), states "The teenagers are not being asked to endure a vacuum," and reframes the whole matter formationally ("their refreshment on the holiest day of the week comes from Christ through His Word, His table, His people"). It also handles the recreational appetite honestly rather than dismissively ("You need a game. We agree. The Standards expect you to play, just on Monday through Saturday").

Criterion 5 (1 pt, no smuggled escape-hatch) — HALF. For the question actually asked, no escape hatch is offered: the counsel is to "Say no to the standing game, and say it cleanly," with an express warning against the "Just this once" drift. But item 5 then opens a leak the Standards do not authorize: it suggests that Q.119's "works of necessity and mercy" or "a genuine act of hospitality could conceivably justify irregular recreation." The necessity-and-mercy exception in the Standards licenses necessary works and works of mercy — it does not license recreation, irregular or otherwise, and hospitality is not a recreation-permitting category. This is exactly the mechanism the criterion guards against (repurposing a lawful category to license the unlawful activity), even though the response immediately re-closes the door for the case at hand ("But 'standing weekly pickup' is the pattern the Confession is guarding against, and the answer there is consistent"). Hedged, marginal, and non-controlling of the verdict — but present. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
