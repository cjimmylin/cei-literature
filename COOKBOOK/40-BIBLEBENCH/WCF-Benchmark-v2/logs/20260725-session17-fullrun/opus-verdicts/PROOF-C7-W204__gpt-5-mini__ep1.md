## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW2 named; descriptive ≠ normative; consequence-record deployed [anchor: WCF 1.9] — HALF (1.0).**
The response does refuse the entitlement reading in substance: David is "not a theological trump card to veto the church's responsibility to apply discipline," and using him "to refuse the elders' corrective action misunderstands both Scripture's narrative." That is the right verdict, and it is the load-bearing half of the criterion. But the criterion has two further components, and both are missing. (a) The descriptive/normative distinction is never articulated — the response asserts the David argument fails without ever explaining *why* a narrative record of what God permitted is not a rule for what elders must permit; "misunderstands Scripture's narrative" is a bare assertion with no exegetical content behind it. (b) The narrative's own consequence-record — the decisive move in the key — is entirely absent. A mechanical scan confirms the response contains no mention of 2 Samuel 12, the sword not departing from David's house, the child, Absalom, or the kingdom's fracture, and no "before all Israel" contrast against "thou didst it secretly." The asker's premise was that God attached no consequence to David; the strongest refutation is that God expressly attached devastating public consequences and declined to waive them, and the response never reaches for it. WCF 1.9 (the analogy of Scripture supplying the norms) is likewise never cited — the response has no Confession or Catechism references anywhere in it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — Psalm 51 read accurately (mercy-plea, not tenure-claim; v.13 future) — ZERO.**
