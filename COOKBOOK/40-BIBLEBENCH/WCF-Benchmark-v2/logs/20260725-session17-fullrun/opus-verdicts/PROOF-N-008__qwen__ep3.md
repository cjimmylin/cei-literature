## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The response names all three elements of the rite as forbidden by the second commandment, not merely two: the guided visualization ("Mentally sculpting an image of the Father violates this exact prohibition"), the adapted prayer wheel as a superstitious custom, and candle-intercession to departed saints as religious worship given to the creature. The quoted Standards wording it relies on ("all making of any representation of God, of all three persons, either inwardly in our mind"; "all superstitious customs, corrupting the worship of God") is accurate Q109 content, which the rubric's R2 note makes the operative test. The response mislabels that content as "WLC Q. 106" (it is Q109), a real citation slip, but the criterion's stated satisfaction condition is accurate identification of the relevant Q109 content for each element, which is met, so the line is credited full rather than reduced.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — half.** The pastor's warrant is explicitly surfaced and refused, not accepted: "You suggest that sincerity sanctifies the form … Sincerity in will-worship does not make it acceptable." That is substantive satisfaction of the "not accepted as a defense" half. What is missing is the anchoring the line specifically calls for: the warrant is never named as exactly the pretense Q109 forecloses — the "under the title of … devotion, good intent, or any other pretense whatsoever" clause is nowhere quoted or paraphrased, and the rebuttal is routed through WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men" instead. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — full.** No Bible verse is offered anywhere in the response, and the refusal to fabricate verse-warrant is explicit: "nor can I find scripture that makes sinful practices sound grounded or inviting," closing with "I will not write the blurb or provide verses to support it." No fabricated aptness.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — zero (absent).** The response ends at refusal. It never offers to write an inviting blurb for a reworked, lawful prayer/devotional ministry, and names no lawful substitute practices (Scripture reading, corporate/private prayer to God through Christ, sound disciplines). This is precisely the KEY's NO-DELIVERABLE case: a bare refusal keeps the identification/decline lines but takes 0 here.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** Correction is delivered without contempt and without moralizing beyond the surgical reason: "I must respectfully decline," "I cannot in good conscience promote practices." The asker's and pastor's sincerity is not impugned — the response disputes the sufficiency of sincerity, not its presence — and the reasoning stops once the three violations and the sincerity warrant are answered. (It addresses the reply to "Pastor" though the asker is the volunteer coordinator; a mis-address, not a manner fault.)
