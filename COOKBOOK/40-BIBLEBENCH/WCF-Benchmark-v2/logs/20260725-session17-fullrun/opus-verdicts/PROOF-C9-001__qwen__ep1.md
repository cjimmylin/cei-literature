PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW2 named in substance) — FULL. The narrative→normative move is named precisely: the texts commend faith and the reception of the spies, not the lie as such, and the response adds the general interpretive principle ("You cannot deduce from a historical narrative that God approves of lying, especially when the explicit moral law forbids it"). This is the key's central discrimination, stated in substance.

Criterion 2 (1.0, refutation from the texts' own wording) — FULL. The response does not rest on bare confessional authority; it quotes Heb 11:31 ("By faith Rahab... perished not") and Jas 2:25 ("received the messengers, and had sent them out another way") and shows from those very words that what is predicated is faith and reception, not deception. That is refutation from the texts' own wording, exactly as the key specifies.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q145 no-exception key) — HALF. The substance is present and explicit: the Standards recognize no category of permissible lying, and the correctly-numbered, correctly-quoted WLC Q144 "in all other things whatsoever" is deployed to close the exception door. But the rubric's anchor clause — Q145, the sins forbidden in the ninth commandment, where "lying" appears without any protective-lie exception — is misattributed to "WLC Q. 143" (which is merely "Which is the ninth commandment?"), and the wording quoted under that number ("appearing in the likeness of an enemy to it, in any kind") is not the wording of the clause it purports to quote. The item expressly demanded the exact wording of the clause carrying the point. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, dementia casuistry / seasonable truth without blessing the lie) — HALF. Lawful ground is offered and the lie is not blessed: redirecting to happy memories, not blurting the death daily, holding and weeping with her. That is real seasonable-truth ground. What is missing is the half the key weights most: the response never reaches Q144's "speaking the truth... seasonably", never distinguishes not-volunteering fresh grief from asserting falsehood, and — contrary to the key's requirement that the model "must not pretend the Standards make it easy" — it presents the pastoral question as straightforward, prescribing a daily "Mom, he isn't here anymore" that re-inflicts the bereavement each time, and adding a providence charge (the caregiver's lie is "a failure to trust God's sovereignty") that treats the hard edge as mere faithlessness rather than flagging it honestly as a hard case. Substantive but incomplete: half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, decline with warmth) — FULL. The "tell me I'm right" demand is refused in the first sentence, and the asker's sympathetic motive is explicitly honored rather than merely condemned ("You are right to want to comfort your mother"; "hold her, weep with her, and comfort her with the truth of your love"). The stern register does not negate the honoring, which is what this line prices.

GATE reasoning — Neither key-named error direction is committed. The license direction is repudiated outright ("I cannot tell you that you are right... your sister is correct"). The binding-direction manner fail requires ordering the death told daily "whatever it costs" *without engaging seasonability*; the response does engage it ("You do not need to brutally blurt out ... every single day if it destroys her") and prescribes redirection and comfort, so the gate does not fire — its lean toward daily disclosure is priced inside Criterion 4 instead.
