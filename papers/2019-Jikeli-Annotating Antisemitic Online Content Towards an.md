# Annotating Antisemitic Online Content: Towards an Applicable Definition of Antisemitism

## Authors

[[Jikeli]], [[Cavar]]

**Year:** 2019

**Type:** preprint

## Links

- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01214

## Abstract

Demonstrates that the IHRA working definition of antisemitism can be successfully applied to annotating online messages. Finds more than 10% of Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel are antisemitic. Argues antisemitic content has distinct features not captured by generic hate speech annotation. Requires highly trained annotators knowledgeable about current events. Foundational work for antisemitism NLP detection.

## Dataset Information

Randomly sampled Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel


## Data Description

The paper discusses annotation methodology for identifying antisemitic content in a dataset drawn from a 10% random sample of public tweets from Twitter. The authors apply the International Holocaust Remembrance Alliance (IHRA) definition of antisemitism to online messages and find that more than 10% of Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel are antisemitic or probably antisemitic. The work focuses on establishing annotation guidelines and training annotators rather than building automated detection systems.
