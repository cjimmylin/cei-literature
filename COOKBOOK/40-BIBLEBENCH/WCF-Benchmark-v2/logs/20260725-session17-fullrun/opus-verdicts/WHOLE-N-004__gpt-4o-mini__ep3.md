## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — Tom CONVICTED at the end (2 pts): full.**
The response renders an unambiguous terminal conviction ("Tom has sinned against the Tenth Commandment"), and it does so on precisely the Q148 material the key names: settled envy and the wish that the neighbor stumble ("Tom harbors envy and a desire for Aisha to fail"), and the private ledger ("the mental tally of her mistakes"). It also makes the load-bearing point the key demands — that flawless outward grace does not clear the heart — twice: "the commandment prohibits not only outward acts of covetousness but also the deeper heart condition that fuels them" and "inner attitude is sinful, even if his outward behavior seems commendable." Under R2, an accurate paraphrase of the envy / grief-at-the-neighbor's-good content suffices, and this is well past a bare "he seems a bit jealous." Shortfall noted but not fatal at this weight: the tally is cited only as evidence of a disordered heart, never flagged as the beginning of a scheme edging toward C9 harm to Aisha's name — the response stops short of that escalation. Full credit stands because every element the R2 clause makes dispositive is present and the verdict is correct and terminal.

**Criterion 2 — Priya ACQUITTED and affirmed (2 pts): full.**
The acquittal is explicit and stated as a verdict, not a hint: "Priya does not ultimately sin against the Tenth Commandment," with the sting itself expressly cleared — "This reaction itself, while revealing a heart that desired the position, does not constitute sin in the way the commandment warns against." The over-convict guard is fully respected: her honesty is praised rather than punished ("demonstrating humility and a desire for integrity"), and the visibility/admission of the sting is nowhere used against her. The Q147 duty side is affirmed in substance — contentment in her own condition plus charitable joy at the neighbor's good — via "lead her towards a place of contentment and joy for her coworker" and "genuine heart change and contentment in her own calling." Two hedges keep this from being an exemplary acquittal: "does not *ultimately* sin" and "genuine heart change" faintly imply an earlier state that needed repenting, and the duty is affirmed as a happy outcome rather than named as the commandment's positive requirement. These are stylistic residue, not conviction, so the line is earned.

**Criterion 3 — commandment located in the heart, verdicts opposite (1 pt): half.**
The response does render the two verdicts in opposite directions and does say "sin is rooted primarily in decisions of the heart," which is directionally the Q99-rule-2 point. But the scenario asked pointedly "where exactly does the verdict sit — on the outward act, or the heart?", and the response's own closing answer fudges it: "Ultimately, both outward actions and the condition of the heart play a crucial role in rightly assessing one's alignment with God's commands." Its framing throughout is "not only outward acts ... but also the deeper heart condition" — an additive reading that leaves outward conduct as co-equal assessment ground, rather than the key's claim that for this commandment the verdict *sits* on the heart's motions and affections. It also never makes explicit that grading outward behavior would invert both verdicts. Substantively present, incompletely delivered: half.

**Criterion 4 — no over-read on Tom's outward acts (0.5 pts): zero.**
This guard is broken, and doubly. The key stipulates Tom's congratulation is gracious and even true, and requires the fault be pinned to the coveting heart and incipient tally, not to his courtesy. The response instead faults the courtesy itself: "congratulatory actions do not fully align with a genuine love for Aisha or contentment in his own situation, making his outward behavior hypocritical in relation to his inner disposition." Naming the outward behavior "hypocritical" attributes a moral fault to the act, which is exactly the over-read this line exists to catch; the later counsel compounds it by implying his grace was not real ("true grace toward Aisha must come from a heart free from coveting"). Per the required format, a line credited zero for failure to satisfy carries QUOTE: (none); the offending text is quoted here in the justification.

**Criterion 5 — manner, no motive-invention (0.5 pts): half.**
For Tom the response stays strictly inside the stipulated interior facts ("His sleeplessness, resentment, and the mental tally of her mistakes indicate a heart that is not in line with the commandment"), inventing no motives. For Priya it steps slightly past the stipulation: the facts give a sting honestly owned ("that stung — I wanted it"), but the response reports her as "honest with God about her feelings of hurt and jealousy," importing jealousy — an inordinate affection — into the party it goes on to acquit, and adds "genuine heart change" where no change of a sinful state was stipulated. Mild misconstruction of an interior state that Q145 guards against, so half rather than full.
