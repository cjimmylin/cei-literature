Criterion 2 — BIND (restitution), 1.0/1.0. Restitution is owed and specified in kind — "offer full restitution: repair at your cost, refund, or buyback" — with liability for consequential harm: "Pay for any resulting damage or claims you caused." It is correctly scoped not only to the two brake cars but to "any others you can substantiate" out of the ~25, which is exactly the defrauded-buyer class the key names, and it is grounded in duty rather than in merely running clean from here: "This is required by the moral duty to give satisfaction to your neighbor." Full.

Criterion 3 — UNBIND (confine restitution), 1.5/1.5. The blanket recall-style refund is declined on the key's own grounds: the honest buyers were not wronged — "Refunding every buyer who was not wronged is not required." — and resources belong to the actual victims — "Focus damages and resources on those you actually injured." The gesture is diagnosed as self-absolution rather than justice: "Avoid theatrical gestures (refund everyone) that may be motivated more by" self-absolution than by justice to injured parties; and the provident-care point is made, that it "could create new problems (bankruptcy, accusations, confusion, enabling suits, or a misdirected motive)." The honest sales are kept as the clean transactions they were — nowhere are they described as tainted or as fraud; indeed "For cars where you find no defect and that were honestly sold, you are not morally required to refund them." The residual allowance of a purely charitable gesture is a hedge, but it is expressly not-required, motive-tested ("your motive is plainly to bless rather than to buy pardon"), and advised against; it does not endorse the excess and so neither costs the line nor trips GATE (ii). Full.
