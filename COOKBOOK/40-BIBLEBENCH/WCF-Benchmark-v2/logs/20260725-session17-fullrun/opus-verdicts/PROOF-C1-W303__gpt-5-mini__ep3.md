PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW6 named — Ps 82's "gods" as human judges under indictment vv.1–2, 7; John 10:34 read a fortiori) — HALF. The response does correctly identify the referent ("divine language is used of magistrates/judges"), which is the load-bearing half of the criterion. But it supplies none of the psalm's own indicting setting: no v.1 courtroom ("he judgeth among the gods"), no v.2 arraignment ("How long will ye judge unjustly?"), and — most damagingly — no v.7 sentence ("ye shall die like men"), which is the single verse that most decisively unmakes the "you carry divine authority" reading. The John 10 handling is a gesture, not an argument: "Jesus appeals to that usage in John’s defense" never states the a fortiori (if Scripture called mere judges "gods," how much more may the One whom the Father sanctified and sent call Himself Son of God), nor that Christ's point defends His own unique Sonship rather than democratizing deity. The model also weakens the correction by hedging its own claim about Mark 11:23 as a "promising passage" without ever exegeting it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, ≥2 analogy counter-texts) — ZERO. The response supplies not one of Isa 45:5, Isa 42:8, Gen 3:5, or Acts 12:22–23. The only Scripture cited beyond the two proof-texts the questioner already brought is a generic "(Matthew/Mark passages encourage prayer)". Notably absent is Gen 3:5 — "ye shall be as gods" as the serpent's lie — which is the key's sharpest single stroke against this teaching, and Acts 12:22–23, the Bible's own case of a man who accepted the honor of a god. This is an omission of required content, not an unraised topic, so it is scored (absent). Zero.

Criterion 3 (1.0, WLC Q105 supplied; Creator–creature line drawn) — HALF. The Creator–creature line is drawn plainly and anchored in the Standards (WSC Q4, WCF 4.2, WCF 5.1), which earns the second clause. But Q105 is nowhere supplied — not by number and not in substance. The response never names the sin of "ascribing the praise of any good we either are, have, or can do, to… ourselves," never names "presumption, carnal security," and never names the teachers' offense of "making men the lords of our faith and conscience," which is precisely the clause that would free this man's conscience from the church's teaching authority. Instead it counsels him to "point to the Standards' teaching on God's uniqueness" — a doctrinal disagreement framing where the key calls for a first-commandment sin diagnosis. Half.
