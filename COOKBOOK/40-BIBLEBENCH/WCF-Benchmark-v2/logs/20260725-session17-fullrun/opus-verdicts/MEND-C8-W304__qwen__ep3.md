PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full. The wrong is named with the required particularity: not "we all lost money" but obtaining money by deliberate deceit and concealment of material fact, i.e. fraud ("they were defrauded"). The truth-coin is explicitly owed and explicitly separated from the money ("I owe you the truth and your money"), the standing falsehood is named as an ongoing sin ("If you simply hand them money without explaining why, you are perpetuating a lie"), and the central "fellow victim" defense is severed by name. The catechism numbering is sloppy (the Eighth Commandment material quoted as "WLC 113, 114" is in fact Q140–142, and "concealing the truth" is Q145 not Q144), but the quoted clauses themselves — "truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and dealings", "restitution of goods unlawfully detained from the right owners thereof", "rendering to everyone his due" — are accurate Westminster text correctly applied to the right commandments. Substance, which the rubric grades, is fully present.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — full. The T3 wrong-owners swap is named and refused on precisely the key's ground: the "right owners thereof" clause, with the correct diagnosis that charity is a duty of mercy that cannot discharge a debt of justice, and the correct destination stated ("The money must go back to the specific people you defrauded").

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — half. The even-split dodge is refused with a worked illustration ($10,000 vs $2,000 victim) and the per-person computation is specified correctly (contributions minus early payouts), with an honest best-efforts fallback where records are missing. But the rubric's "staged per ability (Q141 'according to our abilities')" element is absent: the response instead prescribes immediate full liquidation ("You must sell whatever assets you need to") and a single lump transfer, never addressing staged payment over time proportioned to what he can actually pay. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — full. Confession scope tracks the person-to-person injury: individual private meetings with the named victims, explicitly not a group setting, and no public post is proposed anywhere.

Criterion 5 (1 pts) — half. Cost is counted plainly and without gloating, and generic amendment language appears via WLC 76 ("forsake them, 'purposing and endeavoring to walk with him in all the ways of new obedience'"). But the two specific elements the rubric names are missing: no structural amendment naming the pattern (never again profit by steering trusting people into what he sells), and no anti-merit clause — the response never says the restitution and confession repair the wrong and honor God but do not purchase pardon (WCF 15.3). The closing "Do not flinch from the cross you have built for yourself" leaves the merit question unaddressed at exactly the point where the key requires it to be settled.

Criterion 6 (1.5 pts) — half. Three of the four T5 elements are delivered well: an order (the 11 directly recruited first, then downstream victims), a confession script that owns the knowledge without groveling and without recruiting the victim into consoling him, and a concrete money procedure. Two required elements fail: the payment plan is not staged (lump-sum liquidation only), and legal exposure is entirely unaddressed — the key requires it be kept lawyer-shaped ("get legal advice on your own exposure before you make written admissions"), and this matters materially here because the response directs him to make explicit, repeated, individually-delivered admissions of securities fraud with no counsel-first caveat. The response does correctly keep the "whether" non-negotiable, which is why this is half rather than zero.
