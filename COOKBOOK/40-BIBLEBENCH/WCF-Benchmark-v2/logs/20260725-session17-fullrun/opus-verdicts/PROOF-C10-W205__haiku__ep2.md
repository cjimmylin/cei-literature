PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW7 named: condition-clause + mechanism-conversion. FULL. The response identifies the mechanism-conversion head-on and in the asker's own terms ("You have turned a psalm into a purchase order"), and names the instrumentalized-delight inversion explicitly — the delight is being fed in as a qualifying payment to bind God ("you're delighting in order to *obligate* God to perform. That's not faith; it's bargaining"). It also states plainly that the promise is being misapplied rather than that the promise is false. Both key error-directions are therefore named in substance. Note for the record (not a deduction under this rubric line): the anchor the rubric suggests, WCF 1.9, is never cited, and the response's actual citations are misattributed — the coveting definition it quotes ("all inordinate motions and affections to that which is our neighbor's") belongs to WLC Q148, not the WLC Q82 it is labeled with, and WLC Q154 is the outward-means-of-grace question, not a question on presumption. The clause-content is right; the reference numbers are wrong.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — context deployed (anti-fret/anti-envy setting; his resentment as self-evidence). HALF. One of the two required elements is present and done well: the asker's resentment is turned back on him as self-convicting evidence ("You've already arrived at resentment toward your neighbor for 'standing in the way.' That's the fruit indicating the root problem"), and the response supplies the Q148 substance about inordinate motions and affections toward what is the neighbor's — precisely the key's "grieving at the good of our neighbour" move, in different wording. But the other half is absent: the psalm's own setting is never deployed. Psalm 37 is an anti-fretting, anti-envy psalm addressed to people watching others prosper ("fret not thyself because of evildoers... rest in the LORD," vv. 1, 7), and the crushing point — that his reading of the psalm has produced in him the very state the psalm was written to cure — is never made. The response gestures at posture ("The psalmist speaks from a posture of trust *regardless of outcome*") but never at the psalm's context or argument. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — delight-as-reordering restored positively (the promise given back, not taken away). HALF. There is a partial positive restoration: delight is redefined toward God Himself rather than toward leverage ("is about joyful submission to His will, not leverage"), and the response leaves room to receive the thing lawfully and thankfully ("If the property becomes available through willing sale, receive it with gratitude"). But the key's actual positive move is missing. The key asks that the promise be handed BACK — that the delighted-in-God heart receives desires now reshaped by that delight, that God does give abundantly, that desires are not evil, and that the correction restores the promise's real comfort (contentment, WLC Q147). The response instead trends toward confiscation: the promise is recast as a test the asker must survive ("Your delight in the Lord must survive the disappointment, or it was never delight in *Him*") and the outcome is framed as God simply being God, with no affirmation of the promise's genuine comfort and no reordering-of-desires exegesis. Half credit for the correct redefinition of delight without the restorative half.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — financial-presumption counseled concretely (the 'moves' unwound). ZERO. The asker states he has "started making financial moves assuming it comes through." The response never addresses those moves at any point — no counsel to unwind them, no warning about the exposure he has taken on, no instruction to his stewardship at all. The counsel section deals only with resentment and with releasing the property spiritually ("Release the lake property to God's actual sovereignty"), which is not the concrete financial unwinding the rubric requires. This is an omission of a required element, so `(absent)` rather than `(none)`.
