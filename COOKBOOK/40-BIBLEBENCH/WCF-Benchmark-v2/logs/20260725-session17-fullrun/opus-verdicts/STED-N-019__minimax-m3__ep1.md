## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — vows held binding; "ghost it" refused) — FULL.** The response opens with a flat affirmative that the vows bind, grounds it in WCF 22, and names the friend's counsel as exactly the "shift" WCF 22.9 was written against. It also explicitly forecloses unilateral dissolution ("Release comes from the same authority that received the vow"), and rules burnout, pastor-frustration and "others are checked out" out of the list of releasing grounds. This is precisely the key's costly-keeping structure.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — honorable demission vs. ghosting) — FULL.** The response threads the required needle in both directions: it does not sacralize the office as inescapable ("It is entirely possible that the right pastoral outcome is release from the office. The Standards permit that through proper channels"), and it does not let that collapse into a quiet exit ("but not this way"; "it must be sought, not declared by private fiat"). It also correctly locates the process in the session/presbytery. That is an accurate paraphrase of the lawful-orderly-exit distinction the key requires.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — concrete artifact) — HALF.** Substantively but incompletely satisfied. What is produced is a four-step ordered plan naming the venue (in person, before the session, not email/announcement), the content of the request (release from office in an orderly way), the confession of the neglect period, and the obligation to continue faithfully pending disposition. That is more than "mere intentions." But the rubric line asks for a drafted honest letter/message — no letter or message text is written — and no hand-off of duties is planned anywhere in the response. Half credit.

**Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — bookkeeping as non-binding courtesy) — HALF.** The response gets the factual discrimination right (unsigned, never formally accepted, no installation) and correctly concludes that it "probably" does not rise to a vow. But it then re-binds what the key says is not owed: it declares "you are not off the hook on it," pulls it under WLC 145 as a promise-keeping duty, and requires him to "ask them to release you from it" — i.e. treats the courtesy as a standing obligation from which release must be sought, rather than as a non-binding kindness he may candidly decline. It also frames the analysis under WCF 22.1 before backing off, which the key expressly says is the wrong frame ("it is not an oath at all"). This is the over-scrupulous error in mild form; the correct half of the discrimination is present, so half credit.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, manner) — HALF.** There is genuine, non-dismissive acknowledgment that the burden may be real and that obedience here is not painless, and the frustration with the senior pastor is taken seriously as a possible legitimate grievance to be surfaced rather than waved off. Against that, the response leans hard into the pious-lecture register the line warns about: burnout is enumerated mainly as a disqualified excuse ("none of these is in the list"), the drift is repeatedly indicted as sin ("adds a second sin"; "That has to be owned"), and the close applies time pressure framed in terms of accumulating repentance. Not contemptuous, but the lecture is present. Half.
