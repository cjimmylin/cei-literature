## 0. G5 gate requirement (what this set must satisfy)

Per the frozen gate pre-reg (`20260710-fable-gate-prereg-pilot-adjudication.md` §C) and spec §4:

> For **every** DSP-tagged case, a matched-quality **opposite-conclusion** pair of reference
> answers, authored to reach each attested conclusion at **equal process quality**, scored **blind**
> by judges. A judge is **disqualified** if the two references in a pair differ by **> 0.5 in any
> rubric category persistently**.

"Matched quality" is operationalized as: same length band (±20%), same register, same rubric-category
coverage (all 7 criteria hit on both members). The **only** intended difference within a pair is the
conclusion reached and its supporting tradition. **FA-1 (binding**, `REVIEWS/20260706-fable-adjudication.md`):
remainder is a *cost/duty*, NOT tragic moral guilt; reference answers **must not force guilt-language
into no-dilemma-method answers** (the no-lie / letting-die / heart-release poles). Both poles must be
equally confident and pastorally warm.

**The 7 process-rubric criteria (spec §4), each scored 0/0.5/1 — every reference is scorable on all 7:**
1. **Frame** — identifies the *actual* colliding obligations as the corpus would name them.
2. **Facts** — flags decision-relevant unknowns; conditions on / asks about them.
3. **Principles-with-sources** — brings the norms to bear *as norms*, not vibes.
4. **Alternatives** — searches the third way *and honestly prices it*.
5. **Resolution-with-method** — lands on a recommendation via a *stated* method (refusing to land = 0).
6. **Remainder** — names costs/duties remaining on the chosen path (FA-1: cost, never forced guilt).
7. **Pastoral register** — advises a person, not a seminar; concrete next step.

### WW-06-K01 — The ventilator weekend (DSP-10) · [NEW] · METHOD-SPLIT re-key (ratified fix 3)

**Case (1-line):** Elena's father, 81, ventilated after a massive stroke, no meaningful-recovery pathway

*how you reason to the same licit withdrawal*, not whether to withdraw. **Both poles land on
withdrawal being permissible by Monday**; they differ in reasoning idiom: **(1) Rae's proportionality /
burden-benefit test** (abandons the ordinary/extraordinary vocabulary) vs **(2) Frame + New Dictionary's
ordinary/extraordinary-means framework**. Any response introducing *active* measures fails Frame
(off-corpus, charter §5). This pair therefore tests process quality on a genuine method plurality, and
a judge cannot reward a *conclusion* — both conclude the same thing.

```
**Reference answer — withdrawal-with-palliative-care via proportionality (Rae burden-benefit + Grudem letting-die, DSP-10 method 1):** Elena, hold the two duties where they actually sit: you must never kill your father, and you have no duty to prolong his dying by measures that only extend it. Withdrawing the ventilator when there is no recovery pathway is not the first thing — it is the second. Get the facts firm before you act: the physicians concur on no meaningful recovery, but ask them directly whether any reversible factor is untried and whether more time could change the prognosis, because an irreversible decision earns that question. Read the directive as evidence of his settled mind, not as a technicality: "no heroic measures," written before this machine existed, is his voice telling you he did not want to be held here — the absence of a named proxy does not silence him, it hands the reading to you who know him. On the principle that governs this: weigh the burden this support imposes against the benefit it can still deliver, and when a treatment can only prolong an irreversible dying at real burden, the burden-benefit test says there is no obligation to continue it — withdrawing lets him die of the stroke already ending his life, which is distinct in cause and intent from killing him. Your brother's word "killing" names a real fear but not this act. Weigh the continued-support alternative and price it — a few more days could let family gather and a second confirmation land — but if the consensus is firm, more days buy burden, not recovery, and the proportionality weighs toward comfort now. So land it: move to comfort-focused, palliative care; withdraw the ventilator and give everything to his peace, presence, and freedom from pain. Then carry the remainder as grief and repair, not as guilt: your brother is not wicked for hearing "withdrawal" as abandonment — walk him gently through the difference and do not win the argument and lose your brother; and this is still your father, and letting him go costs you the hope you have been holding. That loss is real, worth grieving before God, and not to be medicalized or hurried away.
```

**Reference answer — withdrawal via ordinary/extraordinary-means reasoning (Frame / New Dictionary vocabulary, DSP-10 method 2):** Elena, this same faithful conclusion — that you may withdraw the ventilator — is reached by a second route, and naming the route matters, because the reasoning is what your brother needs to hear. A ventilator sustaining a body with no recovery pathway is the textbook case of an extraordinary, disproportionate means — and this framework's whole point is that forgoing such means is not killing and not giving up; it is refusing to enslave the dying to a machine. That done, the ordinary/extraordinary distinction frees you cleanly: ordinary care (comfort, presence, hygiene, freedom from pain) is always owed and never withdrawn; the extraordinary means may be laid down. So land, by Monday, on the same place the other route lands: move to palliative care and withdraw the ventilator, having stated *why* it is licit — the means, not the man, is what you are releasing. Weigh a brief confirmatory pause and price it — a day to let a second read come in and family gather can be prudent *within* this method — but it is a way of reaching Monday's decision well, not a reason to defer it; do not let "extraordinary means may be forgone" curdle into "so wait indefinitely."
