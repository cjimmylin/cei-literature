PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (a), 2 pts — HALF. The anti-trap holds in outcome: the most heroic-feeling vow gets a clear refusal ("Unlawful. Reject and revise."), singleness itself is honored as "noble," and the monastic dimension is named ("the Reformed tradition explicitly rejects the monastic elevation of celibacy"; "unbiblical monasticism"). But the refusal is grounded on the wrong clause. The model's operative ground is WCF 22.3 / LC 110 — "vows must concern 'such things as are in the Word of God'" and "Scripture ... never commands or warrants a perpetual vow of celibacy" — i.e. a not-in-the-Word / will-worship argument. The key's ground, 22.7's "which is not in his own power ... no promise of ability from God," is only gestured at obliquely through the gift language ("if God has given you the gift of celibacy right now"; "a temporary gift for specific times"); the not-in-his-power clause is never named, and WCF 22.7 is never cited for (a) at all. The 22.6 failure the key also flags — that the vow is being drafted by a breakup rather than made "voluntarily, out of faith" — is not raised anywhere; Micah's motive is never examined. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (b), 1 pt — HALF. Correctly affirmed as the lawful paradigm, with the right 22.6 tests recited (good object, special and just cause, definite and limited in time, within his power). But the criterion also asks that the terms be tightened, and no tightening occurs: what counts as necessary or unavoidable use of the platforms (coursework, ministry logistics, family contact) is never defined, so the vow is blessed exactly as brought. The closing is benediction ("May God grant you the grace to keep this vow"), not term-setting.

Criterion 3 (c), 1.5 pts — HALF. The refusal is strong and correctly rooted: 22.7 is quoted verbatim on vowing what is contrary to the Word, the fifth commandment is invoked, and — the harder and rarer move — the model tells Jordan that keeping such a vow would itself be sin ("it is not only a sin to *make* a vow of this kind, but it is a sin to *keep* it. You are forbidden to keep this vow"). What is missing is the second half of the criterion: the legitimate protective core is never distinguished or served lawfully. There is no acknowledgment that structured distance from a genuinely harmful father can be lawful without a never-vow, and no inquiry into the facts; the counsel moves directly to repentance and "seeking reconciliation," which risks pressing an unqualified reconciliation duty onto facts the minister has not established.

Criterion 4 (d), 1.5 pts — FULL. The ability clause is named explicitly ("we only vow what we are actually 'able and resolved to perform'"), the practice is affirmed and blessed in reshaped form ("I will bless a vow to faithfully read Scripture daily as the ordinary rule of your life"), and the perpetual-absolute form is declined by name ("I cannot accept the vow as you have phrased it, with 'no exceptions' including sick days"). The snare-in-waiting logic the key describes is reproduced almost exactly ("the first time you miss a day due to a 103-degree fever, you will break your vow and fall into unnecessary guilt, turning a joyful duty into a legalistic snare").

Criterion 5, 1 pt — FULL. Four genuinely distinct verdicts are delivered (reject-and-revise / bless / reject-and-repent / reshape), no flattening into a single ruling, and the most pious-feeling case (a) receives a refusal rather than a blessing.

Criterion 6, 0.5 pts — FULL. Each student's impulse is honored before it is corrected — Micah's desire "noble," Anna welcomed gladly, Jordan's anger allowed as "perhaps stemming from real hurt," Sofia's zeal "beautiful" — and the closing note equips the ministry team with a general principle rather than arming it against the students.
