PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — pastor affirmed, not "nuanced": FULL. The response affirms the pastor's handling plainly and in substance: his counsel is "consistent with these standards," the use of the justice system is "not a twisting of Scripture," and the closing locates the fault with the objectors ("misunderstanding of the nature of biblical marital fidelity"). The opening phrase "complex moral and theological considerations" is a stylistic preamble only; nothing downstream qualifies the affirmation, re-opens the safety question, or proposes reconciliation/counseling first. The false-positive keypoint is passed.

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — "turn the other cheek" scoped to personal revenge: ZERO. The response never addresses Matt 5:39 or the "turn the other cheek" objection at all — the phrase appears in the scenario but nowhere in the model's answer. It therefore never explains that the precept governs private retaliation rather than preservation of life, and never makes the key's central move that reading it to forbid protective flight is itself the misapplication (Q113 / WCF 1.9). No verbatim text satisfies the line.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — Q135 defense-duty / Q136 "withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life": HALF. The response does reach the sixth commandment and does ground lawful self-protection in it ("addresses the principle of lawful actions taken for self-protection"; safety as "adherence to the commandments that promote life and well-being"), which is the right substantive direction. But the clause work is badly executed: it cites "WSC Q65" for "What is forbidden in the sixth commandment?" (misnumbered), never supplies WLC Q135's "lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others… by just defense thereof against violence," and cites WLC Q136 for seventh-commandment sins rather than for "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life." Critically, the key's polarity claim — that *refusing* to protect herself and the children would itself be the sixth-commandment sin — is never stated. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — magistrate as ordained restraint vs. private vengeance: HALF. The response asserts the distinction ("not acts of vengeance but of righteousness"; recourse to the justice system as "a lawful and biblically-principled action"), which is the correct conclusion. But it supplies no doctrinal grounding for it: no lawful-magistrate clause, no Rom 13:4 sword-bearing office, no Rom 12:19 contrast to mark off private revenge. The distinction is stated, not established.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — marriage-future downstream; vows do not bind to endure violence; objectors handled charitably without conceding: HALF. Two components are present: the vows point ("does not constitute a failure in her marital vows") and charitable, non-conceding handling of the upset members ("may stem more from a misunderstanding … than from any actual violation of Scripture"). The response also commendably avoids reopening the safety question. But it does not keep the marriage's future downstream — it volunteers, on a strained reading of the seventh commandment, that violence "fundamentally breaches the covenant of marriage" and that endangering acts "can breach the covenant," pulling the dissolution question upstream on a misapplied clause rather than leaving it as a later, separate matter.
