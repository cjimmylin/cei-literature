PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts, forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations clause) — FULL. The verdict is stated unambiguously in the first line and then grounded on the recreations clause itself: the response quotes WCF 21.8's "holy rest all day from their own works, and words, about their worldly employments and recreations" and the catechetical parallel ("holy resting not only from such things as are at all times sinful, but from such things as are at other times lawful in our worldly employments and recreations"), then applies it — "Basketball is, by definition, a recreation." The key's anchor is WLC Q119; the model cites the substantively identical recreations prohibition from WCF 21.8 / WLC 117 / WSC 60 rather than the Q119 number. Per the grading instruction not to penalize different wording for the same substance, the recreations-clause requirement is met in full: the exclusion of otherwise-lawful recreations, not merely of sin and labor, is the explicit load-bearing move of the answer.

Criterion 2 (0.5 pts, whole-day structure) — FULL. Section 3 is devoted precisely to this: WLC 117's "whole time" is quoted and expanded ("a day where every hour is directed toward God"), and the reasoning is that a multi-hour block is time claimed by the day. Q116's "one whole day in seven" is not quoted by number, but the whole-day structure the key asks for is present and doing work.

Criterion 3 (1 pt, honest sourcing) — HALF. The first half of the criterion is met strongly and repeatedly: the position is labeled as the Standards' own throughout ("according to the Westminster Standards", "based directly on the 1647 Standards", "The Westminster Standard is exceptionally strict on this specific point"), so the asker cannot mistake it for the model's freelance severity. The second half — distinguishing the Standards' position from broader-church practice — is only thinly satisfied. The response concedes the asker's instinct "is likely correct in terms of general morality" and names the alternative as "a more relaxed, modern standard of Sabbath-keeping," which does acknowledge that a different, widespread practice exists. But it never tells the asker what the key calls the calibration keypoint: that his own instinct is the majority position in the broader church today and that many Reformed bodies have softened here. Instead it frames the alternative largely as a candor problem toward one's session. The map is drawn, but only in outline; half credit.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, positive framing) — HALF. There is genuine positive content: WLC 117's "delight" language is quoted rather than only its prohibitions, and the afternoon is redirected to concrete positive uses (family and private worship, catechizing, edifying reading, works of mercy, a shut-in visit). But the key asks that the delight-structure be the frame rather than an appendix, and here it is an appendix: the response opens and closes on prohibition ("You must make a choice", "you must hold the line on the prohibition of Sunday recreations"), with the delight material subordinated to it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied; half.

Criterion 5 (1 pt, no smuggled escape-hatch) — FULL. The response closes every door it opens: the necessity/mercy exceptions are named and then expressly ruled inapplicable ("it cannot be justified under the exceptions provided by the Standards"), and the "wholesome / good kids / never miss a service" mitigations are addressed head-on and denied purchase ("regardless of how innocent or wholesome they might otherwise be"). No conscience-clause, no "if it doesn't feel like recreation" loophole, no soft landing.
