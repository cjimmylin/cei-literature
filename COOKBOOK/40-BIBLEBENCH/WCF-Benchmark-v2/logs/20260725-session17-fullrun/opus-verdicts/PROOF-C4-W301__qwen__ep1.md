PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW6 named) — FULL. The response performs exactly the relocation the key demands: it identifies the "sabbath days" of Col 2:16 as the Levitical feast-calendar rather than the weekly creation ordinance, keeps v.17's shadow-language intact and applies it to the ceremonial apparatus (quoting WCF 19.3's "which are now abrogated under the New Testament"), and never diminishes the verse. The distinction ceremonial-shadow vs. moral-perpetual is drawn explicitly and correctly.

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts from the analogy of Scripture) — HALF. The response asserts the creation-ordinance counter-argument in substance ("instituted at creation", "rooted in the creation order"), which is the Gen 2:2–3 point, but it supplies no reference for it and marshals none of the NT counter-texts the key lists (Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:1–2, Rev 1:10, Heb 4:9). The rubric requires ≥2 of the named texts; the response effectively offers one, unreferenced. The lone Scripture allusion actually cited (the animal in the pit) serves the necessity-and-mercy point, not the analogy-of-Scripture argument. Substantively begun, materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied) — FULL. WCF 21.7 is quoted at length and accurately, carrying both required freights: the command is "positive, moral, and perpetual… binding all men in all ages," and the day is the one "called the Lord's day… to be continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath." That is the standing moral command moved to the Lord's Day, exactly as the key specifies. (Q116 is not cited, but the rubric line is satisfied by either.)

Criterion 4 (1.0, legalism/Judaizing concession at full strength) — HALF. The response does relocate the freedom rather than deny it: it grants that Paul genuinely pries our fingers off the ceremonial shadows, which concedes the real force of Col 2 and, by implication, Rom 14:5's liberty over ceremonial days. But the key requires the concession at FULL strength — that the friend's target is real, that sabbatarian legalism (the day as a merit-ledger) is itself condemned. The response never makes that concession; it moves immediately to "You are not experiencing Christian liberty," treats Rom 14:5 only as a text the man has misused, and offers no acknowledgment that the danger he fears is a genuine one. Half the criterion earned.

Criterion 5 (0.5, counseled toward rest-and-worship end, warmly) — HALF. The END is correctly named: the response directs him to public and private worship and adds one note of trust in God's provision, which is the nearest it comes to presenting the day as gift rather than burden. The MANNER, however, is predominantly a scolding — "you are violating a perpetual commandment," "it requires strict observance," "at the expense of your soul and your family's spiritual life" — with no warmth toward a man who asked in good faith and no framing of the day's rest as blessing. WCF 21.8 is neither cited nor its substance ("an holy rest, all the day") turned to the man's good. End yes, warmth no → half.

GATE reasoning — Neither named error direction is committed. The license direction is squarely refused ("you are not free to work your crew on Sunday"). On the over-bind side, the response is stern and unwarm, but sternness is not the gated error: it does not make the day a merit-ledger or a ground of standing, and its prescriptions (cease Sunday work; return to public worship; works of necessity and mercy excepted) are the Standards' own plain requirements, not an added yoke. The manner deficit is priced through Criterion 5's half, not through a GATE cap.

GRADE: 73
