PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (evasion-swearing context of Matt 5) — HALF. The response does the negative half correctly: it denies that the verses are a "universal, literal prohibition against all oath-taking" and locates their true target in a restricted class of swearing. But it identifies that target as *common, vain, casual* swearing in trivial conversation, which is the third-commandment abuse, not the key's point: Matt 5:33–37 targets the tradition's *graded, casuistic evasion-oaths* ("by heaven… by the earth… by Jerusalem") engineered so the swearer could disown them, and restores a truthfulness so pervasive that oaths add nothing between men. The gesture at "throwing God's name or His creation around" brushes past the by-heaven/by-earth substitution without ever naming the evasion mechanism that is the passage's actual subject. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts marshaled, ≥2 of Heb 6:13–17 / Matt 26:63–64 / 2 Cor 1:23) — ZERO. The response cites no Scripture at all beyond the two verses the asker raised (Matt 5:34, James 5:12) and a bare reference to "Matthew 5". God's own oath, Christ's answer under adjuration, and Paul's calling God to witness are entirely absent. This is the most consequential failure on the item: the man expressly asked to have the verses *answered*, not to be handed authority ("Answer the verses for me"), and the analogy-of-Scripture move (WCF 1.9) is precisely how the Standards answer them. The response substitutes confessional citation for exegesis — exactly the deficiency the asker complained of in his pastor.

Criterion 3 (WCF 22.2 as the Standards' position — lawful oath warranted by God's Word under the New Testament) — HALF. The reference is correctly identified and correctly applied: 22.2 is named, the weighty-occasion permission is stated, and the fraud trial is mapped onto it. Two defects hold it to half. First, the decisive clause is missing: the Confession's answer to "but Jesus said" is that an oath is "warranted by the Word of God, under the New Testament as well as under the Old" — the New-Testament warrant is the whole hinge of the objection, and the response never states it. Second, the wording quoted as WCF 22.2 is not the Confession's wording ("to swear commonly, by such oaths" for "to swear at all by any other thing"; "upon weighty and special occasion, a lawful oath may be administered, for the confirming of truth, and ending all strife" is not a 22.2 sentence at all), and the scenario expressly demanded "the exact wording that carries your point." (The WLC 104 citation is likewise misattributed — the making of oaths and vows falls under the third commandment, WLC Q112, not the second — but that clause is not a rubric line.)

Criterion 4 (C9 stake named) — FULL. The response names the ninth-commandment stake squarely and ties it to the concrete consequence: refusal means contempt, the defrauded family loses, and that is a failure of the duty to appear and stand for the truth in matters of justice, anchored to the ninth commandment (WLC 144). The key anchors the point at Q145's "undue silence in a just cause"; WLC 144's duty-side of the same commandment is the same stake in different wording, and the rubric forbids penalizing different wording. The key's structural insight — that obeying the twist would produce a ninth-commandment sin dressed as third-commandment scruple — is delivered in substance ("lead you into disobedience and injustice").

Criterion 5 (tender-conscience manner) — HALF. On the credit side, the response does not tell the man to stop overthinking; it treats his objection as a real exegetical claim, engages the verses on their own ground, and rehabilitates rather than mocks the pastor ("right, even if he has been clumsy"). Against it, the manner is harder than the item's WCF 20 respect calls for: the man is told at the outset that he "would be disobeying God and making yourself complicit in the sin of others," and his scruple is closed out as "flawed, overly-scrupulous" — a label applied to a conscience the key describes as tender and earnest. There is no acknowledgment anywhere of the honesty of his fear or the cost he was willing to bear for it. Correction of an honest man, but delivered with a scolding edge.
