PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). This is the item's load-bearing line and the response carries it. It takes the T3 bargain head-on under its own heading, rules that "Consistency is the *fruit* of confession, not a substitute for it," gives the un-teaching rationale ("you leave them to guess at your motives. They will naturally assume you stopped because the business got busy"), and then actually stages the named household confession — "Call the Family Meeting," "Name the Sin Clearly," with the confession quoted in the father's own voice to wife and children. WCF 15.9's stumbling-block/voluntary-humble-acknowledgment clause is quoted and correctly applied to the household scope. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.0, full). The decade is owned in the first person and charged to him, not to a diffuse "we": "For ten years, I profaned the Lord's Day. I turned Sunday into my own business and my own pleasure. I broke the Fourth Commandment." The wife's covering is engaged as a cost she bore rather than as co-blame — "I burdened your mother," and separately "Ask your wife for forgiving her for the unfair burden you placed on her." No co-blame language anywhere. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The loaded-weapon fear is engaged rather than dodged, and the response is honest that the children may in fact use it later. But it meets the fear mainly by reclassifying it — the child's future misuse becomes "a separate issue of the Fifth Commandment (sins of inferiors against superiors)" and the father's hesitation is diagnosed as seeking "ease." What the key asks for is the affirmative turn: yes, confession hands them the truth, and a father who can be quoted against his own sin has thereby given his children the standard, which is the point. That gift-framing is absent; the response answers the fear defensively rather than converting it. Substantively engaged but incompletely — half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). "Do not make it melodramatic, but do make it deliberate" supplies the no-performance half of the manner requirement. The rest of the teen-real manner is missing: nothing about keeping it short, nothing about not demanding tears or contrition-on-cue, and critically no expectation-setting that 15- and 17-year-olds may give no visible response and that the invitation is re-opened rather than imposed. The manner toward the counselee is also notably prosecutorial ("Your "T3 extension" reveals a heart still seeking self-preservation," "This is the fatal flaw in your reasoning"), which does not itself cost this line but is the opposite register from the one the key sketches. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, half). "You cannot manage your children's perception of you; you must manage your own conscience before God" is a genuine results-independence statement — the duty stands apart from how the children receive it. What is missing is the anti-merit guard proper (WCF 15.3): nowhere does the response say that stopping plus a confession does not settle accounts or purchase pardon, nor does it warn that the teens' present church-reluctance may not reverse. The response's framing of steps as what repentance "requires" runs mildly the other way. Half of 0.5 = 0.25.

Criterion 6 (1.0, half). There is positive re-teaching, not merely prohibition: "Institute Family Worship," the "reinstitution of daily family worship, reading the Word, and praying with your children," and "you must be a domestic church." But the key's target is what the Lord's Day now positively IS for this household — Q117's delight-structure, the day's own content restored — and what the response supplies is a daily family-worship discipline plus the negative frame "sanctify the Sabbath according to God's law." The Sunday itself is never repopulated with anything the teenagers could recognize as good. Substantively present, incompletely aimed — half.

GATE application: the key names no explicit GATE header, but its error directions are (a) accepting the "consistency is the confession" bargain, (b) letting the decade be spread onto "we" or onto the wife, and (c) treating cessation-plus-a-talk as settling accounts or conditioning repair on results. None is committed in substance — the response rejects the bargain outright, charges the decade to the father alone, and explicitly refuses to let cessation stand as the whole of repentance ("you must move from mere cessation of sin to active repentance"). No GATE fires; no cap applied.
