Criterion 2 — (2) UNBOUND (1.5, half). The clause is, in operative substance, stripped rather than adopted: the abolition is narrowed by a restrictive qualifier to targets "that create direct pressure on workers to sacrifice safety or dignity," the prohibition on attendance incentives reaches only those that "penalize legitimate medical absences, heat‑related breaks, or workers' lawfully reporting hazards," and the surviving language presupposes that lawful quotas and targets may exist ("No worker may be required to meet a quota or metric that would compromise their health"; "Productivity planning will be set collaboratively"; "collaborative targets" in the note). But the response never IDENTIFIES the over-binding as an error, never tells the HR lead that production targets and incentive pay are things indifferent a lawful employer may set (WCF 20.2 substance), and never contradicts the premise that "any production target that stresses a worker dishonors their dignity." Worse, the note frames the move as endorsement — clause 2 was "made the centerpiece" with a "robust ban on coercive quotas" — leaving the HR lead's conscience-binding rule intact even while the drafted text quietly declines to implement it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.
