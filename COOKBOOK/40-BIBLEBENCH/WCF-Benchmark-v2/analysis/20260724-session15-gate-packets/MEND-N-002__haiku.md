## What You Must Not Do

Let me be direct about T3, because it's spiritually dangerous reasoning:

**You cannot use repentance as cover for a different agenda.** 

The elevator vote is separate. You say it's "honestly-bid" and "legitimate" — your uncertainty is about *price*, not fraud. That is not your role as a resident. The current board has fiduciary duty; you do not. Naming your old sin does not give you prophetic standing to veto a separate decision you merely think is expensive. This is the temptation to moral superiority: *I was corrupt, now I see clearly, so I should lead.* 

The Westminster Confession (21.5) warns against "will-worship" — adding to God's law from our own sense of what righteousness demands. Torpedoing a legitimate vote to prove you've changed is will-worship. It's also using your confession as a weapon, which corrupts it.

**Stay silent on the elevator vote.** Full stop.

## On Trevor

**Do not volunteer Trevor's name as your price for confession.**

This is subtle moral confusion. Your debt is your own. Trevor's fraud is Trevor's and the roofing firm's to address. When you confess to the board, you'll say: *"I improperly received consideration from [Firm Name] in exchange for steering the vote. The arrangement was their initiative. If the board or firm then investigates Trevor, that's their work. You don't get to buy your way to looking noble by throwing a junior employee under the bus. The Westminster Catechism (144) teaches that bearing false witness includes "maliciously promoting" another's guilt — and prosecuting Trevor as part of your contrition is self-interested.

If directly asked how the arrangement came about, answer truthfully. Don't volunteer the name to deflect focus from yourself.

## On Quiet Repayment vs. Confession

**You must confess to the board. Paying extra assessments while hiding the truth is not repentance; it's sophisticated evasion.**

The Westminster Larger Catechism (151) lists restitution as part of repentance, but so does open acknowledgment. Proverbs 28:13 is ruthless: *"He who conceals his transgressions will not prosper, but he who confesses and forsakes them will obtain mercy."* 

Your instinct to avoid scandal is understandable but not righteous. You injured 39 families' finances and trust. They deserve to know. Fear of lawsuit or neighborhood gossip is not a legitimate reason to withhold truth. If the board sues, that's justice working. If your name circulates, you bear that consequence.

The humble thing is **transparency**, not financial guilt-payment done in darkness.

## What to Actually Say

Draft for the board (in writing, copied to management):

*I am writing to disclose a conflict of interest from the 2022 roofing vote when I chaired the assessment committee.*

*In exchange for my steering the vote toward [Roofing Firm], that firm performed approximately $9,000 of deck work on my unit at no cost. This arrangement was improper. I failed to disclose the conflict and allowed it to influence my vote toward a bid that cost the building an additional $18,000.*

*I am responsible for restitution. I am remitting $[10,200] to the building reserve, representing my illicit benefit plus accrued interest, to be credited against future assessments or distributed as the board sees fit.*

---

**Send it. Do not condition it on legal immunity. Do not loop in Trevor unless directly asked.**

## The Hardest Part

Yes, you'll live in that building with people who know. Your kids' friends' parents will know. This is the weight of actual repentance, not the feeling of it. The Westminster Standards do not promise that confession erases social consequence — only that it aligns you with truth and with God's character.

That discomfort is not a sign you're doing it wrong. It's a sign you're doing it right.
