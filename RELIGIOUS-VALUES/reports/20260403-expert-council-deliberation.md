## Council Overall Assessment

### Strengths
- Strong Islamic benchmark coverage (Quranic QA ecosystem, inheritance law, fatwa classification)
- Emerging Christian theology benchmarks (FaithBench, Gospel Coalition)
- Growing multi-religion evaluation frameworks
- Multiple format types represented (MCQ, MRC, RAG, scenario, psychometric)

### Weaknesses
- Heavy Abrahamic bias (11/15 selected)
- Most benchmarks test knowledge recall rather than theological reasoning
- Limited denominational depth (most treat traditions monolithically)
- Citation counts generally low (field is young, most papers 2024-2025)

### Recommendations
1. **Prioritize Tier 1 implementation** — benchmarks with public data, MCQ/MRC format, and existing harness compatibility
2. **Invest in gap-filling** — commission or find Hindu, Buddhist, Confucian, and Ubuntu benchmarks
3. **Distinguish knowledge from reasoning** — test both factual recall AND theological reasoning ability
4. **Add denominational depth** — future benchmarks should test within-tradition diversity
5. **Build multi-tradition evaluations** — single-tradition benchmarks risk siloing; need cross-tradition comparisons
