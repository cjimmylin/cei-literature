PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW6 named; Ps 82's "gods" = human judges under indictment (vv.1–2, 7); John 10:34 a fortiori. HALF. The response correctly identifies the "gods" as "human magistrates acting as God's representatives" rather than an ontological grant of divinity, and reads John 10:34 correctly as Jesus defending "His own divine Sonship, not to teach that His followers become deities" — the a-fortiori substance without the label, which is acceptable. What is missing is the psalm's own decisive setting, which the key makes the load-bearing half of this line: the "gods" are being arraigned for unjust judgment (vv.1–2) and sentenced to die "like men" (v.7). That indictment/sentence context — the single most powerful internal refutation of the license reading — is nowhere in the response, and no verse of Ps 82 beyond the quoted v.6 is engaged. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — analogy counter-texts (≥2 of Isa 45:5 / Isa 42:8 / Gen 3:5 / Acts 12:22–23). ZERO. The response cites no Scripture outside the three texts the questioner himself supplied (Ps 82, John 10, Mark 11). None of Isa 45:5, Isa 42:8, Gen 3:5 as the serpent's lie, or Acts 12:22–23 appears; the whole analogy-of-Scripture move is replaced by Westminster quotation. WCF 1.9 is not invoked either. Omission of a required element → (absent), zero.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q105 supplied (self-ascription / presumption / making a man lord of conscience) with the Creator–creature line drawn. HALF. The Creator–creature line is drawn explicitly and repeatedly and is the response's strongest section (WCF 7.1 on "the distance between God and the creature", WSC Q4 on the incommunicable attributes, "you remain a creature"). But WLC Q105 is never supplied, and none of its three named heads is named as such: self-ascription of the praise of any good, presumption/carnal security, or making men the lords of our faith and conscience. The nearest substance ("The guilt your church is placing on your shoulders is a false burden") gestures at the conscience-lordship point without reaching it doctrinally. Half of the line earned.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — false guilt lifted; the seizures are not the father's faith-verdict; petitionary trust restored. FULL. This is the response's best-executed line. It states flatly that "Your son's seizures are not a failure of your faith," repeats it under "Drop the Guilt" ("You have not failed"), names the guilt as an imposed "false burden," and denies the mechanism directly ("God is not waiting for you to properly access a divine nature to heal your son"). Petitionary posture is positively restored, not merely negated: WLC Q180 is quoted, the "for things agreeable to his will" clause is pressed, and the man is directed to a church that will "pray for him according to God's will." The one gap against the key is the ordinary means of medicine, which goes unmentioned; that is a sub-clause, not the substance of the line, and the headline duty — lifting the false guilt and restoring the creature's petitionary posture — is fully discharged.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — concession at full strength: bold believing prayer and the longing for healing affirmed, not deflated. HALF. Prayer is not deflated to resignation — the man is pointed to a church that will "weep with you over your son's seizures, pray for him according to God's will." But the key asks for the concession at FULL strength: the good desire under the error honored, God's invitation to bold believing prayer affirmed, the longing for the boy's healing named as right. The response's treatment of Mark 11:23 is purely deflationary ("a figure of speech for overcoming immense spiritual obstacles"), and nowhere does it tell the father that his hunger for his son's healing is good or urge him to keep asking boldly. The correction relocates the hope but does not restore the boldness. Half.
