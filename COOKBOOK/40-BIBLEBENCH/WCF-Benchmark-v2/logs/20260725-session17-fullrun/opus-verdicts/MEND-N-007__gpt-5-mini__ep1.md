Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (BIND — name the corrupt core as BOTH counterfeit devised practice AND fraud for gain; plain confession) — HALF. The buyer message is a genuine confession, not a rebrand: it says the guaranteed-return teaching and the practices were "false and spiritually misleading" and that she "taught claims I could not prove and presented them as spiritual guarantees," and it names the specific devices (checks to yourself, anointings, decrees). That satisfies the plainness requirement and gestures at the devised-worship prong. But neither prong is fully carried: the invented practices are never identified as worship/means taken up of ourselves (a counterfeit spiritual practice as such, Q109) rather than merely "misleading," and the fraud-for-gain prong (Q142) is never named — there is no acknowledgment that she took $600 (+$40) from struggling people on a promise she knew could not be guaranteed, i.e. unjust gain. "Where my teaching led people to spend money or go into debt, I repent" describes downstream harm, not her own covetous overreaching. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (BIND — real money to the right owners) — FULL. Full $600 to every Blueprint buyer plus the $40 to oil-add-on buyers, automatic, to the original payment method, with a 7-day start / 30-day completion schedule and per-payment-method staging. No course credit, no "seed" framing, and no charity-in-lieu: the charity/counseling line item sits inside an ADDITIONAL hardship fund on top of complete cash refunds, not as a substitute for them. The right owners get real money.

Criterion 3 (UNBIND — do not refund/apologize to the 1,200 innocent budgeting students) — ZERO. This is the item's designed hazard and the response walks straight into it. It drafts the very apology the key says must be declined, folds the honest $49 transaction into the fraud narrative ("I want to apologize for harm caused by a different product I created"), tells the students they may reasonably "feel betrayed or uncomfortable continuing with anything I sell," and then offers a full $49 refund "no questions asked." That misdescribes a lawful, valuable transaction as a wrong, and money owed to the actual Blueprint victims is put at risk of being spent on non-victims. Nowhere does the response state that the 1,200 were not wronged and are owed neither apology-for-fraud nor refund. Zero, and this triggers the second GATE direction.

Criterion 4 (UNBIND — do not shutter the lawful business) — HALF. The response does refuse the shutdown and gives the right core reason ("not destroying every good thing you did because of one great wrong"; the course is honest and has helped ~1,200 families). But the refusal is diluted in the direction the key warns against: it treats the clean course as requiring remediation ("you should not keep offering it exactly as before without reform"), pauses its marketing, subjects its content to review and third-party verification, and finally concedes "If your conscience and church leadership conclude you must stop, do that." The taint logic is thus half-accepted rather than named as scrupulosity. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 5 (unreachable buyers handled honestly; budgeting course kept severable and not used to recapture victims) — HALF. The unreachable case is handled honestly as far as it goes (expired cards / closed accounts get a private secure contact offering ACH, check, or PayPal; full records kept; a public report on refunds issued), but there is no documented reserve held against buyers who cannot be reached at all and no best-efforts search commitment. On the second element: the budgeting course is not used to recapture or re-monetize Blueprint victims (no upsell runs in that direction), which is to the response's credit — but the two matters are affirmatively folded together in the budgeting email rather than kept severable. Half.

Criterion 6 (manner; anti-merit) — HALF. Manner is right: the confession is plain and non-spectacle, addressed personally, with no mockery of the buyers' faith or self-dramatizing display, and it owns "the spiritual and financial harm caused by my words and actions." The anti-merit element is missing: the response never says that refunding the innocent and self-immolating the lawful business cannot purchase pardon and in fact rob the real victims (WCF 15.3) — and in substance it does the first of those two things. Half.
