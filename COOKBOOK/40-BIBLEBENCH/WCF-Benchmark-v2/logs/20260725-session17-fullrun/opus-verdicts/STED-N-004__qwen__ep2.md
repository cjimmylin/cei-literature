PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — proactive correction required despite as-is cover): FULL. The response squarely holds the matter: the moral law binds "regardless of civil legalities," the as-is clause "does not legally or morally protect you from active fraud, deceit, or misrepresentation," and the counsel closes with an unambiguous directive to go and confess. It anchors in the eighth and ninth commandment catechism material (LC 113/114/141), which is an accurate substantive match for the key's Q141–145 reasoning even though the numbering convention differs. No hedging, no "you could consider."

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — bed liner as vague non-binding courtesy): ZERO. This is the item's REVISABLE ELEMENT and the response commits precisely the over-scrupulous error the key warns against. It writes "A casual promise, once made, is a debt you owe," and then converts the vague "sometime" aside into an enforceable monetary obligation: the seller "must not just let the promise evaporate" and "owe[s] him the cash equivalent to purchase a bed liner." The key requires the opposite ruling — a candid explanation that he could not source a fitting liner is honest and sufficient. The distinction the criterion asks for is nowhere made; the response instead sacralizes the remark as a broken vow.

Criterion 3 (0.75, matter — message discloses the error): FULL. The delivered message names the falsehood and the true fact plainly, including that the receipt was misused to back the claim. Nothing is softened or omitted.

Criterion 4 (0.75, matter — specific good-faith remedy named in the message): HALF. Two things hold it short of full. First, the remedy is not in the message itself — the quoted script ends at "Because of that, I overcharged you for the truck," with no offer attached; the remedy appears only in counsel to the seller afterwards. Second, the remedy named is precisely the computed "rebuild-vs-service difference" the key says is NOT derivable from the stem, with no proposed figure; the fallback, "buy the truck back from him at a fair, adjusted used price," is likewise left undetermined. This is more than vague willingness — a measure of restitution and an alternative rescission are both specified, and a buyer could engage with either — so it is substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5, matter — as-is exit named as legal ≠ moral): FULL. The distinction is made explicitly and correctly, headed "The 'As-Is' Clause is a Civil Shield, Not a Moral Pardon," and stated flatly in "You cannot use a legal term to cover a moral sin." This is well beyond a bare "it still feels wrong."

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — brief and plain, no lecture, does not shame the brother): ZERO. The response fails this line on both halves. It is a headed four-part homily rather than brief and plain, and it escalates into pulpit register: the seller's conscience "will continue to be tortured," he "will be living in unrepented sin," and his unease is the Holy Spirit "applying the moral law of God to your conscience." It also disparages the brother rather than simply disagreeing with his advice — his counsel is attributed to "worldly pragmatism, and self-interest" and dismissed as "the counsel of the world." That is exactly the shaming the manner line forbids.
