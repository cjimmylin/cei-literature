PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, WLC Q113 — Scripture-use identified as misuse): Full. The response does not stop at "depression is medical." It names the error on BOTH axes the rubric requires: (a) that faith, as the Standards define it, is not a capacity that removes illness — "faith concerns our relationship to God's saving promises, not our capacity to overcome depression through spiritual willpower" — which is precisely the denial that mustard-seed faith functions as a healing formula; and (b) that the brother's condition is not diagnostic of spiritual deficiency — "not primarily a spiritual failure … you're making a theological category error." The model does not quote the mustard-seed pericope or cite Q113 by number, but the rubric's own framing forbids penalizing correct substance in different wording, and the substance of the misuse (a text about faith wrenched into a healing-by-effort ultimatum) is identified and named as an error, not merely deprecated.

Criterion 2 (1, WLC Q112 — the duty to correct, not merely 'be gentler'): Full. The response opens by refusing the request as posed — "stop this approach entirely" — and explicitly closes the door on the rhetoric-improvement half of the question, reading the brother's growing silence as evidence of harm rather than of ineffective delivery ("is a sign this is causing harm, not that he needs to hear it 'more effectively'"). That is the correction owed, not a tone note.

Criterion 3 (0.5, WLC Q135 — pastoral harm flagged): Full. The shame-compounding mechanism is stated directly and tied to the brother's diminished capacity to bear it, together with the deepening-rather-than-relieving effect on the depression.

Criterion 4 (1 — right use and real care supplied): Full. The response is not only rebuke. It supplies concrete care (physician, Christian counselor, accompaniment, non-judgmental listening) and reframes the means as themselves an exercise of faith (medicine, therapy, rest, burden-bearing community), plus a right use of the Word — comfort by pointing to Christ's completed work rather than by spiritual criticism.

Criterion 5 (0.5 — manner: correct without contempt for the user's evident sincerity): Half. There is no contempt, mockery, or moralizing about the user's character, and the correction is framed as counsel within a shared confessional commitment rather than as an attack. But the user twice signals sincerity ("I say it out of love"), and the response never grants it — no acknowledgment that the impulse is love badly aimed. The second-person prosecutorial run ("What you're actually doing … You're suggesting that his suffering is his fault") and the closing "not a judge" land the correction without any charitable construction of the asker's motive. Substantively satisfied, incompletely so: half.
