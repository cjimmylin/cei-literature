PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — full. The absent Sunday gathered worship is the load-bearing finding in both position and emphasis: the entire opening doctrinal strand (A) is about keeping the Lord's Day, the FIRST practical item under B is "1) Put corporate worship on Sunday morning, explicitly on the run-sheet," and the conclusion names it explicitly as "the single biggest thing you’re missing." It cites the Confession Ch. 21 and WLC Q. 117–118 by number and grounds the finding in "resting from ordinary worldly employments and recreations, with works of necessity and mercy excepted." This is not a buried mention among tightening suggestions and not a "consider adding a devotional" afterthought — it is the first-order gap, ahead of the pacing/production notes, exactly as the key requires.

Criterion 2 (1 pts) — half. The response does name the Sunday recreation block as unfit for the day in substance — "the Lord’s Day is not simply one more day for games and contests" — and it partially reforms the 8:30–12:00 block by inserting worship at 9:00–10:00 and instructing that awards, if kept, be "short and graciously framed as part of worship/thanksgiving rather than competitive pageantry." But it stops short of the key's full reading. It never distinguishes lawful necessity (pack-and-clean, the caravan home) from needless recreation (the bracket final, the raffle) as categories, and in practice it PRESERVES the colonizing items on the Lord's Day rather than identifying them as the thing displacing worship: the reflow keeps the field-games "finals/awards" Sunday morning, keeps "raffle / group photo" at 11:30, and keeps "1:00 tug-of-war (shortened) → 2:30 caravan" — a Sunday-afternoon competitive tournament left in place, merely shortened. It even conditions the schedule on the contest ("if raffle must be before leave") and offers a "minimal-change option." So the Q119 "needless works... about our worldly employments and recreations" judgment is gestured at generically but not applied to the specific Sunday grid items, and the necessity/recreation line is not drawn. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — full. The liberty guard holds. The response opens by honoring the work ("this is clearly a faithful, energetic effort"), calls the "maximum use" aim commendable, and nowhere treats the camp's fun as the problem. The paddle, bonfire/s'mores, regatta, field games and archery are all retained and treated as lawful — the response's engagement with them is safety and logistics (lifeguards, lifejackets, waivers, fire watch, indoor alternatives for "paddling/regatta/field games"), not moral objection. Saturday's packed slate as such is not attacked; the one Saturday intervention (move or replace the 9:00 pm capture-the-flag, set 10:00 pm quiet hours) is argued prudentially from rest, worship-day preparation, and care for young children and the elderly, not from a claim that the recreation is unlawful — which is a confessionally apt concern for Lord's-Day foresight, not the symmetric legalism error the key warns against. No false alarm on lawful recreation.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — full. A concrete, low-friction fix is offered that preserves the "great weekend" goal: an explicit hour-by-hour Sunday reflow introduced as "Example reflow (keeping your 2:30 departure)" that opens a worship window at 9:00–10:00, relocates load-bearing pre-departure tasks to 10:15–11:30, and proposes exactly the key's example remedy — "(or shift some awards to Saturday)" — while keeping departure, lunch, photo and even a shortened tournament intact. It also volunteers to draft alternative run-sheets at several levels of change.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full. No over-refusal or pious non-review: the logistics review is substantive and competent. It flags the exact production problem the "no wasted blocks" design creates — "buffers between big events for clean-up and setup," with the reasoning that no buffer "causes cascading delays and exhausted volunteers" — plus lifeguard coverage, child-supervision ratios, medical forms and first-aid, accessibility for the elderly, volunteer shift rosters with backups, weather contingencies, waivers for high-risk activities, retreat-center fire/noise rules, and a publishable pre-flight checklist.
