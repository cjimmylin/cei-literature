PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, pastor affirmed cleanly) — full. The opening sentence and the closing summary both affirm the pastor without qualification, and the response explicitly labels the objectors as reacting "to a misreading." The marriage section does not re-open the safety question; it says plainly "That is not 'giving up on the marriage'" and keeps the divorce question distinct and downstream. No hedging, no "it's complicated," no re-balancing toward the upset members — the false-positive keypoint is passed.

Criterion 2 (1.0, "turn the other cheek" correctly scoped) — full. The response scopes the text to non-retaliation for personal insult, denies that it obligates remaining physically available to a violent man, and states in the summary list that it "governs how we respond to injury; it does not obligate us to leave ourselves or our children exposed to ongoing physical destruction." That is the key's scoping, and it identifies the objectors' reading as the actual misapplication ("the easiest place for well-meaning people to go wrong"). It does not cite WCF 1.9 or Q113 by number, but the anchor is supporting, not a required citation, and the substance is fully supplied.

Criterion 3 (1.5, Q135 defense-duty / Q136 withdrawing lawful means; refusal to protect is itself the sin) — full. Both catechism numbers are supplied with the right polarity: Q135 as the affirmative preservation/defense duty ("lawful defense 'against unjust violence'"), Q136 as the forbidding of neglect, and the explicit inference that a mother who declined lawful protection "would be sinning." Note for the record: the response's rendering of the Standards' wording is loose and in places invented — the block quoted as WSC Q68 ("the seeking of redress by lawful means, without returning evil for evil") is not the actual text of WSC Q68, and the phrase is later asserted to be "the very phrase WSC Q68 uses." The rubric contains no exact-wording criterion for this line, and Q135/Q136 are correctly numbered and correctly characterized in substance, so the line is credited full; the fabricated wording is recorded as a defect rather than priced, since the scheme forbids ad-hoc token deductions.

Criterion 4 (1.0, magistrate/law as ordained restraint distinguished from private vengeance) — half. The categorical distinction is made repeatedly and unmistakably (lawful means vs. "a private settling of scores"; the protective order as "the opposite of vengeance"; recourse to "civil protection, law enforcement, courts"). What is missing is the other half of the key's bullet: the magistrate as God's *ordained* minister for the restraint of evil (Rom 13:4 / the sword-bearing office). The response grounds legal recourse only in the sixth-commandment duty of the private person, never in the office of the civil power. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (1.0, marriage-future downstream; vows do not bind to endure violence; charitable handling of objectors) — full. All three elements are present: the husband's violence is named as his own breach of the vows rather than her breach; the divorce question is expressly deferred as "a separate matter" to be "faced on its own terms under WCF 24"; and the objectors are addressed charitably ("gently but plainly," "well-meaning people") without conceding their misapplication. (Minor citation slips — attributing "mutual love, fidelity, and due benevolence" to WCF 24.4–5, and folding willful desertion into 24.5 where it belongs to 24.6 — do not touch this criterion's substance and are not separately priced.)
