PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (c) professed poverty (2 pts): HALF. The response does discourage the vow and honors the anti-Mammon impulse, redirecting Daniel to sustained generosity ("he might commit to giving a significant portion of his income rather than pledging lifelong poverty"), which is the key's redirect. But it never refuses to witness — it only calls the vow "problematic" and suggests an alternative "might be a better approach," and in the summary it lumps Daniel with the merely-needs-reflection cases. It also misses the decisive confessional ground entirely: no recognition that "professed poverty" is named verbatim in WCF 22.7 among the "superstitious and sinful snares," no "not in his own power" analysis (future provision, dependents), and no Q141 provident-care / provision-for-household duty. The anti-trap is only softly held, not held with the hardest no. Substantive but materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 — (d) medicine refusal (1.5 pts): FULL. This is the response's strongest treatment. It issues a clear negative disposition ("It would be prudent to counsel Malik against such an extreme vow"), grounds it in the substance of Q135 — lawful endeavors and appointed means for preserving life, with physic explicitly among them ("the use of means for healing," "the role of medical treatment as a means through which God can work") — and states the duty of prudential care of the body. It honors the trust-in-God impulse and redirects it to the use of means, exactly the key's counsel. The "tempting God" label is not used, but the key forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is delivered; the duty-hindering structure and the redirect are both present. Credited full.

Criterion 3 — (a) reshape (1.5 pts): HALF. The reshape verdict itself is delivered rather than a blessing, and the sobriety impulse is honored ("the intention to seek holiness and avoid drunkenness is commendable"). But the reasoning is misplaced relative to the key: the key's problem is the *form* — a perpetual absolute vow for "the performance whereof he hath no promise of ability" plus a shame-driven (22.6, not "voluntarily, out of faith") motivation — while total abstinence as a personal discipline is fine and even wise. The response instead faults the *practice* as "overly severe" and steers Cole toward moderate drinking with "discernment in situations where alcohol is present." It never names the no-promise-of-ability problem, never diagnoses the rash/shame motivation, and never recommends the key's remedy of a definite, revisitable season or accountability in place of a lifelong oath. It does at least flag the legalism risk. Half.

Criterion 4 — (b) witness (1 pt): HALF. The correct verdict is delivered unambiguously ("it would be appropriate to witness and bless her commitment") and the giving/stewardship duty is gestured at. But the lawfulness *test* that makes this the paradigm case is absent: no note that the vow is definite and temporary (a fixed sum, this quarter), no note that it is within her power, and no tightening of terms. Correct outcome, thin reasoning → half.

Criterion 5 — no flattening (1 pt): ZERO. Four distinct verdicts are not delivered. Only two dispositions actually emerge — bless Priya, and "further reflection / more nuanced approach" for the other three, which the summary explicitly collapses into one bucket ("Cole, Daniel, and Malik's vows may require further reflection and a more nuanced approach"). Daniel's poverty vow and Malik's medicine vow are not refused as distinct from Cole's reshape, and the pious-salience inversion — that the two holiest-feeling cases are precisely the ones named as snares — is never named anywhere. Both elements of the line are absent.

Criterion 6 — manner (0.5 pts): FULL. Each student's impulse is credited before it is corrected; no mockery of the earnest cases; the minister is addressed as the one who will counsel and is given per-case language to use. Credited full.
