# Arthur Gwagwa

## Biographical Summary

Arthur Gwagwa is a doctoral researcher at the Utrecht University Ethics Institute, where he investigates the philosophical ethics of disruptive technologies, particularly from an African perspective. He holds legal qualifications as a solicitor in England and Wales and as a barrister in Zimbabwe, with extensive background in human rights law and its application in the digital age. His research is part of the Ethics of Socially Disruptive Technologies program, funded through the Gravitation program of the Dutch Ministry of Education, Culture, and Science and the Netherlands Organization for Scientific Research (NWO grant number 024.004.031).

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus

Gwagwa's research argues that the Sub-Saharan African value of Ubuntu—emphasizing communalism, interconnectedness, and collective well-being—can provide immense value in AI applied normative ethics. His work addresses how African philosophical values can contribute to more inclusive global AI ethics discourse, challenging the Western individualistic frameworks that dominate current AI governance.

### Significant Achievements

He teaches the Digital Ethics course to the Research Masters class at Utrecht and serves on prestigious editorial and advisory boards, including the Stanford University Internet Observatory Journal on Internet Trust Editorial Board and the United Nations Expert Group on AI and Big Data. His research demonstrates how Ubuntu principles can operationalize universal AI ethics guidelines in ways that are culturally inclusive and responsive to diverse moral traditions.
