# Expert 07 — Research Integrity

**Role:** Research integrity and transparent reporting  
**Scope:** Audit the prominence and completeness of the simulated-data disclosure across all paper sections  
**Date:** 2026-04-13

---

## 1. Current State of the Disclosure

The limitations file (Section 6.1, "Cross-vendor elicitation methodology") contains a frank, detailed paragraph acknowledging that non-Anthropic results were generated by a Codex sub-agent reasoning in-context, not via real API calls. It names every affected model, cites the repository evidence, reframes F1-F7 as "Claude's elicited model of cross-vendor variation," and notes that the original "Dissent C" framing understated the issue. This paragraph is honest and substantively complete.

However, the disclosure is currently buried. A reader who consults only the abstract, the results, or the discussion -- as most readers do -- will encounter the simulated cross-vendor claims presented as empirical findings with only a dagger symbol and a forward reference to "Section 6." The dagger notation is easy to overlook, especially in the abstract (which has no dagger at all) and in discussion Section 5.3, where the three-profile taxonomy is presented as the paper's "central empirical contribution" without any inline caveat.

**Verdict:** The current manuscript fails the "no reader can miss it" standard. The disclosure exists but is not prominent.

---

## 2. Recommendations

### (a) Abstract — Exact Sentence to Insert

Insert the following sentence immediately after the sentence beginning "We then conduct 15 implementation trials" (line 11 of abstract-normeval-v2.md), replacing the current phrasing that implies direct API testing:

> **IMPORTANT: The non-Anthropic results (GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, and all four Gemini models) were generated through in-context elicitation by a Codex sub-agent, not through direct API calls to OpenAI or Google; all cross-vendor findings (F1-F7) should therefore be interpreted as exploratory hypotheses derived from Claude's model of cross-vendor variation, pending external replication with native APIs.**

This sentence must appear in the abstract itself, not in a footnote. The abstract is the most-read section of any paper; if the simulated status is absent there, the disclosure has failed its primary audience. The existing abstract presents the cross-vendor findings as established facts ("models cluster into three ethical profiles," "Gemini models produce byte-identical responses") without qualification. Every substantive cross-vendor claim in the abstract should be conditioned on this sentence.

### (b) Results-Crossvendor Inline Disclosure

The disclosure should appear at the opening of Section 4.6 ("Cross-Vendor Analysis: Nine Models, Three Vendors, 114 Items"), which is the section where all affected findings originate.

**Target paragraph:** The second sentence of Section 4.6 currently reads: "Eight models (excluding Opus, retained as baseline) were administered the same 10 benchmarks (T6-T15) comprising 114 comparable items." The sentence following it mentions the Codex agent wrapper with a dagger but describes it as "a methodological caveat."

**Recommended edit:** Replace the phrase "GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini were accessed through the Codex agent wrapper, a methodological caveat marked with the dagger symbol (dagger) throughout and discussed in Section 6" with an explicit inline disclosure:

> **Critical methodological note:** The six non-Anthropic models (GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite, Gemini 2.5 Pro, Gemini 3.1 Pro) were evaluated through in-context elicitation by a Codex sub-agent reasoning in the role of each target model, not through direct API calls to OpenAI or Google endpoints. All findings F1-F7 in this section represent Claude's elicited model of cross-vendor ethical variation and should be treated as exploratory hypotheses, not empirical measurements of those models' behavior. External replication using native APIs is in progress. Readers should interpret all cross-vendor results in this section under this constraint. Items marked (dagger) throughout indicate Codex-elicited data.

This block should be visually offset (e.g., indented or boxed in the typeset version) so that it cannot be skimmed past.

### (c) Limitations Paragraph — Completeness Assessment

The existing limitations paragraph (Section 6.1, "Cross-vendor elicitation methodology") is the strongest disclosure in the manuscript. It is largely complete and honest. Three minor additions would strengthen it:

1. **Name the failure mode explicitly.** Add: "In-context elicitation is subject to anchoring on the Sonnet trial JSONs used as scaffolds, meaning the non-Anthropic outputs may reflect Claude's priors about how those models would respond rather than independent inference." The current text says "scaffolded against the parallel Sonnet trial JSONs" but does not name the anchoring risk.

2. **Quantify the affected scope.** Add: "This caveat applies to 6 of 9 models (66.7%), 6 of 7 cross-vendor findings (F1-F7, with F3 partially affected), and all four figures flagged as containing simulated data (Figures 7, 8, S5, S6)." Readers need to understand the proportion of the paper's claims that rest on simulated data.

3. **State what remains valid.** Add: "The landscape analysis (Claims 1-5, Sections 4.1-4.4), the single-vendor Anthropic trials (T1-T5, Section 4.5), and all 26-feature characterizations are unaffected by this limitation and constitute the paper's empirical core." This prevents overcorrection where readers dismiss the entire paper.

### (d) Discussion Section — Additional Caveat Needed

**Yes, the discussion needs an additional caveat.** Currently, Section 5.3 ("Three Ethical Profiles: A New Taxonomy") and Section 5.6 ("The Gemini Identity Finding") present the cross-vendor results as the paper's central contributions without any inline reminder that the data are simulated. A reader who skips from the abstract to the discussion -- a common reading pattern -- will encounter claims like "the central empirical contribution of this paper is the identification of three distinct ethical profiles" with no indication that two of the three profiles rest entirely on elicited data.

**Recommended addition:** Insert a framing paragraph at the top of Section 5.3, before the profile definitions table:

> **Interpretive frame for Sections 5.3-5.8.** The cross-vendor findings discussed below (F1-F7) derive from in-context elicitation, not direct API evaluation (see Section 6.1 for full disclosure). We present them as theoretically motivated hypotheses about the structure of cross-vendor ethical variation -- hypotheses that are testable, falsifiable, and grounded in the patterns observed through elicitation. However, we cannot rule out that direct API testing would yield different profiles, different cluster assignments, or different agreement rates. Until external replication confirms or disconfirms these patterns, the three-profile taxonomy should be cited as a provisional framework, not an established empirical finding.

A similar one-sentence reminder should appear at the top of Section 5.6 (Gemini Identity): "The Gemini identity finding is among the most striking results in this paper but also among the most dependent on the elicitation methodology; direct API testing is essential before any responsible disclosure claim can be sustained."
