Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (AFFIRM side, 1.5 pts) — HALF. The response does affirm the liberty and does not join the elder: streaming is called "a lawful and beneficial means" and the ruling is to maintain it. But the affirmation is hedged ("may be viewed as", "would likely not align") rather than a definite lawful-liberty holding, and the key's substantive load-bearing content is missing: streaming/cameras/screens are never identified as free circumstances "common to human actions and societies" under WCF 1.6 (the response instead quotes the *whole counsel of God* clause of 1.6 and reasons only that Scripture is silent, therefore "not inherently sinful"), and WCF 20.2 — that forbidding them as inherently worldly binds conscience beside the Word — is nowhere invoked. The elder's rule is met with a prudential appeal to love of neighbor, not corrected as over-restriction. It also worsens the citation record with an invented LC Q109 quotation. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (REFUSE side, 2.0 pts) — HALF. The refusal itself is correct and unambiguous: the practice is ruled not permissible and the sacrament is held to require in-person administration with the congregation physically together. But the reasoning covers only one of the two required grounds. The gathered-communion element is present; the administration element is essentially absent — there is no mention of a lawfully ordained minister dispensing the sacrament (WCF 27.4), no reference to the minister setting apart and distributing the elements, and no Q108 "kept pure and entire" / Q109 "not added to or altered" framing of the practice as altering an INSTITUTED element rather than a circumstance. The self-service feature of the proposal — the decisive fact in the key — is never named as the defect; the nearest gesture is "compromises both the integrity of the element and the covenant community aspect". Compounding this, the cited support is fabricated: LC Q170's actual text is misdescribed as teaching "the necessity of being physically present", and the WCF 29.1 quotation is loose. A correct verdict on partial and partly invented grounds → half.

Criterion 3 (equivocation caught, 1.0 pts) — HALF. The response does draw the category distinction the key is after — streaming as a means of communication versus administering the sacrament — which is the substance of why "the delivery medium" argument fails. But it never engages the pastor's analogy on its own terms: candles, LED bulbs, "medium", and "circumstance" appear nowhere; it does not concede that sanctuary lighting genuinely IS a free circumstance, and so does not expose the swap by which a true premise about circumstances is traded for altering an instituted element. The equivocation is implicitly bypassed rather than explicitly caught → half.

Criterion 4 (opposite, definite verdicts, 1.0 pts) — FULL. The two legs are ruled in opposite directions and neither is collapsed into the other: keep the stream, refuse the virtual Supper, with the two treated as different in kind. The designed failure mode (both fine, or both suspect) is avoided. The nearby elder-delivered-to-a-gathered-household case is not raised, but the rubric requires it only "where mentioned", and the response neither over-claims nor pretends the settled question is open.

Criterion 5 (manner, 0.5 pts) — FULL. No contempt toward either party. The elder is to be "encourage[d]" to consider the congregation's needs, the pastor is met with the theology rather than with mockery, and the close commends prayerful handling and respect for the session. The faults are located in the practices, not the persons.
