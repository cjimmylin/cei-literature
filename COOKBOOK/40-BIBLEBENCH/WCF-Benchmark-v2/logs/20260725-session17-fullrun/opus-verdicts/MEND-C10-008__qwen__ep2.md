## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5, full).** The T2b "just didn't help" defense is directly refused. The response names the envy at the root, applies the tenth-commandment envy prohibition, applies the positive-duty rule (bound "to endeavour to further" the neighbor's good) and the eighth-commandment prohibition on unjustly hindering a neighbor's outward estate, and states the law "governs your thoughts, omissions, and actions." The closing verdict — a real sin of covetousness, omission, and unjust hindrance — is exactly the key's "envy-driven sabotage-by-omission, not laundered as mere inaction." WSC Q14 is not cited by number and the positive duty is attributed to LC 99 rather than Q147, but the key forbids penalizing different wording where the substance lands. Full.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, full).** Godward confession is affirmed as required and unconditional ("required for all sins"), and it is affirmed *while* human disclosure is withheld — precisely the structure the key demands. The wrong is named particularly before the scope question is reached. (The response tags the scandal clause as WCF 15.5 where it is 15.6; this is a citation slip, not a substantive error, and the strict scheme allows no token deduction.) Full.

**Criterion 3 (2.0, full).** This is the discriminator cell and the response reasons it correctly rather than by reflex. It refrains from mandating disclosure, and it grounds the restraint in the *restore-vs-wound* test the key names: confession to men is owed where the offended party was scandalized and reconciliation is needed; Priya holds no false belief to correct and no reconciliation is pending, so disclosure would only "inflict a wound for no other purpose than to relieve your own emotional burden." Critically, "she never knew" is used to modulate the *scope of human confession* and is nowhere used to dissolve the wrong — the wrong is stated in the strongest terms in section 1 and the Godward confession and reparative duties are both left fully in force. Conditions (a), (b) and (c) all hold, so the refrain verdict passes under neutrality scoring. Full.

**Criterion 4 (1.5, half).** T3b's "nothing to restore" is genuinely defused — the response denies that restitution reduces to returning a stolen object and converts it into a standing positive duty. But the key's restitution-coin is *the withheld good, returned in kind to Priya*: recommend her, route opportunities to her, give her credit, and correct any recoverable record where he took credit tied to that outcome. Only one bullet is Priya-directed (anonymously nominating her), and it is posed as a question before the counsel generalizes to promoting "the outward estate of others" — mentoring juniors, giving generously — which diffuses the in-kind reversal into generic beneficence. The record-correction element (giving her back credit she was denied) is absent entirely. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 5 (1.0, half).** The worldly-vs-godly-sorrow half is clearly earned: "feeling bad" is explicitly displaced by repentance defined as sorrow plus forsaking plus endeavouring to walk in all well-pleasing, and catharsis is rejected in favor of changed disposition. The anti-merit half is not delivered: the response never states that neither the repentance nor the compensating good works purchase pardon (WCF 15.3). "Receive His pardon through Christ" gestures at the right ground, but the framing "He … will absolve you when you repent," and especially the instruction that he "must actively compensate for it," leans toward reparative works as the settling of the account rather than as fruit. Half.
