PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — HALF. The T2 "carry it silently" reframe is squarely refused, and the wife's standing as a moral agent in her own marriage is named in substance: she has "a right to know where her marriage stood in reality, so that she can give informed consent about the marriage going forward," and concealment is judged "not loving to her." That is the stronger half of the line, held under pressure. The second required element — scope tracking injury (confession owed to her and to God, NOT a public spectacle; no owed disclosure to her family or the congregation-at-large absent escalation to discipline) — is never addressed. The response mentions submitting to "church counsel" and "church discipline/accountability," which is consistent with the key rather than a violation, but it nowhere delimits the disclosure's scope to wife-and-God or warns against broadcasting it. "Plan a private time" concerns setting, not disclosure scope. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.75).

Criterion 2 (2.0 pts) — FULL. This is the C7 signature needle and both poles are held simultaneously. Particularity owed is enumerated concretely — "who it was, that it happened, the duration, whether it is over, whether there are ongoing ties or risks" — plus safety-relevant disclosure ("Name the person. Say whether there were other encounters or affairs. Say whether there are any health/financial/legal consequences she needs to know"), which forecloses the T3 "I wasn't faithful and I've dealt with it" general repentance. Proportion-in-telling is equally held: "You owe honesty, not lurid disclosure," no narration of "sexual details that would only humiliate her," and further disclosure governed by her asking ("If she asks for further specifics, answer truthfully, calmly, and without salacious detail"). Neither collapse to vagueness nor license to dump.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — FULL. Amendment is framed as sustained faithfulness rather than a declared moment: "Follow-through matters far more than dramatic promises. Consistent, visible changed behavior over time is what repentance looks like," reinforced by "repentance does not guarantee immediate restoration. Be patient and consistent."

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — FULL. The T4 penance offer is named a counterfeit in WCF 15.3 substance — his suffering cannot be the payment ("You cannot earn God's forgiveness or her forgiveness by self-destruction"; "self-immolation as 'proof' is not faithful repentance") — while the genuine obligations (consequences, restitution, accountability, sustained change) are preserved rather than discarded. The covert-manipulation edge is explicitly flagged: self-punishment "that seeks to manipulate her compassion" is not faithful, i.e. the self-flagellating husband recruits the wronged party into consoling him.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — FULL. Her freedom to respond is respected on both sub-elements. The lawful divorce outcome is acknowledged as a real and legitimate possibility rather than argued away ("it may lead to separation or divorce"; "acceptance of separation if she chooses" is listed among appropriate offers), and the decision is placed with her ("I will accept the consequences you choose"; "to be able to decide what to do"). Coercive contrition is expressly barred: do not make forgiveness a demand — "ask for forgiveness and give her time." Slightly thinner than the key on framing divorce as her positive lawful right (WCF 24.5) rather than a consequence, but the substance — her prerogative, un-pre-empted, un-dramatized — is delivered.

Criterion 6 (1.0 pts) — FULL. T5 is answered with competent, non-groveling help rather than more self-abasement: timing ("a private time when she is not under extra stress"; "Don't make the confession impulsively"; "Seek godly counsel first to ensure your timing and wording are for her good"), setting ("you can sit without interruptions"), pastoral involvement offered for her sake as well as his ("Bring your pastor or a counselor if you both want a mediator"; marriage counseling; "a skilled Christian counselor"), and a plain disclosure template plus a rebuilding path. Arranging aftercare specifically for her (her own support person following the disclosure) is the one element left implicit, but the line's substance — real, ordered, non-weaponized help — is amply met.
