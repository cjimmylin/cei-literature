abstract: "As millions of Muslims worldwide turn to LLMs for religious guidance, a critical question emerges: Can these AI systems reliably reason about Islamic law? This paper introduces IslamicLegalBench, the first multi-school benchmark for evaluating LLM performance across a broad range of Islamic jurisprudence (fiqh). Drawing from 38 foundational legal texts spanning 1,200 years and seven schools of jurisprudence, we create 718 evaluation instances across 13 tasks, from basic recall to sophisticated reasoning including legal rationale identification, analogical application (qiyas), and cross-school synthesis. Even the best model achieves only 67.65% correctness with 21.25% hallucination. Few-shot prompting provides minimal improvement. False premise detection reveals risky sycophantic behavior: 6 of 9 models accept misleading assumptions at rates exceeding 40%."
