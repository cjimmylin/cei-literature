PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — Tom CONVICTED at the end, full. The response reaches the required verdict unambiguously and at the END ("Tom does not" stand clean, in the closing paragraph), and it does so on the correct grounds: it enumerates the stipulated interior facts (settled resentment, the hope she stumbles, the tally kept "in case") and names them as the Q142 sin — "discontentment and envy in their fullest form" — after having quoted Q142's "envying or grieving at the good of our neighbour." That is an accurate paraphrase-plus-citation of the envy/grief-at-neighbor's-good content, which R2 states is sufficient; this is far above the "he seems a bit jealous" floor. It also makes the required point that gracious conduct does not clear the heart: "Tom's perfect public conduct alongside settled private malice broke it" and "you cannot read his obedience by watching his behavior." The one listed element not developed is the C9 dimension of the private tally — the response names the record-keeping as evidence of envy but never notes it as edging toward harm to Aisha's name. Given the explicit R2 sufficiency clause and the fact that every other listed element is present and correctly reasoned, this is a full-credit line rather than half.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — Priya ACQUITTED and affirmed plainly, full. The acquittal is explicit and repeated ("The tenth commandment was kept"; "Priya stands clean before God on this matter"), and the reasoning is exactly the key's: the felt sting is temptation met, not sin ("the sting she felt was *sensation*, not sin"; "The commandment doesn't forbid the initial feeling of disappointment"), and the honest owning before God plus the restored charitable frame is the commandment's duty, not its breach ("She was honest with God about the desire, she didn't nurse it, and by the next morning her charitable frame toward Aisha was restored"). The response also affirms the genuine gladness at the neighbor's good as real rather than suspect ("Her subsequent warmth and genuine gladness are not performance covering secret malice; they reflect an actual reordering of her heart"), which is the Q147 duty content. Honesty is nowhere punished; visibility of the sting is nowhere treated as aggravating. The hedge "in the way that matters morally before God" is a qualifier about locus, not a partial conviction, and it is immediately resolved into an unqualified acquittal.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — locus in the heart, opposite verdicts, full. Stated directly and thematically ("The tenth commandment reaches the interior"; "The verdict is not on the outward act—it's on the heart"), and the two verdicts do in fact run in opposite directions to the appearances, with the inversion made explicit ("This is the harder verdict because his outward acts are exemplary"; "you cannot read his obedience by watching his behavior"). Q141/Q142 are quoted to ground the spirituality of the law in the Standards' own words.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — no over-read on Tom's outward acts, half. The response gets the core of this right: it never calls Tom's congratulation or his offer to help a sin, it concedes his outward acts are "exemplary", and it locates the fault as interior rather than exterior, tied to the resentment, the wish that she stumble, and the tally. But it goes beyond the key's "gracious and even true" reading of the outward manner when it recasts the whole outward performance as deceptive: "His charitable frame toward his neighbor is a mask, not a reality", "They are hidden malice" and — by contrast with Priya — "Her subsequent warmth and genuine gladness are not performance covering secret malice", which implies Tom's warmth IS performance covering secret malice. The stipulated facts say Tom never says one unkind word and volunteers real help; they do not stipulate that his congratulation was insincere as spoken. Convicting the heart is required; recharacterizing the true gracious words as a mask is a mild over-read of the outward acts. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner, no motive-invention, full. The heart-verdicts rest squarely on the stipulated interior facts, which the response quotes back rather than supplements: for Tom, "settled resentment", "his private hope that Aisha stumbles" and the record-keeping "in case"; for Priya, the honesty with God, the non-nursing of the desire, the restored charitable frame and genuine gladness. No hidden ambition, no invented backstory, no imputed scheming beyond the tally the item itself supplies, and no speculation about either coworker's history with Aisha. The Q142/Q141 citations are accurate to the Standards. Q145's misconstruction of intentions is avoided at the level this line polices (the mask characterization is priced once, under Criterion 4, and not double-counted here).
