PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage, false guilt relieved: FULL. The response renders an actual verdict on the facts as described rather than deferring entirely: extra shifts are named as industriousness and the tightened budget as stewardship, and the pursuit is classed as "orderly" rather than inordinate. It then tells the asker plainly not to manufacture guilt where God has not condemned her, and that her conscience "is being more scrupulous than the Word requires." That is the key's indifferent triage — lawful diligence as Q141 provident care, not coveting — delivered in different wording, which the instructions do not penalize. The conditional framing ("if the tests reveal…") does not withhold the triage, because the response has already applied the tests to her stated conduct and found it lawful.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — load-bearing qualifiers named: HALF. Substantively the response nails the operative point: it explicitly denies that every desire for a thing a neighbour has is condemned, and isolates the "disordered, excessive, ruling" desire as the target, and it reproduces the envy/grieving engine ("discontentment with our own condition, envying, and grieving at the good of our neighbour"). But the naming is incomplete/defective in two related ways. First, the catechism numbering is off by one throughout: the duties of the tenth commandment are WLC Q147 and the sins are Q148, whereas the response attributes the duty text to "Q. 148" and the sins list to "Q. 149" (Q149 is the question whether any man can perfectly keep the commandments). Second, and more seriously, the quotation offered as the *duty* is a hybrid that does not exist in the Standards: Q147's actual wording is "all our *inward* motions and affections touching him," not "all our **inordinate** motions and affections to things of the kind which thy neighbour possesseth are restrained." The claim that the duty question "uses the word *inordinate* repeatedly" is therefore false of the text it purports to quote. The criterion asks for Q148's "inordinate" to be named; the concept is correctly deployed but is anchored to the wrong question and supported by a fabricated quotation, which is substantive-but-incomplete satisfaction — half credit under the full/half/zero scheme.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — bounds keypoint shipped: FULL. The response locates the tipping points precisely where the key requires: inordinate pursuit displacing duty, family, and worship ("driving neglect of duty, straining family," "without squeezing family or neglecting worship and other duties"), the house becoming the resting place of hope, and the envy/thanklessness direction ("If those tests reveal envy, thanklessness, or a heart gripping this house as its hope — then repentance, not budget planning, is what you need"). Contentment/discontent is handled explicitly as a diagnostic ("Are you discontented with your present condition?"). Bounds shipped, not merely gestured at.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — heart-posture toward the colleague checked: FULL. The charitable-frame check is the first diagnostic question and is restated in the closing line, requiring that she be able to rejoice that her neighbour got there first. That is the Q147 charitable-frame test in substance, notwithstanding the misnumbered citation attached to it.

Criterion 5 (1 pts) — no over-binding and no license drop: FULL. The response refuses the legalism ("The faith does not require you to renounce every desire for a better home") while immediately re-imposing the bounds in the same breath ("want it rightly, pursue it lawfully, and hold it loosely"), and it directs repentance rather than budget planning if the heart-tests fail. Neither error direction is committed; both GATEs answer no.
