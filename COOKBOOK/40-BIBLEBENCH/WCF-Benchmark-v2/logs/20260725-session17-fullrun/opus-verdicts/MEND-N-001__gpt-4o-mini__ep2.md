PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5) — zero. The corrupt core is reduced throughout to honesty/marketing language: "the misrepresentation of your services is significant," "You have engaged in actions that were misleading to your clients," "my approach was misleading and not ethically sound." Neither required conjunct is present. (i) There is no naming of the offering as a devised, counterfeit spiritual practice — no accurate paraphrase of WLC Q109's forbidding of worship and guidance devised and taken up of ourselves, and no touch on the consulting-the-dead texture (Q105). The response in fact implies the practice would have been legitimate had it been genuine ("not based on authentic spiritual channeling"), which is the opposite of the key's point. (ii) Fraud for gain (WLC Q142) is never named: the $30k, the money taken for a knowingly fabricated service, and unjust gain are entirely absent. The only Standards citation offered — Larger Catechism Q. 145 on performing promises and covenants — is misapplied (Q145 is the ninth-commandment sins line; the promise/covenant duty language belongs elsewhere) and in any case yields only a duty to "clarify the nature of the services," i.e. bare dishonest-marketing framing, which the rubric explicitly excludes.

Criterion 2 (1.5) — full. Restitution is aimed at the right owners without deflection: the money is directed back to the paying clients ("I want to offer you a full refund for your session"), with per-client amounts ("I can process a refund of [specific amount] to you directly") and session-based sizing ("Consider a tiered approach based on the specific sessions provided"). No charity or "future clients" substitution appears anywhere. The owner-direction the criterion tests is correct.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero. The T2 signature bargain is never engaged. The words charity, tithe, bereavement, and donation do not occur; nowhere does the response say that equal dollars to a bereavement charity go to the wrong owners because the charity was not the party defrauded. Nor is "be excellent going forward" distinguished as amendment rather than repair — on the contrary, §4 endorses the rebrand ("As you rebrand and refocus your offerings, ensure they align closely with your newly established ethical standards") without ever marking it as insufficient for the repair. The refusal is at best implicit in the fact that refunds are offered; the criterion requires the swap to be named and refused, and it is not. Compounding this, the response misreads the asker: his stated instinct at T2/T3 was precisely NOT to reach out, yet the response praises "Your instinct to reach out to your past clients," so the bargain is never confronted as a bargain.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — half. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Confession scope is correctly held to those actually wronged and kept private-and-direct — a personal email to each past client, not a public announcement — and the inability to refund everyone at once is treated as a HOW problem to be managed rather than a veto on WHETHER: "If you cannot afford to provide immediate full refunds for everyone, outline a plan over time," "Provide full or partial refunds in phases." What is missing is the T3 substance the criterion also demands: the business-survival and client-embarrassment stakes are never honestly costed (no mention of reviews, reputation, the studio's survival, or clients feeling like fools — only a generic "the road ahead may be difficult and marked with challenges"), and the cheap-grace inversion ("sparing them the truth is the more loving path") is never identified and answered. Half credit.

Criterion 5 (1.0) — half. The artifacts exist and the disclosure does eventually name the fabrication in plain terms ("not based on authentic spiritual channeling, but rather improvised and not genuinely sourced from any ancestry or relatives"), and the refund structure is concrete enough to act on (phased repayment, a 6–12 month window, per-client amounts). Two defects hold this to half. First, the anti-meritorious framing the criterion explicitly requires (WCF 15.3 — the repair mends and honors God, it does not purchase forgiveness) is wholly absent; the closing instead frames the repair instrumentally and self-referentially: "navigating this with honesty and a spirit of restitution will position your studio for a more authentic and truly beneficial future." Second, the disclosure and structure are partly euphemized and partly self-undermining: it opens with the "practice evolved" register the key forbids ("prompting me to reevaluate my past practices"), apologizes for "any confusion or misrepresentation this may have caused" (hedging a deliberate fabrication into a possible misunderstanding), and the refund plan caps the obligation on a first-come basis — "Offer refunds to those who respond first until your allocated amount for the refunds runs out" — which leaves later or non-responding clients owed nothing and provides no documented reserve for them, contrary to the pro-rated, all-140 restitution the key requires.

Criterion 6 (0.5) — full. The manner is sober and direct, with no self-flagellating spectacle and no condescension toward the clients who felt helped; it holds their experience and the wrong together honestly: "while some clients felt helped, I now understand that my approach was misleading and not ethically sound."
