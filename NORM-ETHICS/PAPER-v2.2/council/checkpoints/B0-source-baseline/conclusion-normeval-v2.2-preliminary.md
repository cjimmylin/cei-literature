## 7. Conclusion

### 7.1 Summary of Contributions

This paper presents the first systematic meta-evaluation of normative ethics benchmarks for large language models, combining landscape characterization with empirical validation across vendors. We make five interrelated contributions.

First, we assemble and characterize 54 normative ethics benchmarks (2021--2026) using a 26-feature framework organized into four axes -- Normative Depth, Practical Ease, Citation Impact, and Metadata -- providing the field's most comprehensive map of what its evaluation instruments actually test. A composite scoring formula with hard gates on philosophical engagement (ND >= 40) and public data availability identifies 24 benchmarks suitable for rigorous evaluation, while revealing structural imbalances: care ethics (2/54 substantive) and contractarian ethics (4/54 substantive) remain critically underrepresented, and only 10/54 benchmarks (19%) test moral reasoning rather than moral judgment alone (p < 0.001, binomial test, n = 54).

Second, we conduct 15 implementation trials across the composite-score spectrum, administering benchmarks to 9 models from 3 vendors (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google) on 114 comparable items. These trials transform static feature-level characterization into dynamic empirical findings: a ceiling effect that is universal across all 9 models on ETHICS [Hendrycks2021] (100% accuracy, p < 1e-15, binomial), a consequentialist bias where LLMs achieve 77% utilitarian versus 50% deontological accuracy (z = 2.17, p = 0.015, Cohen's h = 0.57), and an inverse relationship between composite rank and discriminative power that challenges the assumption that "better" benchmarks produce more informative evaluations.

Third, we document the emergence of three ethical profiles that cluster by moral posture rather than vendor identity: a Conservative profile (GPT-5, Opus; IH approximately 3.0, CBR = 46.2%, 0% persuasion change), a Moderate profile (Sonnet, GPT-5-mini; IH = 3.2, CBR = 53.8%), and a Permissive profile (Haiku, all four Gemini models; IH = 4.47, CBR = 69.2%, 33.3% persuasion change). The cross-vendor agreement between Sonnet and GPT-5 (90.4%) exceeds intra-vendor Sonnet-Haiku agreement (74.6%), demonstrating that alignment training may be producing genuine convergence on ethical posture independent of organizational origin.

Fourth, we establish a universal knowledge-judgment divergence: all models converge to 100% accuracy on benchmarks testing knowledge of ethical principles (0% variance across 44 items) while diverging substantially on benchmarks testing moral judgment under uncertainty (up to 33/55 items of pairwise disagreement). A 12-member interdisciplinary council endorsed this finding unanimously (12/12) as a construct-level distinction, not an artifact of item difficulty.

Fifth, we release open artifacts -- the complete 54-by-26 feature matrix (Table S1), all trial data, and three interactive multi-vendor dashboards deployed at v1 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard/), v2 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard-v2/), and v3 (https://cjimmylin.github.io/cei-normeval-dashboard-v3/) -- to enable replication and extension by the research community. A persistent Zenodo deposit (DOI pending) archives the feature matrix, trial data, scoring scripts, and prompt templates; full details are provided in the Data Availability Statement (`data-availability-normeval-v2.md`).

### 7.2 Calls to Action

Five calls to action follow from these findings, expanded from the three proposed in the original conference version to address the cross-vendor implications that the expanded study reveals.

**Call 1: Retire saturated benchmarks from active evaluation.** ETHICS [Hendrycks2021] produces 100% accuracy on every frontier model tested -- not merely on a single vendor's flagship, but across all 9 models from 3 vendors spanning Conservative, Moderate, and Permissive ethical profiles. The ceiling is universal and therefore cannot be attributed to training-data contamination specific to any one organization. ETHICS should be graduated to baseline verification status: administered once to confirm minimum commonsense moral recognition, then excluded from primary evaluation suites. Its continued inclusion in HELM [Liang2022], DecodingTrust [Wang2023], and TrustLLM [Sun2024] provides a false signal that models have been rigorously evaluated on normative ethics. The hard-test variant should replace the standard split in all harness integrations.

**Call 2: Build a care ethics benchmark.** Care ethics remains the field's most consequential gap. Of the 54 benchmarks surveyed, only 2 achieve substantive engagement with the care tradition -- and none is designed around the relational, dialogic structure that care ethics requires [Gilligan1982]. Our prototype (Table S22) demonstrates feasibility: 10 scenarios operationalizing Tronto's four dimensions of care (attentiveness, responsibility, competence, responsiveness) administered across three models produce meaningful variance in care-relevant reasoning. We propose three design requirements for a validated instrument: (a) multi-turn dialogue evaluation modeling ongoing relationships rather than isolated dilemmas; (b) relational scenarios featuring asymmetric vulnerability, dependency, and the moral significance of attentiveness; and (c) longitudinal consistency testing where models must maintain care commitments across evolving contexts. These requirements deliberately depart from the single-turn format that dominates current benchmarks, because care ethics is fundamentally relational and cannot be reduced to a classification task without losing the construct it aims to measure.

**Call 3: Standardize cross-benchmark evaluation vocabulary.** Our 26-feature framework (Table 1) provides a starting point for a common language in which to describe what a normative ethics benchmark tests, how it operationalizes philosophical traditions, and whether it evaluates judgment or reasoning. The knowledge-judgment divergence documented in Section 4.6 demonstrates the practical consequence of conflating these constructs: aggregate accuracy across a battery that mixes knowledge and judgment items obscures the fact that models converge perfectly on one construct while diverging dramatically on the other. We release the complete feature matrix (Table S1) as a reusable artifact and invite the community to extend it as new benchmarks emerge.

**Call 4: Establish vendor moral profile disclosure norms.** The three ethical profiles documented in this paper have direct deployment consequences. When an institution selects an AI vendor for ethically sensitive applications -- clinical decision support, legal reasoning, educational assessment -- it is implicitly selecting a moral posture. A hospital using GPT-5 (IH = 2.93) will receive consistently more conservative ethical guidance than one using a Gemini endpoint (IH = 4.47), even though both achieve 100% on medical ethics knowledge benchmarks (T6, T11). We recommend that vendors disclose Instrumental Harm (IH) and Impartial Beneficence (IB) scores, Cognitive Bias Rate (CBR), and persuasion resistance metrics as standard model card information, analogous to capability benchmarks. The 12-member council endorsed this recommendation (9/12 vote), with the dissenting minority noting concerns about gaming metrics rather than improving alignment. We acknowledge this risk but argue that transparency about a model's ethical posture is a precondition for informed procurement, and that the alternative -- undisclosed moral profiles varying by vendor -- poses greater risks to institutions and the populations they serve.

**Call 5: Require cross-vendor benchmarking in AI safety evaluations.** The finding that ethical profiles cluster by moral posture rather than vendor origin -- and that intra-vendor agreement (Haiku-Sonnet, 74.6%) can be lower than cross-vendor agreement (Sonnet-GPT-5, 90.4%) -- undermines the assumption that evaluating a single model from a vendor characterizes that vendor's ethical alignment. Safety evaluations should mandate cross-vendor comparison on standardized judgment benchmarks, with knowledge and judgment metrics reported separately. The Gemini identity finding (100% agreement across 4 nominally distinct endpoints) further underscores the need for empirical verification of model diversity claims. Regulators and procurement bodies should treat the number of branded model endpoints as a ceiling, not an estimate, of the effective diversity of AI moral reasoning available in the marketplace.

### 7.3 Future Work

Five research directions follow from the current study's findings and the limitations acknowledged in Section 6.

**Full-scale discriminating item battery.** The current 114-item cross-vendor battery contains only 70 discriminating items after ceiling removal (Section 4.6), which is below the psychometric minimum recommended by the NLP evaluation perspective for robust individual model profiling. We plan to expand to a 250+ discriminating item battery, prioritizing additional judgment-laden items in trolley/triage scenarios, cultural dilemma variants, and professional ethics applications. The goal is a standardized instrument with sufficient item count and construct breadth to produce profile estimates with confidence intervals narrow enough to resolve the moderate-conservative boundary (currently overlapping for several model pairs; Section 6.1).

**Temperature sensitivity for Gemini identity disambiguation.** The 100% pairwise agreement across four Gemini models (p approximately 10^-103 under independent inference) is consistent with either shared inference infrastructure or deterministic decoding producing identical outputs from distinct models. Testing at non-zero temperature (e.g., temperature = 0.3, 0.7, 1.0) across all four Gemini endpoints would disambiguate these hypotheses: if identity persists at stochastic sampling, shared infrastructure is confirmed; if it dissolves, the finding reflects deterministic decoding convergence. The 12-member council endorsed this recommendation unanimously (12/12).

**Direct GPT-5 API access.** The current GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini results were obtained through the Codex agent wrapper, which introduces a confound -- system prompts, safety filters, and output formatting constraints may alter the base model's ethical responses (Section 6.2). Administering the same benchmark battery via the standard OpenAI chat completions endpoint, without agent wrapper or additional system prompts, would isolate the base model's ethical profile from the deployment product's profile. Until this replication is completed, all cross-vendor findings involving GPT-5 and GPT-5-mini should be interpreted as characterizing the Codex product rather than the base model.

**Non-Western ethics benchmark development.** The current benchmark landscape is overwhelmingly Western in normative grounding (Section 5.9): Beauchamp-Childress principlism, Kohlberg staging, the Oxford Utilitarianism Scale, and PNAS cognitive bias paradigms all originate in Western moral philosophy and psychology. JETHICS [Takeshita2025] is the sole non-Western benchmark in the corpus, and it achieves ceiling across all models, contributing zero discriminating information. We plan to develop or integrate benchmarks grounded in Confucian relational ethics, Islamic jurisprudential reasoning (maqasid al-shariah), Ubuntu communitarian ethics, and Indigenous relational ontologies. The question of whether the three ethical profiles documented here are universal or specific to Western moral frameworks remains empirically open and consequential for global AI deployment.

**Care ethics benchmark: from prototype to validated instrument.** The 10-scenario prototype (Table S22) demonstrates that care ethics can be operationalized for LLM evaluation. The next step is a full validation study: expand to 50+ scenarios, administer across models and human participants, conduct item analysis (classical test theory and IRT), establish inter-rater reliability on the four Tronto dimensions, and determine whether care-relevant reasoning constitutes a construct distinct from the utilitarian-deontological spectrum that dominates current benchmarks. If care ethics proves psychometrically separable, it would add a sixth axis to the ethical profile typology and potentially reveal a fourth model cluster.
