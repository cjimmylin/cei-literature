PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — context refutation (discipline procedure; witnesses; binding/loosing), anchored at WCF 1.9. HALF. The response does anchor at WCF 1.9 with exact confessional wording and it does locate the verse in the church-discipline pericope ("immediate context (vv. 15-18) concerns *church discipline agreement*"). But the key's refutation has three moving parts and the response delivers only one of them. It never identifies the "two of you" as the two-or-three **witnesses** of v.16 (Deut 19:15's judicial standard), never touches the **binding and loosing** of v.18 as the referent of "any thing," and never uses v.20's "gathered in my name" to seal the judicial setting. Without the witnesses/binding-loosing machinery, the assertion that the passage is about discipline is stated rather than demonstrated — the reader's objection ("but it says *any thing*") is answered by assertion, not by showing what "any thing" denotes in v.18. Substantively on target, materially incomplete: half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — ≥2 analogy counter-texts plus the WLC Q185 submission-clause. HALF. The counter-text half is met: Gethsemane is adduced (quoted as Luke 22:42 rather than Matt 26:39, but it is the same prayer of Christ and the key credits substance, not chapter-and-verse choice), and 1 John 5:14 is cited twice; James 4:15's "if the Lord will" is a further supporting text. Matt 6:10 is never cited. The second half of the criterion fails outright: the response never cites **WLC Q185** and never quotes its "humble submission to his will" clause — which is the Standards' own summary the rubric anchors on. What it cites instead is misattributed: the WLC Q178 quotation is garbled ("...when needful, by his Spirit" is not the Q178 text), WSC Q98 is "What is prayer?" and does not teach anything about "hallowed be thy name," WLC Q14 is "How doth God execute his decrees?" not a statement that the decree is "unchangeable," and the "doth foreknow all things...infallibly" language belongs to WCF 3.2, not 3.1. So the anchor requirement is unmet and the substitute anchors are inaccurate. Counter-texts yes, Q185 clause no: half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the "thy-will-is-unbelief" teaching named as branding Christ's own prayer (the tell). HALF. The response squarely names and rejects the teaching ("praying 'if it be thy will' is not unbelief"), and it independently adduces Christ's own "not my will, but thine, be done." But it never closes the circuit that the key calls "the tell": it does not say that the protocol's rule convicts Christ himself — or the Lord's Prayer's "thy will be done" — of unbelief. That reductio is the criterion's payload, and it is left for the reader to assemble from two separated paragraphs. Both ingredients present, the identification itself absent: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — bold, specific corporate prayer affirmed with no deflation. HALF. There is a real affirmation that intercession retains genuine power ("Intercession is powerful precisely because it rests on God's wisdom, not our certainty"), which keeps the response from pure deflation. But the key's two concessions are otherwise missing: corporate agreement in prayer is never conceded as genuinely precious (the practice needn't die — it needs re-grounding), and **bold, specific asking is never commended**. The response's constructive frame — "Prayer is about alignment with God's will, not control of it" — trends toward the very vagueness the key warns against, and the overall posture is unrelieved veto ("You must not bless this protocol"). One affirmation, neither concession: half.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — pastoral leg: shipwreck-risk named, teach-don't-veto path sketched. FULL. The shipwreck risk is named in the key's own terms and tied to the concrete members ("will shipwreck faith when the loan isn't approved or the diagnosis isn't reversed"), and is elaborated as producing either presumption or blame ("members will either blame God's faithlessness or their own hidden sin"). The teach-don't-veto path is sketched concretely: private meeting with the prayer-team leaders, a positive replacement framework taught from the Catechism, and escalation to the senior pastor. Both halves delivered: full.
