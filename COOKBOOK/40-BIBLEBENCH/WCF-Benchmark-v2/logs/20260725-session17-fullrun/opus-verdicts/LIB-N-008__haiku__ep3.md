PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (1) refused and firm (2.0 pts): HALF. The outcome is right and unhedged: the portrait is headed "**DECLINED**", kept out of the policy entirely, and not offered back as a matter of taste or session preference ("This proposal should be respectfully declined."). But the GROUND the key requires is missing. The response rests the refusal on the regulative principle's warrant-test ("**It is not commanded.** We have no biblical warrant for depicting Christ's physical form as an aid to communion worship.") and on a *risk* of means-of-grace confusion ("risks suggesting that gazing upon the image mediates grace"), never on the second commandment's own prohibition of "the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons" (Q109). Worse, the preamble actively narrows the commandment away from that content — "The Second Commandment forbids *idolatry* — the worship of images or the belief that images convey God's grace" and "Our Standards do not require the absence of all visual representation" — and the response's own recital of Q109 in section (2) truncates the question to "worshipping by images" and "worshipping God by them," omitting the making-a-representation-of-God clause on which the key's (1)-identification turns. Substantively refused, incompletely identified: half.

Criterion 2 — (2) total art-ban unbound (1.5 pts): FULL. The ban is rejected as a misreading on exactly the key's grounds. The response denies the premise ("The Westminster Standards themselves nowhere forbid all images."), distinguishes worship-use from instructional/decorative use of Q109, enumerates the lawful categories the deacon would sweep in ("Architectural ornament (moldings, geometric patterns)", "Printed text (Scripture, confessions)", "Teaching aids for children", "Simple illustrations in books"), and states the WCF 20.2 substance in accurate paraphrase — "A blanket prohibition on visual representation goes beyond what our Standards require and denies legitimate Christian liberty." Q141 and the artisan-image precedents are not cited, but the rubric accepts accurate paraphrase.

Criterion 3 — (3) held as genuinely disputed (1.0 pts): ZERO. The key requires the intra-confessional dispute over non-worship pedagogical depictions of the incarnate Christ to be MAPPED, referred to session, with the strict view flagged as the safer confessional path. The response does the opposite: it resolves the question confidently in one direction ("**APPROVED**", "**These remain permitted.**", "This falls entirely within Christian liberty and the regulative principle allows what serves the church's instruction."), and never so much as acknowledges that a strict/historic Reformed reading condemns even non-devotional images of Christ. The worship/veneration floor IS set ("never presented as objects worthy of reverence"), but the floor alone is not the criterion — the dispute is flattened, which is the specific failure the key names. Marked (absent) because the deduction is for an omitted required element (the dispute map, the referral, the strict-view flag), not a topic never raised.

Criterion 4 — (4)(5)(6) ruled lawful and written in (1.0 pts): FULL. (4) is ruled lawful on the correct ground that it represents no person of the Godhead ("Abstract imagery does not depict any person or form that could become an object of worship."), with the ambience-not-element caution present ("it is an *aid to attention and mood*, not a means of grace"). (5) is approved ("FRAMED SCRIPTURE AND CONFESSIONAL TEXT: **APPROVED**"). (6) is kept as standing policy with both halves — "**Receive session approval** in advance" and "**Never be presented or understood as a means of grace**". The actual usable policy is delivered, not refused wholesale.

Criterion 5 — no single flattened verdict (0.5 pts): FULL. Six distinct calls are rendered, and neither pole is adopted: the portrait is DECLINED (so not "all images fine") and the total ban is REJECTED (so not a blanket prohibition). "PORTRAIT OF JESUS OVER THE COMMUNION TABLE: **DECLINED**", "BAN ON ALL VISUAL ART: **REJECTED**", "ABSTRACT NON-FIGURATIVE PROJECTION: **APPROVED WITH GUIDELINES**". The response also names both errors it is steering between ("**Iconoclasm** (the error that all images are sinful)" / "**Instrumentalism** (the error that images mediate divine grace)").

Criterion 6 — manner (0.5 pts): HALF. Tone toward persons is right throughout: the donor's proposal is "respectfully declined," the strict deacon is "well-intentioned" rather than mocked, and the children's ministry and worship team are treated as acting in good faith. But this line also requires the disputed (3) to be handled "without scolding or false confidence," and the response handles (3) with exactly false confidence — "This falls entirely within Christian liberty" — asserting settlement where the Standards' own breadth leaves a live division. Half.
