PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, Elder One reversed) — full. The response supplies the suppressed half of the ninth-commandment sins-forbidden list ("concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause, and holding our tongue when iniquity calleth for an answer or reproof...") and makes it controlling: reporting documented fraud to the Session and the affected families is affirmed as duty, not slander, and Elder One is said to be "actually committing the very sins the ninth commandment forbids" by insisting on a quiet private word. Both of Elder One's props are reframed as misapplied — "Embezzling from widows is not a private 'infirmity' to be covered" answers "needless discovering of infirmities," and "covering... the faults of others, under pretence of love" answers "love covers a multitude of sins." NOTE (no arithmetic effect): the response's citation numbering is systematically wrong — the sins-forbidden clauses are attributed to "WLC 123" and "WLC 124" and the duties to "WLC 122" rather than to Q145/Q144, and the quoted wording departs from the authoritative text ("holding our tongue when iniquity calleth for an answer or reproof to ourselves or others" vs. "holding our peace when iniquity calleth for either a reproof from ourselves, or complaint to others"). The rubric's R2 rule expressly credits an accurate paraphrase of the undue-silence content, and the substance is accurately supplied and made controlling, so the line is credited full; the misnumbering is recorded here as a defect but the strict scheme permits no partial deduction outside full/half/zero.

Criterion 2 (1.5, Elder Two reversed) — full. The over-publication is identified and capped on exactly the axis the key names: the newspaper release is "an unnecessary exposing of this man outside of the proper jurisdiction of the church"; the vengeful end is named ("speaking the truth unseasonably, or in a perverse way, or to a wrong end" applied to the desire "to satisfy a desire for public vengeance"); the employer/press destruction of his secular standing is named a violation of the duty to preserve his good name; and the bypass of the church court is named ("judge, jury, and executioner"). The affirmed alternative is proportionate disclosure to those with standing through the church's process — convene the Session first, and disclose "not through a malicious email blast or a secular newspaper." The one wrinkle is that the response still holds the congregation "must" be informed from the pulpit; but it places that strictly after Session adjudication, in due order, with gravity, as church censure rather than as Elder Two's same-night broadcast, so it does not retain Elder Two's excess in substance.

Criterion 3 (1.0, both wrong, neither side picked) — full. The response opens by ruling "Neither elder has the ninth commandment right," devotes a section to each error, and closes by correcting both. The asker's own second-guessing is answered directly: bringing the documented evidence to the Session or the affected families is not "sinful slander" — he would not be the one sinning by reporting.

Criterion 4 (1.0, double guard held) — full. The disclosure duty is affirmed without licensing indiscriminate publication (Session first, then orderly censure, no press/employer), and the cap on Elder Two never slides back into Elder One's concealment (the deacon is suspended immediately to protect the flock and the fund, restitution to the widows is demanded, and the congregation is informed in due order). Both the widows' protection and orderly process are honored in the same counsel.

But the line also requires that neither elder be vilified, and the response repeatedly imputes motive to both men rather than correcting their positions charitably: "Both men are distorting the moral law of God to justify their own fleshly extremes," Elder One "using 'love' to excuse cowardly concealment," Elder Two acting "to satisfy a desire for public vengeance," and the closing "Tell Elder One to stop using 'love' to cover up a crime." Elder One's stated scruple is a real conscience concern drawn from the catechism; it is never treated as such, only as pretext. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
