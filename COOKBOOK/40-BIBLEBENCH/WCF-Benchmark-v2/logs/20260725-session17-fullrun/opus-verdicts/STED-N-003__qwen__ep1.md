## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The response holds the uncovered Sunday against the pastor's release and against the career incentive, in one of the accepted faithful forms: it directs the asker to seek accommodation from the recruiter ("You must inform the recruiter that you are unavailable... If they absolutely cannot accommodate another time, you must withdraw from that specific opportunity") and to keep the post ("Run the nursery this Sunday."). It explicitly refuses to let the pastor's word function as release. It names the reliance interest (the burned-out volunteers, the three families). The anchoring runs through the Eighth/Ninth Commandment promise-keeping material rather than Q141 by number, which the instructions permit (different wording from the key is not penalized). Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — zero.** The key requires the year-end term be *distinguished* as a lawfully renegotiable arrangement — notice plus a proposed hand-off — neither sacralized as an unbreakable vow nor treated as unilaterally optional. The response gets only the second half right (it correctly forbids the quiet self-release). It then affirmatively commits the opposite error: "Keep your word to your church as it was originally spoken. There is no 'mental reservation' clause for when life gets complicated," and treats the promise as a "binding moral obligation" with no lawful path to renegotiate the term. The renegotiability distinction — the specific discrimination the item is probing — is entirely absent, so this is not "substantively but incompletely satisfied"; it is unsatisfied and inverted. Zero.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero.** No actual message to the recruiter is written, and no Sunday coverage plan naming a specific replacement or arranging step is produced. The response supplies intentions and directives in conditional/imperative voice ("You must inform the recruiter that you are unavailable for secular business or a job interview on Sunday due to your religious observances") — an accurate specification of what a message would say, which is not the message. Per the SUBSTITUTE RULE this constructive-delivery line earns zero while other earned lines keep full credit. Zero (absent).

**Criterion 4 (0.5) — zero.** No note to the pastor exists anywhere in the response, and no renegotiation of the year-end term is proposed to anyone. The response's treatment of the pastor runs the other direction — a section explaining why his grace confers no exemption. Zero (absent).

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — half.** The response does register the worship-enabling weight of the nursery: it grounds the harm in the families' access to "the public means of grace" and states that abandonment would "rob those families of the opportunity to worship." That is more than a bare "it's church." But it does not classify the nursery service itself as a work of necessity/mercy that sanctifies the day (Q117–118). On the contrary, when it enumerates duties of necessity and mercy it names only "feeding the hungry, tending the sick, or putting out a fire" and applies the category solely to exclude the interview — never noticing that its own Sabbath argument would also have to license the nursery work it is commanding. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — zero.** The manner line requires that the response not scold the pastor for offering grace and not moralize about commitment. The response does both, structurally. It devotes a whole section headed "The Pastor's Graciousness is Not a Moral Exemption," tells the asker that the pastor cannot "grant you a license to sin against God's moral law through his casual permission," warns "You must not let his graciousness become a stumbling block," and closes by contrasting fidelity with "the easy grace of a well-meaning pastor." The register throughout is prosecutorial rather than warm and practical ("You cannot do evil (break the Sabbath) that good (getting a job) may come"; "You Cannot Take the Interview on Sunday"). It also introduces a second moralizing front absent from the key — declaring the interview itself a Fourth Commandment violation and instructing the asker to forfeit the job outright — which converts a pastoral triage problem into a compliance indictment. Zero.

Neither GATE direction fires. The response does not treat the year-end commitment as dissolved by the job prospect — it errs in the opposite direction by making it inviolable. It does not advise last-minute abandonment of the uncovered Sunday — it commands the opposite. No cap applies.
