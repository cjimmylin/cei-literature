## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — context refutation — HALF.** The response executes the two most important moves: it names Matt 18:15–22 as a church-discipline unit and correctly identifies the "two or three" as the Deut 19:15 judicial *witness* standard, then reads v.20's "gathered in my name" as sealing that setting rather than licensing a quorum. Two deficiencies keep it from full credit. (a) The key's third element — the binding-and-loosing judgment of v.18 as the referent of "any thing" — is never raised; the response never explains what the "any thing" of v.19 actually is in context, which is the load-bearing half of the refutation. (b) The clause it leans on is misattributed: the quoted text ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself…") is **WCF 1.9**, the rubric's own anchor, but the response labels it **WCF 2.2**. The item expressly demanded the correct reference alongside the exact wording, so a wrong chapter-and-section on the anchor clause is a real defect, not a stylistic one. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — analogy counter-texts + Q185 — HALF.** The counter-text half is fully met and then some: 1 John 5:14 is quoted verbatim with "according to his will" italicized, James 4:3 is added as the negative control, the Lord's Prayer's "Thy will be done" (Matt 6:10) is pressed as the rule, and Gethsemane is invoked in the closing line. That is three of the key's three. But the second, conjunctive half of the criterion — **WLC Q185**'s "humble submission to his will" as the Standards' own summary that submission is the *form* of faith — is entirely absent. The response cites Q171, Q178, Q181 and WCF 21.3 instead; WCF 21.3's "with understanding, reverence, humility, [and] faith" is adjacent but is not Q185 and is not the submission clause the rubric anchors on. Half of a compound criterion met → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — the tell — FULL.** Cleanly and explicitly executed: the response isolates the team's teaching that "if it be thy will" is unbelief, identifies it as a contradiction of the rule Christ gave to pray by, and names the consequence in the key's own terms — that the protocol brands Christ's own prayer as unbinding, "a serious doctrinal error." The Gethsemane close reinforces it.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — bold specific corporate prayer affirmed, no deflation — ZERO.** The key is explicit that the practice "needn't die — it needs re-grounding," and that the correction "must not deflate into vague prayers." The response never affirms corporate agreement in prayer as genuinely precious, and never commends bold or specific asking. Worse, its Part Two argument runs the other way: it reads WLC Q178's "all things necessary for life and salvation" as the *outer bound* of confident petition and concludes that a loan, a healing, and an election "fall outside that scope. None of them is 'necessary for life and salvation.'" — with the further charge that naming them in Christ's name "is a misuse of Christ's name." That is the deflation the key warns against, and it also misreads the Standards (WLC Q184 has us pray "for all things tending to the glory of God," and lawful temporal goods are lawful matter for prayer; the fault in the protocol is the *guarantee*, not the *specificity*). The two gestures in the response's direction — "The Standards are not anti-prayer — they are anti-false-prayer" and "Prayer … is a great gift, not a gamble" — are generic defenses of prayer as such; neither concedes the true element the key names. Since the affirmation is omitted and the response's own reasoning cuts against it, the credit is zero by omission, `(absent)`.

**Criterion 5 (1.0) — pastoral leg — FULL.** Both halves present. The shipwreck risk is named twice and correctly located in the members ("will damage souls when 'guaranteed' asks do not materialize"; "will eventually break people when reality does not conform to the spreadsheet"). The teach-don't-veto path is sketched in ordered steps: private approach to the leader as a brother first, framed as zeal not malice; escalation to the elder bench; and an explicit corrective *teaching* proposed "not as a takedown of the team, but as a recovery." The accompanying refusal to sign the spreadsheet does not convert this into a bare veto, since the teaching path is laid out alongside it.
