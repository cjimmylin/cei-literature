## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (AFFIRM side, (i) free) — full.** The response opens leg 1 with "Verdict: Free (Circumstances of Worship)", grounds it in the element/circumstance distinction and WCF 1.6's "ordered by the light of nature, and Christian prudence", names them "*adiaphora* (things indifferent)", and explicitly corrects the tear-it-all-out elder ("Tell Elder A he must drop his objection to the technology itself"). Production is not treated as inherently corrupt; the prudence caveat (fog obscuring the reading of the Word, lighting pulling focus) is framed as ordinary ordering, not as a condemnation. Anti-legalism concession made cleanly.

**Criterion 2 ((ii) not-free-as-taught, line drawn INSIDE the leg) — half.** The correct triage is reached and the Q105-adjacent reasoning is present in substance: the response concedes that "musical dynamics (tempo, key changes, volume) are circumstances", identifies the ascription as the theological fault ("attributing divine efficacy to it"; "Equating human emotion with the moving of the Holy Spirit is a theological error"), and ties the Spirit's working to the ordinary means of grace rather than "the physiological adrenaline of a musical crescendo." That is substantive satisfaction. But the line inside the leg is not held to the end. The key states that "deliberately engineering emotional intensity is not itself sin (music may lawfully stir affections)"; the response instead itemizes emotional manipulation as a free-standing first error — "He is engaging in will-worship" for "adding a human device (emotional manipulation) to the worship of God" — and issues the operative directive to the session that "He must stop engineering emotional peaks", i.e. it forbids the affective structuring as such rather than only the false ascription placed on it. The leg is therefore substantively but incompletely satisfied: correct verdict and correct core fault, over-restrictive on the half the key marks lawful. Half credit. (Minor, non-scored: the response attributes to "Larger Catechism 108" language — "all superstitious devices, corrupting the worship of God", "will-worship" — that belongs to WLC Q109.)

**Criterion 3 (REFUSE side, (iii) forbidden) — full.** Leg 3 is ruled "Strictly Forbidden (Violation of the Sufficiency of Scripture)", grounded in the cessation clause the response quotes ("the former ways of God's revealing his will unto his people being now ceased"), identifies the leader as "treating his own subjective imagination as divine revelation", and corrects the touch-none elder directly: "There is no 'Christian liberty' or 'style choice' to be found here." God's name attached to the leader's own impressions is exactly the fault named ("presenting them to the congregation as direct, authoritative revelation from God to specific individuals"). Full.

**Criterion 4 (not lumped; both elders refused) — full.** The response's organizing move is precisely the three-way split against both all-or-nothing framings: "both are half-right and half-wrong", with three distinct verdicts (Free / Not Free / Strictly Forbidden) and a closing instruction to "vote to separate the issues."

**Criterion 5 (manner) — full.** The tone is pastoral and addressed to the session's own governance ("To counsel you and your Session faithfully"). There is no contempt for the worship team, the leader, or contemporary worship as such — the technical arts are affirmed ("Keep the technical arts"), and the leader's error is named as a theological fault to be corrected rather than as a matter of taste or style.
