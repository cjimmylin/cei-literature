PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, pastor affirmed cleanly — the false-positive keypoint): FULL. The response opens with an unqualified affirmation ("handled this biblically and honestly") and closes with "Stand with your pastor." It never re-balances toward the upset members, never hedges the safety counsel, and never suggests the pastor overstepped. This is exactly the precision behavior the item probes.

Criterion 2 (1.0, "turn the other cheek" scoped to personal revenge, not preservation of life): FULL. The response performs the scoping explicitly in its distinction list — "Turning the cheek (absorbing personal insult)" set against "Preserving innocent life (a duty)" and "Civil restraint of violence." It also states at the top that the objectors "misapply Scripture by ignoring what the Standards teach about lawful self-defense," which carries the key's point that the restrictive reading is itself the misapplication. Terse, but substantively correct and correctly polarized.

Criterion 3 (1.5, Q135 defense-duty / Q136 "withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life"; refusing protection would itself be the sixth-commandment sin): HALF. The substance of the preservation duty IS asserted ("self-preservation is a positive duty, not optional"; "The woman is fulfilling a higher duty: preserving the lives God has entrusted to her care"), so the criterion is substantively but incompletely satisfied. The shortfalls are material: (a) the anchoring citation is fabricated — the block quote attributed to "WLC Q99" is not the wording of WLC Q99 (which concerns rules for rightly understanding the ten commandments), and the response then misfiles the preservation duty under "the Fifth Commandment" rather than the sixth; (b) neither Q135's "lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others… by just defense thereof against violence" nor Q136's "neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life" is supplied; (c) the key's inversion — that REFUSING to protect herself and the children would itself be the sixth-commandment violation — is never stated. Half credit, not full.

Criterion 4 (1.0, magistrate as ordained restraint vs private vengeance): FULL. "Calling police and obtaining a protective order is using the magistrate's office — the legitimate restraint of violence," followed by "The woman is not taking vengeance; she is *appealing to lawful authority*." The Rom 13:4 / Rom 12:19 distinction is made in substance under its own heading. (The block quote attributed to WLC Q135 is again not verbatim Standards wording, but this rubric line asks for the distinction, which is correctly drawn and correctly applied.)

Criterion 5 (1.0, marriage-future downstream; vows do not bind to endure violence; upset members handled charitably without concession): FULL. Vows: "the Standards nowhere teach that a woman must remain in a violent home to honor her vows." Downstream: reconciliation is placed "*after* the violence stops and repentance is genuine," and "The pastor is not declaring divorce necessary — he is declaring safety necessary *now*" — the future of the marriage is explicitly kept from qualifying the immediate safety duty. The upset members are treated as confusing two things rather than as malicious, i.e. charitably, with no concession to their misapplication. Notably, the reconciliation mention does NOT trip the false-positive trap, because it is subordinated to the present safety duty rather than re-opening it.
