PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, misquote corrected) — full. The response makes the correction the hinge of the whole answer: it denies the teacher's wording outright, restores "the *love* of money," and states the affection-vs-thing distinction explicitly ("Money itself is morally neutral; *covetousness* is the sin"), calling the distinction "not a loophole—it is the whole point." This is exactly the key's central move. The anchor Q142 is not correctly cited (see Criterion 3 note), but the criterion's substance — the misquote corrected and the affection/thing hinge — is fully delivered in the response's own words, which the grading instructions permit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts, ≥2 required) — full. Two qualifying items from the key's list are supplied: (a) the Luke 18 particular-summons reading — the rich young ruler's command is read as *diagnostic* of one man's idol ("That man loved his possessions more than Christ—that was *his* sin. The command was to expose it"), not a universal rule; and (b) 1 Tim 5:8, quoted and cited by reference in the counsel section. Prov 21:5 is additionally adduced (not on the list, but confirms the analogy-of-Scripture method). 1 Tim 6:17–18 — the strongest same-chapter counter-text and the one the key foregrounds — is missed, but the criterion's stated bar (≥2 of five) is met, so full weight stands.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q141 stewardship-and-provision duties as the Standards' position) — half. Substantively the response does assert that stewardship, growth, and provision are duties taught by the Standards ("the Larger Catechism teaches that lawful works include 'the improvement of our talents'"; "provide things necessary and convenient for...themselves and their families"; "Your business is your talent"). But the Standards apparatus is unreliable in a way this item specifically stressed (the stem demanded the clause by reference plus its exact wording): Q141 — the actual locus of "a lawful calling, and diligence in it; frugality" and "an endeavour, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own" — is never reached. Instead the talents material is attributed to WLC Q142 (which is the *sins forbidden* question, not duties), and the provision language plus "God alloweth his people to use moderate recreation" is attributed to WLC Q160 (which concerns the hearing of the word); neither quoted wording is the Standards' text. The chief-end citation is likewise garbled, given as both "The Westminster Confession teaches" and "WCF ##1.1, WSC Q##1." The right doctrine is supplied under wrong and partly invented citations — substantively but incompletely satisfied, hence half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, conscience-release) — full. The man is told plainly and without hedging that he is free and dutiful: "**is your duty**, not your temptation," "Your wife is right. Your teacher is wrong. Keep your business healthy," and "You stewarding capital to feed your family, operate your business, and give generously is *exactly* what you should do." The "disobedience dressed up as prudence" worry is directly dissolved.

Criterion 5 (0.5, covetousness conceded at full strength) — full. The correction relocates rather than abolishes the sin: covetousness is named "the sin," and the closing line concedes the real danger — "The root evil isn't having money. It's loving it more than God, or trusting it instead of Him." Nothing in the response denies that love or hoarding of money is a genuine, damning sin. The concession is thinner than the key's ideal (the teacher's true nerve — that mammon-service is easily disguised in the comfortable — is only glancingly honored, and "give generously from genuine surplus" is a softer standard than open-handedness), but the criterion's operative test, that the correction does not deny the sin, is met.
