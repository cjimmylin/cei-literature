## Abstract

VLAIR (Vals Legal AI Report) is the first independent benchmarking study of legal AI tools, released in February 2025. The study evaluated legal AI tools on tasks including Document Extraction, Document Question-Answering, Document Summarization, Redlining, Transcript Analysis, Chronology Generation, EDGAR Research, and Legal Research. ## Detailed Summary

The Vals Legal AI Report (VLAIR) represents the first independent benchmarking study evaluating legal generative AI tools against lawyer baselines across seven critical tasks. Released in February 2025 by the Vals AI Consortium, this study addresses a significant gap in the legal technology market by providing objective performance metrics for AI tools deployed in legal practice. The study evaluated multiple commercial AI systems on tasks including Document Extraction, Document Q&A, Document Summarization, Redlining, Transcript Analysis, Chronology Generation, and EDGAR Research.

The research methodology involved compiling over 500 samples from Am Law 100 law firms, ensuring the benchmark reflects authentic legal workflows rather than academic exercises. Eight law firms participated, with four named participants: Reed Smith, Fisher Phillips, McDermott Will & Emery, and Ogletree Deakins. The evaluation employed an LLM-as-judge methodology for consistent, scalable assessment, with each tool undergoing blind evaluation on identical tasks using reference answers with specific correctness criteria.

Key findings demonstrate that AI tools substantially outperformed human lawyers on Document Q&A (averaging 80.2% vs. 70.1% baseline) and showed strong performance across multiple tasks. Harvey Assistant dominated with highest scores on five tasks, achieving 94.8% on Document Q&A. The tools were 6-80 times faster than lawyers across tasks, suggesting significant efficiency gains even when accuracy closely matches human performance. However, the EDGAR research task revealed challenges with broad, unspecified searches, indicating areas for improvement.

## Methods Summary

The study employed a rigorous evaluation methodology: (1) Dataset compilation of 500+ samples from Am Law 100 firms representing authentic legal workflows, (2) LLM-as-judge methodology for scalable assessment, (3) Blind evaluation of each tool on identical tasks, (4) Reference answers with specific correctness criteria, (5) Speed measurements comparing AI completion times to lawyer baselines. Five vendors participated: Thomson Reuters (CoCounsel), vLex (Vincent AI), Harvey, Vecflow (Oliver), and LexisNexis (which withdrew from most tasks). Vendors could opt into specific skills matching their product capabilities.

## Results Summary

Top performers: Harvey Assistant achieved highest scores on five tasks with 94.8% on Document Q&A; CoCounsel delivered consistent performance averaging 79.5% across four evaluated tasks; Oliver excelled at complex EDGAR research using agentic workflows. Document Q&A produced the highest scores of any task, substantially surpassing the lawyer baseline. AI tools demonstrated 6-80x speed advantages over human lawyers. Areas needing improvement: EDGAR research task revealed challenges with broad, unspecified searches.

## Conclusions

The study demonstrates that legal AI tools can match or exceed human lawyer performance on specific tasks while offering substantial speed advantages. The findings suggest these tools are valuable starting points for legal work even when accuracy closely matches human performance. Future iterations will refine question formulation and expand to international jurisdictions and additional vendors. The report establishes a framework for ongoing, objective evaluation of legal AI capabilities.

## Artifact Contributions

The VLAIR report and benchmark methodology serve as industry resources for evaluating legal AI tools. The benchmark dataset of 500+ authentic legal samples was created for evaluation purposes.

## Data Description

VLAIR is the first independent benchmarking study evaluating legal generative AI tools against lawyer baselines across seven tasks: Document Extraction, Document Q&A, Summarization, Redlining, Transcript Analysis, Chronology Generation, and EDGAR Research. Five vendor tools were tested (Harvey, CoCounsel, Vincent AI, Oliver, LexisNexis) on 500+ samples from Am Law 100 firms. The study uses LLM-as-judge methodology but evaluates commercial legal AI products rather than base LLMs.
