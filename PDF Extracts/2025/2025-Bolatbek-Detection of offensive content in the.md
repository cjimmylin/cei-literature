Abstract- Offensive content refers to messages which are socially unacceptable including vulgar or derogatory messages. As the use of 
social media increases worldwide, social media administrators are faced with the challenges of tackling the inclusion of offensive content, to 
ensure clean and non-abusive or offensive conversations on the platforms they provide.  This work organizes and describes techniques used 
for the automated detection of offensive languages in social media content in recent times, providing a structured overview of previous 
approaches, including algorithms, methods and main features used.   Selection was from peer-reviewed articles on Google scholar. Search 
terms include: Profane words, natural language processing, multilingual context, hybrid methods for detecting profane words and deep 
learning approach for detecting profane words. Exclusions were made based on some criteria. Initial search returned 203 of which only 40 
studies met the inclusion criteria; 6 were on natural language processing, 6 studies were on Deep learning approaches, 5 reports analysed 
hybrid approaches, multi-level classification/multi-lingual classification appear in 13 reports while 10 reports were on other related methods. 
The limitations of previous efforts to tackle the challenges with regards to the detection of offensive contents are highlighted to aid future 
research in this area.

1 INTRODUCTION 
ocial media is a group of Internet-based applications 
that build on the ideological and technological 
foundations of Web 2.0 and allow the creation and 
exchange of User Generated Content.  Over time, social 
media has greatly influenced how we live. Its pervasive 
tentacles have weaved its way into every aspect of our 
lives.  Social media is used to enhance various sectors 
including research, marketing, entertainment, and 
education. Social media statistics shows 3.2 billion users 
worldwide. This equates to about 42% of current world 
population and this number is still growing (Mohsin, 
2020). The rise in social media usage can be seen as having 
positive connotations as well as negative concerns such as 
addiction, depression and political polarization (Allcott, 
et al., 2019).  Amongst the negative impact of social media 
is exposure of users to offensive contents.  
Profanity is socially offensive language. Profane words 
comprise, but are not limited to, categories of obscenity, 
indecency, profanity, racial insults, cyber-bullying, hate 
speech and taboos. Offensive languages are rude or 
insulting statements intended to cause someone or a 
group of people to feel resentful, upset or annoyed. 
Example of such words include; absolute dick, bullshit, 
drunk as shit etc. The use of offensive language has 
significant impacted on those who they are directed to. 
Legal scholars noted that offensive utterances harm 
people the same way that physical blows do (Matsuda et 
al., 1993). Victims of online verbal abuse may be 
emotionally or psychologically affected by the sharp 
criticism of comments or even exposed to vulgar words 
or thoughts previously not explored. 
Social media administrators ought to ensure that the 
conversations on the platform for interaction are clean 
and non-abusive. Before the application of machine 
learning to the detection of profanity, there is sole 
dependence on moderators who manually screen all 
posts generated by the website which prove to be time-

2.2 DEEP LEARNING APPROACHES 
Deep learning is a branch of Machine Learning that 
leverages on artificial neural networks (ANNs) to 
simulate the human brain’s functionality (Vadapalli, 
2020).  Deep learning uses artificial neural networks 
designed to imitate human learning and thinking process. 
Mathematically, it involves a process of running a set of 
data through large networks of neurons, each of which 
has an activation function. The neuron is activated if a 
specified threshold is reached and the value is propagated 
through the network. (Mulkar, 2019). Deep learning in 
recent times have been applied to text classification 
algorithms due to it’s potential to reach high accuracy 
with less need of engineered features (Text Classification., 
2020). Unlike natural language processing algorithms, 
deep learning algorithms do not reach a certain threshold 
where adding more training data doesn’t improve their 
accuracy, instead the classifiers improve as more training 
data is added. The main deep learning architectures used 
in text classification includes Convolutional Neural 
Networks (CNN) and Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN)  
A convolutional neural network consists of an input and 
an output layer, as well as multiple hidden layers. The 
hidden layers of a CNN typically consist of a series of 
convolutional layers that convolve with a multiplication 
or other dot product. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) 
are a special type of neural network, which can be thought 
of as the addition of loops to the architecture. RNNs use 
back propagation in the training process to update the 
network weights in every layer. Wu, Fei, & Ji (2020) 
presented an improvement of previous methods of 
aggressive language detection by jointly performing text 
normalization (TN) through an adversial multi-task 
learning framework. A BiLSTM classifier was used as a 
decoder for aggressive language detection. The model 
outperformed other baseline models when experimented 
on four English Aggressive language detection datasets. 
The degree of aggression was reflected in the annotations 
of the dataset as follows: covertly aggressive (CAG), non-
aggressive (NAG) and overtly aggressive (OAG). Some of 
the literatures reviewed are summarized in Table 2.

Cleaning data before they are fed into classifier is an 
essential task. Certain  automatic pre-processing software 
includes Regex (Sood,  Antin & Churchill,  2012), Hadoop

analysis 
can

done 
by

Crowdsourcing is data labeling service provided by 
trained people from different countries. There are many 
good points of using crowdsourcing as saving time and 
internal resources and scalability when working on large 
amount of datasets. Sood,  Antin & Churchill (2012)  
discussed that crowd sourcing service is suitable for 
analyzing texts and coding the contents with high 
efficiency.

3.3 PECULIAR CHALLENGES IN PREVIOUS WORK ON THE 
DETECTION OF OFFENSIVE LANGUAGES 
When a large amount of data volume is needed to work 
with, it is possible to encounter data sparsity problem, 
meaning some data points are missing to observe and 
hence it could affect the efficiency of the system. But this 
can be resolved by applying feature selection techniques.  
Another challenge in text classification is sarcastic 
sentences (Bali & Singh, 2016). More enhanced techniques 
are needed to perform a deep analysis of meaning of 
sentences. Due to the nature of natural language, a word 
might have more than one meaning and might have 
different usages which lead to misassumption of original 
sentences. Previous research was limited to texts 
uploaded by the users and did not attempt to classify texts 
embedded in images. 
 
4 CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK 
In this review, we investigated different techniques used 
for detecting offensive language in social media content.  
Certain limitations were identified which include data 
sparsity problem (Malmasi & Zampieri, 2017), data 
ambiguity 
problems 
(Zulfikar 
et 
al., 
2017) 
and 
classification of sarcastic sentences (Justo et al., 2014; Bali 
& Singh, 2016). These limitations are closely tied to the 
logical aspects of natural language, where context is 
deeper than the literal words in a sentence. Enhanced 
techniques such as incorporation of pattern-based 
features are needed to perform a deep analysis of 
meaning of sentences to enable the automatic detection of 
sarcasm and ambiguous words in statements 
Previous researches on the detection of offensive contents 
have focused solely on the detection of profanity in texts 
directly uploaded by the users and have overlooked the 
detection of profanity in texts embedded in uploaded 
graphics and profane gestures in images. This is a 
significant gap which creates a vast field for research with 
regards to the subject.
