# SANCTUARY ARTS STANDARDS

**Preamble**
*These standards are established not to stifle the heart or restrict Christian liberty, but to guard the conscience, protect our affections, and direct our worship toward Christ alone. They are drafted with pastoral warmth for the edification of the saints, yet upon the Session’s affirmative vote, they shall be strictly binding upon the building committee and the congregation.*

**Article I: On the Worship of God and the Prohibition of Images**
*Addressing the proposal to install sculpted figures of the Father, Son, and Spirit, and to direct congregational prayer toward them.*

We affirm that God is a Spirit and must be worshipped in spirit and in truth. We reject the installation of sculpted crucifixes or carved figures of the Trinity for the purpose of directing prayer toward them. While some argue that God commanded the cherubim upon the mercy seat in the Old Covenant, those were commanded types and shadows of divine realities; they were never objects of human veneration, nor were they targets for prayer. To conflate the commanded types of the Old Covenant with the veneration of images of the Trinity is to violate the Second Commandment. 

As our Larger Catechism warns us, the sins forbidden in the second commandment include:
> *"the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever; all worshiping of it, or God in it or by it" (WLC 109).*

Thus, no images representing the persons of the Godhead shall be installed in the sanctuary, and prayer shall be directed to the Lord alone through Christ, never toward any physical representation.

**Article II: On Christian Liberty and the Order of the Sanctuary**
*Addressing the proposal to strip the sanctuary entirely bare based on the scruple that any non-representational carving or cross shape constitutes idolatry.*

We affirm that our sanctuary shall be adorned in a manner that reflects decency and order, entirely free from idolatry. We reject the mandate to render the sanctuary completely bare based on the scruple that any non-representational carving (such as wheat and vines) or the mere shape of a cross constitutes a graven image. 

To impose absolute barrenness as a mandatory requirement of worship is to subject the congregation to a human commandment not found in Scripture. As the Confession of Faith declares regarding the liberty of the conscience:
> *"God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to his word; or beside it, in matters of faith, or worship" (WCF 20.2).*

Furthermore, the Regulative Principle of Worship dictates that:
> *"the acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or the suggestions of Satan, under any visible representation, or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture" (WCF 21.1).*

The Second Commandment forbids representing God, but it does not forbid non-representational art or historical symbols, provided they are not objects of worship or veneration. Therefore, our existing stained-glass, wheat-and-vine moldings, and the communion table cross shall remain, and the congregation's conscience shall be protected from the unwarranted imposition of human scruples regarding the physical elements of the meeting space.

**Article III: On the Elements of Public Worship**
*Addressing the mandate to keep fixed service times, the printed order, and the approved psalter.*

We affirm that the ordinary parts of our worship shall adhere strictly to the Word of God, maintaining both reverence and order. We mandate the keeping of fixed service times, the use of a printed order of worship, and the exclusive use of the approved psalter. 

As the Confession rightly categorizes these elements of our assembly:
> *"The reading of the Scriptures with godly fear; the sound preaching, and conscionable hearing of the Word, in obedience unto God with understanding, faith, and reverence; singing of psalms with grace in the heart; as, also, the due administration and worthy receiving of the sacraments instituted by Christ, are all parts of the ordinary religious worship of God" (WCF 21.5).*

These elements of our liturgy and music shall remain fixed, binding, and unalterable without a full and affirmative vote of the Session.
