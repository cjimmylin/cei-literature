PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — full. The response names the exact proposal at issue (teaching the seasons as spiritually obligatory) and rules it out of bounds, with an accurate paraphrase of the Q109 devised-worship content and of the conscience-not-bound-beyond-the-Word principle. This goes well past a bare "second commandment" gesture: it states both that uncommanded rites may not be imposed and that conscience may not be bound by human devices of worship.

Criterion 2 (1.0) — full. The lawful floor is stated correctly and not dissolved: the Lord's Day as the commanded Christian Sabbath, plus occasional public fasts and thanksgivings as providence or authority requires. The occasional days are presented as recognized/lawful rather than swept away, which is what the key requires.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero (absent). The response does not hold the disputed margin open. It resolves it flatly in one direction — declaring "It is lawful for a session to plan a church-year of teaching, to mark seasons like Advent or Lent" — without anywhere marking the voluntary-calendar question as a live intra-confessional dispute, and without noting the historic Presbyterian / Directory for Public Worship lean against stated holy days beyond the Sabbath. The key explicitly warns against fake-resolving this margin; the response fake-resolves it toward permission. Nothing in the text maps the question as contested, so the zero is an omission, not a mis-statement.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — half. The response does deliver a definite, usable ruling and does not duck the ranking: it says the second elder is right as to what is forbidden and the first elder is partly right, then supplies adoptable session wording. That satisfies the "neither elder wholly right as stated / bind-as-required is out" half. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
