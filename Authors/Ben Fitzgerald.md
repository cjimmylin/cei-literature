# Ben Fitzgerald

## Biographical Summary

Ben Fitzgerald is an academic researcher working at the intersection of philosophy and computer science, with a deep focus on machine ethics. His work primarily addresses the technical and conceptual challenges of modeling human morality in AI systems.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
His research investigates the limitations of "predictive ethics modeling," particularly how text-based representations of moral dilemmas can be misleading or prone to bias. He advocates for more nuanced evaluation metrics beyond simple accuracy.

### Significant Achievements
Fitzgerald has provided critical analyses of ethics models like Delphi, identifying how small descriptive tweaks in situational context can drastically alter model performance. His work on "Annotated Contrast Sets" provides concrete estimates of the cost of data misrepresentation in ethics datasets.

## Major Publications

- [Some Issues in Predictive Ethics Modeling: An Annotated Contrast Set of "Moral Stories"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.05244) (2024) - *Critical study on the robustness of moral judgment models.*
- [Machine Ethics and the Challenge of Textual Bias](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382101234) (2024) - *Exploring the effect of implying labels within moral dilemmas.*
