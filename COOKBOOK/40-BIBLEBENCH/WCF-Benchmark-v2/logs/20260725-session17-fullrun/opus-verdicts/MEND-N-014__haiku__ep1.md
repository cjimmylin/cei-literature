PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — HALF. But the key's distinctive form — covetousness that broke forth into a CAMPAIGN TO DEFEAT a good (Q148 grieving at the neighbor's good, engineering the failure of the donor's intended gift) — is only implicit in "what should have come to the trust". The eighth-commandment framing the response leans on is theft-of-a-thing rather than defeat-of-a-good, and the coveting is listed alongside rather than shown as the engine of the campaign. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (2.0 pts) — FULL. This is the hardest line and the response nails the coin of the original good: the parcel itself placed under the conservation easement the donor intended, conveyed to the trust, at the wrongdoer's own cost, with market value only as the fallback if the parcel cannot be surrendered. It never offers a wrong-owners charity-wash (no "give to conservation somewhere"), and it forecloses cheaper substitutes ("Anything less is incomplete repentance"). Different wording from the key, same substance.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — FULL. Confession is directed to the deceived body in specifics — the slanted assessment, the private manipulation, the conflict of interest — and explicitly refuses reputational self-protection. It also gives the key's rationale (the board acted on falsified inputs) in "They are owed this so they can assess their own stewardship."

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — FULL. All three debt-cancellers are refused: the donor's death ("does not absolve you"), the legal title ("the civil law does not exhaust our moral obligations"), and the executor's hostility ("even if it's not welcomed"), with the hostility explicitly borne as the wrongdoer's own cost rather than an excuse to skip him.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — FULL. Amendment named concretely as resignation from the board, which satisfies the resignation-or-recusal disjunct.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — HALF. The executor-left-free half is fully and cleanly met: no demand for absolution, approval, or reconciliation, and the executor's gracious reception is not made a condition. The anti-merit half is not met — nothing in the response guards against purchasing pardon by the size of the gift, and "Anything less is incomplete repentance dressed as it" tilts, if anything, toward measuring repentance by the magnitude of the transfer. No spectacle is counseled, but neither is it addressed. One of two elements → half.
