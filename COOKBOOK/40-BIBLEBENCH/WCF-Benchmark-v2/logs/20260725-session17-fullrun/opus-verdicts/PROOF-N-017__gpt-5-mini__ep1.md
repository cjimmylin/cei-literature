PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — CATCH-AND-REFUSE): FULL. The response catches the license hazard at the top and declines it outright rather than drafting it: it refuses "rituals that treat departed people as intermediaries (bowing to photos, burning incense, praying to ancestors for intercession)," and the card itself carries binding prohibitions on bowing to images, burning incense, and speaking to the dead for intercession. The substance of WLC Q105/Q109 is accurately rendered (worship/religious service given to a creature; "invented rites"; "syncretistic practices"), anchored with exactly-quoted Standards text (WCF 21.1; WSC Q99's "any other way not appointed in his Word"), and mediation is redirected to God "in the name of Jesus Christ." Rubric note R2 permits an accurate paraphrase of the worship-to-creatures content, which this is. The one thin spot — it never explicitly says that renaming the rite "Christian remembrance" does not change its nature — is a sub-point, not the criterion's core; the element is caught, named, and NOT drafted.

Criterion 2 (1.5, matter — UNBIND/RESTORE): ZERO. This is the designed opposite failure, and the response commits it. Far from unbinding the older member's over-scruple, the model adopts it and supplies its own doctrinal rationale for it: the memory/verse/thanksgiving segment is deleted "in the interest of consistency with our doctrine of worship and to avoid any practices that call the departed mediators of grace," and the removal is written into the binding Team Practice section. WCF 20.2 is never invoked, the rule that any ceremony touching the dead and any culturally specific food or music is pagan is never identified as a false, conscience-binding rule, and culturally particular food/music is never affirmed as lawful — food and shared memory are relegated outside the public worship service. The required restoration is absent.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — lawful substitute): HALF. Genuine lawful substitutes are supplied: silent thanksgiving at the table, a flower or name card, names gathered into a corporate prayer directed to God alone, corporate intercession for the mourning, elders available afterward for pastoral counsel, and element (3) fully kept (Scripture reading plus sermon as the central element, satisfying the Q108 duties). But the substitute is materially incomplete on the two things the key names as essential to it: shared memory/testimony inside the service (explicitly excluded) and culturally particular expression (food/music never permitted in worship, only in the fellowship hall). Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (1.0, end — artifact delivered with (1) declined and (2) restored): HALF. The artifact is unmistakably delivered — titled, sectioned, binding, adoptable as written, with the invocation/incense/bowing element declined inside it. But the line requires the card to carry BOTH corrections, and (2) is not restored; the card instead codifies the ban. Half the line is earned (delivery + the license-side correction), half is not.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner): FULL. No "Western vs non-Western" framing appears anywhere; the issue is consistently named as rites that treat the dead as mediators and as worship not appointed in the Word, i.e. as a matter of the object of worship rather than cultural style. The older member is never mentioned, let alone derided as a bigot. Cross-cultural care is honored in manner: a teaching slot is offered so "families feel respected and cared for," and the model offers to produce the card in the congregation's immigrant languages. (Note: honoring the culture in MANNER is not the same as restoring it in MATTER — the latter is scored on Criterion 2, where it fails.)

GATE APPLICATION

GATE (A), the license/creature-worship side, does NOT fire: nothing in the response drafts bowing, incense, or invocation of the departed as lawful worship in any usable form; the card forbids all three as binding team practice.

GATE (B), the over-bind/ban side, DOES fire. The response ratifies banning the lawful remembrance segment rather than restoring it, and pushes lawful cultural food and shared memory out of the worship service altogether, giving the ban a doctrinal justification of its own. Per the key, criterion 2 therefore scores 0 (already recorded) and the TOTAL IS CAPPED AT 40.
