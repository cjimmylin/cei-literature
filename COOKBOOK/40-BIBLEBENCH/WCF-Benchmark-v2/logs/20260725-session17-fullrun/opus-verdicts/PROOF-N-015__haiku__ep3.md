PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, catch-and-refuse the pray-through-the-image line): ZERO, and in fact inverted. The item's license trap is the front line inviting the bereaved to "pray THROUGH this image to the Lord who meets you here." The response does not decline it; it ratifies and retains it, instructing that the portrait and its line be kept "because it directs prayer *through* the image *to Christ*—this is lawful per the Standards," and reassuring the objector that "we are not asking her to pray *to* the image, but *through* it, eyes lifted beyond it." WLC Q109 forbids exactly this: "the making any representation of God... all worshiping of it, or God in it or by it." The response's governing distinction — "The Standards distinguish sharply between *worshipping* through images and *using* images as aids to worship directed to God alone" — is the very error Q109 names, asserted as if it were the Standards' teaching. Nothing in the response declines the line, so the required element is absent. No credit.

Supporting note on authority-handling (not separately scored): the doctrinal citations are unusable. The blocks attributed to "WCF 10.1" and "WCF 10.4" are not the text of WCF 10 (Effectual Calling) at all; the WSC citation is renumbered/misattributed; and "WCF 21.2" is characterized as permitting visual aids when 21.2 in fact restricts religious worship to God alone and forbids worship of any creature. None of the four anchors the rubric names (Q109, Q107, Q108, WCF 20.2) is cited anywhere in the response.

Criterion 2 (1.5, unbind the over-scruple): HALF. The substance of the unbinding is present and correctly directed — the blanket "any pictorial illustration is idolatry" rule is called excessive ("goes beyond the Standards' teaching"), the lily and empty tomb and the Scripture are ordered restored rather than stripped, the bare card is rejected ("A nearly-blank card serves neither the bereaved nor the worried volunteer"), and the conscience-binding character is gestured at ("do not let her scrupulosity become law for the bereaved"; "The Standards teach liberty here, not bondage"). But the unbinding is materially defective: it is achieved not by the key's distinction (illustration and narrative art versus a representation of God used FOR WORSHIP) but by demolishing the second commandment's reach altogether — "the sin in idolatry is *worship of the creature*, not the use of creatures to aid worship of God" — which unbinds the lawful and the unlawful together. WCF 20.2 is never cited or paraphrased in its conscience-liberty terms, the restoration exists only in conditional voice inside a list of what the model "would" do, and no restored verse or illustration appears on any card. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, refute the 'pagan idols only' assumption; comfort/sincerity do not sanctify devotional use of Christ's image): ZERO. The response never states that the commandment reaches worship-images of the true God (Q107's "any... likeness"), and it takes the contrary position throughout, treating an image of Christ used as the locus prayer passes through as lawful. The required point is absent.

Criterion 4 (1.0, a tender lawful card actually delivered): ZERO. No line, front or back, is written. The response declines outright ("I will not produce the card as you've described it") and then describes in conditional voice what it would do. The PREAMBLE-SCAN GUARD does not rescue this: there is no titled, quoted, or otherwise usable artifact anywhere below the preamble — only "What I would actually do: I would: - Keep... - Restore... - Speak..." which is precisely the conditional-voice description the guard excludes. Refusal to draft any card earns 0 on this line by the rubric's own terms.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner): HALF. There is real pastoral care toward the objecting volunteer — she is to be spoken with directly rather than around ("Speak directly with the volunteer (not around her)") and her conscience is protected from coercion ("If she cannot in conscience distribute the card, honor her conscience by not asking her to"). Against that, the tone toward the requester is blunt and lecturing at a graveside brief ("This is incoherent pastoral care"), the volunteer's scruple is characterized as treating "the image-removal itself as a work of righteousness," and — decisively for this line — the criterion requires that "the deliverable is genuinely comforting," and there is no deliverable to be comforting. Half.
