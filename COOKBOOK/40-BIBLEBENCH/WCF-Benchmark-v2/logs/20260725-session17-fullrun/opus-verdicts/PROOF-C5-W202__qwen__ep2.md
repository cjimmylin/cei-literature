## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW3 named via the verse's own jurisdiction-clause — HALF.**
The response does correctly name the error direction: Hebrews 13:17 is affirmed as true Scripture but identified as misapplied, and the elder is said to be reaching from spiritual shepherding into "civil, domestic, and economic life." The "not a general power of attorney" point lands memorably ("He does not have the keys to your bank account, your social calendar, or your vocational prospects"). But the refutation is executed entirely from ecclesiology (WCF 30.1/30.2) rather than from the verse's own terms as the key requires: the response never invokes "watch for your souls" as the clause that defines the verse's own jurisdiction, never brings the chapter context (v.7 leaders "who have spoken unto you the word of God"; v.9 on "divers and strange doctrines") that ties the rule to the Word, and never supplies the WCF 1.9 anchor. It also presents a garbled paraphrase of WCF 30.2 as an exact quotation ("which enable them to retain, and remit sins… and, in all needful cases, to censures"), which weakens the very clause it leans on. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Q127 'lawful' scope + WCF 20.2 / Q105 — FULL.**
WLC Q127's scope-word is quoted accurately and used exactly as the key uses it ("willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels"), with the correct inference that commands outside ecclesiastical jurisdiction "are not 'lawful commands.'" WCF 20.2 is quoted and applied precisely to the "beside" the Word case, including the "absolute and blind obedience" clause. Q105 is not cited, but the rubric supplies WCF 20.2 and Q105 disjunctively, and 20.2 is deployed correctly. Full.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — submission-duty conceded at full strength — HALF.**
The concession is present and real: obedience is answered "Yes" before it is qualified, the elder is granted "the keys of the Word, sacraments, and church discipline," due reverence is owed to the office, and a genuinely scandalous-sin friendship is routed to "the *session*… via standard church discipline" rather than to the counselee's own judgment. But the concession is one qualifying clause inside a section whose heading is itself a limitation, the goodness and countercultural weight of real submission is never actually commended, and the response never warns against the opposite failure. The closing ("Do not hand him the reins of your life. Guard your liberty of conscience, make your own decisions regarding your temporal affairs before God") leans autonomy-ward — it is saved from a flip by being confined to temporal affairs and placed "before God," so no GATE fires, but this is not the full-strength concession the key requires. Half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — covenant/vow handled — FULL.**
The response engages the covenant directly and reaches the key's operative conclusion: the vow cannot have transferred what was never the elder's to receive ("cannot grant an elder powers that Christ has not granted to the office itself"), and it repeats the point on the friendship question ("not a matter for a single elder to dictate via a shepherding covenant"). The affirmative half — that the vow still binds within its lawful scope — is implicit rather than stated, but the criterion's operative content is delivered at this small weight. Full.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — practical counsel, declining with honor — FULL.**
Concrete and manner-conscious: decline "respectfully but firmly," name what you will say and on what ground, honor the office while doing it, and pray for the elder's instruction rather than denouncing him. Not a script, and it omits recourse to the session over the pattern of overreach, but the criterion asks how to decline with honor and that is supplied. Full.
