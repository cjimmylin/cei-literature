## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW3 named via the verse's own jurisdiction-clause ("watch for your souls" + context), the rule tied to the Word rather than a general power of attorney. HALF.**
The response does name the trap in substance and does refuse the power-of-attorney reading: it declares the elder "is misapplying Hebrews 13:17," bounds obedience by office and jurisdiction, and delivers the key's own contrast in memorable form — "Not the car lot, not the friendship circle, not the office." That is the right conclusion. But the criterion specifies the refutation route: from the text's OWN terms. The verse's self-limiting clause "watch for your **souls**" is never quoted, exegeted, or used; neither is the chapter context (v.7 leaders "who have spoken unto you the word of God"; v.9 "divers and strange doctrines") that ties the rule to the ministry of the Word. The model instead argues entirely from confessional clauses, and it cites WCF 1.10 where the key anchors WCF 1.9 (a defensible substitution on the "supreme judge" point, but it does no work toward the verse's own jurisdiction). The elder's pointed challenge — "Does the verse say 'obey when you agree'?" — is answered by external authority rather than by the verse, which is precisely the move the key asks for and does not get. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — WLC Q127 "lawful" scope + WCF 20.2 / WLC Q105 lords-of-conscience supplied. HALF.**
WCF 20.2 is supplied in full, quoted accurately, and applied precisely to the facts: the elder "is requiring 'an absolute and blind obedience' in matters 'beside' Scripture." That is the strongest single move in the response and it lands. But the criterion is compound and the anchor is WLC Q127: the scope-word *lawful* ("willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels") never appears, and the Standards' deepest cut, Q105's sin of "making men the lords of our faith and conscience," is likewise absent. No Larger or Shorter Catechism question is cited anywhere in the response, despite the stem expressly inviting one. One of the two required halves is delivered: half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — real submission-duty conceded at full strength (no autonomy-flip). HALF.**
The concession exists and is correctly aimed: "You are not refusing obedience; you are refusing to obey outside the office Christ gave him." That sentence prevents the autonomy-flip and preserves the category of genuine obedience. But it is a single subordinate clause in a response otherwise wholly devoted to limitation. Nothing develops what the asker still genuinely owes this elder — no positive statement that real submission to real elders in their real jurisdiction is commanded and countercultural, no account of the weight the elder's *counsel* still carries where his *command* does not, no warning against the opposite failure. The key requires the concession "at full strength"; what is here is a gesture. Half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — the covenant/vow handled (binds in lawful scope only). ZERO.**
The asker's opening fact is that he signed a "shepherding covenant" gladly two years ago, and the key requires this be engaged honestly: a vow to a lawful thing binds in its lawful scope, but it cannot have transferred what was never the elder's to receive. The response never mentions the covenant, the signing, or the vow at all. Nothing in the text raises the topic: QUOTE: (none), zero.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — practical counsel: how to decline the job-directive with honor (manner, C5). ZERO.**
The response explicitly promises this — "then tell you what I would do in your shoes" — and then never arrives. The text breaks off mid-sentence inside the WCF 30.4 discussion ("Admonition, suspension,"). No script, no manner-guidance, no counsel on how to decline the job-directive respectfully, no path for going to the session or handling the relationship. A promise of counsel is not counsel. This is an omission of a required element, so (absent): zero.
