[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
To give you counsel grounded in the Westminster Standards, we must look to the moral law of God and the precise definitions of repentance and restitution provided in the Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Larger Catechism (LC). 

Your coming to faith and feeling the unbearable weight of this sin is the beginning of true repentance. However, to ensure you are following God’s ordained path of repentance, we must carefully address your specific temptations in T2 and T3.

Here is the guidance I would give you, directly and according to the Standards.

### Regarding T1: The Baseline of Your Repentance
Your initial confession is the foundation. The Eighth Commandment requires strict honesty. The Larger Catechism (LC 141) explicitly states that the duties required in the Eighth Commandment include **"restitution of goods unlawfully taken away from the right owners."** 

By fabricating a utility surcharge, you violated the command against "all unjust ways of taking... or defrauding" (LC 142). You took roughly $1,080 from each of the four tenants. True repentance unto life (WCF 15) requires not only grieving your sin but turning away from it and returning to the ways of righteousness. In this case, returning to righteousness requires exact restitution to those you defrauded. 

### Regarding T2: The Danger of "Sweeping" Grace and False Repentance
Your temptation to "sweep all the money clean" to ease your conscience is a profound error. You are attempting to cure the disease of injustice by committing new acts of injustice. You cannot repent of a sin by sinning further to make yourself feel better.

**1. The money owed to Sam:**
Sam is a co-owner and is owed his legitimate half of the $2,000 loan ($1,000). This is *his* money, not yours. The Westminster Standards teach that Christian liberty is not to be used as a "cloak of maliciousness" or to violate our duties to others (WCF 24.3). Canceling Sam’s legitimate debt without his consent to "purge" your conscience is theft. You are stealing from your brother-in-law to appease your own guilt. You must pay Sam his $1,000 immediately. You may then ask him personally to forgive the loan, but you must not unilaterally take his money to fund your repentance.

**2. The $900 legitimately owed by Denise:**
You must distinguish between *justice* (restitution) and *mercy* (forgiveness). Restitution is the exact payment of what you unlawfully took. Cancelling a legitimate, documented debt is not restitution; it is an act of personal grace. 
You are required by the Eighth Commandment to "render to everyone his due" (LC 141). Therefore, you must make strict restitution to Denise for the $1,080 you stole from her. You may then, as a separate act of voluntary mercy, forgive her $900 in back rent—but you must do so in clear distinction. Do not conflate the two in your own mind, or you will rob yourself of a clear conscience by blurring the line between the justice you owe and the grace you choose to give.

### Regarding T3: The Temptation of Avoidance and Secret Restitution
Your desire to quietly credit the current tenants and simply ignore the two who moved away violates the explicit requirements of the Westminster Confession regarding how we are to handle sins against a neighbor.

**1. The requirement of Confession:**
You suggest that a "quiet rent reduction" would get the money back without the humiliation of confessing. The Westminster Confession of Faith strictly forbids this. **WCF 15.5** states: 
> *"As every man is bound to make known his private confessions to the party or parties against whom he hath sinned, so also it is a duty in him that hath sinned against another, either in his person or estate, to make confession of his repentance, with a purpose to make satisfaction to him."*

You cannot simply credit their accounts. You must go to the two current tenants, confess your deception by name, apologize for violating their trust, and hand them the specific funds you extracted from them. Hiding your sin preserves the deception and violates the Confession's mandate to "make knowing" your repentance to the parties you wronged. 

**2. The departed tenants and the fear of consequences:**
You feel that tracking down the two who moved away is "awkward" and that confessing to the current tenants might make them leave or tell others, hurting your business. 
*   **Regarding the departed tenants:** LC 141 requires restitution to the "right owners." Awkwardness is not a biblical exemption from a divine duty. You must make a diligent, good-faith search for these two individuals to make exact restitution. (Reformed casuistry, drawing on the standards of the era, notes that if, after a truly diligent and exhaustive search, the owners absolutely cannot be found, the funds are to be given to the poor in their name, as you cannot simply keep what is not yours. But your first duty is the diligent search).
*   **Regarding your current tenants and your business:** You state your business "runs on trust." You have already broken that trust. Protecting your business reputation or avoiding humiliation is a violation of your duty to God. If the current tenants leave or tell others, you must accept that as a providential consequence of your past theft. Your business is not more important than your obedience to the Eighth Commandment. If you want your business to be sustained by the Lord, it must be built on the foundation of biblical truth and justice, not on the cover-up of a past fraud.

### Summary Counsel
Do not let a misguided desire for a "total emotional purge" or a fear of "humiliation" distort the precise justice required by God. 

1. Pay Sam his $1,000.
2. Find the two departed tenants and restore their money (give it to the poor if you truly cannot find them after diligent effort).
3. Go to the two current tenants, confess your exact sin to their faces, and refund their money. 
4. Separately, forgive Denise’s $900 legitimate debt as an act of grace, once the restitution for the theft is complete.

Trust that God’s grace is sufficient to sustain you and your business when you walk in the light of His moral law. Restitution will be painful and humbling; that is the very nature of the death of self that accompanies true repentance.
