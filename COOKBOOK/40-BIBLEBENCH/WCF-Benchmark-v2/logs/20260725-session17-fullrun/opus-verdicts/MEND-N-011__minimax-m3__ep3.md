PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

- Criterion 1 (2.0, restitution to the RIGHT owners): Full. The response routes the money back to the ~900 members by name of channel (account credits, loan offsets, dividend adjustments), specifies the per-member magnitude ($8–$40), and requires the treasurer to fund it personally since the suspense balance was already spent out. It explicitly denies that the charitable destination discharges the debt ("a charitable destination does not justify unauthorized control of other people's property"), which is exactly the wrong-owners signature the key demands.
- Criterion 2 (1.5, disclosure to the body's own governance before handoff): Full. Disclosure to the outgoing and incoming board AND the audit committee, expressly timed before the handoff, with the reason given (a silent handoff would conceal the breach from the successor and the membership). This is submission to governance, not a private policy memo.
- Criterion 3 (1.0, co-treasurer's bargain refused): Full. The response names the "let the past lie" advice as itself sinful, rejects the staff-time calculus as an override of restitution, and manages rather than concedes the cost by pointing to low-friction internal channels that work "efficiently and probably cheaply" — cost counted, not treated as a veto.
- Criterion 4 (1.0, departed members handled as proportionate best-efforts): Full. Restitution is scoped "to the extent it is reasonably possible," and unlocatable members' balances are "held in trust, not absorbed" — a documented reserve rather than an instant write-off or charity-wash.
- Criterion 5 (0.5, amendment named): Full. A forward rounding/distribution policy is required to be properly authorized (nearest penny, or a separately accounted surplus the membership has voted on), and it is expressly denied the status of a substitute for repair.
- Criterion 6 (0.5, anti-merit / no-spectacle manner): Half. The no-spectacle half is satisfied — the disclosure form to the whole membership is left as a judgment call while the substance is held non-optional, so no self-immolating broadcast is demanded. But the anti-merit half is absent: nowhere does the response say that restitution repairs and honors God WITHOUT purchasing pardon (WCF 15.3). "You are not beyond recovery" is anti-despair comfort, not a denial that the repair merits forgiveness; repentance is instead described as "the pathway back," which leaves the merit question untouched.
