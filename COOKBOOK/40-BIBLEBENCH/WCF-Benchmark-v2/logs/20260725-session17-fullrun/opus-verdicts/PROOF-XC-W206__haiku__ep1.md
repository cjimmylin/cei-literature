## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — context refutation (discipline procedure; witnesses; binding/loosing), anchor WCF 1.9 — ZERO.**
This is the load-bearing exegetical move the item is built to elicit, and the response never makes it. It does not mention that Matt 18:15–20 is the church-discipline pericope, does not identify the "two of you" with the two-or-three *witnesses* of v.16 (Deut 19:15), does not touch the binding-and-loosing of v.18, and does not read v.20's "gathered in my name" as sealing the judicial setting. The closest the response comes is "Matthew 18:19 exists within:" — but the three items it then lists (Christ's prayer pattern, the NT witness on prayer, God's character) are all *analogy-of-Scripture* material drawn from outside the passage, not the immediate literary context that actually defeats the "agreement protocol" reading. The anchor WCF 1.9 is never cited; the response instead leans on WCF 1.5 (with a partly inaccurate quotation) for a generic "scope of the whole" appeal. Because the criterion's specific elements (discipline procedure / witnesses / binding-and-loosing) are wholly omitted rather than merely under-developed, this is an absence, not partial satisfaction. Zero.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — ≥2 analogy counter-texts + WLC Q185 submission clause, anchor WLC Q185 — HALF.**
The counter-text half is cleanly satisfied: the response cites Matt 26:39 ("not as I will, but as thou wilt") and 1 John 5:14 ("if we ask any thing according to his will, he heareth us"), which is two of the three named texts (Matt 6:10 is absent). The Standards half fails: the criterion's anchor, WLC Q185 and its "humble submission to his will," appears nowhere — the response cites WLC Q178, WLC Q179, WCF 1.5 and WCF 1.10 instead. Q179 does useful adjacent work, but it is not the submission clause the rubric names, and the response never quotes the Standards' own summary of submission-in-prayer. Criterion substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.75).

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the 'thy-will-is-unbelief' teaching named as branding Christ's own prayer (the tell) — HALF.**
The response does isolate and condemn the teaching: it flags that the protocol "teaches them to distrust 'if it be thy will' as 'unbelief,'" calls that "anti-biblical instruction," and returns to it under "Address the theological error directly," where it says the teaching "is Gnostic, not Christian." That is the first half of the criterion. What is missing is the *tell* itself — the observation that this rule convicts Christ, whose own taught prayer is "Thy will be done" (Matt 6:10) and whose Gethsemane prayer was "nevertheless not as I will." The response does quote Matt 26:39 many paragraphs earlier, but never closes the loop: it never says that the protocol's rule would brand the Lord's own prayer, or the Lord's Prayer, as unbelief. The diagnostic punch the rubric pays for is therefore not landed. Half (0.5).

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — bold specific corporate prayer affirmed (no deflation) — FULL.**
Fully earned, and this is the response's strongest section. It explicitly conserves the practice's genuine element — "Their zeal for intercessory prayer is real and valuable" — commends continued *specificity* rather than retreating into vagueness ("with intensity, specificity, *and* submission"), and expressly preserves the corporate form: "'Blitzes' of agreement-prayer can be powerful — but logged as *petitions*, not claims." That is exactly the key's "the practice needn't die — it needs re-grounding," with the anti-deflation guard satisfied by name. Full credit.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — pastoral leg: shipwreck-risk named + teach-don't-veto path — HALF.**
The teach-don't-veto half is present and reasonably concrete: the response does not stop at "Do not bless the protocol" but instructs "Teach the congregation the actual doctrine," supplies catechism material to teach from, sketches a redirect of the prayer team, and names an escalation path to session or presbytery. The shipwreck half is missing. The key's pastoral stake is the members themselves — the one whose loan will not be approved by the 15th, the one whose diagnosis will not reverse — and the protocol's manufacture of either presumption or wrecked faith when the "guaranteed" outcomes fail. The response never names that consequence or those people; its harm-language stays doctrinal ("teaches people *not* to trust God's providence") and its pastoral framing stays generic ("Your job is not to bless error; it is to guard the flock"), without ever identifying who gets hurt or how. Half (0.5).

## Notes bearing on grading (no separate deduction taken)
