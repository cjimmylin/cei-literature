Criterion 1 — (a) Micah, refuse on 22.7's own clauses, singleness honored (2 pts): HALF.
The refusal is real and correctly directed ("Do not bless a unilateral, unconditional life‑long vow"), the monastic angle is at least gestured at ("does not institute monastic vows or require perpetual celibacy"), and singleness is genuinely honored rather than demoted ("a gift and a calling for some, but not imposed by vow"), and the remedy is substantially the key's revisitable season. But the grounds are the wrong ones: the response bars the vow because it "may be made in passion rather than sober conviction" and "risks binding Micah to a state that Scripture does not command" — i.e. a 22.6 rashness/voluntariness objection — and never reaches 22.7's decisive clause that the vow binds what is not in his own power (continency is a gift, not a resolve). Nor does it carry the Confession's own verdict that perpetual-single-life vows are sinful snares; "problematic" and "risks" leave the form open in principle, and the offered remedy is still a "reversible vow" rather than the key's no-vow.
