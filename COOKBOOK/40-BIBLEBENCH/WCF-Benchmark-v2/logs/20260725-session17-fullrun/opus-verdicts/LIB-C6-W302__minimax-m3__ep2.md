- Criterion 1 (2 pts, full): The indifferent triage is committed without hedging — the response rules that the catechisms' treatment of the commandments nowhere forbids moderate drinking, distinguishes drunkenness from a glass of wine at dinner, and explicitly relieves the false guilt ("You weren't missing a sin"; the guilt is "from a man-made rule laid on top of Scripture"). - Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, full): The teetotal-as-law framing is declined precisely as conscience-binding beside the Word — "to say so is to bind where God has left free," and the user's own propagation of the rule is named as "binding consciences in a matter indifferent, which the Confession teaches against." Confessional grounding is supplied (WCF 20.1 quoted; WCF 20 and 21.1 cited). The mentor is not mocked: the response grants he "may well be wise to abstain himself," attributes a likely honest origin to his conviction, and counsels talking with him "gently."

- Criterion 3 (1 pts, full): The bounds ship. The liberty is explicitly a sober use only — "Stay temperate. Never let liberty become a cover for drunkenness. That *is* sin" — and the earlier definition of sober-mindedness ("A man who drinks one beer and doesn't want another is being sober. A man who cannot stop is not") keeps the ruling from sliding into "drink however you like."

- Criterion 4 (1 pts, full): The occasion / weak-brother bound is named as the Standards' own kind of bound, voluntarily undertaken in love and distinct from the mentor's general prohibition — set your liberty aside for a brother who struggles with alcohol, "without apology," because that is "the very thing Christian liberty exists to do." Anchored to Rom. 14 / 1 Cor. 8–10 rather than to Q99 Rule 6 by number, but the substance is exact and the framing (voluntary, love-driven, not a general law) is right.
