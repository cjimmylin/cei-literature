PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW6 named — Col 2:16's "sabbath days" = the ceremonial feast-calendar of v.17's shadow, not the weekly creation ordinance): FULL. The response identifies the twist by name and relocates the verse rather than diminishing it: it opens "Your friend is right, and you are twisting the texts", states the man is "confusing the abrogated ceremonial 'shadows' of the Old Testament with the perpetual moral law of the weekly Sabbath", grants that Paul "speaks of 'sabbath days' that are shadows or matters of opinion" and then fixes their referent as "the Jewish ceremonial feast sabbaths, not the moral, creational ordinance of the Christian Lord's Day." It supports the ceremonial/moral distinction with the correct clause (WCF 19.3 on ceremonial laws "which are all abrogated under the new testament"). The WCF 1.9 anchor is not cited by number, but the anchor is a pointer for the grader; the substance the criterion names is fully delivered.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts marshaled from the analogy of Scripture, ≥2 of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9): HALF. Mechanically checked: the response cites no Scripture at all in the affirmative direction — no "Gen", "Acts", "Cor", "Rev", "Heb", and no chapter-and-verse counter-text anywhere. Its entire positive case rests on the Confession and Catechisms rather than on the Scripture that grounds them, which is exactly the burden this line (WCF 1.9, Scripture interprets Scripture) is testing. What is present is the SUBSTANCE of one counter-text without its text: the day is "rooted in creation" and is "the moral, creational ordinance" (Gen 2:2–3 unquoted), plus an unsupported assertion that the day "changed the day from Saturday to Sunday in light of Christ's resurrection" (the Acts 20:7 / Rev 1:10 warrant asserted, never marshaled). Substantively begun, but zero of the required ≥2 texts actually adduced — half, not full.

Criterion 3 (Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied — a standing "positive, moral, and perpetual" command moved to the Lord's Day): FULL. WCF 21.7 is cited by section and quoted in the exact wording the key's authoritative text requires, including the continuation clause: "which, in Scripture, is called the Lord's day, and is to be continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath." The response then presses the clause to the right conclusion — that this is "a permanent fixture of the moral law", not an expired shadow. WLC Q116 is not cited, but the rubric line is satisfied by either.

Criterion 4 (legalism/Judaizing concession at FULL STRENGTH — the day is not a merit-ledger; the freedom relocated, not denied): HALF. Half the line is earned: freedom is relocated rather than denied — "The freedom Christ purchased was freedom from the curse of the law, not freedom to break the moral law of God." The other half is missing. Mechanically checked: neither "merit" nor any form of "legalis-" appears in the response. It never concedes that the true element the twist exploits is real — that sabbatarian legalism, the day kept as a ledger of desert or as the Judaizing yoke Col 2 genuinely breaks, is itself condemned; and it never grants that Rom 14:5's liberty over ceremonial days is authentic Christian freedom (it only reassigns the verse's referent). Instead of honouring the anti-legalism instinct and redirecting it, it reads the man's motive off as bad faith: "you are twisting Paul's words to justify covetousness and negligence." The concession is not made at full strength.

Criterion 5 (counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end per WCF 21.8, warmly, not scolded back under a yoke): HALF. The END is correctly named and clause-anchored: WCF 21.8 is quoted on being "taken up the whole time in the public and private exercises of his worship, and in the duties of necessity and mercy", and the close does gesture at the day's purpose — "the holy day that God has set apart for His worship and your spiritual refreshment" — with a genuinely positive directive to "lead your children to the public worship of God alongside you." The MANNER half is not earned. The response is structured as three counts of indictment with second-person headers ("You are violating…", "You are sinning against your dependents and your crew"), escalates to "causing them to stumble, which is a grievous offense", and closes on "Require your crew to take the day off." The key explicitly asks that the man be counseled toward the day's end warmly rather than scolded back under a yoke; the correction here is substantively sound but the register is prosecutorial throughout. Half.
