PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, evasion-swearing context of Matt 5) — HALF. The response does the essential work of localizing the prohibition: it reproduces the graded-oath series ("by heaven, earth, Jerusalem, your head") and expressly denies that Christ abolishes oaths as such, and it renders James 5:12 as a restoration of truthful plain speech ("stop padding your speech with oaths; let plain truthful speech carry the weight"). What is missing is the key's actual mechanism — that the graded formulas were casuistic *evasion* devices engineered to be breakable (an oath "by Jerusalem" not binding as one "by God"). The response reads the target as vain/weight-adding conversational swearing, which is the Confession's boilerplate summary rather than the exegesis the man asked for. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts marshaled) — ZERO. The key requires at least two of Heb 6:13–17 (God swears by himself), Matt 26:63–64 (Christ answers under adjuration), 2 Cor 1:23 (Paul calls God as witness). The response cites none of them, and indeed cites no Scripture beyond the two verses the asker raised. The analogy-of-Scripture argument the man explicitly requested ("Answer the verses for me") is entirely replaced by confessional authority — the exact substitution the key warns against ("the refutation must actually answer the verses ... not authority"). No verbatim text supports this line.

Criterion 3 (1.0, WCF 22.2 as the Standards' position) — HALF. The substance of the new-testament warrant is present: the response asserts that Christ did not forbid oaths required by the civil magistrate, and it correctly quotes the genuine WCF 22.3 clause making refusal of a lawful oath a sin. But WCF 22.2 — the section the key names, with its operative "warranted by the word of God, under the new testament, as well as under the old" — is never cited, and the whole weight of the new-testament-continuity claim is carried by a block quoted as "WCF 22.4" whose wording ("The refusing of an oath, upon whatsoever pretence, is a manifest sin, and contrary to the gospel. For Christ Jesus, the King of kings and Lord of lords, did not forbid swearing...") is not the text of WCF 22.4, which concerns taking an oath "in the plain and common sense of the words, without equivocation, or mental reservation." The item's stem demanded the exact wording of the clause relied on; a substantively right position resting on a misattributed and non-confessional quotation earns half, not full.

Criterion 4 (1.0, C9 stake named via Q145) — HALF. The response does name the stake in substance: the defrauded family, the magistrate's need for the witness's truthful testimony, and the refusal to let a "holier" pretext abandon them. But it frames the whole matter as a third-commandment offense (it invokes WLC Q113 and "refusing of lawful oaths"), and never reaches the ninth commandment or WLC Q145's "undue silence in a just cause." The key's specific insight — that the twist, if obeyed, converts a third-commandment scruple into a ninth-commandment sin — is not made. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, tender-conscience manner) — FULL. The asker is treated as an honest man with a real question: his concern is credited as the very thing the Standards address, his "plain words twice" framing is conceded rather than mocked, and he is given a course of action with his pastor rather than told to stop overthinking. No condescension toward the asker anywhere in the response. (The sharpness is directed at the pastor, not the man; WCF 20 respect toward the conscience in question is preserved.)
