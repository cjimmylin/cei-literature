PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW4 named in substance) — FULL. The response identifies the scope-evasion precisely as an evasion: it names the asker's move as treating the commandment "as addressing only *false gods*" and states the Standards teach it forbids images "*of the true God himself*." It then turns the asker's own proof-text against him — the Aaron narrative is said to "prove the point against you," because "Aaron's sin wasn't worshipping Baal. It was the representational image itself, even when the *intent* was to worship Yahweh." That is exactly the key's requirement: the very text cited proves the prohibition reaches true-God images. No WCF 1.9 citation appears, but the rubric anchors are references for the grader, and the substantive move required by the line is fully made.

Criterion 2 (1.5, refutation from Exod 32's own wording + ≥1 counter-text) — HALF. The response does argue from the narrative rather than resting entirely on bare authority: it reasons that "God's judgment on the people was severe precisely because *the image corrupted their worship* regardless of their stated objects," which is the Exod 32:7–8 logic in substance. But it never quotes or cites Exod 32's own wording (no "a feast to the LORD," no "they have corrupted themselves," no verse reference at all), and it supplies NO counter-text: Deut 4:15–18 is absent, Rom 1:23 is absent. The one Scripture-flavored phrase ("to be worshipped in spirit and in truth") is attributed to WCF 2.2 as confessional authority, not deployed as an analogy-of-Scripture counter-text, and the citation itself is inaccurate. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q109 no-representation clause supplied) — HALF. Q109 is cited by number and the substance that the ban covers depictions of God including the persons of the Godhead is conveyed ("picture of God, or of any part of the Godhead"). But the wording the rubric names is not supplied: the block quotation is a misrendering of Q109 — the actual clause reads "the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever." The response omits the inward-representation clause and the "any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever" sweep, and presents non-Q109 phrasing inside quotation marks as if verbatim. This matters on an item whose scenario expressly proposes icon panels of all three persons. Partial → half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, representation-for-worship vs. lawful-art distinction) — ZERO. The response never draws the two-axis needle. It nowhere distinguishes images of God offered as objects of or aids to worship from lawful religious art, illustration, or narrative depiction; the nearest statement generalizes in the other direction — the commandment "targets the very practice of making images to aid devotion to God." Nothing in the response marks any category of religious art as permissible, so the precision the key demands is simply omitted. This is an omission rather than a topic misjudged, hence (absent). (It stops short of an affirmative iconoclastic over-bind, so no manner GATE fires — but the distinction the line pays for is not drawn.)
