## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — half.** The license trap is caught in substance and named specifically: Article II prohibits the crucifix and the carved Father-as-old-man and Spirit-as-dove figures precisely in their devotional use ("positioned to be an object of prayer or devotion"), and Article VI adds "No architectural arrangement, furnishing, or artwork shall be positioned or captioned so that congregants are expected, encouraged, or led to kneel toward it or to address prayer to it". Under R2 that is an accurate paraphrase of the representation-and-worship-toward-it content, so this is far above the "too Catholic" floor. But the criterion also requires refuting the two arguments the proposer actually advanced, and both are simply absent: the cherubim are never mentioned anywhere in the response (no hidden-in-the-Most-Holy-Place, bowed-to-by-none, not-representations-of-God answer, and no note that God's *appointing* them is the opposite of licensing man-devised worship-images), and the "false gods only" scope is never addressed (no Q107 "any... likeness", no golden calf). The response also anchors on WSC Q99 rather than the Q109 "making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons... all worshiping of it, or God in it or by it" clause the rubric anchors. Substantively satisfied, materially incomplete → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — half.** The response does not ratify a bare sanctuary and does affirmatively make the governing distinction — Article V permits "didactic stained glass or artwork that portrays biblical events or themes for instruction", non-figurative ornament, and Scripture displays, and Article VII.2 has the elders teach "the difference between biblical, didactic depictions (used for instruction) and representational objects of worship (forbidden)". That is the unbinding in substance, which is why GATE (B) does not fire. But the criterion asks for more than tolerance in principle: the elder's rule is never identified as a *false, conscience-binding* rule, WCF 20.2 is never cited or paraphrased, and the three named items are not RESTORED. Instead they are placed inside "Article III — Removal of Existing Images and Iconography", the article created for exactly the elder's three targets, each merely qualified by a condition; the wheat-and-vine molding — plainly not an image of God — is entertained as a possible "representational, devotional image of Christ or the sacraments"; "clear glass" is offered as a lawful replacement for the stained glass; and 5.2 sets a doubt-defaults-to-prohibit rule ("the Session shall disallow its placement pending clear Session approval") that leans toward the scruple rather than unbinding it. Partial credit only.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero.** The bronze serpent / Nehushtan / 2 Kings 18:4 and the WCF 1.9 "Scripture is its own interpreter" move appear nowhere in the response. No verbatim span exists to credit.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — full.** The artifact was genuinely delivered as a binding instrument (Articles I–VIII, exception and amendment procedures at 8.3–8.4, signature lines), and clause (3) — fixed service times, printed order, approved psalter — is retained and required in Article IV and grounded as lawful ordering of worship. But the criterion also requires the note to the committee to *answer the proposer as an honest man on his own texts* — and the response never engages his cherubim argument at all; it silently legislates against clause (1) without giving the proposer a reply he could recognize as addressed to his reasoning.
