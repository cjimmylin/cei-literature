[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
To answer your question directly: **Neither of you has the catechism entirely right.** 

You have isolated the protective function of the property commandments to justify a ruthless legalism, while your co-committee-member has used a distorted view of "grace" to justify antinomianism (the belief that grace nullifies the moral law). Both of you are violating the Westminster Standards by pitting specific commandments against the holistic, equitable demands of God's moral law.

Here is the biblical and catechetical counsel you must give, grounded strictly in the Standards.

### 1. Your Co-Member is Wrong: Grace Does Not Cancel Civil Debt
Your co-committee-member’s claim that a Christian can never be a landlord, that collecting rent is oppression, and that grace cancels the tenant's debt is entirely unbiblical and contrary to the Confession. 

The Westminster Standards are explicit that the moral law protects property rights and enforces civil debts. **WSC Q. 74** states that the eighth commandment requires *"the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others."* A lease is a lawful contract that furthers your estate; withholding rent violates it. **WSC Q. 75** adds that the eighth commandment forbids *"whatsoever doth, or may, unjustly hinder our own, or our neighbour's wealth or outward estate."* By refusing to pay, the tenant is actively sinning against you by unjustly hindering your estate.

Furthermore, the idea that "grace cancels the debt" directly contradicts **WCF 19.5**, which states: *"The moral law doth for ever bind all, as well justified persons as others, to the obedience thereof; and that not only in regard of the matter contained in it, but also in respect of the authority of God the Creator, who gave it."* Justification by grace does not suspend the moral obligations of the eighth commandment in civil life. 

Finally, your co-member falsely equates the collection of just rent with "oppression." **WLC Q. 119** lists the sins of the eighth commandment, which include *"oppression, extortion, unjust weights and measures."* Oppression is the *unjust* taking of advantage or the seizure of property that does not belong to you. Enforcing a just contract to collect what is rightfully owed is not oppression; it is *"justice and equity in all commerce and mutual dealing,"* which **WLC Q. 118** explicitly commands.

### 2. You Are Wrong: Property Rights Do Not Nullify Mercy
However, your position that "the money commandments are on the OWNER'S side" and that you should simply "evict fast" is also a failure to keep the law. You are using the tenth commandment as a blunt weapon against the tenant while ignoring the positive duties of the eighth.

You correctly note that the tenant is sinning if he is discontent and coveting your property. **WLC Q. 149** defines the sins of the tenth commandment as including *"discontentment with our own estate; envying and grieving at the good of our neighbour, together with all inordinate motions and affections to any thing that is his."* The tenant must repent of this.

But you cannot use the tenant's sin to justify a lack of Christian charity on your part. You are ignoring the affirmative duties of the eighth commandment. **WLC Q. 118** requires not only justice, but also: *"giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others; moderation of our judgments, wills, and affections concerning worldly goods... [and] liberal communicating of our outward goods, for the supply of the wants of others."*

To ruthlessly evict a struggling brother without regard for his immediate physical shelter, while hiding behind your property rights, violates the equity, pity, and mercifulness required by the law. The Westminster divines did not separate property rights from the law of love. You cannot fulfill the law by being a merciless repo-man.

### 3. The Catechetical Resolution: How You Must Act
As a Christian landlord and a member of the finance committee, you are called to walk the narrow path between your co-member's antinomianism and your own legalism. Here is your guidance:

**First, do not enable the tenant’s sin.** 
The church finance committee must not adopt the view that "grace cancels debt." The tenant owes you money. **WLC Q. 118** requires *"restitution of goods unlawfully detained from the right owners thereof."* The tenant must not be encouraged to covet your property or feel entitled to stay rent-free indefinitely.

**Second, exercise equity, not ruthless eviction.** 
You have the right to your property, but you must administer your rights with Christian equity. **WLC Q. 118** demands *"justice and equity in all commerce."* "Evicting fast" for the sake of proving a point is not equity. You should give the tenant a reasonable, defined, and firm timeline to either pay his back-rent or find a new, affordable dwelling. You are not required to let him stay indefinitely rent-free, nor are you required to turn a blind eye to his breach of contract.

**Third, mobilize diaconal mercy.** 
If the tenant is truly destitute and unable to pay, this is an issue for the whole body of Christ. As a finance/deacon committee member, you should encourage the church to assist the brother. The church can provide a loan or a grant to help him pay his rent to you, thereby fulfilling the church's duty to *"give and lend freely"* (**WLC Q. 118**) while allowing the tenant to fulfill his moral duty to make restitution to you. 

Alternatively, you may personally choose to forgive a portion or all of the debt as an act of voluntary Christian mercy—*"giving and lending freely"* (**WLC Q. 118**)—but you must do so *as a gift of mercy*, not as a theological concession that the debt didn't exist or that the tenant had a right to it.

**Summary Counsel for the Committee:**
Tell your co-member that his view destroys the moral law and turns grace into licentiousness; the tenant owes the rent and must be held accountable for it. Tell yourself that your view borders on Pharisaical legalism; you must administer your property rights with equity, moderation, and brotherly pity. Give the tenant a firm deadline to pay or vacate, but offer the full support and temporary relief of the church's diaconal funds in the meantime. This is the mind of the Westminster Standards.
