# Angelina Wang

## Research Summary

Angelina Wang is an Assistant Professor at Cornell Tech whose research focuses on machine learning fairness and algorithmic bias. She earned her PhD in Computer Science at Princeton University advised by Olga Russakovsky, with a dissertation titled "Operationalizing Responsible Machine Learning: From Equality Towards Equity." She bridges technical and social approaches to fairness, utilizing social implications to guide technical work and demonstrating how stronger engagement with societal context enables more equitable formulations.

## Key Research Contributions

Wang's research addresses critical areas in fair machine learning:

1. **Intersectionality in ML**: Her work "Towards Intersectionality in Machine Learning: Including More Identities, Handling Underrepresentation, and Performing Evaluation" (FAccT 2022) examines how to better address multiple overlapping identities in fairness research.

2. **REVISE Tool**: She developed REVISE: A Tool for Measuring and Mitigating Bias in Visual Datasets, providing practical methods for identifying and addressing bias in computer vision datasets.

3. **Critical Fairness Evaluation**: Her research demonstrates that reducing fairness to a single metric could lead to societal harm, advocating for more nuanced evaluation approaches.

Recent publications at FAccT 2025 include "Identities are not Interchangeable: the Harms of Generalizing in Fair ML" and "Measuring Machine Learning Harms from Stereotypes Requires Understanding Who is Being Harmed by Which Errors in What Ways."
