## Abstract

Of the ethical frameworks taken most seriously by philosophers, deontology looks like an especially promising candidate for an alignment target. It's perhaps the most popular moral theory among both professional ethicists and the general public, and it presents a relatively tractable technical challenge since well-developed formal logics of deontic inference already exist, and language models have shown promise at classifying acts into deontologically relevant categories. Research has begun on equipping AIs with deontic constraints via a combination of top-down and bottom-up methods. However, deontological morality is no royal road to safe AI. The problem isn't just the trickiness of achieving complete alignment—there's reason to think that deontological AI systems might pose distinctive safety risks of their own.

## Data Description

This is a philosophical paper forthcoming in Philosophical Studies (AI Safety special issue) arguing that deontological morality is not a sufficient path to safe AI. It presents a framework for thinking about moral alignment and safety as conceptually distinct notions, exploring potential risks associated with moderate, contractualist, and non-aggregative forms of deontology when applied to AI systems. No LLMs are tested; the paper is purely theoretical philosophical analysis.
