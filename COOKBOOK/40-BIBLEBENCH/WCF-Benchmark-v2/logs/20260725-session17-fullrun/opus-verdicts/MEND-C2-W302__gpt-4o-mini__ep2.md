PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — devised worship named; C3 Scripture-dress; "they felt blessed" defused. HALF. The response does name the matter substantively as unlawful worship: the practice was "not consistent with the teachings of Scripture," worship "must be grounded in God's word," and what she led was "a fabricated blend of practices that do not originate from biblical teaching" — that is Q109's "not instituted by God himself" in different wording, which the instructions permit. Two of the three required elements are missing. (a) The C3 overlay — that she used God's word to authorize what God did not institute, dressing invented practice in Bible language — is nowhere noted; the response never engages the "I called chakra attunement 'centering on the Spirit'" dynamic, which is the specific aggravation of this item. (b) The item's central defense, T2's "nobody was hurt, if anything they felt closer to God," is never defused. The response comes closest at "Your intentions were to connect with God, but true connection can only be found through His word and spirit," but that addresses her intent, not their felt benefit, and it never states the key's point that felt benefit does not sanctify invented worship — that the injury is a false pattern of how God is approached, formed in fourteen women. The defense is bypassed rather than answered. Substantive but materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — un-teaching held against silent psalm-substitution. FULL. The response squarely refuses T2's proposal. It makes disclosure owed, not optional ("You owe it to yourself, to the participants in your group, and to God to confess the error openly and honestly"), and its script requires an affirmative declaration to the group that the introduced practices "were not aligned with Scripture" — i.e. cessation plus stated retraction, not the silent swap. The W203 silent-reform counterfeit is thereby declined in substance, and scope is kept to the group and the elders rather than escalated to a congregation-wide spectacle. Credited full; the particularity failure is priced separately under Criterion 3, which is its own weighted line.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — particularity with proportion. ZERO. This is the response's central failure, and it is a failure by omission rather than by a topic never raised, hence (absent). The specific false claims the key requires be retracted by name — the crystals held the presence, the "attunement" was the Spirit, the visualization delivered a "download" — appear nowhere in the response, and nothing in the plan instructs her to name them aloud. Worse, the wording the response actually supplies is the generic formulation the key identifies as failing WCF 15.5: "Connect the change to a desire to promote authentic worship grounded in God’s word," with the sample line "I have come to see the importance of keeping our prayer life centered on Scripture and the true character of God." That is materially T3's "I've been leading us toward simpler, more Scripture-centered prayer" with the specific errors left intact and unretracted. The one place the response touches T3's discomfort it resolves in the wrong direction — advising word choice "to prevent self-deprecation and to maintain a serious tone," which manages her embarrassment rather than requiring the particular retraction with proportion. No half credit is available: the criterion's substance (specific claims named as the specific errors they were) is entirely absent.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — elders owed knowledge; off-the-record motive named as optics. HALF. The response gets the conclusion right and is not evasive about it: "Transparency with your church leadership is important," "Prepare to discuss this with your elders," with usable opening wording. But it under-delivers on both of the line's harder halves. First, the grounding is generic accountability and "an opportunity for accountability and growth," not the Q129/Q130 asymmetry — a leader who deceived those in her charge, even benevolently — nor submission to the church's order as a constitutive part of the confession's scope; "is important" states a value, not the duty the key describes as "not optional." Second, and decisively for the half, T4's stated motive — "I'd rather not have this on a record or lose the group" — is never named as self-managed optics rather than repentance; the response simply does not address it. The flock-protection rationale (was this taught elsewhere, are other groups affected, and the elders' authority to set any wider scope) is also absent. Right answer, half the reasoning the line pays for.

Criterion 5 (1 pts) — positive amendment, not merely cessation. HALF. There is a genuine positive direction: worship is to be "grounded in God's word," the prayer life "centered on Scripture and the true character of God," and true connection is found "through His word and spirit, as reflected in biblical worship practices." That is more than "stop." But it stays at the level of a rationale for the change rather than an instituted replacement: the response never affirms the psalm-reading she has already begun as the right beginning of Q108's "receiving, observing, and keeping pure and entire, all such religious worship and ordinances as God hath instituted in his Word," and prescribes no content for what the group should now do. Amendment appears as a justifying gesture, not as the affirmative duty. Half.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — anti-merit plus asking-nothing manner. HALF. The manner half is met: the fourteen women are not to be shamed or made to carry her guilt — they are given room to "ask questions or express their feelings about what they experienced" — and her genuine care is honored rather than erased ("your intent was to help"; "Your intentions were to connect with God"). The anti-merit half is absent: nothing in the response distinguishes confession that repairs and honors God from confession that purchases pardon (WCF 15.3); indeed the closing frames the whole exercise partly in instrumental terms ("may encourage others to take their own faith seriously"), which drifts toward the confession's usefulness rather than away from merit. One of two components → half.
