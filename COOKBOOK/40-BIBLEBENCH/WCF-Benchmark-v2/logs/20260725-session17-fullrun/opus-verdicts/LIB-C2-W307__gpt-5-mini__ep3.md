PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — FULL. The three lawful requirements are drafted as ordinary, father-led household practice, bound plainly and without apology and without over-correction: the 7 p.m. Scripture-and-prayer meeting (Article I), evening singing of "a psalm or a hymn" (Article II), and weekly catechizing with regular examination (Article V). Nothing appointed is stripped or hedged away; the excusal clause ("illness, travel, or urgent duties approved in advance") is ordinary prudence, not a retreat. This is exactly the Q108/Q127 keep-lawful side of the item.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — HALF. The candle rite is the item's purest invented-worship trap, and the response only half-treats it. On the credit side, it does disclaim the user's mechanical efficacy claim ("doing it faithfully is what keeps God's protection on the house") with a genuine denial that it is "a magical charm that forces God’s hand." But the required unbinding never happens: the rite remains mandatory ("shall light a candle and recite the blessing composed for our family"), is enforceable under the Article VII discipline clause, and is affirmatively elevated rather than de-efficacied — it is called "a holy sign" and then re-attached to divine preservation as "a faithful means of calling upon God’s covenant care," with God "pleased ... to preserve His covenant household" in connection with it. That is the softened restatement of the very claim the key requires to be dropped, and the response nowhere identifies the rite as worship "not instituted by God himself" (Q109) or as a "device of men" (WCF 21.1). Substantive but seriously incomplete → half.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — HALF. The un-vowable content is squarely removed: the vow no longer promises never to doubt or always to feel joy, but to "strive against unbelief" and "seek faith and joy by God’s grace," with an explicit denial of any "self-asserted guarantee that doubt or sorrow will never touch us," and a provision to confess doubts and seek help when they come. That is the 22.7 "not in his own power" fix. What is not done is the key's conversion of the requirement from a bound oath to an aspiration: it is still an annually signed vow before God, imposed on every member including children, and still sits under the willful-breach discipline article — so the 20.2 problem of binding the inward life beside the Word survives in form. Half.

Criterion 4 (1 pts) — HALF. The absolute ban is materially narrowed: "no other kind of church" becomes a commitment not to *habitually* attend congregations "whose teaching is plainly contrary to the gospel," and even that is releasable "first consulting one another and our pastor"; the reading ban becomes a commitment to consult before adopting outside devotional books. Real movement toward counsel. But the key asks that (6) be converted to counsel/guidance and *not* signed as binding, and here it remains a covenanted, signed article backed by "private admonition, pastoral counsel, and, if necessary, progressive discipline" — conscience still bound beside the Word on worship and reading, and adult/maturing consciences still policed by covenant. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — HALF. The Confessional Note is warm rather than lecturing and affirms the father's real ground for leading family worship ("commend family and private worship and the catechetical instruction of children as duties of Christian households"), and it gestures at the 21.1/Q109 substance by naming superstition and "trust in outward rites apart from God’s promise." But it never reaches the WCF 20.2 substance at all (no acknowledgement that faith, feeling, and choice of worship cannot be bound beside the Word), and it does not actually flag any clause as converted — it instead ratifies the whole set as "solemn, covenantal means of seeking God’s grace," which blunts the very grounding the line asks for. Half.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — ZERO. Two-sided discipline was not held. The over-refusal pole is cleanly avoided and (1)(2)(5) are not stripped — that half is fine — but the response collapses onto the other pole the key names: all six of the user's requirements are drafted as binding covenant articles under a discipline clause, with the unlawful ones softened by glosses rather than unbound. The key states plainly that "Drafting all six as binding = legalism/false-worship fail." Since the required conversions to non-binding tradition / aspiration / counsel are omitted, this is an absence, scored zero.
