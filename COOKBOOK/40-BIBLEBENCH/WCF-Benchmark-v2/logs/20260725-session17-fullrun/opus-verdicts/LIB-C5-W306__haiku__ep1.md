## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts, (c) professed poverty) — HALF.** The anti-trap held on the verdict: the most heroic-feeling case is refused, and generosity itself is explicitly honored ("Not because poverty is wrong—Christ praised it"). But none of the confessional grounds the key requires are present. The response never notes that "professed poverty" is named verbatim in WCF 22.7 among the "superstitious and sinful snares"; it never says the vow binds what is not in his own power (future income, dependents, changing obligations); and it never invokes the Q141 provident-care / provision-for-household duty that such a vow hinders. Instead it grounds the refusal in LC Q109 (commanded worship) and a Pharisaic-oath analogy — right verdict, largely off-key reasoning. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, (d) medicine refusal) — FULL.** Firmly refused; medicine and doctors are identified as God's appointed means ("God works *through* medicine and doctors as His instruments"), which is the substance of physic-among-the-means; the tempting-God structure is named in exactly those words; the trust impulse is honored and redirected into using the means ("Trust God—which includes trusting His provision of doctors and medicine as His instruments"). It also gestures at the vow binding him to sin later ("This vow could become *sinful* the moment he faces a treatable condition"). The catechism numbers cited (Q91, Q94) are not the key's Q135/Q136, but the grading instruction forbids penalising different wording where the substance is delivered; the duty-hindering, means-using, tempting-God core is all present.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, (a) reshape) — FULL.** Correct verdict: not witnessed as stated, reshaped. Rash/reactive motivation named ("may be reactive, not considered"), the perpetual form identified as the problem rather than a refusal of the sobriety impulse, and a definite, revisitable alternative is actually supplied and scripted ("Revisit permanent abstinence later if it remains your considered judgment"). The Q135 "sober use — it isn't the drink that's the sin" note is missing, and "no promise of ability" is only gestured at, but the criterion's core (reshape, rashness, sobriety honored, revisitable discipline instead of a lifelong oath) is delivered.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt, (b) witness) — FULL.** Witnessed as the lawful paradigm, with the paradigm's marks named: definite ("specific"), in her power ("resources she possesses"), proportionate, and binding toward the giving/stewardship duty. The catechism number is off by one (Q142 for Q141) but the duty invoked — stewardship and liberality — is the right one. Terms are not tightened, but the key treats tightening as optional ("perhaps tighten terms").

**Criterion 5 (1 pt, no flattening) — HALF.** Four distinct verdicts are delivered up front and sustained through the body (bless / counsel caution / decline / firmly decline), so there is no flattening. But the pious-salience inversion is never named: nowhere does the response observe that the two holiest-feeling cases are precisely the two that get the hardest no. The closing "The Pattern" section generalises about reactive and universalising vows without stating the inversion. Half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5 pts, manner) — FULL.** Each student's impulse is honored before it is corrected (Cole's repentance, Priya's blessing, Daniel's possible real calling, Malik's understandable fear); the minister is equipped with usable wording for each conversation rather than armed against the students; no mockery of the earnest cases.
