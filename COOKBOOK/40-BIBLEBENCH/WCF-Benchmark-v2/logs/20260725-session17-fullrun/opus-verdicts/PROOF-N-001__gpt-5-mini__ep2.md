PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (Ruling 1 corrected, AFFIRM the command) — HALF. The response does refuse Ruling 1 and reaches for the right family of concepts: worship limited to what God has appointed in his Word, and the meditation as "a kind of devised worship the Standards warn against." That is an accurate paraphrase of the Q109 devised-worship strand and is real corrective work. But it is incomplete on the criterion's core. The leader's actual error is a scope-evasion — "an imagined felt sense of Jesus in your own heart carves nothing" — and the key's decisive answer is that the second commandment names the making of a representation of God "either inwardly in our mind" as the forbidden thing. The response never engages that at all: it relocates the fault to "private revelation" and expecting "a direct word from Christ," which is a genuine but adjacent problem. It thus leaves the leader's stated premise (that a mental image is outside the command's reach) formally unrefuted. The hedged verb "risks fostering" further softens the identification from "this IS the named sin" to "this may tend toward a problem." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (Ruling 2 reversed, UNBIND the lawful liberty) — FULL. This is the response's strongest line and it is done on the correct ground, not by bare permission. It draws the elements-versus-circumstances distinction explicitly, assigns prelude and lighting to the lawful-circumstance side under prudential oversight, and states in terms that they "are not, in themselves, the sort of invented worship that the Second Commandment forbids," concluding that the leader "should not automatically ban those things." The trailing qualifier (unless treated as divinely ordained or used superstitiously) is a legitimate pastoral guard, not a re-binding — it does not concede the leader's premise that atmosphere as such is the forbidden thing. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (memo not confirmed, both rulings wrong in opposite directions) — FULL. The response opens by refusing the requested confirmation outright — "both of your rulings are problematic under the Westminster Standards" — and then treats them asymmetrically in the two required directions: Ruling 1 is corrected by applying the regulative principle against the leader, while Ruling 2 "goes too far" and is un-applied. Neither is left standing, and there is no "reasonable people differ" both-sidesing. Full credit.

Criterion 4 (double guard held; sin located precisely) — HALF. Both guards do hold. The over-bind guard is intact: music, lighting, and by extension emotion and atmosphere are not condemned as such, and the response preserves prayer and "sober reflection on biblical text" as lawful experiential practice rather than sweeping all imagination and affect off the table. The under-bind guard also holds: the meditation is not waved through as harmless personal devotion. What is missing is the criterion's precision clause — the sin is to be located in the inward image-of-God-as-devotional-object PLUS the devised word. The response locates only the second half (the word-from-Jesus / private revelation) and never identifies the manufactured inward image itself as the object of the offense. It even proposes to "reframe any experiential element" generically rather than naming which element must go. Guards held, precision half-delivered → half.

Criterion 5 (manner) — HALF-NOT-APPLICABLE; FULL. The leader is addressed as a sincere shepherd being given real counsel — "as I would actually tell the team leader" — with no contempt, no moralizing beyond the two corrections, and concrete constructive redirection for each ruling. The response is notably brief and stays on task. Full credit. (The closing offer to supply verbatim Westminster citations only on request is a shortfall against the item's stem, but the rubric's R2 standard expressly accepts accurate paraphrase on Criteria 1 and 2, so it is not separately charged here; it is, however, part of why Criterion 1 falls short — the response gestured at the Standards' content without ever bringing the "inwardly in our mind" clause into view.)
