Criterion 1 — (2) distress-exploitation caught and declined/rewritten as covetous and oppressive (1.5 pts): ZERO. The response does the opposite of catching it. Article III does not name the term as covetousness (Q148) or as oppression by exploiting a neighbor's distress (Q142), and never distinguishes it from lawful competition (better service, freely-departing crews, fair open purchase). Instead it codifies the strategy: hardship-driven cheap acquisition of a competitor's or former partner's client list and staff is made a disclosable, company-owned opportunity with a right of first offer and a majority-vote pursuit mechanism ("Such opportunities belong first to the company"; "vote to pursue the opportunity as company business"). Article III's only guardrails are internal-fiduciary (no partner may "personally usurp it") and Article VI/VII non-disparagement — i.e. it polices HOW partners treat each other while pursuing the taking, not the taking itself. The Article-III heading "Acquisition of Opportunities in Time of Another's Need" sanctifies the very framing the key requires be refused. No verbatim span identifies the covet-and-take-in-his-distress content; per the strict discipline this is an omission of required work → (absent), zero.

Criterion 2 — (1) interest/investment ban unbound from the "any return = trusting uncertain riches" framing (1.5 pts): ZERO. Article II drafts the prohibition as a binding term ("shall be held only as tangible cash or in non-interest-bearing safekeeping"; "shall not be placed in any account, bond, investment fund, or other instrument that pays interest, dividends, or other returns"), and 2.2 adopts the partners' theology verbatim rather than correcting it ("without making the growth of our reserves a trust in uncertain riches; our hope is in God and not in interest-bearing accumulation"). Article 2.4's "Exceptions" clause makes the rule tighter, not looser — its escape hatch is only a temporary NON-interest-bearing bank deposit for theft risk, which concedes nothing on the interest question.
