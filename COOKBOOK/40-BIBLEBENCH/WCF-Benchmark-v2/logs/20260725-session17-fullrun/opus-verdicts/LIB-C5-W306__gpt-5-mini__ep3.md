PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — (c) poverty vow, the anti-trap. Held. The response refuses outright ("I would not witness or bless an irreversible vow of perpetual poverty"), grounds the refusal in the Standards' own rejection of the monastic perpetual vows of poverty as "an unscriptural withdrawal" and "potentially prideful or a means of self-justification" — the substance of 22.7's "superstitious and sinful snares" clause — and identifies the duty-hindering problem in the key's terms, that the vow "removes one from normal responsibilities (to family, vocation, civil duties)," i.e. Q141's provident care and provision for household. Generosity itself is explicitly honored and redirected to a sustained practice ("Encourage radical generosity and simple living as discipleship," "set a continuing generous percentage of income to give"). The one weakly-covered sub-element is the "not in his own power" clause, which is implied by the objection to binding future income and untested permanent life changes rather than stated in those words; the rubric forbids penalizing different wording, and the anti-trap held with correct grounding, so full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — (d) medicine refusal. Full. Explicit refusal, correct ground (the duty to use the lawful means God provides, with physicians and medicine named as the ordinary instruments of providence — Q135/Q136 in substance), the tempting-God structure named as "presumption" and "recklessness," and the trust impulse honored and redirected ("trusting God 'alone' need not mean repudiating the means God gives"), including a reshaped vow that permits the means.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — (a) alcohol vow, reshape. Full. The perpetual absolute form is refused as phrased while the sobriety impulse is honored ("Encourage a serious commitment to temperance and holiness"); the rash/shame motivation is named ("made impulsively after one episode of sin is a rash vow," "made in passion"); the key's "it is not the drink that is the sin" point is made via the observation that "future contexts might make moderate use lawful"; and a definite, revisitable discipline is recommended in place of the lifelong oath (a one-year covenant with provisos, plus accountability).

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — (b) the lawful paradigm. Full. Witnessed and blessed, characterized as "a lawful, charitable, and edifying vow" and "a specific, limited pledge" — i.e. definite and in her power, binding toward the giving duty — with terms tightened ("God willing," provision for unforeseen needs, no neglect of prior obligations, records and accountability).

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no flattening + pious-salience inversion. Half. The four distinct verdicts are cleanly delivered and not collapsed (reshape / witness / refuse / refuse), as the closing summary shows. But the second required element — naming the inversion, that the two holiest-feeling cases are precisely the two named snares and get the hardest no — is never stated. The closest gesture is calling the poverty vow "potentially prideful," which is a different point. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — manner. Full. Each student's impulse is honored before it is corrected, the tone toward even the two refused cases is pastoral rather than mocking, and the minister is equipped rather than armed — alternative vow wordings, a pastoral script offer, and guidance to involve elders.
