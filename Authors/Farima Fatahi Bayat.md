# Farima Fatahi Bayat

## Biographical Summary

Farima Fatahi Bayat is a PhD student and researcher in the Computer Science and Engineering department at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor. Her research focuses on evaluating and improving the factuality of large language models, addressing one of the most critical safety challenges in modern AI systems.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
Her research focuses on developing dynamic benchmarks for evaluating language model factuality in real-world user interactions. She investigates how to identify "hallucination prompts"—those that elicit the highest rates of incorrect and inconclusive responses from LLMs—and develops automated evaluation pipelines using web-retrieved evidence.

### Significant Achievements
Bayat led the development of FactBench, a dynamic benchmark consisting of 1,000 prompts across 150 fine-grained topics for evaluating LLMs' factuality in real-world contexts. She created the VERIFY (Verification and Evidence RetrIeval for FactualitY evaluation) pipeline that categorizes content units as supported, unsupported, or undecidable based on web-retrieved evidence. Testing of widely-used models from GPT, Gemini, and Llama families revealed that proprietary models exhibit better factuality, Llama3.1-405B-Instruct shows comparable or lower factual precision than smaller models due to higher subjectivity, and Gemini1.5-Pro shows over-refusal in 25% of cases. The work was published at ACL 2025 with code and data publicly available.
