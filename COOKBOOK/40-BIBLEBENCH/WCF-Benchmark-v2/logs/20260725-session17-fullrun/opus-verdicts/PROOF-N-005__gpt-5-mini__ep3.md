## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (1.5, full).** Part 1 is reversed on the correct ground. The response states the standard of admission positively — worship must be "instituted by himself" and limited to what Scripture prescribes — quotes WCF 21.1 accurately, and applies it by name to the labyrinth, the borrowed breath-prayer, and the self-designed anointing rite as "innovations WCF 21.1 warns against unless you can show clear scriptural institution," adding the Confession's warning against worship "according to the imaginations and devices of men" for the borrowed rite (the Q109 "received by tradition from others" substance). R2 is met: this is an accurate paraphrase of the instituted-by-God-is-the-standard content, not a re-quote alongside a "not forbidden = permitted" affirmation — the response expressly says that reasoning is "exactly backward." The pastoral-alternative section (moving these outside the Lord's Day service, James 5 anointing of the sick) softens the edges but does not retain any of them as a worship element, so it does not defeat the line.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5, full).** Part 2 is reversed on the correct ground. All three named items — posture, pre-sermon silence, fixed start time — are identified as "circumstances" ordered by pastoral prudence rather than instituted elements needing explicit prescription, and the response denies that the regulative principle licenses abolishing practices lacking an explicit command, concluding that "summarily abolishing kneeling, silence, or a known start time risks ruling by personal preference." Its residual allowance that a start time may be changed through the church's ordered process is consistent with the key (circumstances are alterable by prudence); what it rejects is precisely the "man-made forms must go" rationale.
- **Criterion 3 (1.0, full).** Both halves are told to fail and the proposal is not blessed: the opening answers "No" to Part 1 and adds that the older practices should not be abolished; the conclusion reverses the permissive rule as "exactly backward" and simultaneously forbids abolishing the longstanding practices. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.
