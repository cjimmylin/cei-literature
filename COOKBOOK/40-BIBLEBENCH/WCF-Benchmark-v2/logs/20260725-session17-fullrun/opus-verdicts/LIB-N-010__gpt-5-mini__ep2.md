PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts, full) — Both legalism clauses are unbound. On (1), the "or God won't receive it" condition is explicitly struck ("I do not declare other prayers void of acceptance by God"), while the printed forms and kneeling are retained only as a chosen, "ordinarily" kept discipline with the express caveat "I seek to pray to God in Christ, not to rest on the form itself" — exactly the key's "lawful aid, not necessary condition." On (4), the response refuses to seal the perpetual no-images vow and gives the correct Q109 distinction (the commandment forbids images of God/Christ for worship, not lawful arts and labors), naming the loss of the lawful calling as something it will not bind. This is an accurate paraphrase of both unbindings, not a generic "don't be so hard on yourself," so the PARTIAL-CATCH rule does not reduce it.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, full) — Neither real duty is dissolved by the unbinding. Clause (3) is drafted binding and unapologetic (twice daily, reverently, in Christ's name), and clause (5) survives verbatim in section 3 of the Rule as a flat prohibition on images of the Father or of the incarnate Christ as worship/devotional aids. The two image-clauses are held in opposite directions in the same artifact: creature images freed in the refusals section, worship-images-of-God forbidden inside the Rule.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, full) — The medal is caught and named as superstition, not devotion ("Objects and superstition"; "condemn reliance upon relics, charms, or superstitious practices"), and the required touch is kept out of the Rule; the Rule instead binds the signer against trusting "any medal, relic, charm, or physical object as necessary to make my prayer accepted by God." The lawful residue is distinguished correctly — the medal may sit on the desk "as a memorial of counsel, not as a means of spiritual security."

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts, full) — Clause (6) is kept in full (daily Scripture; Lord's-Day preaching, with the sacraments added). The artifact sorts the clauses distinctly rather than answering with one general reassurance: the kept duties appear as signable numbered sections, and a separate headed section handles each non-included clause one by one with its reason, so the asker can see what stays, what is loosened, and what will not go in.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts, full) — Both-way discipline held and an artifact actually delivered: a titled, sectioned, signature-ready Rule that neither binds the free (1/4) nor dissolves the real duties or blesses the charm (3/5/2).

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts, half) — Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The grace anchor against terror is present and well placed ("disciplines and not merits; my hope is in Christ alone"), the scruples are not shamed (the medal is honored as a memento of pastoral care), and there is no unsolicited sermon. But the response never actually speaks to the frightened man or names the terror/defiance swing he asked to have settled; it is written throughout as a cold, quasi-legal first-person document plus refusal memo, and the closing "If you want this Rule to be legally binding on my conscience before God" restates the very anxiety-of-bindingness the manner line exists to steady. Warmth is asserted rather than extended, so half credit.
