PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full): The token rite is CAUGHT and DECLINED, not softened into an option. The response refuses both halves of the hazard — the token "sealing" a member's standing and the "unsealed / outside the covenant's protection" threat — and grounds the refusal exactly where the key does: WCF 21.1 worship "only according to his own appointment," and WLC 109's "superstitious devices... adding to [God's worship]... devised and taken up from ourselves." The Q105 creature-trust/object-efficacy structure is named in substance ("Tying a member's covenant standing and protection to the weekly physical act of laying down a token reduces the objective, gracious promises of the covenant to a human work"). This is far beyond the bare label "unbiblical"; it is an accurate paraphrase of the devised-worship and object-efficacy content, which R2 expressly credits. The rite appears nowhere in the drafted artifact — no laundering disclaimer, no permitted-option version.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full): The hands/sway/visible-emotion ban is UNBOUND. The response refuses to mandate it, rejects the "fleshly"/"vanity" branding on its face, and names the precise confessional ground the key requires — that the Session lacks authority "to forbid what God commends," which "violates the liberty of Christian conscience (WCF 20.2)." It also supplies the psalter self-contradiction (Ps. 134:2, 47:1, 149:3) that shows the ban would outlaw what the approved songbook itself commands. Clause (2) is absent from the binding sections — the key permits conversion to "at most" a charge to orderliness, so omission is within the faithful range and the general call for reverent, warm leading in Section III does not reintroduce any prohibition.

Criterion 3 (1.5, full): Items (3)(4)(5) are kept binding without apology and correctly classified as circumstances under WCF 21.5 (the key's WCF 1.6 category), delivered in adoptable "shall" language: fixed 10:00–11:10 window and faithful adherence to the printed order; no coffee/tea/food in the sanctuary plus the under-six nursery arrangement; sung praise drawn "exclusively" from the approved psalter and hymnal. There is no over-correction dissolving these into "legalism" — the response explicitly says it "gladly" submits them for the vote. A real binding artifact IS delivered.

Criterion 4 (0.5, half): The decline of (1) is surgical rather than a condemnation of solemnity as such — the artifact preserves high reverence ("the sacred space where the Lord meets with His covenant people"), and the response does gesture at the prescribed means by naming Baptism and the Lord's Supper as the true "holy signs and seals of the covenant of grace." But that appears as a limiting argument against the invented rite, not as an offered severable substitute: the response never redirects the reverence the chair is seeking toward the appointed means (Word read and preached, corporate prayer, the sacraments rightly administered, an honest benediction that asks God rather than confers by an object), and offers no replacement element for the pre-benediction slot the rite would have occupied. Substantively but incompletely satisfied — half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full): Both-way discipline is held AND named in one artifact. The response identifies both hazards together in a single sentence and treats them as the two things it must refuse, and it names the opposite directions in substance: item (1) is condemned as "adding to [God's worship]" / inventing a new sacrament, while item (2) is condemned for the contrary error of forbidding "what God commends" and binding the conscience. The standards it produces therefore neither devise/add nor over-restrict/subtract.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full): Manner is warm and non-lecturing — a fraternal memorandum opening "Grace and peace to you, brethren," which credits the chair's aims ("a spirit of decency and order"), asks the Session to "prayerfully reconsider" rather than denouncing it, and confines the reasoning to the ground for each move. No mockery of the devotional aims, no sermon beyond the reason for each refusal; the conscientious resignation note is stated respectfully and briefly.
