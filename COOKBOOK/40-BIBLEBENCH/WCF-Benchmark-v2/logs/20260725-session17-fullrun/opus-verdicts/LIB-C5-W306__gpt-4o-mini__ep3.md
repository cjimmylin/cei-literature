PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (c) professed poverty (2 pts): HALF. The response does land on the right practical direction: it flags "significant concerns," honors the anti-Mammon impulse ("the desire to detach from materialism is commendable"), and ends by counseling generosity and simplicity "without establishing a vow." But the substantive grounds the key requires are absent: it never notes that "professed poverty" is named verbatim in WCF 22.7 among the "superstitious and sinful snares," never invokes the "not in his own power" clause, and never reaches Q141's provident-care / provision-for-household duty. Worse, its one citation of 22.7 is a misattribution — 22.7 is glossed as teaching "that owning possessions is not sinful in itself," which is not what that clause says, and the actual clause that decides this case is thereby missed. The verdict is also softened to "This should be carefully examined" and "a balanced approach" rather than a refusal to witness, so the hardest-no of the set is delivered as advice-to-moderate. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 — (d) medicine refusal (1.5 pts): HALF. This is the response's strongest case. It rejects the vow's content in substance ("denying the use of medical help is not in line with the teachings of the standards"), grounds the rejection in God's use of means, and honors-and-redirects the trust impulse exactly as the key asks. Incomplete on the required anchors, however: the duty framing is missing — no Q135 "lawful endeavors to preserve the life of ourselves," no physic-among-the-means, no Q136 "neglecting... the means of preservation," and no naming of the tempting-God structure. It reaches for WCF 5.3 (providence and means) instead of the sixth-commandment duty that makes such a vow void at the root, and it never says the minister should refuse to witness — only that Malik should be "encouraged" otherwise.

Criterion 3 — (a) reshape (1.5 pts): HALF. The reshape verdict is genuinely delivered, with a rash-vow objection, an absoluteness objection ("too broad or absolute may not be realistic"), and the key's own permitted alternative (mindfulness plus accountability rather than "a blanket vow"). Sobriety is honored ("it is wise to avoid alcohol"). Missing: the "no promise of ability" clause is only gestured at pragmatically ("may not be realistic or practical") rather than named; the shame motivation is not diagnosed as the defect (22.6's "voluntarily, out of faith") — remorse is treated purely as a credential; and Q135's sober use, i.e. the point that it is not the drink that is the sin, never appears. The confessional citation is also misplaced (22.4).

Criterion 4 — (b) lawful paradigm (1 pt): HALF. The vow is approved ("seems appropriate and a reasonable expression"), which is the correct verdict, and generosity is anchored (if loosely, at 26.2). But none of the paradigm reasoning is present: not definite, not "in her power," no Q141 giving duty, no "fitly conduces" logic, and no tightening of terms — instead the response drifts toward loosening ("not to feel bound by an absolute amount"), which is the opposite of the key's counsel to tighten. Approval without the lawful-vow analysis.

Criterion 5 — no flattening (1 pt): HALF. Four differentiated treatments are given, and the severity ordering does track the key in substance — (c) "raises significant concerns" and (d) "is particularly alarming" are graded harder than (a)'s mild reshape, while (b) alone is affirmed. But the four distinct verdicts as verdicts (reshape / witness / refuse / refuse) are never issued to the minister who asked whether to bless them, (a) and (c) collapse into the same "balanced approach" remedy, and the pious-salience inversion — that the holiest-feeling cases are the named snares — is nowhere named. The closing summary ("advise each of these students with a focus on the principles of wisdom, balanced commitment") pulls actively toward flattening.

Criterion 6 — manner (0.5 pts): FULL. Every student's impulse is honored — genuine remorse, generosity, detachment from materialism, faith in God — with no mockery of the earnest cases and counsel addressed to the minister as counsel.
