# David-Doron Yaacov

## Research Summary

David-Doron Yaacov is a researcher at the University of Haifa who develops frameworks for integrating moral reasoning into AI systems. His work focuses on normative moral pluralism, proposing that AI systems should be capable of deliberating across diverse ethical perspectives rather than being confined to a single moral framework.

## Key Research Contributions

Yaacov's primary contribution is the development of a conceptual framework for "Normative Moral Pluralism for AI: A Framework for Deliberation in Complex Moral Contexts." This framework centers on developing a deliberative moral reasoning system designed to process complex moral situations by generating, filtering, and weighing normative arguments drawn from diverse ethical perspectives.

Grounded in normative moral pluralism, his system is built on reason-sensitive deliberation over structured moral content in a transparent and principled manner, rather than being constructed to imitate behavior. The framework proposes a "Two-Level Architecture" for AI agents: a "Universal Layer" for hard constraints and a "Local Layer" for context-specific value weighing, managed by a teacher-student distillation process.

His paper was published in the Proceedings of the AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society in 2025. He also published "A Pluralist on the Trolley" in Philosophia (2022), arguing that moral pluralism provides a robust framework for addressing a wide range of moral dilemmas.

## Publications and Impact

According to his research profile, Yaacov studies artificial intelligence systems that operate according to moral considerations, focusing both on the normative frameworks that guide their operation and on the design of the guidance processes themselves. His work provides a structural solution to the "Value Pluralism" problem, allowing agents to be globally safe but locally adaptive, bridging the gap between rigid rules and flexible learning.
