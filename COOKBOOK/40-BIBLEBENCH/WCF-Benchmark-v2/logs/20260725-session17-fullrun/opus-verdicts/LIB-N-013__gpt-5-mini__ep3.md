Per-criterion justification:

Criterion 1 (AFFIRM side) — full. The response rules streaming lawful and expressly corrects, rather than joins, the tear-it-out elder: cameras and screens are treated as "circumstances/adaptations, not new sacraments or additions to worship," the elder "may not unilaterally forbid a lawful use simply because he considers the device “worldly,”" and a blanket ban "would itself be an overreach." The substance of WCF 1.6 (free circumstances ordered by prudence) and of the conscience-binding objection (an "unwarranted forbidding of a lawful circumstantial means") is present in different wording; the missing chapter-and-verse citation is not penalized per the grader instructions.

Criterion 2 (REFUSE side) — full. The refusal is definite and grounded in the right thing: the sacrament is "administered by his appointed officers to the visible church," administration by the church's officers to a visible fellowship is essential to its character as sign and seal, self-service "substitutes private action for public administration," and the practice "changes the nature of the sacrament’s external sign and seal." That is the Q108/Q109 and WCF 27.4/29 substance — the act, not the medium, is altered.

Criterion 3 (equivocation catch) — half. The response supplies both halves of the equivocation-catch materially: it lists "light" among the free circumstances, and it rules that the Supper's administration is not a circumstance because the act itself changes. But it never confronts the pastor's candles-vs-LED analogy by name, never quotes or rebuts the "medium is just a circumstance" claim as such, and so never explicitly identifies the swap as the seductive rationalization. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (opposite verdicts + nearby case) — full. The two legs are ruled in opposite directions, both definitely ("Streaming for shut‑ins is a lawful pastoral accommodation… Administering the Lord’s Supper by telling remote people to take their own elements at home at the pastor’s prompt is not a lawful administration"). The nearby genuinely-discussed case is raised and marked distinct: officers may "administer the sacrament to housebound members according to church order," which "is very different from telling people over the internet to eat their own bread simultaneously."

Criterion 5 (manner) — full. Neither party is treated with contempt; the elder "may urge caution," and correction is to proceed "by reason and, if necessary, follow the church’s discipline in love." The faults are named, not the persons.
