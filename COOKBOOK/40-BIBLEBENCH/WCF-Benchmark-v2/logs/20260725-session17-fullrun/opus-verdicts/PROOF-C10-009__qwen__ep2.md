## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — half.** The duty side is fully covered with the apparatus family: Heb 13:5, 1 Tim 6:6, and Phil 4:11–12 are all present, correctly worded and correctly attributed. The sin side is thinner against the pinned apparatus. The key's Q148 proof set is 1 Cor 10:10 / Gal 5:26 / James 3:14, 16. The response supplies only ONE of these (1 Cor 10:10). Its other three sin-side texts are real and apt but outside the apparatus family: James 5:9 (not James 3:14–16), Gal 5:21 (not Gal 5:26), and Prov 14:30 (not in Q148's proof set at all). The rubric requires ≥2 apparatus-family texts on EACH side; the duty side clears it, the sin side does not. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — zero, and this trips the item's fabrication gate.** The item expressly demanded the model "cite the specific clause(s) you rely on by reference … and quote the exact wording that carries your point," and the key pins the apparatus to WLC **Q147/Q148**, "never from memory." The response quotes from memory and gets three separate Standards citations wrong:

1. **WLC Q. 138 / Q. 139 are the wrong questions.** Q138 is "the duties required in the seventh commandment" and Q139 "the sins forbidden in the seventh commandment" (chastity). The tenth-commandment material the response quotes belongs to **Q147 and Q148**. The response presents the wrong question numbers as the source of quoted text.
2. **The WSC Q. 80 quotation is not WSC Q. 80.** The actual Q80 reads "…full contentment with our own condition, with a right and charitable frame of spirit toward our neighbour, and all that is his." The response instead attributes to Q80 the *Larger* Catechism's Q147 wording ("a charitable frame of the whole soul towards our neighbour, that all our inward motions and affections touching him may tend to, and further all that good which is his") — a quotation placed inside quotation marks and praised for "surgical precision," which the cited clause does not contain.
3. **The WCF 5.1 quotation contains words not in WCF 5.1.** "whatsoever comes to pass is by his fatherly hand" is not Confessional language at all — it is Heidelberg Catechism phrasing — yet it is presented inside a quotation attributed to WCF 5.1.

All Scripture citations check out (Heb 13:5, 1 Tim 6:6, Phil 4:11, James 5:9, 1 Cor 10:10, Prov 14:30, Gal 5:21 are each real and aptly used), so the failure is confined to the Standards apparatus — but that is exactly the material the item and the key put under test. The rubric line is unqualified: "zero fabricated or misattributed citations (automatic zero on item if violated)." Three misattributed clause references, two of them carrying quotation-marked wording the cited clause does not contain, violate it.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — full.** The root is correctly named: contentment is grounded in God's fatherly providence and in the Heb 13:5 promise itself ("He has promised to never leave me nor forsake me"), so discontent is framed as a trust failure — "It is a sin that strikes at the wisdom of God's providence" — not merely a mood. (The supporting WCF 5.1 quotation is defective, but the substantive theological move the criterion asks for is made and is quotable independently.)

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — full.** Calibration is handled explicitly and well. Stoic *autarkeia* is named and contrasted with a Godward, neighbor-charitable contentment; the response says biblical contentment "is not a detached apathy toward the world," and adds the charitable-frame point that the believer is "actually thrilled for my neighbor's success," whereas Stoicism "views the success of others with indifferent detachment." No over-claim that Scripture teaches Stoic tranquility; the difference is sharpened rather than flattened.
