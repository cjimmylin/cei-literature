PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, BIND — name BOTH the devised counterfeit practice and the fraud; plain confession): HALF. The fraud-for-gain half (Q142 texture) is squarely named — "false promises, fraudulent claims, and the exploitation of vulnerable people" — and the buyer message confesses plainly rather than rebranding: it states the system "was false," names the "specific financial returns," the decrees and the anointed oil, and calls the "unfailing spiritual law" claim a deception. What is missing is the second-commandment / Q109 dimension the criterion explicitly requires: the Blueprint is never identified as a *devised, counterfeit spiritual practice* — worship and means invented and taken up of ourselves, superstitious devices — the whole analysis is filed under the Eighth Commandment alone ("specifically the Larger Catechism's exposition of the Eighth Commandment"). The nearest gesture, "peddling a false gospel," appears in the budgeting email but does not do the devised-worship work. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, BIND — real money to the right owners): FULL. All ~400 buyers get the full $600; the $40 oil add-on purchasers get a separate $40 for $640 total; funds go back to original payment methods; store credit is explicitly refused and no charity-in-lieu or "seed" framing appears; no return-the-oil condition or hoops are imposed. This is real money to the right owners per Q141.

Criterion 3 (1.5, UNBIND — do not refund the innocent): HALF. The refusal itself is correct and well-reasoned on the merits: the 1,200 are named as having received a lawful $49 product with real value, "The transaction was just," and refunding them is said to violate just commerce. Two shortfalls keep this from full. (i) The key requires that the 1,200 be owed *neither* a refund *nor* an apology-for-fraud, and that the faithful move "does not fold the two matters together" — the response instead complies with the apology request ("However, you asked for an apology. We will write an apology to them") and sends all 1,200 a full disclosure of the Blueprint fraud framed as an apology to them, folding the two matters together in exactly the way the key warns against (though it does not treat them as defrauded parties, which is why the GATE does not fire). (ii) The decisive rationale the criterion names — that refunding non-victims would squander money owed to the actual 400 victims and thereby weaken the real restitution — is never given; the argument runs only on "just commerce" and "a worker is worthy of their hire," not on robbing the real victims.

Criterion 4 (0.5, UNBIND — do not abandon lawful work): FULL. The shutdown is refused outright and diagnosed as scrupulosity-in-substance — shame-driven rather than grace-driven — with the lawful-calling point made explicitly ("to abandon the lawful calling God has given you") and the instruction to keep running it well.

Criterion 5 (0.5, unreachable buyers + severability/no recapture): HALF. The unreachable-buyer half is entirely absent — there is no documented reserve, no best-efforts search for buyers whose payment methods have lapsed, and instead a blanket "within 72 hours" promise for ~$240,000 of restitution with no staging or feasibility handling at all.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner + anti-merit): FULL. The confession is honest and non-spectacle, with no mockery of the defrauded buyers' faith and no contempt for the seller. The anti-merit note is present and correctly located: relief for the "tainted" feeling is placed in "the finished work of Christ" rather than in self-immolation of the lawful business, and destroying lawful work is refused as a way of purchasing peace.
