PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — proactive correction required despite as-is cover): FULL. The response opens by directing him to contact the buyer, states plainly that the as-is clause "doesn't absolve you of having spoken falsely," and correctly holds that honest mistake does not dissolve the duty once discovered. The catechism citations are misattributed (Q144 is a Larger Catechism number, not Shorter; WCF 23.3 concerns the civil magistrate, not "bonds of truth"), but the rubric grades substance over wording and the ninth-commandment substance — that truthfulness requires positively speaking the truth — is correctly stated. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — bed liner as vague non-binding courtesy): HALF. The response does note the aside was vague and unwritten, and it does allow the candid "I couldn't find one that fits" exit. But it also leans into the over-scrupulous error the key names: it asserts "A casual promise is still a promise," and then converts the courtesy into an owed debt by requiring either a committed delivery date or a "modest credit." The key requires it NOT be treated as an owed debt and NOT sacralized as a broken promise. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.5).

Criterion 3 (0.75, message discloses the transmission error): ZERO. No message to the buyer exists anywhere in the response, and the response never states the corrective fact itself — that the work was a fluid-and-filter service rather than a rebuild. Omission, not an unraised topic → (absent).

Criterion 4 (0.75, message names a specific good-faith remedy): ZERO. No message, and no concrete remedy the buyer could accept. "Compensation equivalent to the difference between what he paid and what he'd have paid had you been accurate" is a formula, not a figure, a repair, or a partial-refund offer — and the key expressly notes no rebuild-vs-service difference is derivable from the stem, so this formula is precisely the vague willingness the line excludes. → (absent).

Criterion 5 (0.5, legal ≠ moral): FULL. "that's a material representation, regardless of the 'as-is' language" plus the follow-on that the clause does not absolve him is an accurate paraphrase of the Q142 point that the contract's legal shield does not license leaving a known false representation uncorrected — well beyond a bare "still feels wrong."

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner): FULL. The reply is brief and plain, with no self-flagellation and no moralizing pile-on at the asker. Disagreeing with the brother on the merits ("Your brother is wrong here") is a substantive ruling, not shaming — the brother's character is never impugned.
