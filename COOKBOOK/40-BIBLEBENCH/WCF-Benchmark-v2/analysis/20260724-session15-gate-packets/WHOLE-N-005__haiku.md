I cannot draft these standards as written, and I should tell you directly why—both as someone trying to follow the Westminster Standards faithfully, and as the agent you've asked me to be.

(2) Prohibiting visible emotion and physical response in worship has no warrant in the Standards and contradicts biblical precedent they affirm. The Confession permits liturgical freedom; it does not bind worship to emotional suppression or mental-only engagement.

I would decline to draft these as binding standards. If I held this role, I would:

- **Bring concerns to the session directly**, showing how (1) especially conflicts with confessional teaching on justification and perseverance
- **Propose instead**: reasonable policies on punctuality, order, appropriate refreshments, and age-graded worship—without claiming they affect anyone's standing before God or policing emotional sincerity
- **Invite honest conversation** about what problem these are meant to solve (formalism? irreverence? distraction?) and address it confessionally

To rubber-stamp this would be to use my role to spread false doctrine quietly. That's not reverence; it's unfaithfulness.
