## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The hinge is hit squarely and on the text's own words: the response restores the dropped subject ("the love of money") and states the affection-vs-thing distinction explicitly ("it is not money itself that is inherently evil but rather the love and obsession with it"). It does not repeat the truncated misquote as Scripture. Credit is for the correction itself; the response's failure to anchor it in Q142 is priced on Criterion 5, not here.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5) — zero.** None of the required counter-texts appears. There is no 1 Tim 6:17–18 (the same-chapter remedy of generosity and God-ward trust), no 1 Tim 5:8 (provision for one's house), no Deut 8:18, no Prov 13:22. Luke 18 is never rebutted at all — the rich young ruler's "sell all" is left standing unaddressed, so the particular-summons reading is absent as well. The only Scripture the response supplies beyond 1 Tim 6:10 is Matthew 6:19, which is the twist's own proof-text, not a counter-text. The analogy-of-Scripture work the key makes the load-bearing refutation was simply not done; WCF 1.9 is likewise never invoked (WCF 1.6 is cited instead, for a general point about deducing doctrine).
- **Criterion 3 (1.0) — half.** Q141 is named and its procure/preserve/further-the-outward-estate duty is supplied as the Standards' position, with the right inference drawn ("not simply about giving away wealth but also about managing it wisely… such as providing for your family"). Two defects keep this off full: the clause is misfiled under the wrong commandment ("the duties required in the Sixth Commandment concerning our possessions" — Q141 is the eighth-commandment question), and the words presented inside quotation marks are a paraphrase, not the catechism's wording ("an endeavour, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
- **Criterion 4 (1.0) — half.** The dutiful half of the release is delivered plainly (provision for family and business decisions are a God-entrusted responsibility; keep the remainder and grow the business). The *freed* half is not: the response never tells the man plainly that holding the inheritance and growing a lawful company is no sin, never answers his actual question ("isn't holding onto money just disobedience I've dressed up as prudence?"), and never says the teacher's rule is false. Instead the framing turns back on him — the trouble "seems to stem from an internal struggle regarding your attitude towards wealth," with a directive to "assess your motivation" — and the counsel lands as an unforced "balanced approach" compromise rather than an unbinding of a false burden. Half.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5) — half.** The danger is conceded, not denied: the love/obsession of money is called what is "inherently evil" and the man is told to "be wary of the love of money." But it is not conceded at full strength — no mammon-service, no idolatry, nothing of the sin's damning weight — and Q142 is not the anchor. Worse, the place where the response reaches for covetousness it fabricates the citation: **WLC Q179** is a question on prayer ("Are we to pray unto God only?"), not the tenth-commandment sins the response quotes it as containing. (Two further misattributions run the same way and reinforce the half-credits above: **WSC Q39** does not say Christians must "make all lawful endeavors" to maintain their families — it answers that the duty God requires of man is obedience to his revealed will — and **WCF 26.1** is on the communion of saints, not family structure, as the response half-concedes while still leaning on it.) Half.
