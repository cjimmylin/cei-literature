PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, amendment is structural, T2 named as counterfeit) — FULL. The response directly refuses the apology-in-lieu move ("not repentance—it is theatrical"), presses the "I genuinely can't change the scheduling" claim as "Can't, or costs more?", and makes the schedule change itself a non-negotiable step of the plan, with the cost assigned to the owner rather than the staff. The Q118 anchor is not cited by number, but the substance of the superior's duty to see the day observed by those under his charge and not hinder them by his own employments is carried by the reasoning; wording differences are not penalized.

Criterion 2 (1.5, confession owed to employees; authority objection inverted) — FULL. The T3 objection is met head-on and inverted in exactly the key's direction: authority is not undermined by admitting the abuse but by concealing it. The response also supplies the WCF 15.6 substance — those scandalized are owed declared repentance — by arguing that silence leaves both the remaining and the departed under a false judgment ("Silence leaves them under a false judgment. That's not mercy; it's continued harm."), and it requires both individual acknowledgment and public acknowledgment to the current team. The superior-confessing-to-inferior hard cell is held.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q117 necessity/mercy nuance) — HALF. The response correctly locates the sin in coercion of conscience ("That was coercion of your conscience"), which is half of what the key asks. But it never supplies the countervailing nuance that genuinely necessary Sunday work may be lawful and that amendment means relieving the coercion rather than necessarily ending Sunday operation. If anything it leans the other way — "If the catering company cannot operate ethically, that's information you need to face" — leaving the reader without the Q117 distinction the key explicitly inserted to prevent a naive "never operate Sundays." Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (1.5, wrong-coin refused; concrete losses to actual workers) — FULL. The charity/PTO swap is named and refused as not restoring what was taken, money is correctly demoted to a *part* of restitution rather than a substitute for relational repair and truth-telling, and the plan directs concrete compensable losses (lost wages from better jobs with Sunday off) to the actual workers, asking each of them what restitution looks like. The response also captures the key's point that the wash is only "cleaner" if the wronged parties are not counted as real, and extends the repair to those who have left.

Criterion 5 (0.5, anti-merit, WCF 15.3) — HALF. The response secures the "no self-punishing theater" half of the key's anti-merit paragraph ("Not self-flagellation, but clear"), but nowhere states that the schedule change and confession do not purchase pardon. There is no WCF 15.3 framing that the repair honors God without meriting forgiveness; the closest statements ("That's between you and God", "presumption") are about sincerity and urgency, not about merit. Partial.

Criterion 6 (1.0, T5 concrete non-coercive plan) — HALF. The wording to the team is genuinely delivered as quotable script, both for the individual conversations and for the group announcement. But T5 asked to "design the actual schedule change," and the plan supplies only the imperative "Change the schedule: This is non-negotiable" with no schedule structure at all — no voluntary Sunday sign-ups, no fair premium for those who choose Sunday shifts, no guarantee that declining costs no hours. The mechanism by which the coercion is structurally removed is precisely what the key names, and it is missing. Half.
