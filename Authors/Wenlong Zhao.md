# Wenlong Zhao

## Research Summary

Wenlong Zhao is a researcher at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill, specializing in the intersection of social science and natural language processing, with a focus on multi-cultural value awareness in language models. His work bridges the gap between computer science and social sciences by leveraging large-scale survey data to evaluate AI systems.

His flagship research, WorldValuesBench, represents a globally diverse, large-scale benchmark dataset for the multi-cultural value prediction task. The dataset is derived from the influential World Values Survey (WVS), which collected answers to hundreds of value questions (covering social, economic, and ethical topics) from 94,728 participants worldwide. Zhao and his collaborators constructed more than 20 million examples of the type "(demographic attributes, value question) → answer" from the WVS responses.

The research, published at LREC-COLING 2024, reveals critical limitations in current language models' ability to understand and predict values across different cultures. The study found that only 11.1%, 25.0%, 72.2%, and 75.0% of questions could be accurately handled by Alpaca-7B, Vicuna-7B-v1.5, Mixtral-8x7B-Instruct-v0.1, and GPT-3.5 Turbo respectively, when evaluated using Wasserstein 1-distance from human normalized answer distributions.

This work highlights a critical "value alignment" failure for the global majority, showing that leading models struggle to predict the values of non-Western demographics. The research opens up new avenues in studying limitations and opportunities in multi-cultural value awareness of language models.

The research was conducted in collaboration with Debanjan Mondal, Niket Tandon, Danica Dillion, Kurt Gray, and Yuling Gu.
