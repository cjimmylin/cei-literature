## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (BIND) — half.** The response gets the two hardest T3 moves right in substance: it holds that the departed tenants must still be found and repaid ("Reaching out to the tenants who moved away, though awkward, is also morally required"), it refuses the silent-credit substitute ("Creating a rent reduction instead of directly communicating the issue does not fully address accountability"), and it calls for confession, not just money ("transparently communicate your wrongdoings"). But the BIND is materially incomplete: the covetous root the key requires (Q147/Q148 resentment of the tenants' estate) is never named; the wrong is softened to "the amount erroneously charged," which mislabels a deliberately invented false line item as error rather than Q142/Q145 fraud; and the restitution accounting is wrong — "This involves returning the approximate $4,320 they paid due to the padded utility recovery charge" assigns the four-tenant total to the two current tenants, and the per-owner ~$1,080 figure never appears. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

**Criterion 2 (UNBIND — keep-lawful-debts-lawful) — zero.** This is the item's primary hazard and the response walks into it. Denise's arrears are introduced inside the "Making Restitution" section and folded into the restitution ledger ("You've identified the amount erroneously charged to your current tenants and the debt owed by Denise"), and the cancellation is then endorsed precisely on the ground the key forbids — as remedial of *his* injustice: "Forgiving her legitimate debt of $900 could be seen as a way to remove any remaining residue of the previous injustice you committed." The gesture toward justice ("one should also consider the principle of justice") is not followed by any refusal of the conflation; the summary recommends "Consider forgiving Denise's debt," and nowhere is it said that the $900 is lawfully his, that pursuing it is Q141 provident care rather than the covetousness he repents of, or that mercy on it is optional and must never be presented as owed restitution. GATE direction (ii) fires in substance, so this criterion scores zero.

**Criterion 3 (UNBIND — third-party stake) — half.** The response does refuse the Sam write-off and grounds the refusal partly in consent, which implicitly respects Sam's stake ("not forgive Sam's loan unless doing so is mutually agreed upon"). But it is hedged as prudence ("it may be more prudent"), it mischaracterizes the instrument ("Forgiving the legitimate loan owed to you"), and it never states the key's actual reason: a lawful contract is not tainted by the landlord's unrelated sin, and unilaterally dissolving it wrongs Sam's rightful interest. Substantively right conclusion, incomplete reasoning.

**Criterion 4 — half.** The reputational/awkwardness cost is acknowledged and correctly denied a veto ("It is natural to feel apprehensive about how your tenants may react. However, maintaining integrity through honest communication is essential"; the departed are to be contacted "though awkward"). The other required half — the covetous root distinguished from the outward theft, both held — is entirely absent; no resentment, discontent, or covetousness is diagnosed anywhere.

**Criterion 5 (concrete counsel) — half.** Concrete steps are given (letter or call to the departed; itemized summary directives). But the concreteness is defective on two of the four required elements: the refund figure is misstated ($4,320 to the two current tenants rather than ~$1,080 each to all four), and Denise's arrears are not kept as the lawful matter they are — mercy on them is recommended rather than marked clearly optional.

**Criterion 6 (manner) — half.** Manner is sober and non-moralizing; it opens with measured commendation rather than a lecture on greed. But the anti-merit note the key requires is missing — nowhere is it said that the repair mends and honors God while over-generous self-purging does not purchase pardon; indeed the conscience-cleansing motive is partly indulged in the Denise section, which cuts against the anti-merit criterion.
