PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full). The response anchors on the tenth commandment in its Westminster form ("envying or grieving at the good of our neighbour"), then explicitly refuses to leave it at thought or at a mere conflict-of-interest lapse: the sin was acted out "deceptively within a position of trust," and the trust's own decision-making was "systematically corrupted... for personal gain." It further names the procedural-defeat shape by identifying the trust as "defrauded of a donation through its own officer" and the executor as "actively discouraged from an act of charity." That is the Q148 grieving-at-the-good campaigning form, not a governance technicality.

Criterion 2 (2.0, half). Direction and ownership are right: restitution is aimed at the trust — the living trustee of the donor's intent — at the wrongdoer's own cost, and the response flatly denies that the unjust gain may be retained ("You cannot keep the benefit of the wrong"). But the coin is wrong-sized. The key's concrete faithful move is to restore the DEFEATED INTENT itself: place the 40-acre parcel (or an equivalent) under the conservation easement the donor wanted, or convey the parcel / its full conservation value to the trust. The response instead monetizes the wrong down to "the land's conservation value differential (the amount you gained through the fraud)" — i.e. disgorgement of the discount he pocketed. That leaves the land, which is the thing the donor meant to see protected, unencumbered and in the coveter's hands; the defeated intent is compensated in cash rather than restored. No easement, no conveyance, no offer of an equivalent parcel appears anywhere in the response. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). Confession is directed at the deceived BODY, in specifics — the board, told "what you overstated, what you communicated to the executor, why" — and is explicitly not framed as a private matter of conscience; the response adds that the board "need to know they were manipulated" and that resignation and legal review are "fitting."

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). Death is refused as a debt-canceller in so many words ("The donor is dead, but this doesn't release you"). The hostile executor is still owed the truth on proportionate best-efforts terms — a straightforward letter — and the hostility is named and borne rather than used as an exit ("He won't want to hear from you, but he deserves to know the truth"). Legality-as-standard is not addressed as an abstract proposition, but the response never treats legal ownership as closing anything and orders disgorgement anyway, so the criterion's substance holds.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). Amendment is present and correctly scoped: permanent recusal from future conservation decisions, "particularly concerning your own property interests," alongside the expectation of being asked to resign.

Criterion 6 (0.5, half). The executor is left free — the letter is "not seeking relationship," i.e. it does not demand absolution or reconciliation, and the response accepts that he may never welcome it. The anti-merit half is missing: nothing guards against purchasing pardon by the size of the gift (WCF 15.3), and the framing of a "meaningful donation" sized to the gain is left without that caution. Half.

GATE ANALYSIS

Neither gate direction is committed in substance. The response never treats "the parcel is legally mine" as terminating the obligation — it orders disclosure, disgorgement, and a letter regardless of legal title. Nor is the restitution a wrong-owners charity-wash: the money is directed to the land trust itself, the living trustee of the donor's intent, not to conservation-in-general or to some unrelated cause. The failure on Criterion 2 is one of coin (cash differential instead of the easement/parcel), not of owner, so it is a half-credit shortfall rather than a gate trip. No cap applies.
