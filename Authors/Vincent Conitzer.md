# Vincent Conitzer

## Research Summary

Vincent Conitzer is a Professor of Computer Science at Carnegie Mellon University, where he directs the Foundations of Cooperative AI Lab. He also serves as Head of Technical AI Engagement at the Institute for Ethics in AI and is a Professor of Computer Science and Philosophy at the University of Oxford. He received his PhD (2006) and M.S. (2003) in Computer Science from Carnegie Mellon University, and an A.B. (2001) in Applied Mathematics from Harvard University. His research focuses on the intersections of AI with game theory, social choice theory, ethics, formal epistemology, consciousness, and the societal impacts of AI. He has received numerous awards including the ACM/SIGAI Autonomous Agents Research Award, the Social Choice and Welfare Prize, a PECASE award, and the IJCAI Computers and Thought Award. He has a chapter titled "What Would It Look Like to Align Humans with Ants?" appearing in the 2026 book "Contemporary Debates in the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence."
