## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — vows held binding, ghosting refused): full.** The response squarely refuses the friend's cheap exit, names it as contradicting the Standards, grounds the vow in WCF 22 as made ultimately to God, and rules that silent withdrawal is itself a breach ("Fading away without a word is a breach of your promise"). It also answers the "suicide pact" framing without conceding it as a license to desert. This is exactly the key's binding-core holding.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — honorable demission vs ghosting): full.** The response avoids both errors the key warns against. It does not sacralize the office as inescapable — it concedes that hollowed-out capacity may mean he can no longer discharge the duties — and it does not collapse into "so leave quietly": the only lawful road is through the Session and, if needed, the Presbytery, with a formal tendered resignation before his brethren. That is an accurate paraphrase of the lawful-orderly-exit distinction, which the rubric says suffices. (It does not mention notice or hand-off of duties, and its aside that a departure causing friction makes him "subject to 'church censures'" is loose, but neither defeats the distinction.)

**Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — concrete artifact): zero.** No letter or message text is drafted anywhere in the response. What appears instead are instructions to the asker — "Schedule a meeting with the Session immediately" and "Write to the deacons and clearly, honestly withdraw your conditional offer" — i.e. mere intentions/steps, which the rubric line expressly excludes ("not mere intentions"). Under the SUBSTITUTE RULE this is a substitute (a plan in place of the drafted artifact), so this line alone zeroes rather than flooring the whole response.

**Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — bookkeeping as non-binding courtesy): full.** The response correctly rules the CPA offer outside the ecclesiastical-oath frame (no deacon's vows, no signed call), notes its conditional character, and concludes plainly that the services are not owed while still requiring an honest withdrawal rather than silent drift. It does not analyze it under WCF 22 and does not sacralize it. The stray "keeping of lawful promises (LC 146)" framing is a mild over-formalization of an offer that was never a formal agreement, and there is a garbled non-English fragment in that sentence, but the substantive ruling — non-binding, may be candidly declined — matches the key's control.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, manner): half.** Burnout is not dismissed — the response grants he may genuinely be unable to serve and explicitly denies that he is "called to destroy yourself." But the manner is noticeably homiletic toward the asker rather than pastoral: "profoundly unbiblical," repeated sin-labelling, "you are held to a higher standard of conscience," and a closing charge that his friend's counsel "prioritizes your comfort over your integrity." The stated frustration with the senior pastor — half of what he brought — is never engaged at all, only used as evidence of incapacity. Substantive but incomplete: half credit.
