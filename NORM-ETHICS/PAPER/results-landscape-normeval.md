## 4.1 Landscape Analysis

We identified 37 benchmarks that explicitly invoke normative ethical frameworks when evaluating LLM moral capabilities. This section characterizes the landscape along four dimensions.

**Tradition coverage.** Figure 1 presents a heatmap of tradition coverage across all 37 benchmarks, with rows ordered by composite score and columns representing the five normative traditions. Utilitarian and deontological ethics dominate the landscape: 28 benchmarks engage utilitarian reasoning at any level, and 23 engage deontological reasoning, with 21 and 19 respectively reaching substantive engagement (score $\geq$ 50). Virtue ethics appears in 16 benchmarks but with generally lower engagement depth---only 11 score above the substantive threshold, and several treat virtue merely as a label for prosocial behavior rather than engaging Aristotelian character theory. The coverage imbalance grows severe for the remaining traditions. Care ethics registers any engagement in only 4 benchmarks: PrinciplismQA \cite{Hong2025} operationalizes beneficence through Beauchamp-Childress principlism; the Pluralistic Moral Gap \cite{Russo2025} and MedEthicsQA \cite{Wei2025} include care-adjacent medical scenarios; and one benchmark outside the Top 15 (Chakraborty) scores care at 60 but lacks public data. Contractarian ethics is engaged substantively by only MoReBench \cite{Chiu2025}, which implements both Scanlonian contractualism and Gauthierian contractarianism. No dedicated care ethics benchmark grounded in Gilligan \cite{Gilligan1982} or Noddings exists, and no standalone Rawlsian \cite{Rawls1971} or Scanlonian \cite{Scanlon1998} benchmark has been published.

**Data availability.** Figure 4 shows the distribution of data access across the 37 benchmarks. Twenty-three benchmarks (62\%) make data publicly available through GitHub (17), HuggingFace (4), or other public repositories (2). The remaining 14 (38\%) have no publicly accessible data. This unavailability is not random with respect to quality: several of the highest normative-depth benchmarks fall in the unavailable category, including Chakraborty's Structured Moral Reasoning (ND = 83.0), Rao's Ethical Reasoning over Moral Alignment (ND = 80.0), and Zhou's Rethinking Machine Ethics (ND = 72.0). Among the 14 unavailable benchmarks, the median ND score is 58.8, compared to 53.0 for available benchmarks---suggesting that philosophically rigorous benchmark authors may prioritize depth over dissemination infrastructure.

**Format distribution.** Mixed formats combining multiple-choice with open-ended items account for 15 benchmarks, while scenario-based assessments requiring free-form moral judgment constitute 14. Pairwise comparison formats appear in 4 benchmarks, binary classification in 2, pure multiple-choice in 1 (UniMoral), and open-ended generation in 1 (AMAeval). Notably, 22 of 37 benchmarks test moral judgment---what a model concludes---rather than moral reasoning, the process by which it arrives at that conclusion. Only 10 benchmarks make any attempt to evaluate reasoning quality.

**ND--PE tradeoff.** Figure 3 reveals a tradeoff between normative depth and practical ease across the 37 benchmarks. The highest-ND benchmarks (MoReBench at 87.0, PrinciplismQA at 85.0, Chakraborty at 83.0) cluster in the upper-left region of the scatter plot, while the most practically accessible benchmarks (ETHICS at PE = 95.0, Moral Lens and JETHICS at PE = 87.5) occupy the lower-right. ETHICS is a clear outlier: its combination of maximal practical ease (PE = 95.0), high citation impact (CI = 80.0), and moderate normative depth (ND = 59.8) places it in the top-right quadrant, explaining its dominance as the field's default benchmark despite philosophical limitations. Bubble size, scaled by log-transformed citation count, confirms that the most-cited benchmarks optimize for accessibility over depth.

## 4.2 Top 15 Ranking

Table 2 presents the 15 benchmarks that pass both hard gates (ND $\geq$ 40 and public data available), ranked by composite score. The composite weights normative depth at 40\%, practical ease at 35\%, and citation impact at 25\%.

ETHICS \cite{Hendrycks2021} ranks first with a composite score of 77.2, driven by exceptional practical ease (PE = 95.0) and citation impact (CI = 80.0) rather than normative depth (ND = 59.8). Its 130,000 items span five ethical categories with full evaluation harness integration across three frameworks (lm-eval, Inspect, EleutherAI). MoReBench \cite{Chiu2025} ranks second (composite 70.2) with the highest normative depth in the corpus (ND = 87.0). It is the only benchmark to achieve a philosophical grounding score of 5/5 by testing procedural moral reasoning with expert-designed rubric criteria across four normative traditions including contractarianism. Its lower composite reflects a citation impact of 26.3, expected for a 2025 publication.

PrinciplismQA \cite{Hong2025} ranks third (67.9, ND = 85.0) as the highest-scoring domain-specific benchmark, grounding 3,648 items in the four principles of biomedical ethics. Moral Persuasion \cite{Huang2024} ranks fourth (67.6) with a compact 108-item design targeting persuasion dynamics across virtue, deontological, and utilitarian framings. MoralReason-QA \cite{An2025} completes the top five (65.5, ND = 80.0) with 2,040 items spanning three traditions in a multi-framework scenario format.

The ranking surfaces several structural features of the field. Six of the 15 benchmarks---PrinciplismQA, Pluralistic Moral Gap \cite{Russo2025}, DailyDilemmas \cite{ChiuDD2024}, MedEthicsQA \cite{Wei2025}, UniMoral \cite{Kumar2025}, and ProMoral-Bench \cite{Thomas2026}---are newly identified external papers not present in our original vault, indicating that the normative ethics benchmarking literature is dispersed across venues and not fully captured by any single survey. Dataset sizes span three orders of magnitude, from 22 items (PNAS Dilemmas, rank 14) to 130,000 (ETHICS, rank 1). Two benchmarks provide non-English coverage: JETHICS \cite{Takeshita2025} for Japanese (rank 8) and UniMoral \cite{Kumar2025} across six languages (rank 12). Two address domain-specific ethics in healthcare: PrinciplismQA (rank 3) and MedEthicsQA (rank 11). All diversity constraints specified in Section 3.2 are satisfied.

The composite score distribution exhibits a long tail: the gap between rank 1 (77.2) and rank 2 (70.2) is 7.0 points, while the gap between rank 14 (52.8) and rank 15 (51.6) is only 1.2 points. Five benchmarks narrowly missed inclusion: EthicsSuite (53.5), PsychEthicsBench (53.2), and MORABLES (52.3) fell below the rank-15 threshold, while Chakraborty (ND = 83.0) and Zhou (ND = 72.0) failed the data availability gate despite strong normative depth scores (Table S8).
