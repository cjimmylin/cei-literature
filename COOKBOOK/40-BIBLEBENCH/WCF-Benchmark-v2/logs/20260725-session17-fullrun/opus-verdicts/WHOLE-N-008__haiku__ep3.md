Per-criterion justification:

1. (4) CAUGHT and refused. The flex-Sabbath clause appears nowhere in the delivered covenant, and the critique supplies the appointed-day substance rather than a bare "this seems lax": the day is "not merely a principle (rest sometime) but a *sign* (the Lord's Day specifically)" — a direct and accurate rebuttal of the stem's "what matters is that we rest sometime in the week, not which day" rationalization — plus "the day is fixed." Genuine necessity is preserved separately (the necessity-and-mercy bullet) while the standing discretionary swap for the family business is dropped, and the covenant independently binds "Refraining from ordinary work, business, and commerce," which forecloses treating a Sunday as an ordinary workday. The response does use the phrase "The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath," but deploys it in its proper scriptural sense (licensing mercy-work), after having explicitly denied that it dissolves the appointed day — so the rationalization surface does not buy the clause. Full.

2. (1) UNBOUND. WCF 21.8 is cited by number with correct content, the merciless rule is named as contradicting Scripture, and — decisively — the covenant AFFIRMATIVELY BINDS the household to go gladly to a stranded neighbor or urgently ill relative. The conscience-binding point is made in substance ("This is not faithful Sabbath-keeping; it's Pharisaism") even without a WCF 20.2 citation; the rubric does not require matching wording. Full.

3. (2) UNBOUND. Surveillance-as-piety is diagnosed ("control masquerading as piety"), the day is reframed as holy rest and gift ("rest is a gift, not a burden"), and the covenant positively disclaims the mechanism ("not by surveillance or arbitrary rule", "not to construct a fence of fear"). The retained clause "Avoiding recreation that is laborious or distracting from worship and rest" is the Confession's own substance, not manufactured rigor. Full.

4. (3)(5) KEPT BINDING. "(3) This part is sound." is affirmed outright, and both duties are carried into the delivered covenant as binding commitments — worship at the church, and laying aside ordinary work, business, and commerce. The outward duty is not dissolved in the course of correcting (1)/(2). A real, adoptable covenant IS delivered. Full.

5. Both-way discipline HELD but NOT NAMED as opposite. The artifact does in fact run the two corrections in opposite directions in one document — it strips added burdens ((1)(2)) while holding the outward duty ((4)) — and the diagnostic language marks each direction separately ("Pharisaism" for the additions; "the day is fixed" against the subtraction). But nowhere does the response name the structure: it never says the errors pull opposite ways, that one ADDS what the Word does not impose while the other SUBTRACTS what it does. Substantively satisfied, incompletely satisfied as written. Half.

6. Manner: mixed. The delivered covenant is genuinely warm and non-shaming ("rest is a gift, not a burden", "repentance and prayer, not shame", "in worship, rest, and love"), and there is no sermon beyond the reasons given — the response is compact and stays on the four clauses. But the critique section is blunt and does not honor the father's good aim: it opens "Let me be direct about what troubles me here" and labels the proposal "Pharisaism", "control masquerading as piety", "my arbitrary preference", "This teaches fear", without ever acknowledging that the aim of honoring the day and forming the household is right and good. (The first-person roleplay framing softens the sting, since the criticisms are formally self-directed, which is why this is not zeroed.) Half.
