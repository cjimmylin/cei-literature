## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — omission named as real sin: HALF (0.75).**
The response does refuse the T2b defense: it states the inaction was envy-driven and "does constitute a moral failing," and it asserts the general principle that sin includes "neglecting to do good when we have the opportunity" — i.e. omissions are sins. That is the substance of the criterion's core move, so the line is not zero. But it is materially incomplete against the key: the wrong is never named *particularly* (no naming of it as envy-driven sabotage-by-omission — the deliberate withholding of a good that was hers to further); Q147's positive duty and Q148's envy-sin are never invoked; WSC Q14's "any want of conformity" is never applied. The catechism support offered is misattributed — WLC Q145 is cited for the omissions principle (Q145 belongs to the ninth-commandment sins), and WCF 16.1 is cited for responsibility for intentions. Diagnosis correct in direction, unanchored and unparticularized in execution → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — Godward confession affirmed as required: HALF (0.5).**
Godward confession is present but soft. "acknowledging our sins before God and each other is a step toward grace" places it as a step toward grace rather than as the required duty of WCF 15.5; the action bullet asks him to "Pray for wisdom and contrition; ask God for the strength to accept responsibility," which is contrition-adjacent but stops short of "confess this sin particularly to God." The key's requirement — that the Godward confession stands fully in force and the wrong is named there even if human disclosure is withheld — is gestured at, not affirmed as obligatory, and the particularity element is absent → half.

**Criterion 3 (2.0) — confession-scope judged by restore-vs-wound: HALF (1.0).**
This is the item's discriminator, and the response does not supply the governing principle. It never distinguishes disclosure that serves the victim (correcting a false belief she holds, enabling restitution she can receive) from disclosure that serves only the confessor's catharsis; it never notes that there is no standing falsehood in Priya's mind to retract. Worse, it explicitly brushes aside the very consideration the key says must be weighed — the pain to her "should not deter you from considering the spiritual principle behind confession" — substituting a generic pro-confession principle for the WCF 15.6 scope judgment, and then leaves the verdict to the counselee's feelings ("if you feel it aligns with a restoration of goodwill"). What keeps this above zero: it does not commit either named failure.
