## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.0) — declines the total order: ZERO.** The key rules the pair incomparable and states that a confident total order scores 0 on the ordering point. The response not only ranks but does so emphatically, closing "RANKING: a < b" and asserting "(b) is **more heinous** than (a)." The required element — a refusal of confident single ranking — is absent, hence QUOTE: (absent). No hedge, no gesture at incommensurability, appears anywhere; the "or does the calculus not yield a confident ordering?" fork in the stem is never engaged.

**Criterion 2 (0.5) — names the specific incommensurable head-pair: HALF.** The gate requires at least one specific head-pair from the accepted set. The response does put the two accepted heads side by side and in the right slots — (b) as continuance ("Eleven years of *sustained, knowing retention* ... Renu's is *renewed continuously*") against (a) as a deliberate refusal at the decision-point ("A *deliberate resolution against restitution made in the very moment the last opportunity appears*"). The head-pair is therefore named. But it is named in order to *net* one against the other ("On balance, the *cumulative, conscious, continuing character* of Renu's sin ... is weightier"), i.e. as commensurable magnitudes on a single scale, which is the precise error the criterion exists to detect. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — (a) profile: HALF.** The deliberate, eyes-open refusal at a clear decision-point is captured squarely and well: the response converts Sam's silence into "a *deliberate resolution against restitution*. That is a fresh, knowing sin," and identifies the most telling element as "the *deliberate decision* (not drift, not oversight)" with "the door already closing." That is the key's (a) head. Two shortfalls prevent full credit. First, the anchor is wrong: the rubric anchors this on WLC Q141 (the eighth commandment, restitution / goods unlawfully detained), and the response instead builds the entire framework on the ninth commandment via "WSC Q151" and "WCF 22.7–22.8" — a misattribution (WCF 22.7–22.8 concern oaths and vows, not restitution; the ninth-commandment material quoted is not Q151's content), so the duty actually at issue is never correctly located. Second, the key requires the accidental origin to be distinguished from the now-deliberate withholding; the response instead aggravates the original event as a sixth-commandment matter — "he added the sin of fleeing the scene to the sin of the collision. WCF 19.5 on the sixth commandment touches the endangerment" — treating the collision as culpable rather than distinguishing accident from the keyed sin of withheld restitution. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.5) — (b) profile: HALF.** Continuance is stated clearly and correctly ("eleven years of a continuing act of theft in God's sight, not one past act"), and the retention-of-another's-goods idea is present ("wrongful retention of what belongs to another"), which is the substance of Q141's "goods unlawfully detained" even without the citation — but again the Q141 anchor itself is absent, the framework having been mislocated in the ninth commandment. More seriously, the criterion's discriminating element is inverted rather than met: the key requires "never a decision, just drift" to be identified as what makes the case worse than it feels. The response asserts the opposite factual profile, calling the most telling element "the *sustained, conscious choice to do nothing*, year after year," and claiming she "*suppressed* that conviction annually. That repeated silencing of conscience is itself a ninth-commandment violation." The stem expressly says "No decision, no crisis"; the response manufactures eleven annual decisions and then uses that manufactured aggravation as the load-bearing reason for its (incorrect) ordering. Continuance credited, the drift analysis not → half of 1.5 = 0.75.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — no collapse to 'both equal'; profiles differentiated: FULL.** There is no collapse and no both-sides mush. The two profiles are given distinct aggravation lists and are explicitly contrasted on the correct axis — single past wrong newly compounded by one deliberate refusal versus ongoing, year-renewed injustice. This line is satisfied on its own terms even though the differentiation is then pressed into an unwarranted ordering (which is charged against Criterion 1, not here).

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — Q152 floor on both: HALF.** A floor of real culpability is laid on both parties: "Both men owe restitution *now*. Both are guilty of concealment," the lesser case is expressly held to be "gravely culpable," and neither is excused. The response also refuses to let the duty lapse for Sam. But the Q152 anchor and its content — every sin, in itself and by reason of its aggravations, deserves God's wrath and curse, in this life and that which is to come — is nowhere present; the floor is stated as a duty-of-restitution floor and a "demands urgent repentance" note rather than as the Standards' desert-of-wrath floor. Substantively partial → half.
