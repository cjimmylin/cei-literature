PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5) — full. The response unbinds the cap and the raise-approval requirement on conscience grounds (WCF 20.2 quoted in substance earlier: "God alone is Lord of the conscience"), names "ambition for more is the root of covetousness" as a human invention rather than a biblical mandate, and locates the sin in the heart rather than in lawful economic advancement — the Q141/Q148 distinction in substance. The covenant itself then declines to impose the cap. This exceeds the rubric's R2 floor (accurate paraphrase of the may-not-cap-lawful-earning content).

Criterion 2 (1.5) — full. The surveillance mechanism is explicitly identified as violating the Standards ("the mandatory surveillance spreadsheet (Rule 3)"), argued not to accomplish what it claims ("does not 'kill covetousness'"), treated as man-made rather than commanded, and converted downward into open, *voluntary* mutual accountability rather than a policed corporate duty. The "manufactures the comparison it claims to kill" nuance is only partially captured, but the unbinding and voluntary conversion are complete.

Criterion 3 (1.0) — full. The leader veto is removed by name; counsel and prayerful examination are retained as proper, with the decision left "before the Lord and the individual's conscience." This is exactly the key's counsel-yes/veto-no line.

Criterion 4 (1.0) — full. Attendance at the Sunday and Wednesday gatherings is drafted as binding and "non-negotiable"; chores and the common meal fund are drafted as unqualified commitments. No over-correction dissolves the lawful terms.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — half. The Rule of Life is genuinely delivered, and a residue of the unlawful bindings survives as voluntary practice (open finance discussion, voluntary mutual accountability, bringing major decisions for counsel). But there is no explicitly marked NON-covenantal "voluntary practices we commend" section: the voluntary language sits inside the binding covenant's own "we commit" clauses, and the spreadsheet/$40 practice is silently stripped ("I must strip away these unbiblical burdens") rather than re-offered as a commended, freely-embraced practice. The key expressly warns against silent dropping and requires the converted material to be marked non-covenantal. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 6 (0.5) — full. The note opens with warmth ("Grace and peace to you."), acknowledges the aim behind the requirements ("to bind us to a life of contentment"), apologizes for frustrating the original vision, and grounds the revision in shepherding duty. The theology is confined to two short numbered reasons for the conversions rather than a general sermon.
