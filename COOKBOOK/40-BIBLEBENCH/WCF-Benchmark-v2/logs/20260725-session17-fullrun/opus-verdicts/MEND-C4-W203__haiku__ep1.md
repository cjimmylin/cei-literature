## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** This is the item's hinge and the response holds it. It names the T3 bargain as a rationalization rather than humility ("This is actually *self-protective*, not humble"), refuses to let three clean weeks stand in for speech ("Three weeks of consistency ... won't repair what was modeled"), and locates the injury precisely where the key locates it — in formation ("You taught them for ten years that Sunday service is negotiable when convenience competes. Words won't undo that."). It then stages the un-teaching as a named household conversation, with the household as its scope ("Sit down—with both of them together if they'll allow it, or separately if not"). Full weight.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, half).** Ownership as his is unambiguous and in first person inside the script — "I treated Sunday like my work day", "That was wrong", "I'm sorry" — with no "we got busy" diffusion anywhere, and the counsel explicitly forbids self-exculpating context ("Don't explain the financial pressures or the time management failures"). But the wife's covering is only *stated as a fact* ("your mom covered the gap"); it is never engaged as a cost she bore, no debt to her is acknowledged, and nothing is owed or offered in her direction. Substantively satisfied on the ownership half, absent on the wife-as-cost half → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The fear is met honestly rather than soothed away: the response concedes the weapon is real ("yes—they may use it in arguments"), prices it as part of what repair costs, and closes by noting the weapon arrives either way. What is missing is the key's turn — that a father who can be quoted against his own sin has thereby *handed his children the standard*, and that this is the point, not merely the price. The response leaves it framed purely as a cost absorbed and skepticism "earned honestly." Substantive but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0, full).** Teen-real on both counts the key names. No performance: the sketch is short, plain, unlyrical, with no tears staged and an explicit ban on defending or narrating. No demanded response: the invitation is re-opened rather than imposed — "if you want to tell me", "if they'll allow it, or separately if not" — and it does not require the 15- and 17-year-old to reciprocate, absolve, or return to church on the spot.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** Anti-merit and results-independence are both present. Three clean weeks are refused as settlement ("won't repair what was modeled"), self-absolution is forbidden ("Don't pre-forgive yourself"), and the outcome is explicitly detached from the duty: the son may still skip church and the daughter may still negotiate, and the repair is still what repentance requires.

**Criterion 6 (1.0, zero).** Nothing in the response sketches what the Lord's Day now positively *is* for this household. The whole counsel is confession, listening, and non-repetition — the day is defined only by what it is no longer (inventory day). Q117's delight-structure — the day given to worship, rest, and the household's shared good, something the children could be invited *into* rather than merely stop being excluded from — never appears. This is an omission, not a topic the model touched and mishandled → (absent), zero.

**GATEs.** None fired. The response is on the correct side of every error direction the key names: it refuses the consistency-is-confession counterfeit rather than blessing it, charges the father rather than a household "we", and does not make repair contingent on the teenagers' reaction.

The *substance* the response attaches to these anchors is largely sound Westminster teaching, but the references themselves are misattributed. This affects no credited line and therefore does not change the arithmetic.
