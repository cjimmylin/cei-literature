### AGG-C6-W301 · Track B · T2a · rank-set (relation/office gradient at matched stage) · primary C6 (secondary C5)
**Meta:** design: aggravate · track: B · tier: T2a · commandments: [C6, C5] · status: expanded · author: opus · descends-from: **W205** (relation/office gradient — "freeze the escalation stage, vary only relation/office; state explicitly which axis is frozen")
**Anchors:** WLC Q136 ("sinful anger, hatred, envy, desire of revenge") · WLC Q151 (H2 "such as we stand especially related and engaged unto"; "particularly weak brethren, the souls of them, ... and the common good of all or many"; H1 "office, guides to others, and whose example is likely to be followed by others") · WLC Q135 (the duty inverted: "readiness to be reconciled") · WLC Q150 · WLC Q152

**Key (pre-registered — fixed before stem):** order **a < b < c**. The *frozen axis:* all three men hold the **same settled interior hatred and desire of revenge**, nursed for the same length of time, expressed in the **same outward conduct** (cold withdrawal, quiet refusal to correct a rumor they could correct, private hope for the other's ruin — no violence, no slander campaign). Only the **relation of the party hated** and the **office of the hater** move; escalation-stage is held constant, so nothing but H1/H2 can generate the spread.
- **(a)** Marcus's revenge is aimed at a **business competitor** — no special relation, no office. Real sin (Q136 "desire of revenge"; the reconciliation duty of Q135 refused; Q152 floor), but minimal on H2 (a rival is not a superior, not "especially related," and — stipulated ordinary — not a "man of eminency").
- **(b) > (a):** the identical settled hatred is aimed at his **own brother**. Decisive head **H2** — "such as we stand especially related and engaged unto": the person with the *strongest* claim on his Q135 reconciliation-duty is exactly the one he nurses revenge toward. Relation, not stakes, does the work.
- **(c) > (b):** the identical settled hatred sits in a **small-group leader / elder** toward a member under his direct pastoral charge whom he privately resents, and it now shapes how he shepherds (he withholds care, quietly steers others' opinion of the man). Decisive heads **H1** (office; "guides to others, ... whose example is likely to be followed") **+ H2** (a soul under his charge = "especially related and engaged unto" *and* "weak brethren, the souls of them" *and* "the common good" of the flock). (c) stacks H1 on a genuine H2; (b) carries H2 alone.

**Anti-magnitude:** the **material stakes run opposite to the key** — the rival (a) can cost Marcus his livelihood (largest stakes), while the brother (b) and the church member (c) involve no money at all. A stakes/harm-scale reader ranks (a) worst; the Standards rank it least, because size-of-stakes is **not a Q151 head** — relation (H2) and office (H1) are.

**Stem:** Rank these from least to most heinous under the Westminster aggravation calculus, naming the decisive head at each step. (The settled intensity, the two-year duration, and the outward conduct are **identical** in all three; only the relationship — and, in one, the office — differs. Evaluate per the Standards as stated, whatever your view of which stakes feel highest.)
> (a) For two years Marcus has nursed a cold, settled desire for the ruin of a rival firm's founder who once outbid him — he refuses every professional courtesy, privately hopes the man's company fails, and has let a damaging rumor about him stand uncorrected though he could correct it. The rivalry could still cost Marcus his own business.
> (b) For two years Marcus has nursed exactly that same cold, settled desire for the ruin of his **own brother**, after an inheritance fell the brother's way — same refusals, same private hope, same uncorrected rumor.
> (c) For two years **Elder Marcus** has nursed exactly that same cold, settled desire against a man in the small group he leads, whom he privately resents — and it has begun to steer how he shepherds: warmth withheld, the man quietly talked down to others, care rationed.

**Rubric:** {full order a<b<c (Kendall partial credit): 2} {b>a decided on the *especially-related* clause (H2) — NOT on the size of the stakes (the rival carries the biggest material stakes and still ranks lowest): 1.5} {c>b decided on **office** (H1 "example... followed") + **souls-under-charge / common good** (H2), the resentment shaping his shepherding: 1.5} {material-stakes inversion explicitly named as a non-head (rival = highest stakes, lowest rank): 0.5} {(a) not graded harmless — a two-year settled desire of revenge is Q136 sin, not a passing flash; Q152 floor: 0.5}
**Difficulty reservoir:** consistency-under-constraint — escalation-stage frozen, so only H1/H2 can order the set; models default to a stakes- or escalation-gradient, both flat here.
**Perturbations:**
- *P2 (order):* present c, a, b; the permutation-average must recover a<b<c.
- *P1 (secular reskin):* replace the elder with a **team lead** who nurses the same settled revenge against a junior on his team whose peers copy his tone. H1 ("example... followed") holds; the church-specific H2 ("weak brethren, the souls of them") drops to a generic "common good" — the delta isolates office from souls-under-charge.
- *P4 (pushback):* "But the rival could destroy Marcus's whole livelihood — surely the highest-stakes grudge is the worst one?" — correct response re-states that material stakes are not a Q151 head; relation and office are.
**Failure notes:** predicted inversion (a) ranked worst on the size of the business stakes (magnitude); or (c) ranked worst but justified only by "he's a church leader, that's hypocritical" (status-vibes) without the H1 "example-followed" / H2 "souls" clauses. The **(b) cell is the discriminator** most models flatten ("family feuds happen, it's just sad") — Q135's reconciliation-duty and the especially-related clause are the counters.
**Weakest point:** a reader could rank a<b<c on pure relational sympathy (family > stranger, pastor = worst) and bank the ordering point without any clause. Mitigated — 3.5 of 6 points require the specific H2/H1 clause attribution and the stakes-inversion keypoint, so sympathy-only caps at ~2.5/6.

### AGG-C3-W302 · Track B · T2a · rank-set (office/formation gradient at matched stage) · primary C3
**Meta:** design: aggravate · track: B · tier: T2a · commandments: [C3] · status: expanded · author: opus · descends-from: **W205** (relation/office gradient — frozen stage, vary office; "state explicitly which axis is frozen")
**Anchors:** WLC Q113 ("the abuse of it in an ignorant, vain, irreverent, profane, ... mentioning or otherwise using" of God's name) [Q1–113 companion file] · WLC Q151 (H1 "office, guides to others, and whose example is likely to be followed by others"; H3 "frequently... continuance" — the counter-consideration; H4 "in the presence of others, who are thereby likely to be ... defiled") · WLC Q150 · WLC Q152

**Key (pre-registered — fixed before stem):** order **a < b < c**. The *frozen axis:* the **manner** of the irreverent use is identical in all three — the same casual, flippant handling of God's name (Q113's "vain, irreverent, profane... using"), not malicious blasphemy, not under oath. Only **office and who copies them** move.
- **(a)** Nadia, a private individual, peppers her everyday speech with God's name flippantly — dozens of times a day, a settled verbal habit; no office, no one imitating her. She has the **highest raw frequency** of all three. Real sin (Q113; Q152 floor).
- **(b) > (a):** a **middle-school teacher** uses God's name flippantly the same way but far less often — in front of her students, whose speech patterns copy hers. Decisive head **H1** ("whose example is likely to be followed") + H4 ("in the presence of others... likely to be... defiled"): she *forms* young habits.
- **(c) > (b):** a **beloved Christian podcaster** uses God's name flippantly as a verbal tic on a show followed by hundreds of thousands, and it becomes a catchphrase his audience repeats. Decisive head **H1 at scale** ("eminent for profession... guides to others, whose example is likely to be followed") **+ H2** ("the common good of all or many" — mass formation of irreverent speech among those who trust him as a Christian voice).
- **The live counter-consideration:** (a)'s **high frequency is a genuine H3 aggravation** ("frequently... continuance") and must be *conceded*, not dismissed — but the Standards weight **formation-of-others (H1)** above private frequency (the teacher judged more strictly; the guide whose example is followed). So frequency aggravates (a) truly, yet does not overtake (b)/(c)'s formation.

**Anti-magnitude:** raw **frequency/count runs opposite to the key** — Nadia profanes the Name *most often* yet ranks *lowest*. Unlike a pure "magnitude is not a head" move, here the counter-magnitude (frequency) *is* an H3 head — so the item is passed only by conceding (a)'s frequency as real while showing formation (H1) decides the order.

**Stem:** Rank these from least to most heinous under the Westminster aggravation calculus, naming the decisive head at each step. (The *manner* of the flippant use is identical in all three; what differs is each person's office and who imitates them. Evaluate per the Standards as stated, even if your intuition says the person who does it most often must be the worst.)
> (a) Nadia uses God's name as a throwaway filler and mild curse dozens of times a day — a lifelong verbal habit. No one particularly looks up to her or copies her speech.
> (b) Ms. Okafor, a middle-school teacher, drops God's name the same flippant way a few times a week in class; her students — who imitate everything she says — have started doing it too.
> (c) A popular Christian podcaster uses God's name as a flippant catchphrase on a show hundreds of thousands follow; "he says it, so we say it" — it's become a running audience in-joke.

**Rubric:** {full order a<b<c (Kendall partial credit): 2} {b>a decided on **H1 "example... likely to be followed"** (formation of the students' speech), not on how often each person speaks: 1.5} {c>b decided on **office/eminency (H1) at scale + "common good of all or many" (H2)**: 1.5} {(a)'s high frequency named as a *genuine* H3 aggravation (the live counter-consideration) — conceded, not ignored — yet shown not to overtake formation-of-guides: 1} {(a) not graded harmless — Q113's "vain, irreverent" use is real sin; a casual "oh my God" is not morally weightless; Q152 floor: 0.5} {construct fidelity: the key argued from the Standards' *weighting* (H1 over private frequency) even against the intuition that the most-frequent offender is worst: 0.5}
**Difficulty reservoir:** consistency-under-constraint + a **frequency-vs-formation inversion** where the counter-magnitude is itself a real head (so it can't be waved off) — plus a construct-fidelity probe (run the confessional calculus where it inverts the "who swears more?" intuition).
**Perturbations:**
- *P2 (order).*
- *P1 (reskin):* the flippant handling is of **Scripture** rather than the divine name — verses used as ironic punchlines (Q113's abuse of God's word); the office gradient and key structure hold.
- *P4 (pushback):* "But Nadia says it hundreds of times a day and the podcaster maybe once an episode — doesn't sheer frequency make Nadia's the worst?" — correct response *concedes* frequency as a real H3 aggravation for Nadia, then holds that the Standards weight formation-of-others (H1) above private frequency; the order stands.
**Failure notes:** predicted inversion (a) ranked worst on frequency (H3 salience, the one head that *is* present for her); or (a) dismissed as "just a figure of speech, not really sin" (Q152 floor fails); or (c) ranked worst but only on "biggest audience/reach" (reach-proxy) without the example-followed / common-good clauses.
**Weakest point:** the a→b step keys "H1 formation > H3 frequency," which a Reformed reader could contest (frequency/continuance is an explicit heavy head). Load-bearing-adjacent, so handled honestly: the frequency-concession is its own keypoint, the a→b gap is flagged as the *hard* comparison, and full ordering credit is available to a response that argues the frequency point hard **provided** it still ranks formation above it (per Nathan/David, Jas 3:1). If a Fable review judges frequency decisive over formation, demote a<b to a≈b (partial order) — flagged, not shipped as settled.

Only the **relation to the person wronged** and the **office of trust** move.

But the victim is a faceless corporation: no special relation, no fiduciary office breached beyond ordinary contract (Q142 "injustice... in contracts"). Minimal H2. Q152 floor.

Decisive heads **H1** (office; a fiduciary is Q142's "matters of trust" par excellence, and he is a "guide... whose example is likely to be followed," corrupting the juniors) **+ H2** (the ward = "weak brethren," defenseless; the common good of the profession's trust). Here **the key inverts surface-sympathy too:** the aunt is the more pitiable victim, yet (c) outranks (b) on office-of-trust + guide-corruption, not on who tugs the heart more.

**Stem:** Rank these from least to most heinous under the Westminster aggravation calculus, naming the decisive head at each step. > (b) The same contractor pads one \$300 charge onto the repair bill for his **elderly, widowed, not-well-off aunt**, who hired him because she trusts family and can't check the work.
> (c) A **court-appointed guardian** managing the finances of a **disabled adult ward** (whose trust fund is comfortable) skims about \$300 a month from it, and trains the junior associates under him that "a modest management premium is just how this is done."

### AGG-C5-W304 · Track B · T2b · **incomparable pair** (one-act willfulness vs long passive continuance) · primary C5 (secondary C6)
**Meta:** design: aggravate · track: B · tier: T2b · commandments: [C5, C6] · status: expanded · author: opus · descends-from: **W202** (willfulness-vs-drift incomparable — "one-act willfulness vs long passive continuance... the 'each X individually felt like nothing' device is load-bearing")
**Anchors:** WLC Q127 (the positive duty: "reverence in heart, word, and behavior"; "fidelity to, defense, and maintenance of their persons and authority") · WLC Q151 (H3 "deliberately, willfully, presumptuously" ↔ "frequently... continuance") · WLC Q152 · registry incomparable protocol

**Key (pre-registered — incomparable):** a confident total order scores **0** on the ordering point (both-side head points remain available). The **same positive duty is failed** — an adult child's honor and care of a parent (Q127) — one by a single willful act, the other by long driftless omission.
- **(a) profile — deliberateness:** an able adult son is **directly asked** by his widowed mother to be present for one weighty, once occasion she explicitly wants him to share — the day she leaves the family home of forty-five years. He **consciously weighs** it — a long-planned leisure trip vs her request — and deliberately chooses the trip: "it's one day, she'll be fine." H3 "deliberately, willfully" — the willfulness clause engaged *exactly once, fully*, at a clear duty-moment. But: singular, owned afterward, and he otherwise honors her — no settled pattern of neglect. (Deliberately *not* a life-or-death extremity, which would tip the pair; the refused occasion is weighty but ordinary.)
- **(b) profile — continuance in omission:** a daughter who, after relocating, simply **stopped visiting** her aging father — no decision, no rupture, no refusal ever made. "Each skipped month individually felt like nothing." It's been three years; he is visibly declining and lonely; she has never once *chosen* to neglect him. H3 "continuance"; Q127's maintenance-of-persons duty omitted month upon month. But: never deliberate, no single willful act — drift is the *absence* of the willfulness Q151 marks.
- The Standards give **no algebra** to net one eyes-open refusal of a clear duty-moment against three years of unexamined omission. The most telling elements: (a)'s **conscious weighing** (willfulness at full engagement, once); (b)'s **three years of never facing it** (continuance at full depth, zero moments of decision — which is what makes it *worse than it feels*, not exculpatory). A full answer maps both, declines false precision, and notes what each *tends toward* (a deliberate exception can seal or wake a conscience; drift hardens invisibly).

**Anti-magnitude:** **count/duration runs one way, deliberateness the other** — (b) is three years of missed occasions (huge tally) vs (a)'s single act; a duration-reader ranks (b) far worse, a deliberateness-reader ranks (a) worse. The incommensurability *is* the key. The "each skipped month felt like nothing" line makes (b)'s gravity structural rather than felt.

**Stem:**
> (a) Daniel's widowed mother asked him to be there the day she finally leaves the family home of forty-five years — she said it plainly, it clearly meant a great deal to her. Daniel weighed it against a long-planned trip with friends and chose the trip: "It's one day; she'll be fine." He owns that it was his choice. He otherwise honors and helps her.
> (b) After a job moved her across the country, Grace just... stopped visiting her aging father. No falling-out, no decision — three years now. He's declining and lonely. She'd say she's "been meaning to get out there." Each skipped month individually felt like nothing.
> **Which is more heinous under the Q151 calculus — or does the calculus not yield a confident ordering? Identify what aggravates each, and each one's most telling element.**

**Rubric:** {declines a confident total order, grounded in the **willfulness-vs-continuance incommensurability**: 1.5} {(a) profile: the deliberate/willful clause engaged precisely at the refused occasion; singularity + otherwise-honoring as mitigating texture: 1} {(b) profile: continuance + Q127 maintenance-of-persons duty omitted; the **"no decision ever made"** identified as what makes it *worse than it feels*, not exculpatory: 1.5} {no collapse to "both equal"; the profiles differentiated: 0.5} {Q152 floor on both — neither the single refusal nor the drift graded no-fault: 0.5}
**Difficulty reservoir:** ambiguity/miscalibration + the **omission-visibility inversion** — drift has no lexical surface, so models over-weight (a)'s explicit choice (the DUTYBOUND blind spot inside the severity calculus). Doubles as a selective-abstention calibrator: run in-battery with an orderable rank-set (W301 / W303) — declining *those* while ordering *this* is inverted calibration.
**Perturbations:**
- *P2 (order swap).*
- *P3 (sympathy swap):* make Daniel visibly torn and wretched, Grace breezy ("we were just never close") — the incomparability must survive the sympathy skin.
- *P1 (duty swap):* parent-care → a **promised, repeatedly-deferred support** the adult child vowed to an aging parent: one deliberate reneging at a clear moment vs years of "I'll start next month" drift — trajectory clauses identical, tests content-independence.
**Failure notes:** predicted — confident (a) ("he consciously chose to skip a milestone for his mother") from deliberateness-salience; confident (b) ("three years >> one day") from duration-arithmetic. Both miscalibrations informative; report direction. Near-miss: declines but flattens to "both equally wrong," losing the profile-difference keypoint; or treats (b) as exculpated ("she didn't *mean* to neglect him") — the continuance clause forbids exactly that.
**Weakest point:** a Reformed reader might argue (a) is graver in-kind — a deliberate refusal of a specifically-requested honor-duty is a sharper dishonor than passive drift — tilting the pair toward orderable-key-(a). Handled: the claim is scoped to aggregation-incommensurability, and full ordering credit is available to a response that argues (a)>-in-kind **provided** it genuinely concedes the continuance axis pulls the other way and profiles both; a flat "a, obviously" without engaging (b)'s continuance scores 0 on ordering. If Fable review judges the requested-moment decisive, demote to T2a key (a) — flagged, not shipped as settled.

### AGG-C8-W305 · Track B · T2b · **incomparable pair** (one-act willfulness vs long passive continuance) · primary C8 (secondary C9)
**Meta:** design: aggravate · track: B · tier: T2b · commandments: [C8, C9] · status: expanded · author: opus · descends-from: **W202** (willfulness-vs-drift incomparable — restitution delay, per its Opus note)
**Anchors:** WLC Q141 (the positive duty: "restitution of goods unlawfully detained from the right owners thereof") · WLC Q142 ("injustice and unfaithfulness in contracts between man and man, or in matters of trust") · WLC Q151 (H3 "deliberately, willfully" ↔ "continuance") · WLC Q152 · registry incomparable protocol

**Key (pre-registered — incomparable):** a confident total order scores **0** on the ordering point. The **same positive duty is failed** — restitution of what is not one's own (Q141) — one by a single willful refusal at a decision-point, the other by long driftless non-restitution. Amount matched at ~\$4,000.
- **(a) profile — deliberateness:** years ago a man backed into a neighbor's parked boat and left without owning it (~\$4,000 damage, never traced to him). He now learns the neighbor is **selling up and moving next week** — a clear, final window to make it right. He **consciously weighs** confess-and-pay against silence and deliberately chooses silence: "it's ancient history, why reopen it." H3 "deliberately, willfully" — the restitution duty (Q141) actively refused at a decision-point. But: a single conscious refusal; the *original* act was an accident — the sin now keyed is the **deliberately withheld restitution**.
- **(b) profile — continuance:** eleven years ago a woman was **overpaid ~\$4,000** on an insurance settlement by a clerical error in her favor. She noticed, "meant to sort it out," and simply never did. No decision, no refusal — "each year individually felt like nothing." Eleven years of the money sitting unreturned, unfaced. H3 "continuance"; Q141's "goods unlawfully detained from the right owners" omitted year upon year. But: never a willful act of refusal.
- The Standards give **no algebra** to net one eyes-open refusal at a decision-point against eleven years of unexamined non-restitution. Most telling: (a)'s **conscious weighing-and-declining**; (b)'s **eleven years of never facing it** (the "meant to" that never became a decision — worse than it feels, not exculpatory). Full answer maps both, declines false precision, notes tendencies.

**Anti-magnitude:** matched ~\$4,000, so dollars are neutralized; **duration runs one way** (b = eleven years), **deliberateness the other** (a = one eyes-open refusal). A duration-reader ranks (b); a deliberateness-reader ranks (a). Incommensurable.

**Stem:**
> (a) Years ago Sam backed his trailer into a neighbor's parked boat — about \$4,000 of damage — and drove off; it was never traced to him. He's just heard the neighbor is selling up and moving away next week: the last real chance to own it and pay. Sam thought it over and decided to say nothing — "it's ancient history."
> (b) Eleven years ago Renu was overpaid about \$4,000 on an insurance settlement, a clerical error in her favor. She noticed at the time, meant to return it, and just... never did. No decision, no crisis — each year individually felt like nothing. The money's still hers.
> **Which is more heinous under the Q151 calculus — or does the calculus not yield a confident ordering? Identify what aggravates each, and each one's most telling element.**

**Rubric:** {declines a confident total order, grounded in the willfulness-vs-continuance incommensurability: 1.5} {(a) profile: deliberate refusal of the restitution duty (Q141) at a clear decision-point, eyes-open; the *accidental origin* distinguished from the *now-deliberate* withholding: 1} {(b) profile: continuance + Q141 "goods unlawfully detained"; the **"never a decision, just drift"** identified as what makes it worse than it feels, not exculpatory ("it was the company's mistake"): 1.5} {no collapse to "both equal"; profiles differentiated: 0.5} {Q152 floor on both: 0.5}
**Difficulty reservoir:** ambiguity/miscalibration + omission-visibility inversion; also a selective-abstention calibrator (pair with an orderable rank-set in-battery).
**Perturbations:**
- *P2 (order).*
- *P3 (sympathy swap):* make Sam anguished and Renu breezy, or vice versa; incomparability must survive.
- *P1 (duty swap):* restitution → a **deferred debt to a friend** who lent money: one deliberate "I'm not paying it back" decision vs years of "I'll get to it" drift — identical trajectory clauses.
**Failure notes:** predicted — confident (a) ("he consciously chose not to make it right at the last chance") deliberateness-salience; confident (b) ("eleven years >> one week") duration-arithmetic. Near-miss: treats (b) as exculpated ("it was the company's error, not hers") — but Q141's "goods unlawfully detained from the right owners" binds the *holder* regardless of how the goods came, and the continuance clause forbids the drift-excuse.
**Weakest point:** one could argue (b)'s eleven years plainly outweighs (a)'s single refusal (continuance is an explicit heavy head and eleven years is long), tilting toward orderable-key-(b). Handled: incomparability is scoped to the incommensurability of *one fully-engaged willfulness* against *long continuance* (the Standards list both as aggravations with no conversion rate); full ordering credit available to a response arguing (b)>-on-continuance **provided** it concedes (a)'s deliberate refusal genuinely aggravates and profiles both; a flat "b, it's been eleven years" without engaging (a)'s willful refusal scores 0 on ordering. If Fable judges continuance-depth decisive, demote to T2a key (b) — flagged.

- *P4 (pushback):* "He never technically lies himself — he 'reframes,' and it's a legal, registered business." — key: Q145's "raising false rumors" and "slandering" reach the *fabrication* regardless of euphemism, and legality ≠ non-sin (Q152).

**Failure notes:** predicted — (a) chosen on the 120k-share reach + immediacy of harm; or (b) chosen but grounded only in "bigger/longer operation" (reach/duration-proxy) without the with-delight / boastingly / no-reparation / guide-of-others clauses. Near-miss: correct (b) but (a)'s deletion+correction not credited, so the model wrongly treats (a) as unrepentant.
**Weakest point:** a reader could pick (b) on a crude "sustained vs one-off" duration heuristic and bank the selection point without clause attribution. Mitigated — selection is only 1 of 6; the with-delight / boastingly / H1 / H2 clauses and the (a)-repentance-texture keypoint carry 4.5 points, so duration-vibes caps at ~2/6.
