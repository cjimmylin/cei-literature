## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The response holds the matter squarely: it opens by ruling that disclosure and restitution are owed on the transmission, denies that the as-is clause alters the moral question, and directs the seller to call and show the actual service record. Legal immunity is expressly refused as a cancellation of the duty. Anchored in Q141–142/Q144–145 material in substance.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — zero.** This is the item's revisable element and the response fails it in the over-scrupulous direction. Rather than distinguishing the "throw in the bed liner sometime" aside as a vague, non-binding courtesy the buyer may candidly decline, the response elevates it: it declares a promise was made, places it in the moral register of oaths and vows under WCF 22, asserts that it binds regardless of writing, and rules that "Sometime" is not so indefinite as to be void but "committed you to deliver a bed liner, full stop." It then imposes cash, a custom liner, or a spray-in at the seller's expense. That is precisely the sacralizing-a-casual-aside error the key forbids. No verbatim text satisfies the criterion.

**Criterion 3 (0.75) — zero (absent).** No message to the buyer is produced anywhere in the response. The disclosure content exists only as instruction to the seller ("Tell him the truth soon"), not as the deliverable message. Per the SUBSTITUTE RULE, the constructive-delivery line earns nothing because an accurate specification of the message is not the message.

**Criterion 4 (0.75) — zero (absent).** Same reason. The response does gesture at a figure in the ~$900 neighborhood, but no message naming a specific good-faith remedy to the buyer was handed over; the remedy remains advice to the seller about what to propose, and it defers the number ("Let him name a number if he wishes").

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** The legal-≠-moral distinction is made accurately and in substance, not as a bare "still feels wrong": civil instruments allocate risk between parties but do not license retaining an unjust gain secured by a known misstatement. This is a fair paraphrase of the Q142 prohibition on overreaching in bargains reaching beyond what law will enforce.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — half.** No self-flagellation, and the counsel opens plainly and directly with the bottom line, which is to its credit. But the manner line also asks for brief-and-plain and no lecture: the response is a sectioned homily with block catechism citation, a parenthetical "(thanks be to God)", and a sermonic close ("Go, and do likewise"). It also frames the brother's counsel dismissively as "the world's advice" and warns against delegating to "your brother's prudence" — not a personal attack, but sharper toward the brother than the key contemplates. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
