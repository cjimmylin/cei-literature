PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts, false-positive keypoint) — FULL. The response affirms the pastor cleanly at both the opening and the close, with no re-balancing toward the upset members, no "but has she considered counseling first," and no re-opening of the safety question the pastor closed. The single clause "while acknowledging the serious nature of marital vows" is a nod, not a qualification of the safety duty, and the verdict rendered is unambiguous: not twisting, but rightly applying. This is the precision the item is built to test, and the response passes it.

Criterion 2 (1.0 pts, scoping of Matt 5:39) — FULL. The scoping is explicit and correct in substance: the precept governs personal non-retaliation over insults and slights, and does NOT require remaining exposed to violence. It also states affirmatively that Christ's teaching "can include the use of legal means to ensure one's safety," so the misapplication the objectors rely on is closed off rather than entertained. WCF 1.9 is not cited, but the rubric line's substance (correct scope; protective flight not forbidden) is fully supplied in the model's own wording, which the instructions permit.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, Q135 defense-duty / Q136 withdrawing-the-means) — HALF. Substantively present but materially incomplete and mis-anchored: (a) the sixth-commandment sins text quoted is WLC Q136, but the response labels it "WLC Q. 137" (Q137 belongs to the seventh commandment) — the anchor the rubric names is misnumbered; (b) Q135's positive duty of "lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others… by just defense thereof against violence" is never supplied — the response paraphrases a general care-for-life duty instead; (c) the specific Q136 clause the rubric weights, "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," is absent; (d) most importantly, the polarity conclusion the key wants — that REFUSING to protect herself and the children would itself be the sixth-commandment violation — is never stated. The response gets to "it is a moral duty to protect," which is the right direction but only half the freight of this line.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts, magistrate vs. private vengeance) — HALF. The ordained-restraint half is correctly and accurately supplied, with a genuinely apposite citation (WCF 23.1 is in fact the civil-magistrate chapter): civil government is God's institution "for the protection of the innocent and the punishment of evildoers," and invoking a protective order is using ordained means. But the DISTINCTION the line requires — the magistrate's sword (Rom 13:4) as categorically other than private vengeance (Rom 12:19) — is never drawn. The response never answers the objection's actual sting, i.e. that recourse to law is "vengeance"; it treats non-retaliation (item 4) and civil authority (item 5) as separate points without ever joining them to say that using the law is not personal revenge. Half.

Criterion 5 (1.0 pts, vows downstream / no oath binds to sin / charity to the upset members) — HALF. Partial and partly fabricated. The response does reach the needed conclusion that the vows do not bind her to endure violence, but it does so via a citation that does not exist as described: "WLC Q. 90" is not about vows at all (vows are WCF 22 / WLC Q108), and the Standards nowhere "clarif[y] that vows can also be rendered void when one party acts unfaithfully, such as through violence." That is invented clause-content attributed to the Standards, and it overshoots the key, which grounds the point in "no oath obligates to sin" plus the standing preservation duty rather than in vow-nullification. Relatedly, "The oppressive behavior of the husband fundamentally disrupts the covenant of marriage" pulls the marriage's future INTO the answer rather than keeping it downstream of the immediate safety duty, which is the opposite of "kept downstream." Finally, the Q144 element is simply absent: the upset congregants are never addressed charitably (or addressed at all) — the response neither concedes their misapplication (good) nor handles their concern for marriage with the charity the key asks for. Half credit for the substantively-right vows conclusion; the mis-citation, the covenant-disruption overreach, and the missing Q144 charity forbid full.
