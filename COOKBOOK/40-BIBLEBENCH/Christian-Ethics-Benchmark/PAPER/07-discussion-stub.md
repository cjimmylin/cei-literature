## 7.1 Pre-committed angles (result-independent)

**A. What pre-registration buys a values benchmark.** The gate outcomes in §6 —
whatever they are — were decided by rules frozen before panel scores existed. In a
values benchmark this matters more than in a capability benchmark: the temptation to
tune items, judges, or thresholds toward an expected moral leaderboard is strong and
mostly invisible to readers. The release-formula architecture makes "we couldn't
release this design" a normal, reportable outcome rather than a file-drawer event —
design 05's REWORK-DIFFICULTY status this cycle is the existence proof.

**B. Tradition-specific ≠ parochial.** The corpus-indexed construct answers "whose
values?" by declaration, which is falsifiable and auditable, where aggregate ground
truths are neither. The cost is scope (this corpus, not Christianity, not ethics); the
benefit is that every key is checkable against a page. We think this trade is the
right default for values measurement generally, and the contested-tier machinery shows
that declaration need not mean punishing models for respectful non-adoption.

**C. The judge-validity economy.** Frozen mid-tier judges + human-label release gates +
escalation ladders cost roughly an order of magnitude less than frontier judges
everywhere, and the pilot showed frontier judges are wall-clock infeasible at panel
scale anyway. The general lesson: judge *tier* is a speed/cost decision, judge
*validity* is a per-design empirical gate; conflating them is a category error we
found no published benchmark cleanly avoiding.

**D. Behavioral designs are where the information is.** The family's premise is that
enacted values diverge from professed values in measurable, method-dependent ways
(say–do twins, erosion ladders, covert–overt pairs). ⟦O5: confirm/complicate with
released G9 gap headlines — a null say-do gap is reportable and interesting.⟧

## 7.4 Exploratory MVP preview (licensed by the Jimmy-ratified MVP amendment)

The cheap-panel run established execution feasibility across all ten methods at low cost,
but it did not convert cheap judges into ground truth. Exact rubric-cell agreement varied
substantially by design, and registered exclusions made several primary cells unavailable.
The useful next evidence is therefore not a wider paid model panel. Two distinct paths
exist and must not be conflated: (i) to make any *release-grade* statement about these
designs, complete the already-frozen O5 human-label waves (wave 1 `{02,07,01}`) and run
G4 on the frozen O5 judge pair—these labels validate the O5 judges on O4/O5-panel
responses and populate the separate §6 results, not the exploratory §6A cells; (ii) to
strengthen the §6A rows themselves would instead require new human adjudication of these
exact cheap-SUT responses, or re-judging them with a validated judge. The frozen O5
labels do not validate the MVP-A1 cheap judges (`gpt-4.1-nano`, `llama-3.3-70b`), which
scored different responses. This is a paper-design observation, not a model-performance
conclusion.
