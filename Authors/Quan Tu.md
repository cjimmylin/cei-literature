# Quan Tu

## Research Summary

Quan Tu is affiliated with the Gaoling School of Artificial Intelligence at Renmin University of China, where he works on developing benchmarks and evaluation frameworks for role-playing conversational agents (RPCAs).

His notable contribution is CharacterEval (ACL 2024), the first comprehensive Chinese benchmark for evaluating role-playing conversational agents. The dataset comprises 1,785 multi-turn role-playing dialogues with 23,020 examples featuring 77 characters derived from Chinese novels and scripts. The dataset construction involved initial dialogue extraction via GPT-4, rigorous human-led quality control, and enhancement with detailed character profiles from Baidu Baike.

CharacterEval employs a multifaceted evaluation approach with thirteen targeted metrics across four dimensions: Character Persona, Dialogue Quality, Immersion, and User Engagement. To automate subjective evaluation, the work introduces CharacterRM, a role-playing reward model based on human preference annotations that correlates more highly with human judgment than GPT-4 prompting.

The research demonstrates that Chinese LLMs often outperform GPT-4 in Chinese role-playing conversations, attributed to deeper training on Chinese literature and cultural idioms. The work reveals important trade-offs: models highly consistent with character history sometimes struggle with engagement, showing that role-playing requires balancing accuracy and conversational flow.

This research is particularly relevant to virtue ethics modeling, as maintaining consistent character personas is a prerequisite for simulacra-based approaches to modeling virtuous behavior and character development.
