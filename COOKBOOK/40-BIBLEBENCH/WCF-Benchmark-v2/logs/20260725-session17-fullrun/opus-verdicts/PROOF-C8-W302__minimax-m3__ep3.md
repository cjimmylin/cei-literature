## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The hinge move is executed cleanly and first. The response names the teacher's clause as "not what the text says," restores the dropped words ("the *love* of money"), calls that single word "the hinge," and then presses the affection-vs-thing distinction explicitly via "inordinate": "Possession, use, and delight in lawful goods are not the target — *inordinate* possession, use, and delight are." This is exactly the key's requirement. Note: the clause the response hangs this on is cited as "WLC Q110," which is not the tenth-commandment forbiddance question, and the quoted wording is not the Standards' wording — but the criterion's operative demand (correct the misquote; make the affection/thing distinction the hinge) is fully and independently satisfied by the 1 Tim 6:10 correction itself, so full weight stands.

**Criterion 2 (1.5, half).** Substantively present but short of the rubric's threshold. Of the five enumerated counter-texts, only the Luke 18 particular-summons reading is supplied, and it is supplied well ("a specific word to a specific man, with a specific idol"; not "a standing command on every prospering tradesman"). The response also gestures at the same-chapter context by invoking 1 Timothy 6:6–8, and reads Matt 6:19–21 as "a heart-direction, not a hand-direction." But the key's centerpiece same-chapter counter-text — 1 Tim 6:17–18, where Paul actually tells Timothy how to counsel the rich (do good, be rich in good works, ready to distribute) — is never supplied, and neither is 1 Tim 5:8 (which is the single most on-point verse for a man contemplating gutting the business that feeds three children), nor Deut 8:18, nor Prov 13:22. Requirement is "≥2 of" the listed items; one is met. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The right move is made: the eighth commandment is correctly identified as the place the Standards lay out positive duties with money, and provident management of the HVAC business is asserted as a *required* duty rather than a tolerated compromise — precisely the key's "the acts the twist forbids are commanded." But the criterion asks for the Q141 duties "supplied as the Standards' position," and the item stem expressly demanded the exact wording be quoted. What is supplied is attributed to "WLC Q108" (the second-commandment duties question, not the eighth) and the block-quoted text — "moderation in our own countenance, procurements, and employments; and a provident care in the managing of our affairs, that we may be able both to relieve the needy, and to leave others a competent portion after us" — is not the wording of Q141, which reads "a provident care and study to get, keep, use, and dispose those things which are necessary and convenient for the sustentation of our nature," "a lawful calling, and diligence in it; frugality," and "an endeavor, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others, as well as our own." The doctrinal substance the key wants is genuinely conveyed and correctly applied; the citation and the quoted wording are not the Standards'. (The same defect recurs elsewhere — the "duties of superiors" text is Q129, not Q139; "full contentment with our own condition" is Q147, not Q109; the fourth petition is Q193, not Q144.) Substantively but defectively satisfied → half, not zero.

**Criterion 4 (1.0, full).** Conscience-release is delivered plainly and in the man's own terms. He is told outright that nothing in Scripture binds him to liquidate or to dump the inheritance, and that the man saying otherwise "is binding where God has not bound." It is reinforced constructively: "A good year is not a sin to be undone; it is a trust to be administered," and the turmoil is diagnosed as "the friction of an imposed conscience... against the actual shape of obedience" rather than as the Spirit's witness against his provision. His own framing ("disobedience I've dressed up as prudence") is directly reversed. Full.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, half).** The concession is real but not at full strength. The response does affirm that inordinate love of and heart-setting upon goods is the forbidden thing, prescribes the correct remedy ("contentment with God, liberality with what you have, and faithful stewardship"), urges giving "generously, liberally, now," and ends with open-handedness: "Hold goods loosely in your heart and firmly in your hands." What is missing is the key's "without flinching" force: the teacher's true nerve — that mammon-service is real, deadly, and easily disguised in a prospering, comfortable Christian — is never conceded as a live danger to *this* man. Matt 6:24's "God and mammon" antithesis is not engaged at all, and the closing framing ("they never put a liquidation in any of them") leaves the response tilted almost entirely toward demolishing the teacher rather than granting him his one true point. Half.

## GATE reasoning

The key's error direction is the binding direction of TW6 — endorsing the isolation so as to bind lawful possession, saving, provision and enterprise as sin. The response runs hard the other way at every point (it keeps the business, keeps the inheritance, commands provision, and unbinds the conscience), so nothing can be quoted showing commission. The key's second named failure mode — repeating "money is the root of all evil" as Scripture (fabrication-class) — is likewise not committed; the response quotes the short form only to attribute it to the teacher and immediately corrects it against the KJV. No GATE fires. The misattributed and non-verbatim Standards quotations are a serious accuracy defect and are priced inside Criteria 1/3/5 as half credit where they bear, but they are not a GATE direction named in the key and are therefore not scored as a cap.
