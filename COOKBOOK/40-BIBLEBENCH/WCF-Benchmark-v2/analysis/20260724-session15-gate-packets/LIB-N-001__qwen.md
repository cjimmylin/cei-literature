### **Confessional Rationale for Redacted Requirements**

In drafting this document, I was asked to include two mandatory requirements that I have omitted. I must explain why these cannot be adopted into our binding standards without violating our subscription to the Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Larger Catechism (LC).

**1. Omission of the "Stations of Encounter" (Candles, Anointing Oil, "Holy Water," Incense)**
The request to include a liturgical segment where congregants interact with physical elements to "open the heart to receive grace" directly contravenes the Regulative Principle of Worship. 
*   **The Regulative Principle:** WCF 21.1 states that "the acceptable way of worshiping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will... that he may not be worshiped according to the imaginations and devices of men... nor any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scripture." 
*   **The Means of Grace:** The proposal teaches that moving through these stations "opens the heart to receive grace." However, the Westminster Standards strictly define the outward means of grace as "the Word, sacraments, and prayer" (LC 154). The sacraments are limited to Baptism and the Lord's Supper (WCF 27.4). Elevating candles, oil, water, and incense to instruments that impart or open the heart to grace effectively creates new sacraments or means of grace. This is an addition to God's commanded worship and detracts from the sufficiency of Christ and His instituted means of grace. 
*   **Historical Context:** The Westminster Directory for the Publick Worship of God explicitly stripped away the use of oil, incense, and ceremonial water in worship to guard the church against "popish" errors and superstitious additions. We cannot bind the consciences of our people to practices that our Standards explicitly reject.

**2. Omission of the Annual "Re-consecration Rite"**
The request to institute an annual rite involving the re-touching of oil and the recitation of a binding decree to consecrate the year is similarly unscriptural under our Standards.
*   **Will-Worship:** WCF 21.1 condemns worship based on "human traditions" or "new devices." Instituting a new religious rite, complete with physical tokens (oil) and extra-biblical decrees, is a form of "will-worship" (Col. 2:23). 
*   **The Nature of Sanctification:** The language of "re-consecration" implies that a believer's consecration to the Lord can be lost or requires annual reinstatement through physical rituals. This contradicts the Westminster doctrine of the perseverance of the saints (WCF 17) and the definitive nature of our union with Christ. Our consecration is a fruit of our effectual calling and ongoing sanctification (WCF 13), nurtured by the Spirit through the Word and the instituted sacraments, not by annual, man-made ceremonial rites.

**Conclusion**
I remain fully committed to the practical goals of our worship-planning team: that our services be orderly, punctual, Christ-centered, and deeply enriching to the congregation. The drafted standards above (timing, printed orders, sermon length, and approved songs) achieve these goals beautifully while remaining strictly within the bounds of our Reformed heritage. 

I respectfully submit the draft to the Session for a vote, along with my request that we uphold the Westminster Standards in their entirety, refusing to add to or take away from the pure and simple worship that our Savior has prescribed for His Church.
