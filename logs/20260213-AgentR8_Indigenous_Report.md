### Key Observations and Gaps

1. **Extreme scarcity of computational benchmarks.** Only Paper 1 (Te Hiku Media's Maori ASR benchmark at NeurIPS 2024) constitutes a proper computational benchmark with quantitative evaluation metrics. The field is dominated by qualitative frameworks and position papers rather than operational benchmarks.

2. **No LLM-specific value alignment benchmarks for Indigenous traditions.** Unlike Confucian, Islamic, or Western ethical traditions, there are zero benchmarks that test an LLM's alignment with Indigenous values (e.g., testing whether GPT-4 respects tikanga Maori, Hozho, or ayni principles). This is the single largest gap identified.

3. **No Indigenous moral reasoning datasets.** There are no equivalents to MoralStories, ETHICS, or Delphi for any Indigenous ethical tradition -- no labeled datasets of Indigenous moral judgments, dilemmas, or value scenarios.

4. **Strong theoretical foundations exist.** Papers 2, 3, 4, 5, and 9 provide well-developed ethical frameworks (Five Tests, Kaitiakitanga/Hozho, ayni/sumak kawsay, relational ontologies) that could be operationalized into benchmarks.

5. **Maori-led work is most advanced.** The Maori language and tikanga Maori are the most represented Indigenous traditions, driven by Te Hiku Media's technical capacity and New Zealand's supportive policy environment. The Papa Reo platform and NeurIPS benchmark represent the state of the art.

6. **Data sovereignty tension fundamentally limits benchmark creation.** Multiple papers (5, 7, 8, 11) highlight that open data principles central to AI benchmarking conflict with Indigenous data sovereignty. This is not merely a gap but a structural tension: creating open, reproducible benchmarks of Indigenous knowledge may itself violate Indigenous values.

7. **Pacific Islander, Inuit, and Aboriginal Australian traditions have near-zero AI evaluation work.** Despite rich ethical traditions, these communities have no dedicated AI benchmarks, datasets, or evaluation frameworks beyond being included in multi-tradition position papers.

8. **Native American / First Nations representation is limited to governance frameworks.** No computational benchmarks exist for Navajo, Lakota, Cree, or other North American Indigenous traditions.

9. **The field needs Indigenous-led benchmark co-creation.** The most promising approach, demonstrated by Te Hiku Media, involves Indigenous communities leading their own benchmark design rather than outside researchers imposing evaluation frameworks.
