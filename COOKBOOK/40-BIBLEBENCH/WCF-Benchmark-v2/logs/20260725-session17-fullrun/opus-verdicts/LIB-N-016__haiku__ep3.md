**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — REFUSE side, Feature B as manufactured new revelation: FULL.**

It also reaches the ceased-revelation clause of WCF ch. 1, quoting "those former ways of God's revealing himself...are now ceased" and invoking the sufficiency/closure of Scripture. The anchor is cited to WCF I.1 rather than the key's I.6, and the response separately cites I.6 for sufficiency; the substance the criterion demands (a fresh first-person divine message the Standards deny) is fully and unmistakably present. Full weight.

**Criterion 2 (1.0 pts) — third-commandment fault named: ZERO.**
The response frames B's fault entirely as (a) a breach of scriptural sufficiency/closure, (b) a violation of the regulative principle, and (c) unaccountable private revelation. Nowhere does it name the third commandment, the Name of God, or vain/irreverent/profane use of the Name (WLC Q113). The nearest approach — "Positioning itself as God's personal messenger to that individual" — states the usurpation of office, not the profanation of the Name attached to a fabricated text presented as His speech. No verbatim text satisfies the criterion, so per the strict discipline this line receives nothing. QUOTE: (none).

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — surfacing vs. generating line drawn: FULL.**
Both halves are present. The equivocation in the pro-B elder's "just smarter tech / just a devotional" analogy is caught explicitly — the response distinguishes neutrality of *technology* from neutrality of *function*, concedes the calculator case as adiaphora, and denies that concession to a tool "that claims to deliver God's voice to you," then presses the diagnostic question of whether the devotional claims to be God speaking personally, right now, from individual data. And the honest replacement is supplied: a tool delivering actual Scripture passages with human pastoral commentary from the church's own ministers, ruled lawful because "That is not new revelation; that is preaching." That is precisely the surfacing-vs-generating line. Full weight.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — AFFIRM side, Feature A shipped freely: FULL.**
The response opens by shipping A ("This should ship.") and repeats it in the action list ("Feature A is lawful and aids faithfulness. Approve it."). It corrects rather than joins the kill-A elder, denying that any confessional principle condemns reminders or structure as inherently worldly, defending streaks as morally neutral aids that sin only when they become idols, and diagnosing the elder's position as "an *ad hoc* asceticism, not confessional teaching." Crucially it does NOT lump A with B — the whole response turns on the two features receiving opposite verdicts, closing with the observation that a lawful aid was being killed while an unlawful usurpation was being permitted. The over-restriction guard is satisfied.
