## 1. Gist
This 2020 conference paper (IFIP AICT) by Vassilis Galanos and Mary Reisel critically examines what the authors call the "Japanese turn" in AI and robot ethics — the Western scholarly practice of importing Japanese philosophical and religious values (especially Shinto) into AI ethics discourse without sufficiently rigorous examination. Using an ethnographic study of cross-cultural debate surrounding the AIBO robotic dog, the paper asks whether Shinto-derived values are genuinely and practically relevant to human-robot interaction or whether their invocation risks a kind of ethical exoticism. For values-alignment benchmarking, this paper matters because it raises the meta-level question of whether culturally specific moral frameworks can be straightforwardly transplanted, and it provides empirical grounding (via ethnographic evidence) for thinking about when cultural concepts like kami translate into actionable ethical distinctions.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a theoretical/ethnographic paper, not a benchmark. Its core argument is methodological: Western AI ethics researchers often cite Japanese acceptance of robots as evidence for culturally specific moral values (animism, Shinto, Buddhist non-dualism) without subjecting these claims to empirical scrutiny. The paper uses the AIBO case to test whether these values surface as meaningful cross-cultural differences or remain surface-level rhetorical moves. It argues for a middle path between exoticism (uncritical importation of Japanese difference) and empiricism (dismissing cultural specificity altogether).
