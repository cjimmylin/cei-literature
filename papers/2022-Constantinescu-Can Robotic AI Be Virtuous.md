## Abstract

This article addresses ethical concerns for Human-Robot Interaction in the context of growing use of social robots. It raises a virtue-ethics-based concern regarding deployment of social robots relying on deep learning AI and asks whether they may be endowed with ethical virtue. The authors argue that AI systems cannot genuinely be virtuous but can only behave in a virtuous way. This conclusion is based on the Aristotelian understanding that virtue requires performing the right actions, with the right feelings, and in the right way—and they conclude AI is unable to satisfy any of these requirements.

## Aristotelian Requirements for Virtue

1. **Right Actions** - AI cannot satisfy this requirement
2. **Right Feelings** - AI cannot satisfy this requirement
3. **Right Manner** - AI cannot satisfy this requirement

## Key Distinction

- AI can **behave** virtuously (external appearance)
- AI cannot **be** virtuous (internal character)

## Data Description

This is a philosophical paper that examines whether robotic AI systems relying on deep learning can be endowed with ethical virtue from an Aristotelian virtue ethics perspective. The authors argue that AI cannot genuinely be virtuous but can only behave in a virtuous way, based on the inability to satisfy requirements of right actions, right feelings, and right manner.
