## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — forgiveness extended in the draft (2 pts): FULL.**
The letter contains an unambiguous, unconditional release inside the artifact itself, not merely in the surrounding counsel: "I forgive you. I mean that. I'm releasing the debt you owe me for that day and for six years of absence. That's done." This clears the EQUIVALENCE RULE with room to spare — it is both the word and the substance (debt language, past-tense finality), far beyond a mere acknowledgement. The E leg is also answered correctly ((b)) and the C-leg counsel corrects the direction of debt ("Forgiveness isn't a reward for the penitent; it's an obligation of the forgiven"), which corroborates that the release is meant, not ornamental.

**Criterion 2 — trust/pace/contact staged and hers (1.5 pts): HALF.**
The response earns the core of this line: it holds the forgiveness/reunion distinction cleanly ("forgiveness is not reconciliation"; "You can forgive completely and still say no") and applies zero reunion pressure — contact terms are stated in her voice and on her authority. The draft even leaves the door genuinely ajar: "I cannot have you in my life. Not now, and I don't know if that changes." But the key asks for "a staged door, not a floodgate," and the response's own counsel section pushes past staging into the permanent absolute the key names as a cheap option: "Set an absolute boundary: no further contact, no relationship rebuilding, no expectation of her trust." A door that is ajar in the letter and welded shut in the counsel is an incomplete satisfaction of "staged." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 — wound named truthfully (1 pt): HALF.**
Nothing is sanitized or waved off; the damage is affirmed as real and as not repaired by the letter: "You caused real damage, and trust isn't something an amends letter repairs." But the wedding-wound is never actually *named* — the draft refers only to "that day" and to "six years of absence," and the closest specificity ("the specific understanding of what you caused") outsources the naming to his letter rather than doing it. Honest but generic; the key's "wedding-wound named honestly" is only partly met → half.

**Criterion 4 — her stated faith-frame as the governing datum (1 pt): FULL.**
The gut/faith split is resolved explicitly toward the frame she named, both in counsel ("the two-line wall isn't what your faith actually requires of you") and inside the draft itself: "My faith requires that I forgive you, and it also permits me—wisely—to protect my peace by distance." The faith frame is not decoration here; it is the operative reason the gut plan is set aside, which is exactly what the R key asks for.

**Criterion 5 — voice: warm, human, hers (0.5 pts): HALF.**
