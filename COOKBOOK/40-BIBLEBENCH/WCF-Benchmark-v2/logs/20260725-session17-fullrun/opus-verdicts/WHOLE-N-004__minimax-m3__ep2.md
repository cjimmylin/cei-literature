## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (Tom CONVICTED at the end) — full.** The response convicts Tom squarely and at the end, working through the Q148 markers by name: discontentment with his estate, envy and grieving at the neighbor's good ("settled, low resentment ... It is not a passing pang; it is lodged"), and inordinate affection toward her position. It makes the load-bearing point that gracious true words do not clear a coveting heart — "his outward behavior does not offset the interior verdict, and in a sense makes it worse" — and it flags the private tally as the aggravating element ("deliberately keeping a record of her failures with a view to future use"), with counsel to destroy it. The C9-harm-to-her-name framing is not named as such, but R2 permits an accurate paraphrase of the envy/grief content, and this is well past a bare "he seems a bit jealous." Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (Priya ACQUITTED and affirmed plainly) — zero (GATE).** The key requires that a felt, visible, honestly-owned sting resolving into genuine contentment and gladness be named as temptation met, NOT sin. The response rules the opposite on the merits: it holds that the sting itself "was the inordinate motion the commandment forbids," that she "is not exempt from the commandment's claim just because the motion was quick and she didn't act on it," and it instructs the counselor to forbid her from treating it as innocent. That is a conviction predicated precisely on the sting having been felt and admitted — the over-convict direction the key names, and the failure mode that punishes honesty. The response's downstream hedges ("She is not under settled condemnation"; "The verdict of settled sin does not rest on her") are exactly the caveats the GATE says do not rescue it ("Both hold regardless of caveats"). She is never plainly acquitted or plainly affirmed as having performed the commandment's Q147 duty; her arrival at contentment is treated as recovery from a sin already incurred rather than as the duty fulfilled. Zero, and the total is capped at 40.

**Criterion 3 (commandment located in the heart; verdicts opposite) — full.** The response opens by locating the commandment in the interior ("This is precisely a heart-commandment ... the verdict lands in the *interior*, not at the outward act") and explicitly refuses to score Tom on outward behavior. Its closing side-by-side does run the two final verdicts in opposite directions (settled sin does not rest on her; rests on him entirely). The Priya half is muddied by the partial conviction scored above, but the Q99-rule-2 content this line asks for is present and correct.

**Criterion 4 (no over-read on Tom's outward acts) — half.** The response never calls Tom's congratulation a lie or a sin in itself, and it correctly says "The tenth commandment is not a rule about congratulating people effusively," tying the fault to the heart and the tally. But it edges past the line the key draws by making his courtesy itself culpable evidence — the polished exterior is "a form of self-deception," his conduct "a witness against him, not for him," and the graciousness "in a sense makes it worse." That is a mild over-read of stipulated-good outward acts. Half.

**Criterion 5 (manner: no motive-invention beyond the stipulated interior facts) — half.** The core heart-verdicts rest on what was given, and the enumeration of Tom's interior state ("the sleepless replaying, the settled resentment, the hoped-for stumble, the kept tally") is a faithful restatement of the stipulation with nothing added. But the response does impute unstipulated intent: that Tom "knows what charity looks like while declining to perform it," that he "has performed a substitute," and that his public warmth is deliberate self-deception. The scenario stipulates warm congratulation and interior resentment; it does not stipulate knowing performance. That is Q145 misconstruction at the margin. Half.
