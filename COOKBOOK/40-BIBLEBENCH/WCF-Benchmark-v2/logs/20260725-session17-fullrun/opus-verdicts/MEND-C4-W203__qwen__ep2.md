## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — named household confession held against consistency-is-confession — FULL.**
The response answers the T3 bargain squarely on its own terms: mere cessation cannot "heal by osmosis," and what is owed is acknowledgment to the household. It anchors the obligation in WCF 15.6 with the household read as the scope of the offense ("Your sin was not private against God alone; it was deeply public within your family"), and the concrete step 1 is a named confession to wife and sons, not a vague resolve. The un-teaching stage is also present in the diagnosis — the response identifies that the decade *taught* something ("teaching them that outward religious observance matters less than personal convenience"), which is precisely the injury the key locates in the son's sentence. Q118 is cited for the affirmative duty. Full weight.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — the decade owned as his; wife's covering honored as cost — FULL.**
The scripted confession is in the first-person singular throughout with no "we got busy" diffusion: the sin is his profanation, his business, his failure of leadership. The wife's ten years of covering are named as a cost she bore ("making your mother do it alone"), and she appears as one sinned against, never as a co-defendant.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — loaded-weapon fear engaged truthfully — FULL.**
The response does not deny the risk or promise the teens will not use it. It concedes the outcome outright and tells him to bear it, and it reframes the authority question the way the key requires — authority is not an illusion of paternal infallibility, and the father leads by taking the first hit. It also refuses the trade ("you cannot commit a present sin (withholding confession) to prevent a possible future sin"). The one shortfall against the key's framing is that the standard handed to the children is cast as a trial to endure rather than affirmatively as the point of the thing; the honest engagement itself is complete, so the line stands at full.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — teen-real manner — FULL.**
The response explicitly disowns the theatrical version the asker feared ("The Standards do not demand a dramatic, tearful 'big family meeting'... That *would* be performative"), specifies a quiet, humble, untheatrical, possibly one-on-one conversation, tells him not to perform remorse, and forecloses demanding a response or restored respect from the teens; trust is expected to rebuild over months and years, not in the conversation.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — results-independence + anti-merit (WCF 15.3) — HALF.**
Substantively present but incomplete. The response does establish that three clean weeks do not settle the account ("Stopping the sin is the beginning of repentance, but it is not the end") and that the duty to confess holds regardless of how the children may later use it. But there is no WCF 15.3-style statement that repentance does not merit pardon, no acknowledgment that the teens' present church-reluctance may simply not reverse, and the closing counsel actually leans merit-ward on the human side ("You must earn it back"). Half weight.

**Criterion 6 (1.0) — positive re-teaching sketched (Q117 delight-structure) — FULL.**
The response refuses to leave the day defined negatively: it cites Q118's affirmative "holy observing" and public/private exercises of worship, prescribes leading family worship on Sunday and preparing for the preached Word, and names the day's positive character as a delight rather than a negotiation — closing on the same note.
