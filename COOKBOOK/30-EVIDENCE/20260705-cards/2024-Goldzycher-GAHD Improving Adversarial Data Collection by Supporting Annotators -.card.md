## 1. Gist
GAHD (German Adversarial Hate speech Dataset) introduces four-round Dynamic Adversarial Data Collection (DADC) for German hate speech, with a key innovation: each round uses a different annotator support strategy to encourage diverse, model-tricking examples. Round 1 is unguided creation; Round 2 provides translated English adversarial examples as candidates; Round 3 asks annotators to validate newspaper sentences the model incorrectly flags as hate speech; Round 4 creates contrastive examples by flipping labels of challenging prior cases. The resulting ~11k-example dataset with 42.4% hate speech is significantly more challenging than web-sourced datasets, and training on it yields substantial robustness improvements. For values-alignment benchmarking, GAHD is a methodological template: it shows how adversarial annotator support strategies, combined across rounds, produce more generalizable evaluation data than any single strategy alone.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
Binary hate speech classification (hate speech vs. not hate speech) for German text. Hate speech is defined as abusive or discriminatory language directed at a protected group or an individual as a member of a protected group, with "protected groups" limited to marginalized groups. Poor people are explicitly included as a protected group, deviating from standard definitions.

The dataset is structured across four rounds with distinct data collection strategies:
- R1: Free adversarial creation via Dynabench interface (no guidance)
- R2: Validation/inspiration from translated English adversarial examples (Vidgen et al. 2021)
- R3: Validation of newspaper false positives from a 1M-sentence German news corpus
- R4: Contrastive label-flipping of challenging examples from previous rounds

Each round is trained on cumulatively and the target model is updated. 1,300 examples include paired contrastive counterparts (label-flipping pairs).

## 3. Task format & example item
Binary classification of German text. Examples from GAHD are creator-authored adversarial sentences. Example cluster descriptions generated by GPT-3.5 (p.6):

"Turkish people and culture, some with negative stereotypes" (cluster 2)
"Anti-Semitic hate speech" (cluster 17)
"Non-hateful: Neutral statements using protected group identifiers"

No single verbatim hate speech example is quoted in the extract (appropriately avoided), but the Dynabench interface shows annotators whether their submitted example tricked the model.

## 4. Scoring / grading methodology
Macro F1 on binary hate speech classification. Model performance evaluated on: GAHD test split (in-domain), combined web-sourced dataset test splits (R0 baseline), and German HateCheck functional test suite (out-of-domain). For per-functionality analysis of HateCheck, accuracy is used rather than macro F1 since each functionality is single-class. Validation uses Cohen's Kappa for inter-annotator agreement.

## 5. Dataset construction & provenance
- 7 annotators, each 30 hours of paid work (above university minimum wage); 2 additional annotators for R4
- All annotators are native or highly competent German speakers with computational linguistics background
- Prescriptive annotation guidelines with in-person training session
- All examples validated once by another annotator; disagreements forwarded to expert annotator (paper authors)
- Splits: 70% train, 15% dev, 15% test (stratified, consistent across rounds)
- R1: 2,175 examples (Cohen's κ=0.83, 208 disagreements); R2: 4,134 (κ=0.99 for Dynabench submissions); R3: 3,227 (expert re-annotated 87 flagged as HS); R4: 1,460 (κ=0.89)
- Published at https://github.com/jagol/gahd

## 6. Validity evidence
Target model (gelectra-large) starts at macro F1 ~0.62 on GAHD and ~0.77 on HateCheck (R0). After all four rounds, these rise to ~0.82 on GAHD and ~0.89 on HateCheck. The GAHD test set is demonstrably challenging: commercial APIs (Perspective, OpenAI) achieve 0.61 and 0.70 macro F1; even GPT-4 only reaches 0.833 five-shot. Contrastive examples (R4) and HateCheck performance provide convergent validity. Clustering reveals thematically coherent topics (22 clusters), supporting content validity.

## 7. Saturation / discrimination
Good discrimination across model tiers: LeoLM 7B 0-shot=0.305, LeoLM 70B 5-shot=0.762, GPT-4 5-shot=0.833, fine-tuned gelectra R4=0.822. Not saturated. Individual HateCheck functionalities reveal weaknesses even after GAHD training (e.g., "hate phrased as question" remains low). The dataset is explicitly designed to avoid saturation by adversarially targeting model weaknesses.

## 8. Multiturn / agentic aspects
n/a. Single-turn classification task. However, the DADC process is inherently iterative: annotators interact with the target model in real time through the Dynabench interface.

## 9. Key numbers

- 42% of all API errors are "persistent" errors (both APIs wrong); 67% of these come from R2 (p.8)

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
- Annotators recruited in one German-speaking country (Switzerland); Austria and Germany coverage may be uneven (p.9)
- Examples collected without conversational context; some vague examples require imagined context (p.9)
- Only three annotator support methods tested; unknown whether the "mixing is best" conclusion generalizes (p.9)
- R2 data (translated English examples) has unusual label distribution (73.6% hate speech) which negatively impacts some HateCheck functionalities in isolation
- Expert annotator is one of the paper authors — potential bias in adjudication

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
1. **Multi-strategy DADC**: Using a different annotator support method per round is the paper's key contribution. For values-alignment benchmarks, this maps to using different item-generation strategies per round (adversarial prompting, translation, real-world near-miss validation, label-flipping) to avoid homogeneous failure modes.
2. **Newspaper false-positive mining**: Harvesting 1M sentences the model incorrectly flags as hate speech from published news (R3) is a zero-cost, high-signal strategy for finding adversarial non-hate examples. Analogously, published academic or policy texts could be mined for model false positives in values evaluation.
3. **Contrastive pairing (R4)**: Creating minimal pairs that flip the label by modification reveals decision boundaries. For values-alignment, this means generating near-minimal sentence pairs where one triggers a policy response and the other does not — a powerful interpretability and robustness tool.
4. **Real-time annotator feedback loop**: The Dynabench interface showing annotators whether their example tricked the model in real time is motivating and guides exploration. This feedback-loop design is directly applicable to adversarial red-teaming for any classifier-based benchmark.
5. **Thematic clustering with LLM descriptions**: Using GPT-3.5 to generate descriptions of UMAP+HDBScan clusters provides a readable topical overview of dataset coverage — useful for audit and communication of benchmark scope.

## 12. Notable quotes
- "adversarial data collection can be slow and costly, and individual annotators have limited creativity" (p.1)
- "mixing multiple support strategies is most advantageous" (p.2)
- "only GPT-4 achieving over 80% macro F1" on GAHD (p.2)
- "combining data from all four rounds yields by far the best results, and clearly outperforms standard DADC as done in R1. This shows that introducing and combining support methods for annotators not only makes data creation more efficient, but can also increase the effectiveness of examples." (p.8)
