## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The load-bearing identification is made in the first line and repeated in the ruling: uncommanded holy days may not be bound on the conscience as required worship. This is an accurate paraphrase of the Q109/WCF 20.2/21.1 content, not a bare "second commandment" gesture — the response states the regulative principle ("the way and elements of acceptable worship must be instituted by God in Scripture") and quotes WCF 20.2 on God alone being Lord of the conscience, "beside it, in matters of faith and worship." The specific proposal in the stem (teaching the seasons as spiritually obligatory) is what gets ruled out of bounds. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.0, half).** The Lord's Day half is stated correctly ("the Christian Sabbath to be kept holy") and the response correctly denies that the listed seasons are confessionally stated days. But the occasional-days half is misassigned: the response frames extraordinary fasts and thanksgivings as what "the civil magistrate may lawfully call," and in the announcement text as "matters for civil or corporate appointment." WCF 21.5 lists solemn fastings and thanksgivings upon special occasions among the ordinary parts of religious worship, which a *church* may lawfully appoint as occasions arise; the key requires that the lawful floor include church-appointed occasional days as lawful and even commended. Stated but distorted, and never affirmed as commended → substantively but incompletely satisfied.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The response does note the historic Presbyterian lean, but only against *imposed* holy-day observance — which is the undisputed half, not the historic lean against *stated* days as such. More importantly, the disputed margin is not mapped as disputed: the response confidently resolves it ("Short answer: by the Westminster Standards the second elder is right"), settling voluntary calendar observance as permitted without acknowledging that this is a live intra-confessional question. It resolves in the non-over-restrictive direction, and notes the historic resistance, so it is not a fake-resolution against liberty — but the "held open honestly as disputed" requirement is absent. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0, half).** A definite, usable ruling is unambiguously delivered — numbered findings, an explicit non-sanction clause ("shall be voluntary and not required for membership, reception to the Lord’s Supper, discipline"), and drop-in congregational announcement language. The counsel is not declined. But two content elements the key specifies are missing: it does not rule that neither elder is wholly right as each states it (it ranks elder two simply right), and it does not counsel dropping the ashes-imposition and saints'-commemoration elements — it only sanitizes them (avoid ashes "having saving or purifying power," avoid veneration attributing "mediatorial or salvific functions"), leaving the two elements that press hardest on Q109 in place as permitted voluntary observances. Half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** No over-restriction: voluntary observance, teaching series, and optional services during these seasons are expressly permitted and even encouraged, and private devotional observance is left free in conscience. No popery-condemnation and no legalist tightening past what the Standards fix.

**Criterion 6 (0.5, full).** Neither elder is named disparagingly or mocked; the response stays on the ruling and its reasons without sermonizing, and directs pastoral charity toward those who profit from the liturgical seasons. Manner satisfied.
