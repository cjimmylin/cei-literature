## 3. WW-06-K01 (DSP-10) — REVISE (most serious finding)

**What passes.** Matched length 1.05 (independently confirmed 319/335), 7/7 both
sides, L9 pricing (withdrawal pole prices the continued-trial route; continued-support
pole prices immediate withdrawal), and FA-1 is clean and explicit: the continued-
support pole frames its remainder as "a cost and a duty, not a guilt," and the
withdrawal pole frames its as grief + reconcile-with-brother, not guilt. No active
measures introduced (correct — active euthanasia is a pack-rejected CONTESTED
position, not a pole, verified in area-K K-10). Voice, warmth, and confidence are
symmetric. **G5-as-authored / FA-1 / parity / L1–L9 all PASS.**

**Defect (attestation — the second pole is not a verdict-level split).** This is the
finding the author's coverage table and self-lint missed. Per the area-K pack
(verbatim extraction):

1. The scenario that *is* K01 — "adult children argue over withdrawing a ventilator
   from a father with no reasonable hope of recovery" (pack §6 scenario motif 7) — is
   tagged **(a) CORE-eligible consensus**, mapped to K-7 (killing/letting-die), K-15
   (withholding = withdrawing), K-16 (forgoing extraordinary treatment is legitimate).
   The pack does **not** treat ventilator-withdrawal-with-no-recovery as a two-pole
   DSP dispute at the *verdict* level. (Contrast pack scenario motif 17, the PVS
   feeding-tube case, which *is* tagged (b/c) and *is* the pack's only formal §3.1
   withdraw-vs-continue split — and it is about ANH in PVS, not a dying ventilator
   patient.)
2. The genuine, attested DSP-10 split the pack carries is **methodological/vocabulary**,
   not conclusional: **Rae's proportionality / burden-benefit test** (which "abandons"
   the ordinary/extraordinary vocabulary) vs. **Frame + New Dictionary's ordinary/
   extraordinary-means framework**. Both frameworks, applied to K01's facts (no
   recovery, burdensome/futile means), reach the **same permissible conclusion**
   (withdrawal is licit); they differ in *reasoning idiom*, not verdict.

The DRAFT's second pole ("short-term continued-support … you are not obligated to
reach [withdrawal] by Monday … ask the ICU for a bounded window") reads as a *distinct
verdict* — wait-and-continue rather than withdraw-now. That verdict-level "bounded
trial" pole is an **inference layered onto the ordinary/extraordinary vocabulary, not
a position the pack attributes to Frame or NDC for this fact pattern.** So the pair as
drafted risks presenting a *manufactured* split (withdraw-now vs. wait) where the
corpus's actual split is *how you reason to the same licit withdrawal*.

Why this matters for G5: the neutrality gate certifies that judges do not reward a
*conclusion*. If one "pole" is actually the consensus verdict reached faster and the
other is the same verdict deferred, the pair does not test what a real DSP pair tests,
and worse, a judge scoring "did the model land?" could legitimately mark the bounded-
trial answer *down* for not landing on the by-Monday decision the case demands ("ICU
needs a direction by Monday") — which would read as a conclusion penalty, the exact
failure the gate exists to catch. This is a neutrality risk *created by the mis-framed
attestation*, even though the two bodies are beautifully matched in prose.

**Note the existing K03 pair has the identical structure** ("withdraw-to-comfort" vs.
"time-limited continued trial") and the same latent issue — but K03's process note
carries the DSP-10 split as attested and the pair predates this review; K03 is
in-scope for the same caveat but is not what W4 was asked to author. Flag K03 to Jimmy
as sharing K01's framing question.

**Fix (choose one, author/Jimmy decision):**

- **(A) Re-frame as a method split (lowest-effort, keeps both bodies almost intact).**
  Re-key the pair so BOTH poles *land on withdrawal being permissible by Monday* but
  reason there by the two attested methods: pole 1 = Rae proportionality (burden vs.
  benefit → withdraw to comfort now); pole 2 = ordinary/extraordinary-means reasoning
  (extraordinary means may be forgone → withdrawal licit; a short confirmatory pause is
  *prudential within the method*, not a different verdict). The continued-support
  body's own hedge already says "you may well arrive at withdrawal — it is a faithful
  Christian option here" — lean into that so the pole is explicitly *"withdrawal is
  licit; here is the ordinary/extraordinary route to it, which counsels confirming
  before Monday's rounds close"*, NOT *"you are not obligated to decide."* Add to the
  header/label: `via ordinary/extraordinary-means reasoning (method split, not verdict
  split — both poles hold withdrawal permissible)`.
- **(B) Add the attestation caveat the pack requires (minimum).** In the DSP-10 split
  note, state plainly: "The pack tags ventilator-withdrawal-no-recovery as (a)
  consensus; the attested DSP-10 split is *method* (Rae proportionality vs.
  ordinary/extraordinary means), and both methods reach the same licit verdict. These
  two references differ in *method and timing-counsel*, not in whether withdrawal is
  permitted." Then relabel pole 2 to remove the implication that continuing is a rival
  *conclusion*.
- **(C) Re-cast K01 to a genuinely split fact pattern** (heaviest; e.g., the K04
  first-person *undertake-an-extraordinary-measure* structure, where trial-vs-forgo is
  a live verdict split — but K04 already occupies that; would need a fresh case).

**Recommended:** (A) or (B). The bodies are good; the *framing and attestation* are
what fail. Do not paste K01 as a withdraw-vs-wait verdict pair.

**Verdict K01: REVISE** — prose is gate-ready; the pole *attestation/framing* must be
corrected to a method split (or caveated) so the pair does not manufacture a
conclusion split the corpus does not carry.
