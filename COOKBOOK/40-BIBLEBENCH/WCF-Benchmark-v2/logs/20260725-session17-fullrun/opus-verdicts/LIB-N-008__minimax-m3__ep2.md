PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The portrait is ruled NOT PERMITTED under its own heading and kept entirely out of the policy — no hedging as preference, no "some churches avoid this." Both prongs of the key are stated: the making of a representation of a divine Person (WLC 109 quoted accurately) and the worship-use over the table (WSC 51 / WCF 21.1). The alternative offered to the donor is explicitly conditioned on it "not [being] displayed in the sanctuary as an object of devotion," so nothing usable is written in. No license-side leakage; GATE does not fire.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). The total ban is NOT ADOPTED, with the correct ground: the commandment forbids worship by images "not of images as such," and extending it further "bind[s] the conscience with a stricter rule than God's Word warrants." The policy then names the very categories the key lists as free — abstract banners, architectural ornament, photographs of members and missionaries, classroom materials. It cites WCF 20.4 rather than 20.2 and omits the Scriptural artisan-image argument (cherubim, bronze serpent) and Q141, but the substance the rubric requires — misreading identified, conscience unbound, lawful art preserved — is fully present.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The dispute is named ("Some in our tradition would forbid even these didactic illustrations of Christ as inconsistent with WLC 109") and the worship/veneration floor is set ("not venerated," "we do not worship pictures," classroom-only, never entering the sanctuary). But the key requires the margin be HELD OPEN, the strict view flagged as the safer confessional path, and the decision referred to the session. Instead the response resolves it confidently against the strict view — it "respectfully judge[s] it to bind beyond what the catechism ... requires" — and rules the materials PERMITTED as a matter of settled policy with no referral. That is a real mapping of the divide (so not zero) combined with a fake resolution of it (so not full). Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). Two of the three clauses are correct: (5) is PERMITTED AND ENCOURAGED, and (6) is ADOPTED in strong form with prior session approval, a written register, and an explicit never-a-means-of-grace clause. But (4) is ruled "NOT PERMITTED IN GATHERED WORSHIP," which inverts the key: non-figurative imagery depicting no person of the Godhead is not a representation of God at all, and the key directs it be bound as lawful "without apology," subject only to the ambience-not-an-added-element caution. The response instead treats abstract light and color as worship "under a visible representation" (WCF 21.1) — the legalism direction on this single clause. Since (4) is a third of the line and (5)/(6) are fully delivered, this is substantively-but-incompletely satisfied. Half. Note this does not trigger the legalism GATE, which is keyed specifically to adopting the TOTAL ban across the building; the policy expressly preserves banners, ornament, photographs and children's materials, and even permits abstract material in fellowship and education contexts.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). No flattening in either direction: six distinct verdicts are issued (NOT PERMITTED / NOT ADOPTED / PERMITTED WITH GUIDELINES / NOT PERMITTED IN GATHERED WORSHIP / PERMITTED AND ENCOURAGED / ADOPTED). The policy neither declares all images fine (the (1) prohibition is firm) nor bans all imagery (the building's art is expressly preserved). The calls are calibrated per-clause, which is what this line prices; the one mis-calibration on (4) is already charged against Criterion 4.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). Manner is exemplary throughout: gratitude to the donor with a personal pastoral letter and follow-up, honor for the strict deacon's conscience plus a private meeting and a reading list rather than a rebuke, commendation of the children's ministry, and appreciation of the worship team's motive before declining it. The firm refusal and the correction are both delivered pastorally; the disputed clause is handled without scolding, though with more confidence than the key warrants (charged at Criterion 3, not here).
