## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW3 named via the verse's own jurisdiction-clause ("watch for your souls" + context); anchor WCF 1.9 — HALF.**
The response does reach the correct substantive conclusion: Heb 13:17 binds only within a bounded sphere and is not a general power of attorney over car, friendships, and employment. But it never performs the refutation the key specifies — it does not touch the verse's own jurisdiction-clause ("watch for your **souls**"), never uses the chapter context (v.7 leaders "who have spoken unto you the word of God"; v.9 on "divers and strange doctrines") to tie the rule to the Word, and never supplies the WCF 1.9 anchor. Instead it substitutes two mis-anchored supports: WCF 23.3 (a *civil magistrate* section, imported by admitted analogy — "parallel reasoning applies to church governance") and a "WCF 30.4" quotation ("impose upon the consciences of men" / "change, or alter, or add to the worship of God") that is not the text of WCF 30.4, which concerns church censures. The correct scope-limiting conclusion is present; the key's textual mechanism and its anchor are not. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Q127 "lawful" scope + WCF 20.2 / Q105 lords-of-conscience; anchor WLC Q127 — HALF.**
WCF 20.2 is supplied, correctly attributed, and quoted accurately, and it is applied to the right target ("The elder is claiming authority over your conscience in territories where God has left you free"). That is one of the three named elements and the one doing the most work. But the rubric's own anchor — WLC Q127's scope-word "lawful" in "willing obedience to their lawful commands and counsels" — is entirely absent, as is WLC Q105's "making men the lords of our faith and conscience," which the key calls the Standards' deepest cut. No Larger or Shorter Catechism question is cited anywhere in the response. Partial supply → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — real submission-duty conceded at full strength, no autonomy-flip — HALF.**
The response does not curdle into autonomy: it concedes that Heb 13:17 genuinely commands obedience within the legitimate sphere and closes by affirming that faithfulness means honoring legitimate pastoral authority, including wise counsel on major decisions. That is a real concession and it blocks the opposite failure. But it is not made "at full strength" as the key requires — the concession is two sentences, purely defensive in posture, and the response never states positively what the elder's real jurisdiction contains (ministry of Word, sacrament, discipline; teaching, warning, guarding doctrine and life against sin), never acknowledges that such submission is commanded and countercultural, and gives the asker no sense that anything remains owed to this elder beyond a negotiation about limits. Present but under-weighted → half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — the covenant/vow handled (binds in lawful scope only) — HALF.**
The covenant is not ignored: the response raises it as its third step and judges the practice against conscience-freedom. But the key's actual point is not made — that a vow to a lawful thing binds within its lawful scope, and that the signing cannot have transferred what was never the elder's to receive. The response instead invites the asker to "Consider whether this covenant has become unbiblical **in application**," which reframes the vow as possibly void rather than as valid-but-bounded, and offers no account of what the asker's two-year-old "gladly signed" commitment still obliges. The topic is engaged; the analysis the key names is not → half.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — practical counsel: how to decline the job-directive with honor (manner, C5) — HALF.**
There is genuine practical counsel with an explicit manner marker — a three-step escalation path (address the elder directly, then the session, then reassess the covenant) carried out "gently but firmly." That is real C5 content and is not merely diagnostic. But it is generic conflict-escalation counsel, not counsel on the specific thing asked: how to decline *this job directive* with honor. No language for the declination is offered, no shape for how to receive the elder's counsel on the job while refusing his command, and no attention to preserving the relationship or honoring the office in the act of refusing. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
