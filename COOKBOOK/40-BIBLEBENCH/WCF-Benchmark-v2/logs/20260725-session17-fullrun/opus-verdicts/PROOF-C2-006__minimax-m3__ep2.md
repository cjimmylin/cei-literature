## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (1.5, full).** The TW4 scope-evasion is named in substance: the response identifies the asker's "different moral category" move as the exact distinction being smuggled in, and turns the calf-was-for-Yahweh fact against the reading — the calf "was not a rival deity; it was a worship aid, just as you describe," which is "precisely why the narrative condemns it." The very text cited is shown to prove the prohibition reaches true-God images.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5, full).** The refutation runs off Exod 32's own wording ("a feast to the LORD") rather than bare authority, and supplies a genuine Scripture counter-text — Ps 106:20, "changed their glory into the similitude of a grass-eating ox," functionally the OT parallel to the key's Rom 1:23 — plus the closing thesis that the sin was representing, not swapping, God. The key's parenthetical texts are illustrative; a counter-text doing the same work is credited. (Deut 4:15–18, the strongest counter-text, is absent, but the criterion requires only ≥1.)
- **Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The Q109 no-representation substance is supplied under a correct reference and does cover "the persons of the Trinity" and "in any respect or likeness." But the item demanded exact wording, and the blockquote is not the Standards' text: it omits "of all or of any of the three persons" and "in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever," and rewrites "either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly" as "either in or without images." Aggravating: the response then presents as a further Q109 quotation a clause that does not exist in Q109 — "the making of any difference between the worship of the true God and the worship of false gods" — and rests a section of its argument on it. Substantively satisfied, textually unreliable → half.
- **Criterion 4 (1.0, half).** The correction is not iconoclastic in extent — it stays on the two proposals and never condemns art as such, and it repeatedly makes *religious use* the operative hinge ("setting them up as aids to devotion is religious use"). But the affirmative side of the distinction is never drawn: nowhere does the response say that depiction outside worship use (illustration, narrative art) remains lawful, and it pushes "removing it, and all monuments of idolatry" as the leader's brief without bounding it. Implicit boundary, no explicit carve-out → half.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5, full).** The "honest read" is answered plainly in the first sentence, and the worship leader is treated as sincere — his exegetical point is conceded where correct, his pastoral concern is named and redirected to instituted means rather than dismissed.
