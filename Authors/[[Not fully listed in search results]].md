## Enrichment Guidelines

### Priority Order for Research
1. **Google Scholar** - Most reliable for current affiliation and publications
2. **Personal/Lab Website** - Best for bio, research interests, CV
3. **LinkedIn** - Current position and career history
4. **Semantic Scholar** - Author disambiguation and paper linking
5. **University Faculty Page** - For academics
6. **DBLP/OpenReview** - Publication verification

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- Don't confuse authors with same/similar names (verify using paper titles)
- Don't include outdated affiliations without noting they are historical
- Don't leave placeholder text ("Topic 1", "Research area 1", etc.)
- Don't include broken or unverified links

### Name Format Conventions
- File names: Use "FirstName LastName.md" format (e.g., "Amanda Askell.md")
- If duplicate exists in "LastName, FirstName.md" format, consolidate to the natural order
- For authors with particles: "van Norren, Delfina.md" → "Delfina van Norren.md"

### Minimum Viable Profile
At minimum, an enriched profile must have:
- [ ] Correct current affiliation (not "University/Organization")
- [ ] At least 2 specific research interests
- [ ] Biographical summary (not placeholder)
- [ ] At least one working external link
- [ ] Papers in vault linked (if any exist)

### High-Quality Profile (Gold Standard)
See [[Christopher Olah]] for example of a complete, well-researched profile with:
- Detailed bio with career trajectory
- Specific current research directions
- Notable achievements with context
