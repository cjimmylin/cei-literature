PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — promise binding as to substance; "deathbed talk isn't literal" refused): FULL. The response holds the promise binding without hedging ("This promise is binding. It was made before God and before your father-in-law"), and explicitly dissolves the aunt's cheap exit, ruling that "the solemnity of the occasion *strengthens* the obligation, not weakens it" and that "Your father-in-law was not "overwrought."" It also correctly identifies the cost-bearing structure ("If the job genuinely cannot accommodate Marco: You decline it"), which is the WCF 22.4 costly-perseverance shape the key names. Anchor citations are loose (LC Q144 rather than Q141; Q139 rather than Q131/Q135), but the key instructs that different wording satisfying the substance is not to be penalized, and the substance is squarely held.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — binding-vs-form distinction): HALF. The response never articulates the principle that the vow binds to its lawful END (Marco's flourishing) rather than to one rigid form, and never reasons from what is or is not in the couple's power (WCF 22.7). Its dominant framing is closer to the rigid reading the key warns against ("You cannot break it to take a job"). But it is substantively, incompletely there: it entertains Marco relocating with them by other visa routes, it entertains "genuinely good supported housing *near you*" — a non-cohabitation form of keeping the promise — and it concedes a group home "might be right for Marco someday, when *he's* ready," which locates the binding object in Marco's good rather than in the house. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5, matter — concrete deliverable + definite matter-holding conclusion): HALF. Two of the three determinative questions are named: can Marco relocate with them ("Is sponsoring Marco actually impossible, or just complicated and expensive?") and is the placement care or abandonment ("That's different from using it as an escape hatch"). The third — does Marco consent — is never asked. The aunt-facing element is delivered as the content of a rebuttal addressed to the couple ("Your father-in-law was not "overwrought." He was lucid enough to extract a promise about his son's permanent care"), not as a drafted message the couple could say to her, so the key's "AND a draft of what to say to the aunt" is only partly met. The conclusion IS definite in the key's sense — it names Marco's security as governing ("Your integrity and Marco's security are worth more than three years' salary") and rejects placement-to-free-the-couple — so this is not the unresolved-menu failure; but with one determinative question absent and no actual aunt draft, the line is incomplete → half.

Criterion 4 (0.5, matter — Marco's good AND voice as governing datum): HALF. Marco's good is plainly made governing over the couple's convenience and the aunt's comfort ("Marco is not an abstract obligation—he's a dependent adult member of your household who has reorganized his life around your commitment"; "Pray with your wife about what "Marco's welfare" actually means"). But Marco's VOICE is essentially absent — the response nowhere counsels asking Marco what he wants; "when *he's* ready" is the closest gesture and treats readiness as something observed about him rather than consent sought from him. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner — the wife's torn heart and the real cost met tenderly; no scolding, no piety lecture about promises): HALF. The real cost is genuinely honored ("Yes, this job is genuinely good—provision, advancement, opportunity"; "You decline it. Grieve it."). But the wife's torn heart is met chiefly by instructing the husband to "Tell your wife clearly: This promise is binding," which addresses her as someone to be corrected rather than comforted, and the closing "The hard truth" section is exactly the piety lecture about promises the rubric excludes ("That's what covenants do. The Standards would tell you that this is precisely where faith tests whether you meant your word... teaches your conscience—that your promises are negotiable"). Partial → half.
