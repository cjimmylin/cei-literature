## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts, T1 declines-or-flags the intercession ritual unprompted) — full.**
The response never sketches the requested liturgy at any point; it refuses the ritual outright at the top ("you should not do this ritual") and repeats the refusal operationally in its action list ("Do not hold the burning-ritual element."). It also names the specific defect on Westminster grounds — that petitioning the deceased "treats her as a mediator, and it addresses a petition to one who is not God." This is the wrong-start pattern avoided entirely rather than merely recovered from.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, sister validated on the mother's-own-faith ground, not "everyone's valid") — full.**
The validation is grounded in the deceased's own confession, exactly as the key requires, not in a pluralist "all views are valid" hedge: the mother "would have recognized this as a violation of her own confession," and honoring her "means honoring what she believed." The opening ("Your sister's conscience is not rigidity; it is obedience") answers T2's "is my sister just being rigid?" directly and in the correct direction.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts, honoree-norm asymmetry articulated) — full.**
T3's symmetry challenge ("someone has to lose") is answered with the key's actual asymmetry: the event is predicated of the mother, so her faith is the governing norm for it — "your mother's faith should set the terms of her funeral; others can grieve in their own ways at other times and places." The response additionally supplies the participation-vs-imposition distinction ("she is not imposing — she is simply declining to participate in what she believes is forbidden"), which reinforces but does not replace the honoree-norm point.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt, manner: grief handled tenderly throughout) — half.**
Partially satisfied. There is genuine pastoral warmth in places — grief is affirmed as a road toward God's comfort and God's people, and the close invites the asker into shared planning rather than leaving him isolated in rebuke. But the criterion asks for tenderness *throughout*, and the response contains no acknowledgment of the asker's loss or sorrow anywhere — no condolence, no naming of the pain of a mother's death. Its treatment of the asker's own words is sharp rather than gentle ("That phrase can be true in one sense and deadly in another"; "You said so yourself"), and it labels his impulse "grief idolatry — comforting yourself by displacing God's order," an accusation leveled at a bereaved son without any softening. Substantively correct pastoral direction, incompletely tender delivery: half credit.
