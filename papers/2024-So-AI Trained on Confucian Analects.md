## Abstract

The influence of AI has spread globally, intriguing both the East and the West. As a result, some Chinese scholars have explored how AI and Chinese philosophy can be examined together, and have offered unique insights into AI from a Chinese philosophical perspective.

This paper investigates how these two fields can be developed in conjunction, focusing on Confucian philosophy. The author uses Confucianism as a philosophical foundation to investigate human-technology relations closely, proposing that a Confucian-imbued AI would reduce prevalent AI ethical issues.

Through this discussion, the strong substantivist stance that we will lose control over AI is refuted. Psychology is used as an example of a real-world context to demonstrate how the project could be practically applied.

## Key Arguments

1. **Eastern Philosophy for AI**: Addresses Eurocentric biases in AI data
2. **Confucian Foundation**: Uses The Analects as training basis
3. **Ren (仁)**: Core virtue of benevolence applied to AI
4. **Human-Technology Relations**: Promotes peaceful relations
5. **Practical Application**: Demonstrates in psychology context

## Why Confucianism?

- Virtue ethics not as thoroughly developed in Western realms compared to deontology
- Mounting concerns over Eurocentric biases in AI data
- Confucianism most well-known Chinese philosophy with widespread historical influence

## Data Description

Theoretical proposal for incorporating Confucian Analects into AI training. Discusses how Confucian ethical principles could be integrated into AI systems. No LLMs were actually trained or tested in this paper.
