## Abstract

Values are core drivers of individual and collective perception, cognition, and behavior. Value systems, such as Schwartz’s Theory of Basic Human Values, delineate the hierarchy and interplay among these values, enabling cross-disciplinary investigations into decisionmaking and societal dynamics. Recently, the rise of Large Language Models (LLMs) has raised concerns regarding their elusive intrinsic values. Despite growing efforts in evaluating, understanding, and aligning LLM values, a psychologically grounded LLM value system remains underexplored. This study addresses the gap by introducing the Generative PsychoLexical Approach (GPLA), a scalable, adaptable, and theoretically informed method for constructing value systems. Leveraging GPLA, we propose a psychologically grounded fivefactor value system tailored for LLMs. For systematic validation, we present three benchmarking tasks that integrate psychological principles with cutting-edge AI priorities. Our results reveal that the proposed value system meets standard psychological criteria, better captures LLM values, improves LLM safety prediction, and enhances LLM alignment, when compared to the canonical Schwartz’s values.

## Dataset Information

Psychologically-grounded method for constructing LLM value systems with three benchmarking tasks

## Data Description

The study applies the Generative Psycho-Lexical Approach (GPLA) to 33 instruction-tuned LLMs, each paired with 21 value-anchoring profiling prompts, resulting in 693 LLM subjects. Value-laden corpora are drawn from ValueBench, GPV, BeaverTails, and ValueLex. Three benchmarking tasks are proposed: Confirmatory Factor Analysis, LLM Safety Prediction using SALAD-Bench prompts and safety scores, and LLM Value Alignment using human preference data.
