## Abstract

The researchers examined how well large language models align with human moral judgments by collecting responses to moral scenarios from both humans and LLMs. They discovered important misalignments: "both LLMs and humans tended to reject morally complex utilitarian dilemmas, LLMs were more sensitive to personal framing."

In a study with 230 participants, evaluators actually preferred the AI responses but showed bias against them. The analysis revealed that "subtle linguistic differences in responses, influencing detection and agreement" shaped how people perceived and evaluated the moral judgments, highlighting the intricate dynamics of human-AI interaction in ethically charged contexts.

## Key Findings

1. **Misalignment**: LLMs more sensitive to personal framing than humans
2. **Preference Paradox**: Humans prefer LLM moral responses...
3. **Anti-AI Bias**: ...but rate them lower when they know source is AI
4. **Linguistic Cues**: Subtle differences enable detection
