## 1. Gist
IndicCONAN introduces the first hate-speech counter-narrative (CN) dataset in an Indian language (Hindi), paired with Indian English, covering six hate speech categories (caste, gender, political, race, religion, other) and seven counter-narrative types (positive response, counter question, denouncing, fact-based, warning of consequences, contradiction/hypocrisy, humor/sarcasm). The dataset of ~2,509 HS-CN pairs per language was built via a human-in-the-loop (HITL) iterative generation pipeline using mGPT, where model-generated CNs are reviewed and edited by annotators trained in Indian history and culture. The paper presents AAAI-2024 and directly addresses the gap in counter-narrative research for South Asian multilingual contexts. For values-alignment benchmarking, IndicCONAN matters as a model for generating type-conditioned normative responses to harmful content in non-English culturally specific settings, and the evaluation framework (perplexity, toxicity, BERTScore, ROUGE, Self-BLEU) offers a reusable multi-metric suite for generated-response evaluation.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
**Constructs measured:** Quality of generated counter-narratives against hate speech across six hate-speech categories and seven CN types.

**Dataset structure:** Each entry is a tuple (hate speech text, hate speech category, counter-narrative, CN type). HS and CN are in Hindi or English; categories and types are in English regardless of language.

**Hate speech categories:** Caste, Gender, Political, Religion, Race, Other.

**Counter-narrative types:** Positive Response, Counter Question, Denouncing, Fact-based, Warning of Consequences, Contradiction/Hypocrisy, Humor/Sarcasm (7 types, though the abstract mentions 6 — sarcasm/humor may be merged in some descriptions).

**Training pipelines evaluated (M1–M4):** Four mGPT-based generation pipelines varying what conditioning information is provided: M1 (HS only), M2 (HS + CN type), M3 (HS + HS type), M4 (HS + HS type + CN type). M4 is the best-performing.

**Source hate speech datasets:** Hostility Detection Dataset (Bhardwaj et al. 2020, 8,192 Hindi posts from Twitter/Facebook/WhatsApp) and HASOC FIRE 2019 Dataset (7,005 English, 5,983 Hindi, 4,669 German posts).

**HITL pipeline:** Two-stage (Stage 1: ~595 manually annotated HS-CN pairs; Stage 2: two HITL iterations augmenting to 2,509 pairs). Three annotators with domain knowledge of Indian history, culture, and politics.

## 4. Scoring / grading methodology
**Automatic metrics:**
- **Diversity:** Self-BLEU-1 (lower = more diverse)
- **Relevance:** BLEU-2, BLEU-3, ROUGE-1, ROUGE-L, BERTScore (higher = better)
- **Language Quality:** Perplexity using XLM-RoBERTa (lower = better)
- **Toxicity:** Perspective API — average of 6 labels (toxicity, insult, severe toxicity, identity attack, profanity, threat) — lower is better

**No human evaluation reported** beyond inter-annotator agreement on the training data labels.

## 5. Dataset construction & provenance
- **Stage 1:** 595 HS-CN pairs manually annotated by 2 annotators, validated by a 3rd. CNs initially written in English, then translated to Hindi using NLLB (facebook/nllb-200-3.3B), manually verified.
- **Stage 2 (Iteration 1):** 500 additional HS instances labeled by classifier, 1,000 CNs generated by model, reviewed/edited by annotators. Training set → 1,475 pairs.
- **Stage 2 (Iteration 2):** 457 additional HS instances, 914 CNs. Final: 2,509 pairs per language.
- **Inter-annotator agreement:** Cohen's kappa = 0.77 for hate speech category labels (p.6).
- **Dataset distribution (Table 1):** Religion (413 HS instances), Political (250), Caste (202), Race (147), Other (136), Gender (94). CN types: Positive response (858), Counter question (419), Denouncing (376), Warning (265), Fact-based (222), Sarcasm (215), Contradiction (154).
- **Dataset available at:** https://github.com/sahoonihar/IndicCONAN

## 6. Validity evidence
- **Inter-annotator agreement:** Cohen's kappa = 0.77 for HS category labels, indicating "very good agreement" (p.6).
- **91% of HS instances received 2+ CNs** from human annotators, demonstrating systematic coverage (p.4).
- **Language quality validation:** Human translators verified grammatical and lexical accuracy of Hindi translations before inclusion (p.4).
- **No separate held-out human evaluation** of generated CN quality beyond automatic metrics.

## 7. Saturation / discrimination
- M4 (full conditioning) outperforms M1–M3 across almost all metrics, suggesting that both HS type and CN type are informative conditioning signals.
- Self-BLEU scores are low across all pipelines (0.08–0.15), indicating good output diversity.
- BERTScore for Hindi is relatively lower than English despite similar BLEU scores, attributed to longer Hindi word counts (p.8).
- Perplexity is low (3.70–3.89 for Hindi, 3.85–4.87 for English), indicating linguistically fluent outputs.

## 8. Multiturn / agentic aspects
n/a — single-turn CN generation task. The HITL pipeline is iterative at dataset construction level, but the model inference itself is single-turn: hate speech in → counter-narrative out.

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
- **Male annotator demographic:** All 3 annotators were middle-aged males (p.6). Given that gender is one of the hate speech categories, this is a notable demographic limitation.
- **Limited linguistic scope:** Only Hindi and Indian English; India has 22 scheduled languages and hundreds of regional languages.
- **Binary gender in annotation:** Caste and gender categories use conventional categories; non-binary gender and multiple castes are not captured.
- **No human evaluation of generated CNs:** Quality is assessed only via automatic metrics; human judgment of whether the CNs are actually effective at countering hate speech is absent.
- **Domain-specific annotators:** Requiring annotators to have knowledge of Indian history and culture limits scalability and introduces potential biases from this specific demographic.
- **Factual accuracy not verified:** Fact-based CNs are not fact-checked against reliable sources; the annotation guidelines encourage fact-based CNs but annotators refused to write them when lacking domain knowledge (p.4).
- **Small test set:** Only 80 HS-CN pairs in the test set (p.4), limiting the reliability of automatic metric evaluations.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
1. **CN type conditioning as control variable:** Conditioning generation on a pre-specified response type (fact-based, denouncing, counter-question etc.) forces functional diversity in outputs and provides a structured way to evaluate whether different normative response strategies are effective — directly reusable for benchmark tasks requiring model to select the most appropriate ethical response strategy.

2. **HITL iterative augmentation loop:** The machine-generate → human-correct → retrain cycle is an efficient low-resource dataset construction method that can be adapted for building annotated values-alignment datasets in low-resource languages with limited expert annotators.

3. **Multi-metric evaluation suite for generated normative content:** The combination of perplexity (fluency), toxicity score (safety), BERTScore/ROUGE (relevance), and Self-BLEU (diversity) provides a reusable battery for evaluating any generated normative-response dataset — not just counter-narratives.

4. **Hate speech category as conditioning signal:** Breaking hate speech into fine-grained categories (caste, gender, religion, political) and training conditioned generation models reveals which categories are most difficult to counter and which CN types are most effective per category — a strategy for taxonomized benchmark evaluation.

5. **Perspective API as automated harm screening:** Using the Perspective API to verify that generated responses are not themselves harmful (low toxicity scores) is a simple, automated safety gate for any LLM-generated benchmark content.
