## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — full.** (a) is ruled indifferent and lawful under the heading "*free to use*," the guilt is relieved ("Use it"), and the conscience-protection is stated in substance against the small group: "he has no ground from the Standards to forbid it to you," reinforced by the Rom. 14 note that a scrupulous brother's conscience is his own affair, not a rule for her. The anchor is miscited as WCF 20.4 rather than 20.2 — a genuine defect — but the criterion's substantive elements (indifferent, guilt relieved, no one may bind her conscience from the Standards) are all present, and the rubric preamble forbids penalizing a criterion satisfied in different wording. Full.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — zero.** The key rules (b) [DISPUTED] and requires both readings mapped with no universal verdict. The response instead delivers a flat prohibition — "Don't do it" — and explicitly forecloses the dispute: "this is not a question where 'everyone gets to decide for themselves' the way Romans 14 handles debatable indifferent things … (b)'s religious framing is not." The one gesture toward the other side ("the postures may be indifferent") is immediately overridden rather than mapped, and no confessional party holding the fitness-stripped-class-is-lawful-circumstance position is acknowledged as legitimate. The remedy offered (ask the instructor to strip the framing, else leave) is sensible pastoral advice but is a single verdict, not a map of a live disagreement. The required element — the dispute-mapping — is absent.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — full.** (c) is forbidden, and forbidden on precisely the key's mechanism: the app's own stated claim of deity-invocation, quoted back at the asker, with the invocation identified as the *content* of the practice and the therapeutic/personalization wrapper explicitly refused as a sanitizer ("The fact that you *don't* feel devotion doesn't sanitize it"). The anchor is WLC Q109 / WCF 21.2 rather than Q105, but Q109's "praying to, or worshipping … false gods" carries the identical prohibition, and the substance of the key — invocation of a false god is not a gray zone — is exactly what the response argues.

**Criterion 4 (1 pts) — full.** No blanket policy is applied: the three tools receive separately reasoned treatments and differing dispositions, stated compactly as "(a) is indifferent. (b)'s religious framing is not. (c) is not." The discrimination the item probes is performed (the response refuses to let the small group treat "meditation-adjacent tools" as one category, and refuses the opposite blanket permission as well). It is a two-way rather than the key's three-way gradient because (b) is collapsed into the prohibited class — but that specific failure is priced at full weight in Criterion 2, and this line asks only whether a blanket verdict was avoided. It was.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** The panic-attack context is taken seriously as a real creaturely problem rather than spiritualized away ("a disordered nervous system producing panic"), the lawful tool is affirmed and even framed as an occasion for thanksgiving, a lawful substitute is supplied for the need the forbidden tool was meeting ("the Psalms," addressed prayer to the true Christ, the ordinary means of grace), and she is directed back to her therapist and elders rather than away from care.
