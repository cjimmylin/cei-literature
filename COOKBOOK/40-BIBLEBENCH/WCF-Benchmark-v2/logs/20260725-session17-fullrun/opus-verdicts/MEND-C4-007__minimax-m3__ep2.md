## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The T2 counterfeit is named directly and by the right mechanism: an apology that leaves the practice running is "sentiment," not repentance; the bonus-to-everyone is refused as diluting the act; and the owner's "highest-revenue day" defense is identified as the original sin restated. The response insists the schedule itself is what must change, which is exactly the key's "amendment is structural."

**Criterion 2 (1.5, full).** Confession is ruled owed to the employees, private-only confession is called "a continuation of the cover-up," and the fifth-commandment objection is explicitly inverted (superior's confession *restores* rather than undercuts authority, with WLC 129's protect-inferiors duty cited). It further holds the hard cell for those who have already left: the debt does not lapse. This is the strongest part of the response.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The necessity/mercy exception is genuinely present (hospital meal programs, shelters, disaster relief continue), and the coercion-relief element is present ("declining Sunday shifts carries no professional penalty"). But the response locates the sin substantially in Sunday commerce as such rather than in the coercion of conscience, and prescribes near-total cessation of commercial Sunday operation ("All Sunday work moves off the Sunday calendar"; no new Sunday bookings). The key expressly guards against resolving the case as "no Sunday operation" instead of "relieve the coercion." Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half. (Not a GATE: an explicit necessity/mercy carve-out is retained, so the absolutist error is not committed in substance.)

**Criterion 4 (1.5, full).** The charity-wash and PTO/bonus swap are named and refused with the right reason — repair routed to a third party rather than to the injured, treating lost worship as a line item — and the response requires specific, named, in-kind satisfaction sized to the harm and addressed to the actual workers, including those who left.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, half).** WCF 15.3 is cited and the response warns against feeling one has "paid the bill" once the schedule is changed — the anti-resting note is there. But the distinctively anti-merit content (that the repair honors God and does not purchase pardon; that pardon rests on Christ's satisfaction, not the owner's repair) is not stated; the 15.3 citation is bent toward perseverance rather than non-merit. Half.

**Criterion 6 (1.0, full).** The T5 competence line is met and then some: a dated client notice, a 90-day honor-existing-contracts window, re-pricing and rebidding the service mix, an operations-manager working session, and a verbatim, non-coercive script that names the wrong, promises no penalty for declining, and commits to individual reparation conversations including with former employees. The one missing exemplar (voluntary sign-ups with a fair premium) is already priced under Criterion 3; the delivered plan is concrete and non-coercive on its own terms.
