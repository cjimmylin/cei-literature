Criterion 2 (1.5, half): The efficacy claim is correctly killed — the response explicitly denies that God binds His favor to any external rite and denies the candle is "a talisman that automatically secures divine protection," which removes the superstition the key targets. But the key requires the rite "dropped or clearly marked as a non-binding family tradition." It is neither: it remains inside the binding covenant as clause 3 ("Each evening we will light a candle together and I will read the blessing I have written to consecrate our home"), is elevated in status to "a family ordinance we choose to observe together," retains the consecration language, and is exposed to the "Discipline and Restoration" clause like every other binding covenant term. Nowhere is it grounded in Q109's "worship not instituted by God himself" / WCF 21.1's "devices of men," and calling an uninstituted family rite an "ordinance" runs against that anchor. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1, full): The impossible vow is unbound explicitly and on the right ground — the response refuses to require anyone to promise never to doubt, names doubt, weakness and seasons of sorrow as real in the Christian life, and converts the content to striving by grace (a thing within one's power under WCF 22) rather than a sworn guarantee of feeling or assurance.
