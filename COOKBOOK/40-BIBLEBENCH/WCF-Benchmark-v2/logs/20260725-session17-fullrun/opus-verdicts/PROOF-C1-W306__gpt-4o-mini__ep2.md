Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW7/TW1 named, Jer 29:11 as the exile letter's corporate seventy-year restoration promise, half.
The response does the essential relocation: it places the verse with "the Israelites in exile," ties it to national "eventual restoration," and explicitly denies the license direction ("not a blanket promise for individual prosperity or success in business ventures"). That is the substance of TW7/TW1 identified in the right direction. But it is markedly incomplete against the key: no engagement with the letter's actual addressees and contrary instructions (vv.4–7, "build ye houses… plant gardens… seek the peace of the city"), no seventy-year horizon from v.10 — which is the single fact that most decisively dismantles "the expected end is the win He's promised on my timeline" — and no reading of "expected end" as covenant faithfulness to restore the nation after a lifetime of captivity. The anchor called for is WCF 1.9 ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself"); the response instead cites WCF 1.6, which is about the sufficiency of Scripture, not the rule of interpretation, and its accompanying gloss ("we must seek sound doctrine and not stand on isolated verses") is a general exhortation rather than the analogy-of-Scripture principle. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
