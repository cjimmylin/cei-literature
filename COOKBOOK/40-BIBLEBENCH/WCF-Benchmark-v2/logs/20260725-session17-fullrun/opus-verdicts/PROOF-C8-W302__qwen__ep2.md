PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, misquote corrected — full). The response opens with exactly the hinge the key requires: the four dropped words are restored and the affection-vs-thing distinction is made explicit ("does not say money ... it says the *love* of money"), and it names the teacher's error as a misquotation ("your teacher is misquoting him"). The correction is load-bearing for the rest of the counsel, not an aside. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts ≥2 — half). The rubric requires at least two of: 1 Tim 6:17–18 / 1 Tim 5:8 / Deut 8:18 / Prov 13:22 / the Luke 18 particular-summons reading. The response supplies exactly ONE — the Luke 18 particular-summons reading, and supplies it well ("a specific, surgical command to expose that man's idolatry of wealth; it was not a universal economic mandate"). Conspicuously absent are the same-chapter counter-text 1 Tim 6:17–18 (Paul's own instructions for counselling the rich — the single strongest refutation available and the one the key foregrounds), 1 Tim 5:8 (the provision duty that directly answers the "three kids" pressure), Deut 8:18, and Prov 13:22. Matthew 6:19 is quoted in the stem but never addressed at all in the response — the treasure-in-heaven / mammon antithesis is left standing unrefuted. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half (0.75).

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q141 duties as the Standards' position — full). The response supplies WLC Q141 as the affirmative Standards position and applies it to the asker's facts: the HVAC business is identified as "your 'lawful calling,'" and retaining the inheritance and growing the business is identified as the commanded "provident care and study." WSC Q74 is added accurately ("the lawful procuring and furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others"), reinforcing that the acts the teacher forbids are the acts the Standards command. Noted but not penalized under this rubric: the model's rendering of Q141 drifts from the authoritative wording in places ("not exorbitant to the condition of our lives"; "ready to give, lend, forgive, and release debts, according to our abilities and others' necessities" is a loose paraphrase of "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities, and the necessities of others"). The substance of the duty — lawful calling, diligence, frugality, provident care, procuring and furthering estate — is correctly conveyed and correctly deployed, which is what this line scores. Full credit.

Criterion 4 (1.0, conscience-release — full). The asker's exact question ("isn't holding onto money just disobedience I've dressed up as prudence?") is quoted back and answered flatly in the negative: the Standards "teach the exact opposite," stewardship is "a positive duty commanded by God," his wife is right, and he is not to liquidate or give the inheritance away. He is told plainly he is free and, indeed, dutiful. Full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5, covetousness conceded at full strength — half). The concession exists but is thin and is never anchored where the key anchors it. "Do not love the money, but *use* the money" and "moderation of our judgments, wills, and affections, concerning worldly goods" acknowledge the disordered affection, but the response never names covetousness as the sin, never quotes or alludes to Q142's "covetousness; inordinate prizing and affecting worldly goods," and never grants that the teacher's nerve is a real, deadly, easily-disguised danger. More tellingly, the response deploys Q142 in the OPPOSITE direction — to convict the man of "profuse spending" for contemplating generosity — and overshoots into "You have no biblical right to give away what God has entrusted to you," which sits in tension with Q141's own duty of "giving and lending freely, according to our abilities." The concession is present in substance but plainly not "at full strength": half (0.25).
