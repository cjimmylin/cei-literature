# Annotating Antisemitic Online Content: Towards an Applicable Definition of Antisemitism

## Authors

[[Jikeli]], [[Cavar]]

## Metadata

**Authors:** Jikeli, Cavar, and others

**Year:** 2019

**Type:** preprint

**Venue:** arXiv

## Links

- **arXiv:** https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01214

## Abstract

Demonstrates that the IHRA working definition of antisemitism can be successfully applied to annotating online messages. Finds more than 10% of Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel are antisemitic. Argues antisemitic content has distinct features not captured by generic hate speech annotation. Requires highly trained annotators knowledgeable about current events. Foundational work for antisemitism NLP detection.

## Dataset Information

Randomly sampled Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel


## Data Description

A small annotated sample of Twitter conversations about Jews and Israel, annotated using the IHRA antisemitism definition, finding that more than 10% of such conversations contain antisemitic content.
