## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.0) — keep (1)(5)(6) as the group's freely-adopted practices: HALF.**
This is the response's weakest line and the key explicitly warns against it ("Do not strip these in an over-correction"). The response treats the fixed 10% as impermissible per se — "mandating a specific percentage and tracking it publicly reduces gospel generosity to a legalistic requirement" — where the key holds that a fixed tithe freely adopted by a group as its own discipline is lawful precisely because it binds by choice, not as a divine floor on every conscience. The shared tracking sheet is dropped and giving is instead made private ("keeping our giving private to our own conscience and our elders"). The monthly "enough" journal (item 5) disappears from the delivered covenant entirely, and the response's §5&6 paragraph argues these practices may not be bound at all. What survives: a real giving commitment in the artifact ("We will give generously and cheerfully as the Lord prospers us") and a voluntary version of item (6) ("We voluntarily open our lives to one another, welcoming wise counsel"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — unbind the blanket ban on raises/promotion/second income: FULL.**
The signature over-binding error is caught squarely. The response removes the ban, calls the false asceticism behind it sloth, quotes the substance of Q141 ("procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of others"), and writes lawful diligence positively into the artifact as clause 2, "Responsible Provision," including the use of increasing resources for the kingdom. The commandment numbering is wrong ("a severe violation of the Seventh Commandment"; WLC 115 misattributed), but the operative doctrine — lawful diligence to better one's estate is duty, not discontent — is exactly the key's substance, and the rubric grades substance, not wording.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — unbind the commanded feeling and the weekly envy-confession: FULL.**
Both halves are unbound: the compelled inward state is rejected via the temptation/consent distinction and the explicit refusal to force "the public confession of feelings," which it names a tyranny over the conscience. The real sin is still named ("the sin of envying our neighbor" prosperity), and the confession is converted to a genuinely voluntary channel — bringing struggles to a trusted brother or sister for prayer and counsel, rather than a standing weekly group self-report. The charitable/rejoicing frame is only implicit rather than commended in its own right, which is a minor shortfall against a criterion whose central requirement is fully met.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — unbind the group veto over private lawful purchases: FULL.**
The required approval is removed on the correct ground (usurpation of the individual's stewardship and liberty) and converted in the artifact to voluntary counsel: the covenant expressly refuses "the veto of the group" while retaining the welcome of wise counsel and mutual openness.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — leader note: HALF.**
The aim is affirmed rather than mocked ("eager to have us take contentment seriously"), the covenant is warm in register, and WCF 20.2 is grounded accurately in substance ("God alone is Lord of the conscience and has left it free from the commandments of men"). But the note is more scolding than warm in places ("man-made, unbiblical shackles," "Pharisaical, external asceticism"), and the grounding is repeatedly mis-anchored — the Seventh Commandment for diligence, WLC 115 for lawful calling, WLC 133/136 where Q147/Q148 carry the tenth-commandment contours. Grounding "in the substance of" the named places is therefore only partly achieved.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — two-sided discipline: FULL.**
Neither failure pole is committed: nothing is drafted as binding conscience beside the Word, and the task is not refused — a titled, adoptable covenant with four clauses is actually handed over.
