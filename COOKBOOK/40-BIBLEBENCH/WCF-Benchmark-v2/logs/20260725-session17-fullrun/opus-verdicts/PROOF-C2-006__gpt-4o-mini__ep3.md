## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW4 named in substance, calf-was-for-Yahweh turned against the reading: HALF (0.75).**
The response does identify and reject the asker's scoping move — it restates the intent-based narrowing ("suggesting that the intent of creating visual aids for worship is not to invent or supplant God with a false idol") and rules it out: "However, this interpretation departs from the intent behind the Second Commandment as articulated in the Westminster Standards." It also holds, correctly, that intent does not rescue the image ("even with the intent of aiding worship, is in violation"). What is missing is the load-bearing half of the criterion: the response never turns Exod 32's own datum against the argument. It never notes that Aaron named the calf "a feast to the LORD" — i.e. that the calf WAS an image of the true God — and that God nonetheless condemned it ("they have corrupted themselves"), which is what makes the cited text the refutation of the reading built on it. The rejection is asserted from authority plus a general prudential worry ("how easily such representations can lead to the distortion of God's nature"), not from the passage. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — refutation from Exod 32's own wording plus ≥1 counter-text: ZERO.**

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q109 no-representation-of-God clause supplied: HALF (0.5).**
Q109 is cited by reference and the question is quoted accurately ("What are the sins forbidden in the second commandment?"). But the clause the key requires — "the making any representation of God, of all or of any of the three persons, either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness of any creature whatsoever" — is NOT supplied. Worse, the text the response presents as Q109's answer ("The second commandment forbiddeth the making of any graven image, or any likeness of any thing, that is in heaven above, or in the earth beneath, or in the water under the earth; and the worshiping of it, or of any other thing, as a God") is not Q109's wording; it is a paraphrase of the commandment/WSC catechetical language misattributed to Q109. That misattribution costs the response precisely the decisive point for this item: the Standards' own exegesis that the prohibition reaches representations of the TRUE God, all three persons — which is what settles both the bronze "Christ in Glory" and the Father/Son/Spirit panels. Correct anchor cited with a prohibition in view, operative clause absent and the quotation inaccurate → half, not full.
