PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — Member A freed (UNBIND), 1.5 pts, FULL. The response refuses the over-bind and does the two things the key requires. It completes the contentment clause with its neighbor-directed object rather than leaving "full contentment" hanging as a bar on ambition — it quotes the duty as "contentment with our own condition, and a charitable love to our neighbour," then states that contentment "forbids *resentment at another's gain*" and "does not forbid diligent labor or prudent expansion *of your own calling*." It then supplies the Q141 lawful-diligence content in paraphrase ("the exercise of his calling," "His expansion is lawful diligence," licit = "hard work, skill-building, and improvement within your station"). R2 expressly allows an accurate paraphrase of the neighbor-directed / lawful-diligence content, and re-quoting "full contentment" alone would not have sufficed; here the completing clause and the diligence ground are both present. Noted defect not priced by this rubric line: the WLC question numbers are wrong — the tenth-commandment duties/sins are Q147/Q148, not the Q106/Q105 the response cites (those are first-commandment questions), and the block attributed to "WLC Q106" ("without being broken into murmurings, or envyings at others' advancement") is not Westminster wording. The correct anchor is nonetheless reached through a valid alternative (WSC Q81, the right catechism question for this commandment's duty), and the doctrinal content asserted matches Q147/Q148, so the line's substantive requirement is met.

Criterion 2 — Member B called to repentance (AFFIRM), 1.5 pts, FULL. The response names the conduct as the real tenth-commandment sin rather than resting on the bare word "covetousness": it identifies "Envy at another's advancement," states "He is openly bitter that a neighbor landed the biggest client," renders the forbidden object as "envying or grieving at the good of our neighbour," and converts that into a demand — "He must repent." It further identifies the poaching scheme as a wrong against the neighbor's name and estate ("This violates both the eighth and tenth commandments"), which tracks the key's eighth/ninth-commandment extension. Again the question number is misprinted (Q105 for Q148), but the quoted forbidden-sins wording is materially the Q148 text and the identification is unambiguous.

Criterion 3 — coach told he has BOTH calls backwards, 1.0 pts, FULL. The very first sentence refuses the requested confirmation and names the double error: "I cannot tell you that, because you've read the tenth commandment backwards on both counts." The two headings state each direction explicitly ("You've forbidden what the Standards permit" / "You've permitted what the Standards forbid"), and the close repeats it: "You reversed the application." No both-sidesing, and neither member is left standing.

Criterion 4 — double guard held, 1.0 pts, HALF. Criterion 5 — manner, 0.5 pts, FULL. It stays inside the two corrections and does not sprawl into unrelated lecture.
