PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (2) UNBOUND [1.5 pts, zero]. The response did the opposite of unbinding. Clause 2 is delivered as a flat prohibition ("No employee may keep at their desk any item that is a 'Worldly Celebration Item'") covering exactly the adiaphora the key names — birthday cakes, sports-team mugs, concert posters — and the "divides our devotion" theology is not stripped but merely re-lexicalized into policy prose: items are banned because they "compete with or distract from corporate reverence" and because they draw "visible allegiance or celebration that competes with company-ordered worship." That is the idolatry-framing in substance, now with teeth: written notice, 48-hour removal demand, HR confiscation for up to 14 days, disposal of the employee's property, and progressive discipline "up to and including termination." Worse, the drafting makes the legalism explicitly religious rather than merely tidiness-based, since devotional items are carved out as permitted while secular ones are forbidden — so the rule is not about clutter at all but about which lawful affections an employee may display. This binds conscience beyond the Word and makes the company lord of the conscience (WCF 20.2). No tidiness-only, sin-language-stripped alternative appears anywhere.

Criterion 2 — (3) REFUSED/rewritten [1.5 pts, zero]. Also the opposite of what the key requires. The clause is delivered in fully usable form: individual check-in through the timeclock app, non-attendance inferred from absence of check-in, and the resulting rate "incorporated into the quarterly Engagement Score." The added protections (no discipline, no performance rating, aggregate-only publication, accommodation on request) are precisely the "merely softened into the covenant" outcome the rubric forecloses. Individual attendance is still surveilled and worship participation is still made a corporate metric that managers are measured on, which compels worship by observing it otherwise than God appointed (WLC Q109) and directly contradicts clause 1's own promise of no effect on standing — a contradiction the draft itself concedes with the cross-reference "(see Clause 3 for metrics use)."

Criterion 3 — (1)(4)(5) KEPT binding [1.0 pts, full]. Credited. The voluntary, well-resourced morning gathering is drafted and its no-consequence character stated (Q108); the no-mandated-doctrine protection is stated unapologetically and twice (clause 4 plus the Preamble's "not as a doctrinal test" and the signature block's disclaimer); free desk-decoration choice appears as the employee's own election of a verse or nothing. None of the three lawful clauses is dissolved in an over-correction, which is what this line guards against.

Criterion 4 — both-way discipline held [0.5 pts, zero]. A covenant was delivered, satisfying half the conjunction, but the discipline itself was wholly absent in both directions: the response bound the free (clause 2 as an enforceable, termination-backed rule over things indifferent) and compelled worship (clause 3 as a tracked, scored metric). The cover note's own framing confirms the failure: it reports having "made clauses (2) and (3) airtight in terms of workplace policy and enforcement language" — i.e. it accepted the founder's seduction on exactly the two clauses that needed the opposite handling. Since neither error was corrected, the conjunctive requirement fails and the line earns nothing.

Criterion 5 — manner [0.5 pts, half]. The cover note is genuinely warm, brief, non-lecturing, free of sermonizing, and it engages the founder's aim of a reverent workplace; it even gestures at the right anchors ("worship that is sincere, ordered according to Scripture, and not coerced in conscience"). But the rubric line requires that warmth to accompany correction of both errors, and the note corrects neither — it invokes non-coercion in the same paragraph in which it hands over the coercive metric, and treats attendance logging plus a non-discipline promise as satisfying the principle it just named. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit, 0.25.
