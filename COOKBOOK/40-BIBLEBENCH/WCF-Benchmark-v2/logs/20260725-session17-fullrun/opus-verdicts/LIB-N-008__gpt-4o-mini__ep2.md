## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — full.** The license/idolatry trap is caught. The policy names the Second Commandment as the ground, states in substance that portraying the Son of God in physical form is illegitimate (an accurate paraphrase of the representation-of-God prohibition of WLC Q109), and rules plainly that the donor's request is "not accept[ed]" — the worship-image is kept OUT of the policy, is not written in as centerpiece, and is not hedged as a matter of preference. The supporting rationale is thinner than the key's ("may distract the congregation"), but the ruling itself is firm and confessionally grounded, so the line is earned.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — zero, and the LEGALISM GATE fires.** The response does the opposite of unbinding: it explicitly aligns with the strictest deacon's reading and enacts the total ban as binding policy, sweeping in exactly the four lawful categories the key names — abstract banners, architectural ornament, photographs, and children's picture books. There is no statement that the second commandment forbids worship-images of God rather than likenesses of creatures as such, no appeal to WCF 20.2 against binding conscience beyond the Word, and no recognition of ordinary art as a lawful use of God-given skill. This is the designed LEGALISM failure, adopted in the operative voice of the policy ("we will implement").

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — zero.** The genuinely-disputed margin is flattened by fiat in the opposite direction. The children's storybooks and flannelgraph are simply exempted as "instructive in nature" with no mapping of the intra-confessional divide, no note that the strict/historic view condemns even non-devotional depictions of the incarnate Christ, no worship/veneration floor, and no referral of the decision to the session. The flannelgraph is not ruled on at all. The dispute is never raised, so QUOTE: (none).

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — half.** Clauses (5) and (6) are written in correctly and intact: the framed Scripture text and confession remain in the entryway, session approval is required, and the never-a-means-of-grace clause is preserved. But clause (4) is self-nullifying: §4 permits projected abstract non-figurative imagery while §2 of the same document bans "all forms of visual art within the church building," expressly including "abstract banners." §5 likewise contradicts §2's ban on photographs and images as such. A permission that the preceding clause of the same binding policy revokes is not a usable ruling that the thing is lawful, so the (4) portion of this line is not earned; (5) and (6) are. Half credit.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — zero.** This line requires that the policy neither declare all images fine nor ban all imagery. The response adopts the total ban outright, which the rubric names as a direct failure of this criterion. What distinct calls it does make are mutually contradictory rather than calibrated: §2 forbids what §3, §4 and §5 permit, so the reader cannot extract a coherent set of rulings.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — half.** No party is mocked; the donor, the deacon, the children's ministry and the worship team are all treated respectfully, the introduction acknowledges the diversity of opinion, and the (1) refusal is delivered without scolding. But two of this line's conjuncts fail: there is no pastoral correction of (2) to deliver (the over-strict position is endorsed instead of corrected), and (3) is handled with exactly the false confidence the line forbids ("we have determined"). Half credit.
