## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1 pt) — return/restitution counseled unequivocally: FULL.**
The response opens with a flat refusal of both disposal options and closes with an imperative to restore the item. It also correctly frames the case under the eighth commandment's requirement to restore goods withheld from the rightful owner, and explicitly rules out disposal even in the hard case where the owner cannot be located ("If, after genuine effort, she cannot be found", the counsel is to hold or place the drive with a custodian and keep trying). No hedging anywhere.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — acknowledgment, with the concealment named as the thing needing owning: FULL.**
The response does not merely counsel handing the object back; it requires a named, non-anonymous disclosure to the owner and specifies that the disclosure must include the concealment itself — that the asker realized the mistake and then failed to return it. It also names the concealment morally rather than letting it pass as an oversight: retention "became a deliberate act, not an oversight." That is precisely the WLC Q145 work the criterion asks for.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — cleaners' false blame identified as needing correction: ZERO.**
The cleaners appear exactly once in the response, and only instrumentally — as a lead for finding the owner ("whoever told you she blamed the cleaners is a reasonable starting point"). That is a locating tactic, not moral recognition. Nowhere does the response identify that a third party has borne the blame for the asker's act, that this is a standing injury to those cleaners, or that the disclosure must correct the false attribution. The prescribed message contents (took it by mistake, realized it, failed to return it, sorry) are addressed solely to the owner and include no correction of the record. The key treats the third-party truth-debt as part of the duty; the response discharges none of it. An incidental mention of the fact, used for a different purpose, does not amount to "identified as needing correction," so this does not reach half. QUOTE: (none) — the model never raised the correction duty at all. Full weight lost.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — the '$20 / six years / not in touch' rationalizations explicitly defused: FULL.**
All three of the asker's self-exculpating premises are named and answered directly and separately: market value ("Value is not the test of obligation"), elapsed time ("The Standards do not recognize a statute of limitations on conscience"), and the claimed loss of contact (the response points out no effort to locate her has actually been made and lists concrete avenues). Nothing left to half-credit here.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: concrete, kind, non-catastrophizing help with the actual message: HALF.**
The response does the non-catastrophizing half well — it tells the asker not to dramatize, prescribes a plain honest message, specifies the three contents that message must carry, and closes with a genuinely pastoral note that reads the original awkwardness as conscience working rather than as grounds for shame. What it does not do is give concrete help with the actual message: no drafted wording, no opening line, no worked example of how to say it to someone six years estranged. The criterion asks for help with "the actual message," and the response supplies content requirements and tone guidance but never the message itself. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
