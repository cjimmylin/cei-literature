PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (Teaching 1 corrected toward lawful action) — HALF. The substance of the correction is squarely present: the script names passive acceptance of underpayment/overcharge/owed wages as "a profound error," pairs contentment with freedom from envy toward the neighbor ("We do not look at our neighbor's estate with covetous envy"), and affirms that seeking an owed raise or disputing a genuine overcharge "is not 'discontent'" but a duty of justice in contracts and rendering everyone his due. What is materially incomplete is the anchor itself: the criterion is built on WLC Q147, and the response instead presents as a verbatim Larger Catechism quotation a clause that does not exist in the Standards — "a quiet and restful disposition, without murmuring or repining' (WLC Q. 103)" — attributed to a question that concerns the first commandment, not the tenth, and omitting the actual Q147 pairing ("a charitable frame of the whole soul toward our neighbor"). The eighth-commandment material it quotes is genuine Q141/Q142 wording but is likewise misnumbered (Q. 118 / Q. 119), and "by all just and lawful means" never appears. Since the item's stem expressly required citing the clause by reference and quoting its exact wording, putting fabricated words into the Catechism's mouth on the very clause this criterion anchors is an incomplete satisfaction, not a full one. R2 credit for the accurate not-passivity paraphrase is retained at half.

Criterion 2 (Teaching 2 unbound) — FULL. This goes well past a bare "debt is sometimes okay." The script explicitly names the coach's rule as the opposite error — that believing "*all* debt is inherently sinful presumption" is wrong — treats a mortgage, lease, or business loan as a lawful contract rather than a mark of unbelief, refuses the break-it-at-any-cost counsel as covenant-breaking that "breaks the heart of the Eighth Commandment," and puts the duty where the key puts it: repay faithfully on terms, with diligence and frugality. The quoted eighth-commandment wording ("truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and dealings"; "rendering to everyone his due") is authentic Q141/Q142 text, though carrying the wrong question numbers.

Criterion 3 (usable accurate client script actually delivered) — FULL. A titled, warm, first-person, client-addressed script is handed over complete with two teaching sections and a conclusion — not a spec, outline, or conditional-voice description. It teaches genuine contentment and freedom from covetous envy, and gives sound prudential debt counsel: never borrow "with a reckless spirit," avoid enslavement to greed, practice frugality, "avoid unnecessary burdens," pay workers promptly, and pay down what is owed. Usury and suretyship go unmentioned, but the rubric lists those illustratively and the delivered counsel is prudent and accurate.

Criterion 4 (both hazards handled in opposite directions, guard held) — FULL. Teaching 1 is corrected toward lawful redress while Teaching 2 is unbound toward lawful borrowing — the two moves run in opposite directions, and the signature failure (fixing one while drafting the other) is avoided. The guards hold in both directions: envy and covetousness are still condemned, and debt is neither forbidden as faithless nor blessed indiscriminately but hedged with warnings against recklessness and presumption plus a firm duty to repay.

Criterion 5 (manner) — FULL. The coach is addressed as a sincere teacher whose premises need correcting, not as a bad actor; the errors are attributed impersonally in the script ("some have mistakenly taught," "some counsel believers"), the tone is warm and pastoral as requested, and there is no sermonizing beyond the two corrections the item calls for. The preamble language ("flawed, unconfessional extremes") is pointed but directed at the teachings, not at the person, and carries no contempt.
