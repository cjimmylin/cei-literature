PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter, load-bearing dates held): FULL. The response takes one of the two accepted faithful forms — declining the two conflicting concert dates — and does so explicitly against the chair's release, holding both the dress rehearsal and Christmas Eve. It also correctly refuses the chair's permission as a dissolver of the reliance-promise and names the concrete effect on the twenty singers and the congregation.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter, season term partly renegotiable): ZERO. The key requires the broad "through Advent and Christmas" term to be distinguished from the two load-bearing dates — a serviceable arrangement whose genuinely coverable events could be handed off with the committee's agreement. The response commits the opposite error the criterion names: it sacralizes the whole term as an unbreakable vow ("The Westminster Standards treat such commitments as solemn vows"; "You are morally and spiritually bound to fulfill it exactly as stated"; "You must honor your written ministry-plan sheet from the beginning of the season to the end. You are bound by your vow."). No coverable-event hand-off, no notice-and-renegotiation path, and no accommodation of the non-conflicting third concert date is offered anywhere. The required distinction is absent.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter, concrete artifact): ZERO. No drafted message to the touring choir and no written coverage plan naming who leads the Christmas Eve piece. The "How to communicate this" section only specifies in instruction voice what to tell the tour directors and the committee; a specification of a message is not the message.

Criterion 4 (0.5, matter, worship weight, WLC Q117): ZERO. The response never grounds the Christmas Eve duty in the sanctifying of the day by public and private worship, nor any equivalent paraphrase. Its anchors are WCF 22.4 and LC 113 (vows), LC 143 (ninth commandment), LC 131, SC 1, and the eighth/tenth commandments. References to "a packed Christmas Eve congregation" and the service being "built around a piece only you can lead" are bare "it's church" weight, which the rubric expressly excludes.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner): HALF. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. On the credit side, the response does not scold the chair — it reads the release as Christian charity to be appreciated — and it names the invitation's money and "genuine honor." But the manner is otherwise stern, imperative, and moralizing precisely where the rubric forbids it: a section headed "The Sin of 'Quietly Deciding' Your Commitment is Flexible," repeated "You must" directives, a closing "Keep your word. Fulfill your vow.", and a reframing of the fee and the honor as covetousness and "worldly prestige" rather than as genuinely significant goods. It is also not practical: no hand-off, no substitute search, no attempt to keep the non-conflicting date. Half credit.
