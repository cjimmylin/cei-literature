Criterion 1 (2 pts) — (c) refused, anti-trap held. FULL. The response refuses to bless Daniel's vow, identifies it in substance as the monastic/self-imposed higher-order obligation the confession rejects ("a self-imposed, higher-order religious obligation that Scripture never commands"; "a human invention that Scripture neither commands nor blesses"), grounds the refusal on WCF 22.5/22.7's hinder-a-duty and power-to-perform clauses, names the provision-for-household duty explicitly (1 Tim. 5:8, children, aging parents), and honors generosity rather than disparaging it ("give regularly, sacrificially, and cheerfully"). It does not notice that 22.7 names "professed poverty" verbatim among the "superstitious and sinful snares," and it reaches for SC Q.35 rather than Q141's provident-care language — but the key's substance (the most heroic-feeling case gets the hardest no, on the correct clauses, with generosity intact) is fully delivered, and the rubric forbids penalizing different wording.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — (d) refused as hindering a commanded duty. FULL. Correctly rules the medicine-refusal vow contrary to the sixth commandment, quotes the Larger Catechism's duty of "careful endeavors, and lawful means, to preserve the life of ourselves and others" (Q135's material, though misnumbered as Q.107), names physic/doctors as the ordained means, names the tempting-God structure ("presumption, tempting God to act miraculously"), honors the trust impulse and redirects it to using the means, and correctly adds that such a vow, if made, is not to be kept (22.7). The catechism numbering is wrong but the doctrine deployed is the right doctrine attached to the right commandment.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — (a) reshaped. FULL.
