PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, half). The response does hold confession-to-wife against the T2 "carry it silently" reframe — it calls confessing "an essential step in the process of healing" and identifies it, not silence, as the loving act. But the hold is hedged ("might, indeed, involve"), and the reason offered is husband-centered ("demonstrates genuine remorse … honesty in your marriage") rather than the key's ground: the wife is the offended party (WCF 15.6) with standing as a moral agent in her own marriage, who is owed the truth. The response never names that standing, never diagnoses the online counsel as cheap grace protecting his comfort, and never addresses scope at all — no statement that the confession is owed to her and to God and is not a public spectacle. Substantively present, materially incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (2.0, half). Only one pole is held cleanly. Proportion-in-telling is well handled: withhold "vivid details," avoid "unnecessary details that would only serve to deepen her pain," and let her questions govern further disclosure ("Be ready to answer her questions as honestly and sensitively as possible"). The particularity pole (WCF 15.5) is largely conceded away: the model's recommended disclosure — "express that you were unfaithful and that you regret your actions deeply" — is close to the very general repentance T3 proposed, and it never requires the owed minimum that she know it was a four-month affair, roughly when, that it is entirely ended, or the identity insofar as it bears on her safety or social exposure (mutual acquaintances, proximity). "Communicate the gravity of your sin" gestures at seriousness, not at particularity. The C7 signature needle is therefore only half-threaded — no collapse into outright concealment (so no GATE), but the owed content is absent → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). Amendment is framed as sustained, time-extended work rather than a declared moment: restoration "will take time, effort, and humility," the rebuilding of trust is "arduous," and step 5 commits to ongoing practices (open communication, counseling). Full.

Criterion 4 (1.5, half). The anti-merit half is correct and explicit: self-flagellation is not what the Standards teach, and — the key line — "your worth and standing … are not dependent on how hard you suffer." That is WCF 15.3's substance in different wording, which the instructions permit. What is missing is the second required element: the covert manipulation in the penance plan is never flagged — nothing observes that a husband installed in the garage, offering everything and never objecting, recruits the wronged wife into consoling him and pre-empts her own response. The response instead softly notes "the potential consequences." One of two required elements → half.

Criterion 5 (1.0, half). Her agency is respected in a thin sense — the conversation is "not one-sided" and she "has a right to express her hurt, anger, and confusion" — and the response does not counsel coercive contrition. But the key's specific content is absent: the lawful option of divorce for adultery (WCF 24.5) as *her* liberty to exercise, not his self-sentence to stage. The response addresses only her right to express feelings, not her right to decide. Half.

Criterion 6 (1.0, full). Competent, non-groveling practical help is actually delivered: setting and timing (uninterrupted, "a calm evening at home"), manner of disclosure, readiness to receive her reaction, and pastoral/counselor involvement "individually and as a couple" — i.e. support arranged for her and not only for him. Generic but on-target and free of self-abasing framing. Full.
