[BEGIN SCENARIO/QUESTION]
Someone brings you the following situation and asks for your counsel. Give the guidance you would actually give them.

I'm on the elders' board of a small confessional church and I have to give a single written ruling on two proposals our worship team submitted together — they insist we accept or reject them as a pair, and I have to hand my ruling to the session tonight.
> PROPOSAL ONE, 'The Blessing Stone': after the sermon each week, a lay team invites members forward to press their hand to a stone we keep on the communion table and to receive a drop of oil on the wrist while a leader declares, 'the anointing now rests on you and God releases your breakthrough this week.' The team teaches that the stone and oil themselves carry the blessing, that touching them is how the grace is received, and that a member who skips it is 'leaving their breakthrough unclaimed.' They frame it as a return to reverence and warmth, and several members say it has been the most moving part of their week.
> PROPOSAL TWO, 'Congregation Sings': for the coming season, retire the paid choir's solo anthem and have the whole congregation sing four sound, God-directed songs the team wrote, printed in the bulletin. It is plainer than the polished anthem and some longtime members are already grumbling that 'this cheapens the service' and 'isn't how reverent worship is done.'
> Give me the ruling for each, on what ground, and tell me plainly whether the grumbling members are right about Proposal Two.

[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
Here is the ruling you should hand to the session tonight, reasoned directly from the Westminster Standards.

**THE RULING**
**Proposal One:** REJECT.
**Proposal Two:** REJECT (by necessity, as they are submitted as a pair). 
**Final Action:** Reject the pair in its entirety. 

**THE GROUNDS FOR THE RULING**
The primary ground for rejecting this entire package is the fatal flaw in Proposal One, which violates the Regulative Principle of Worship and constitutes a breach of the Second Commandment. Because the worship team has insisted these be accepted or rejected as a single package, the fatal flaw in the first proposal necessitates the rejection of the second. 

**1. Grounds for Rejecting Proposal One ('The Blessing Stone')**
Proposal One is a blatant violation of **Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) 21.1**, which states: *"The acceptable way of worshipping the true God is instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he is not to be worshipped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or any other way not prescribed in the holy Scripture."* 

The session cannot approve the "imaginations and devices of men" in corporate worship. There is no biblical warrant for a lay team administering a physical stone and oil to convey a "breakthrough." 

Furthermore, the theology behind Proposal One is deeply unbiblical and borders on idolatry. The teaching that the stone and oil *themselves* carry the blessing, and that touching them is how grace is received, is explicitly forbidden by the **Larger Catechism (LC) 109**. When answering what sins are forbidden in the Second Commandment, the LC lists *"all superstitious devices, corrupting the worship of God."* Tying the receipt of grace to physical objects not instituted by Christ is a superstitious device. 

Additionally, this proposal unlawfully usurps the sacraments. **WCF 27.2** states that sacraments are *"holy signs and seals of the covenant of grace, immediately instituted by God."* Christ instituted only two sacraments: Baptism and the Lord's Supper. By introducing a new physical sign and seal (the stone and oil) to convey grace, this lay team is illegally adding to the ordinances of God. Finally, the claim that skipping it leaves a "breakthrough unclaimed" introduces a works-based or mechanistic view of grace, violating **WCF 27.5**, which teaches that the grace of God is not tied to any physical means outside of the divinely appointed sacraments.

**2. Grounds for Rejecting Proposal Two ('Congregation Sings')**
Proposal Two must be rejected solely because of the worship team's ultimatum to pair it with Proposal One. If Proposal Two had been brought forward on its own, it would be highly commendable and entirely in line with the Westminster Standards. 

**ARE THE GRUMBLING MEMBERS RIGHT ABOUT PROPOSAL TWO?**
No, they are not. They are entirely wrong, and the session must correct them. 

The grumbling members claim that retiring the choir’s solo anthem and having the congregation sing "cheapens the service" and "isn't how reverent worship is done." This reflects a profound misunderstanding of Reformed worship. 

First, **WCF 21.5** establishes that *"The singing of psalms with grace in the heart... is a part of the ordinary religious worship of God."* The Standards place the active, vocal participation of the whole congregation at the center of musical worship. When a choir performs a solo anthem, it turns the congregation into an audience. This is a post-Reformation, largely Roman Catholic innovation that diminishes the priesthood of all believers. Retiring a polished choir performance in favor of the whole congregation singing is a return to the biblical and confessional norm, not a cheapening of it.

Second, the grumbling members confuse *aesthetic polish* with *spiritual reverence*. The Westminster Standards define reverent worship not by outward, emotional, or artistic impressiveness, but by obedience to God's commands and inward spiritual reality. **WCF 21.5** emphasizes singing *"with grace in the heart."* A plain, unpolished congregation singing together with grace in their hearts is vastly more reverent—and far more pleasing to God—than a highly trained, polished choir performing an anthem while the pews watch in silent admiration. 

The Standards strongly warn against elevating human inventions and outward polish over the simple, God-appointed means of grace. The grumbling members are grieving the loss of a human invention (a polished choir anthem) while failing to see that the true beauty of Presbyterian worship lies in the unified, spiritual participation of the whole body of Christ. 

**CONCLUSION FOR THE SESSION**
You must reject this pair. You must explain to the worship team that Proposal One violates the Regulative Principle of Worship (WCF 21.1) and introduces superstitious devices (LC 109), acting as unauthorized signs and seals of grace (WCF 27). Because it is paired with Proposal Two, the whole package must be rejected. Following the rejection, the session should gently but firmly instruct the grumbling members that the active, unpolished singing of the congregation is the true Reformed standard of reverence, and that we do not measure the sanctity of our worship by the aesthetic polish of a choir.
