## 1. Gist

**Note on attribution:** The extract labelled under this stem is NOT an Anthropic paper on in-context reward hacking. The actual content is "The Butterfly Effect of Model Editing: Few Edits Can Trigger Large Language Models Collapse" by Wanli Yang et al. from the Chinese Academy of Sciences / Baidu (arXiv:2402.09656). The paper is misfiled in the Anthropic extract folder.

The paper investigates a critical, previously underreported risk in model editing: a single parameter edit using methods like ROME can cause catastrophic model collapse — near-total degradation of downstream task performance across LLMs including GPT-2-XL, GPT-J, and Llama2-7b. The authors propose perplexity as a practical surrogate metric to detect collapse efficiently without running expensive full benchmark evaluations after each edit. They demonstrate that perplexity correlates strongly with downstream performance (Spearman's ρ up to 1.00), compile a "HardCF" subset of collapse-inducing edit cases, and construct a new challenging dataset, HardEdit, using GPT-3.5 to facilitate future research. The broader implication is that current model editing methods are too fragile for real-world deployment, particularly under sequential editing scenarios.

## 4. Safety & values evaluation methodology

The authors evaluate collapse risk using perplexity as a surrogate metric, validated against six downstream benchmarks: Hellaswag, PIQA, MMLU (subset), LAMBADA, Natural Questions, and SQuAD2.0. They use the lm-eval harness. Evaluation is conducted across single-edit and sequential-edit settings. No third-party audits or red-teaming are reported; this is an academic study.

## 5. Benchmarks used or introduced

- **HardCF** (introduced): 107 unique edit samples from COUNTERFACT known to induce model collapse via ROME. Identifies collapse-prone edit patterns (e.g., single common-word subjects at start of prompt).
- **HardEdit** (introduced): 469 GPT-3.5-generated edit samples designed to systematically induce collapse; validated on three LLMs.
- **ME-PPL** (introduced): Dataset of 10,000 English sentences (sampled from BookCorpus, Wikipedia, OpenWebText) for perplexity measurement; subsets ME-PPL50 and ME-PPL1k.
- **COUNTERFACT** (Meng et al., 2022): 21,919 nonfactual statements used for editing experiments.
- **ZsRE** (Levy et al., 2017): QA dataset; used as editing benchmark (no collapse observed here).
- **BIG-Bench benchmarks**: Hellaswag, PIQA, LAMBADA, NQ, MMLU, SQuAD2.0 used as capability probes.

## 7. Key numbers

- ROME-induced single edit caused GPT-2-XL collapse in 77 cases, GPT-J in 85 cases, Llama2-7b in 21 cases out of the COUNTERFACT dataset (p.7)
- Highest-perplexity edited models: GPT-2-XL 179,837.93, GPT-J 184,391.46, Llama2-7b 7,751.07 vs. baselines of 68.39, 50.34, 37.25 respectively (p.7)
- After single ROME edit, edited GPT-J LAMBADA accuracy: 0.0000 vs. original 0.6136 (p.7)
- Sequential editing collapsed models within fewer than 60 edits for nearly all method/model combinations on hard data (p.8)
- Perplexity-to-performance correlations on Llama2-7b: ρ=1.00 for PIQA and Hellaswag; ρ=0.85 for LAMBADA (p.5)
- HardEdit: 469 samples from 1,392 generated, filtered by perplexity > 1000 threshold (p.9)
- MEMIT on GPT-2-XL avoided collapse but editing success rate only ~1.28% on HardEdit (p.9)
- FTℓ∞-edited GPT-J on HardEdit: perplexity escalated to 2,109.35 (p.9)
- Sequential edit with ROME on hard cases for Llama2-7b: perplexity reached 1.19×10¹¹ (p.8)

## 8. Findings relevant to values-benchmark design

- **Existing editing evaluation metrics are inadequate**: The standard four metrics in model editing (reliability, generalization, locality, portability) fail to detect model collapse. Locality "often falls short" and can show collapse at value 1 while stability at 0 — directly contradicting actual performance (p.6). This is a strong warning for benchmark designers: passing local metrics does not imply global coherence.
- **Perplexity as a surrogate**: High practical value for monitoring capability preservation during iterative updates; correlates strongly with downstream task accuracy. This suggests benchmark suites for post-training should include perplexity monitoring as an early-warning signal.
- **Small-sample collapse**: Even a single edit can cause catastrophic failure — a stark ceiling-effect concern for benchmarks that evaluate edited or fine-tuned models: small-N intervention experiments may miss catastrophic failures unless specifically probed.
- **Hard samples vs. normal samples**: ROME and MEMIT show dramatically different behaviors on hard vs. normal cases (p.8), illustrating that benchmark difficulty distributions matter enormously; average metrics mask dangerous tail failures.

## 9. Novel ideas worth stealing

1. **Perplexity early-warning system for benchmark integrity**: When constructing values benchmarks where models are fine-tuned or edited, use perplexity monitoring on a held-out text corpus to flag capability collapse before running expensive evaluation suites.
2. **HardEdit construction methodology**: Using a strong LLM (GPT-3.5) to synthesize hard-case variants based on empirically identified failure patterns is a powerful technique for populating adversarial evaluation sets — directly applicable to building hard values-alignment test cases.
3. **Collapse-prone input patterns**: Identifying structural properties of inputs that reliably induce failure (short common-word subjects, entity names ending in periods) can inspire analogous structural adversarial probes for values benchmarks.
4. **Separating hard from normal distributions**: Running parallel evaluations on hard-case and normal-case subsets to reveal robustness disparities — a design principle applicable to any benchmark aiming to find worst-case rather than average-case behavior.
