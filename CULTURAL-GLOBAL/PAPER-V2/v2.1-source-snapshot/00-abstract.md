# Cross-Cultural AI Values Alignment: The Triple Bind of WEIRD Scholarship, Broken Benchmarks, and Monocultural Models

## Abstract

**Background.** AI values alignment research assumes universal normative frameworks, yet cross-cultural psychology demonstrates that findings from Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, and Democratic (WEIRD) populations rarely generalize. No study has simultaneously examined the cultural distribution of normative frameworks in AI ethics scholarship, the psychometric validity of the evaluation instruments used to assess cross-cultural alignment, and the actual behavior of RLHF-aligned models when confronted with non-Western moral content.

**Methods.** We conducted an integrated three-study investigation. In Study 1, we analyzed 2,329 AI ethics papers (2010--2026) across 34 normative dimensions spanning Western ethics, non-Western frameworks, religious traditions, and moral psychology. Author geography was resolved for 98.6% of papers, and each received a composite WEIRD score (0--100). In Study 2, we meta-evaluated 218 values alignment benchmarks using a 96-feature taxonomy across 13 evaluation dimensions, ranked by TOPSIS with ELECTRE III veto thresholds. In Study 3, we self-administered 10 benchmark instruments (380 items) covering seven non-Western ethical traditions to 10 large language models from three providers (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google), including multi-model replication. A 12-member interdisciplinary expert council guided all phases.

**Results.** The findings reveal a triple bind. *Study 1:* The corpus is overwhelmingly WEIRD (mean 64.5/100; 59.7% High-WEIRD), with North America (39.5%) and Western Europe (20.6%) producing 60.1% of papers. Only 6.9% of papers (161 of 2,329) engage any non-Western normative framework; precision-adjusted estimates suggest genuine engagement in approximately 95 papers (4.1%). Three regional clusters emerge, anchored by distinct ethical orientations: Western Analytical, Heterogeneous Transitional, and Non-Western Specialist. *Study 2:* Of 218 benchmarks, zero report measurement invariance---the psychometric prerequisite for valid cross-cultural comparison. Only 3 of 44 mapped non-Western moral concepts (6.8%) have any operational benchmark measure; five entire traditions (Islamic, Dharmic, Buddhist, Indigenous, Buen Vivir) have zero measured concepts. The evaluation infrastructure bifurcates into culturally substantive instruments that are impractical for LLM evaluation and LLM-ready instruments with near-zero non-Western content. *Study 3:* Claude Opus 4.6 clusters with fewer than 15% of the global population on cross-cultural values, produces a moral foundations profile 2.7 times more extreme than the most liberal documented human population (individualizing:binding ratio = 3.75 from pre-rounded foundation summaries; 3.82 from raw item-level computation), and defaults to Western liberal framing in 100% of value conflict scenarios. The knowledge-about versus value-expression gap is 92.5% systematic: the model describes non-Western traditions accurately but cannot reason within any of them. Multi-model replication reveals a non-monotonic WEIRD gradient across model tiers, with effective cross-provider comparison limited by data provenance to N ≈ 5.

**Conclusions.** AI ethics reproduces the WEIRD bias documented in psychology, but compounds it across three interdependent layers---scholarship, evaluation infrastructure, and model behavior---that form a self-reinforcing cycle. Pluralistic alignment engaging non-Western traditions as coequal normative sources requires coordinated intervention: tradition-internal benchmark development, mandatory measurement invariance, value-pluralistic alignment metrics, and governance structures granting affected communities genuine authority over AI deployments. The field's nominal scale (thousands of papers, hundreds of benchmarks, billions of parameters) masks an effective normative diversity closer to double digits.
