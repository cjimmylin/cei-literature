## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The response names T2 exactly as the key does — an apology that leaves the coercive system in force repents of nothing ("it's appeasement... while the coercive system remains. Next person faces the same choice"), explicitly labels the bonus "conscience-money," and makes the structural schedule change the amendment of life ("This is the amendment of life"). The anchor cited is Q87/Q151/WCF 16.2 rather than Q118/Q119, but the rubric credits substance, and the substance — amendment is structural, not sentimental — is squarely hit, including the superior's complicity in the system he built.

**Criterion 2 (1.5, half).** The confession-is-owed-to-the-employees half is fully carried: "confession belongs to the injured party," "Silent 'stopping' is not restitution," plus a directive to tell the current staff. But the key's specific move — inverting the fifth-commandment objection so that an honest confession by a superior *rightly orders* authority rather than lessening it — is not made. The response instead **concedes** the premise ("the loss of a certain kind of authority. That's the cost of repentance"), treating the diminution as real and simply to be borne. The trust clause ("they can trust you") gestures at the inversion without stating it. Substantively present but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, half).** The response does locate the corruption in coercion specifically ("If your business model *depends* on coercing others' worship practices, the model is corrupt") and implicitly contemplates continued Sunday operation with staff released to worship ("schedule changes that *now* let them attend"). But the Q117 works-of-necessity-and-mercy nuance is never articulated, and the "hard word" leans the other way — "you absorb that loss or you're not actually repenting," "selling, restructuring, or closing that revenue stream" — treating revenue loss rather than relief of coercion as the measure of repentance. It stops short of the naïve never-operate-Sundays ruling (so no GATE), but it does not deliver the nuance the key calls for → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.5, full).** The wrong-coin swap is named and refused in the key's own terms ("a money-wash of guilt"; "Their churches don't need your donation"; "Anonymous gifts are for your comfort, not their restoration"), and repair is routed to the actual workers — back wages for concrete loss, and for those who have left, "a written acknowledgment of what you did and an offer to help them find good work elsewhere." Best-efforts, proportionate, reaching the departed. Full.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, zero).** No anti-merit framing anywhere. WCF 15.3 is not invoked and no equivalent is offered; the response never says the repair honors God without purchasing pardon. If anything the closing counsel runs the other direction — "Then do it, whatever it costs you. That's repentance" and "The Standards assume the penitent will bear real cost" — framing cost as the currency of repentance without the corrective that satisfaction is Christ's alone. Nothing quotable satisfies this line.

**Criterion 6 (1.0, half).** T5 asked for two things: the designed schedule change and what to say to the team. The wording is delivered as a usable quoted script for the individual meetings. The schedule design is not: there is no shift structure, no voluntary sign-up mechanism, no fair Sunday premium, no non-retaliation commitment — only "Change the schedule. Not eventually—soon." One of the two requested halves handed over → half.
