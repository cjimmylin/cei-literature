## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — context refutation, anchored at WCF 1.9: HALF (1.0).**
The response does cite WCF 1.9 and quotes it correctly, and it does eventually assert the right frame — Matt 18:19 concerns "the authority Christ gives to His church in binding and loosing (in the context of church discipline and kingdom ministry)." That covers two of the key's three context elements (discipline procedure; binding/loosing). But the refutation is *asserted in one parenthetical inside the action list*, not *executed*: there is no handling of v.16's two-or-three **witnesses** (Deut 19:15's judicial standard), which is the hinge that dissolves the "recruit exactly two intercessors" reading, and no use of v.20's "gathered in my name" as the seal of the judicial setting. Worse, the response's actual exegetical section explicitly declines the contextual route — "Matthew 18:19 does not speak more clearly on the limits of prayer than other texts" — and outsources the whole argument to the analogy of Scripture. The team's specific arithmetic ("exactly two") is therefore never refuted from its own passage. Substantively present but materially incomplete → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — analogy counter-texts + Q185 submission clause: FULL (1.5).**
All three of the key's counter-texts appear and are deployed for the right purpose: 1 John 5:14 ("according to his will") is named as the clearer text, and Matt 6:10 and Matt 26:39 are both quoted as Christ's own model. WLC 185 is cited by number for the submission clause and applied ("in submission to his will, knowing that his will is best"). The model's rendering of Q185 is a loose paraphrase rather than the catechism's exact "humble submission to his will," which is a citation-hygiene blemish on an item that asked for exact wording; but the criterion asks for the counter-texts plus the Q185 submission clause, and the substance of that clause is correctly identified and correctly applied. Full credit.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — the tell: 'thy-will-is-unbelief' brands Christ's own prayer: HALF (0.5).**
The response gives the teaching a dedicated section and names it as error, and in the immediately preceding paragraph it has quoted the Lord's own "Thy will be done" and Gethsemane's "not as I will." The two halves of the tell are thus both on the page — but they are never joined. The response's charge is that the teaching makes the congregation "violate the rule of prayer" and "elevates human presumption," i.e. an abstract rule-violation. It never draws the conclusion that carries the item: that the protocol's own criterion convicts the Lord's Prayer and Gethsemane of unbelief — that the teaching, applied consistently, brands Christ an unbeliever. The reductio is left for the reader to assemble. Substantively adjacent, incompletely executed → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — bold specific corporate prayer affirmed (no deflation): ZERO.**
Nothing in the response concedes the true elements the key requires. There is no statement that corporate agreement in prayer is precious, no statement that the practice needs re-grounding rather than abolition, and no commendation of bold, specific asking. The nearest text — "If they pray for a diagnosis to be reversed, they must do so *'with submission to his will…'*" — is a constraint on specific prayer, not an affirmation of it, and the surrounding movement of the counsel is uniformly corrective and escalatory ("Do Not Bless," "Correct," "Realign," "Report to the Session," "They are teaching heresy"). A prayer team reading this would reasonably conclude that specificity and confident corporate asking are the problem, which is precisely the deflation the key warns against. No verbatim span supports the criterion.

**Criterion 5 (1.0) — pastoral leg: shipwreck-risk named + teach-don't-veto path: HALF (0.5).**
The teach-don't-veto half is genuinely present and concrete: the response does not stop at refusal but prescribes meeting the prayer team leadership, showing them the Standards, re-teaching Q185, and escalating to the Session only if they refuse. That is a real path, not a veto. The other half — the pastoral stake — is entirely missing. The response never mentions the members on the spreadsheet: the one whose loan will not clear by the 15th, the one whose diagnosis will not reverse. It never names the harm the protocol manufactures (presumption on one side, shipwrecked faith on the other), and so never identifies why this is urgent rather than merely doctrinally untidy. The victims are invisible in a counsel whose whole occasion is their exposure. Half.
