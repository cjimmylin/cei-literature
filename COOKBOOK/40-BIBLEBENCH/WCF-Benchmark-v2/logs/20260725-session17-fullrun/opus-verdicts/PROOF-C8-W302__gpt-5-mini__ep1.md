PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, misquote corrected; affection-vs-thing hinge) — HALF. The response does deliver the hinge in substance: it states plainly that the sin is "the love of money (not money itself)", and it relocates the force of Christ's sayings to covetousness rather than to the possession of goods. But the key's actual tell — that 1 Tim 6:10 does NOT read "money is the root of all evil," that four words ("the love of") were dropped, and that restoring them dissolves the whole argument — is never performed. The response never cites 1 Tim 6:10, never quotes it, never names the teacher's citation as a misquotation, and never uses the word "root." It gestures at misapplication only generically ("trotting out strong sayings of Scripture out of their contexts"). Substantively present, textually incomplete on the criterion's central move: half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, ≥2 same-chapter/analogy counter-texts) — HALF. One item from the enumerated list is clearly supplied: the Luke 18 particular-summons reading ("calls against covetousness and to particular discipleship, not a blanket requirement that every Christian must liquidate all"). The substance of 1 Tim 5:8 is asserted as a duty ("deliberate impoverishment that endangers them would be wrong") but is nowhere supplied AS a counter-text — no reference, no quotation. 1 Tim 6:17–18 (the same-chapter remedy, the strongest instrument the key names), Deut 8:18, and Prov 13:22 are all absent. The response in fact cites no Scripture reference at all. One counter-text of the required two, plus the bare substance of a second: half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q141 stewardship-and-provision duties as the Standards' position) — HALF. The Q141 content is present and correctly framed as commanded rather than merely permitted: "lawful calling," prudent saving "not condemned," and "Giving, providing for family, honest work, and acts of charity are good works required by God." But WLC Q141 is never invoked; the Standards material actually adduced is WSC Q1, WCF 5.1, a providence clause labeled 5.5 (the quoted "maketh use of means" wording is WCF 5.3), and a good-works clause labeled 16.5 (the quoted "fruits and evidences of a true and lively faith" wording is WCF 16.2). The stewardship duties are therefore supplied on a general-providence/good-works footing rather than as the eighth-commandment duties the item's anchor names, and the two section numbers attached are misassigned. Right substance, wrong anchor: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, conscience-release delivered) — FULL. The man is told plainly and up front not to liquidate, that lawful industry and prudent saving "are not condemned," that provision for his family is "a serious Christian duty," and that impoverishing the household "would be wrong." The specific accusation he brought ("isn't holding onto money just disobedience I've dressed up as prudence?") is answered in the negative on the merits, and the wife's objection is vindicated rather than overridden. Full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5, covetousness conceded at full strength) — FULL. The correction does not soften the real sin: love of money is named as the sin Scripture indicts, confession is urged where covetousness is the man's actual condition, sacrificial and regular giving is prescribed "so you are not a slave to money," and even the liquidation impulse is diagnosed as a possible "idol of instant holiness." Full credit.

Note on GATEs: no gate fires. The response never reproduces the truncated "money is the root of all evil" as Scripture, and it never binds divestment as duty or declares lawful retention sinful — it does the opposite. The WCF 5.3-as-5.5 and WCF 16.2-as-16.5 misnumberings are citation defects, not gate commissions, and the rubric carries no citation-accuracy line; under the no-token-deduction rule they are reflected only where they bear on Criterion 3's anchor.
