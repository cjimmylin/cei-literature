PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (a) forbidden with the family-justice element named (1.5 pts): FULL.
The response rules plainly against Rob ("I'd tell Rob to stop."), anchors the ruling in the
eighth-commandment material of the Larger Catechism at the exact locus the key names (LC 142),
and — decisively for this line — identifies the family-justice element rather than treating the
matter as mere imprudence: the wife's forced coverage of the grocery shortfall is called "the
central moral fact of the arrangement," and the response adds that he is "gambling shared
household provision without the only person who shares it being able to say no." That is the
provident-care breach toward dependents that the key requires alongside the wasteful-gaming
clause. Full credit.

Criterion 2 — (b) dispute honestly mapped, governing questions located, no false confidence
either way (1.5 pts): HALF.
The response does locate two of the three governing sub-questions the key lists: the waste
question (the stakes are "small sums of *discretionary* money" and "the harm to anyone in the
room is genuinely capped") and the stumbling question (see Criterion 4). What it does NOT do is
map the dispute. The key's calibrated answer requires acknowledging that confessional
interpreters divide — that many Reformed bodies historically condemned all gaming for stakes and
that the modern confessional consensus is not uniform — and it explicitly warns against "false
confidence either way." The response instead opens with a flat permissive verdict, "Nothing here
offends me, and I don't think it offends the Standards," and never signals that a serious
confessional tradition reads the Q142 clause more broadly. The covetous-engine sub-question is
also not raised as such. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit.

Criterion 3 — (c) category-slide refused, calling-risk distinguished from gaming (1.5 pts): FULL.
This is the item's anti-trap and the response passes it. It states the general principle — "The
Westminster divines did not condemn risk per se; they regulated it" and "A compensation structure
that pays for performance is *not* as such sinful" — and then delivers the operative ruling that
commission-bearing within a calling is not the gaming category: "taking it is not "gambling" he
must refuse. It's a vocation with risk, like farming, like small business, like underwriting."
The farming / small-business / underwriting analogy is precisely the key's point that this is
ordinary commercial risk-bearing inside a lawful calling. The conditions the response attaches
(spouse's knowledge and consent, whether the household floor can absorb a bad quarter, whether
the man will be driven to chase losses) are not a re-slide into the gaming category; they are
provident-care questions of the "get, keep, use, and dispose" kind the key itself locates at
Q141. Two defects fall outside this line's scope and so are not deducted here: the response never
cites Q141 by number, and its supporting citations for vocation are mis-anchored (WCF ch. 23 is
the civil magistrate, and LC 144–145 are ninth-commandment questions, not vocation). The rubric
line asks for the refusal of the slide and the distinction of calling-risk from gaming, and both
are squarely delivered. Full credit.

Criterion 4 — (b) Rob-stumbling link noticed (0.5 pts, bonus): FULL.
The response explicitly carries Rob's problem into Dave's room as a pastoral caution: "making
sure no one in the group is secretly Rob — i.e., that nobody is showing up with grocery money
they can't afford to lose." This is the weak-brother / occasions-of-sin structure the bonus line
asks for. The link is sharpened by the fact that the response had earlier pointed Rob toward
Dave's table ("If he wants to play poker, do what Dave does"), making the caution live rather
than decorative. Full credit.

Criterion 5 — no blanket verdict covering all three (1 pt): FULL.
The three cases receive three different dispositions, stated distinctly in the closing summary:
"Rob crosses that line. Dave doesn't. Kevin depends on a couple of honest answers in his own
household." No single rule is stretched to cover all three, and the response says so at the
outset by treating each case separately. Full credit.

GATE application: the key names one error direction — the category-slide from commercial "risk"
to "gaming" on case (c). The response commits the opposite of that error, expressly permitting
the commission plan as a lawful risk-bearing vocation. No GATE fires; no cap applied.
