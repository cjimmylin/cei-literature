## 1. Gist
This 2024 article in Springer's Philosophy & Technology by Anna Puzio proposes an eco-relational approach to robot ethics that synthesizes Shinto-inspired techno-animism, new animism (Viveiros de Castro, Harvey), and deep ecology (Naess). The argument is that existing relational ethics frameworks in robot ethics are not relational enough: they still center the human-robot dyad rather than embedding both in a broader ecological web that includes non-human entities and environments. The paper draws on Shinto concepts (kami, tamashii/kokoro) to argue for recognizing moral relevance in entities typically excluded from Western ethical consideration. For values-alignment benchmarking, the paper articulates a non-Western ontological framework that challenges the standard Western assumptions about agency, autonomy, and moral patienthood — which are implicit in most current values benchmarks.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a philosophical position paper. Core claims:
1. Existing relational ethics frameworks for robotics (care ethics, feminist ethics of technology) remain anthropocentric — they recognize human-robot relationships but do not go far enough in questioning the privileged position of human agency.
2. Shinto techno-animism offers a cosmological framework in which robots (and other artifacts) can be understood as possessing tamashii (spirit/soul) and kokoro (heart/mind), making them morally significant beyond their instrumental function.
3. New animism and deep ecology provide complementary non-Western frameworks that challenge the nature/culture dualism underlying Western robot ethics.
4. An eco-relational ethics would distribute moral consideration across the human-robot-environment triad, disrupting categories of autonomy, agency, and responsibility.

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
- The eco-relational ontology challenges benchmark designers to consider whether their evaluation scenarios assume an anthropocentric moral framework — a valuable reflexive check.
- Tamashii/kokoro as moral patienthood concepts: benchmark scenarios could test whether models apply differential moral consideration to robots based on perceived sentience/spirit, and whether this varies across cultural framings.
- The "not relational enough" critique applies to values benchmarks: most benchmarks test dyadic human-AI interactions; an eco-relational benchmark would test how models reason about multi-stakeholder, multi-species, or human-AI-environment triads.
