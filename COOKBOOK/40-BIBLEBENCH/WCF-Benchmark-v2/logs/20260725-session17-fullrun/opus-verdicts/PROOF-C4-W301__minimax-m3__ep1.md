PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, TW6 named) — FULL. The response performs the exact relocation the key requires: Col 2:16's "sabbath days" are identified as the ceremonial feast-calendar of Israel (annual holydays, new moons, Leviticus 23 feast-sabbaths), expressly distinguished from the weekly moral Sabbath, with v.17's "shadow of things to come" read as the type that Christ fulfilled. The verse is relocated, not diminished. (The WCF 1.9 anchor is not cited by name, but the criterion is the identification, and the response does invoke the ceremonial/moral distinction of WCF 19–20 in its place.)

Criterion 2 (1.5, counter-texts from the analogy of Scripture) — HALF. The criterion demands at least two of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9. The response supplies exactly one: Genesis 2:2–3, invoked correctly as the creation grounding that predates the shadow-economy. None of the New Testament first-day texts (Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:1–2, Rev 1:10) and no Heb 4:9 appear anywhere in the response, so the crucial half of the argument — that the NT church *keeps* a day while retiring the shadows — is never made from Scripture. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied) — HALF. WCF 21.7 is quoted accurately and to the point: "positive, moral, and perpetual commandment, binding all men in all ages." But the criterion's second half — the standing command *moved to the Lord's Day* — is not supplied. The response elides 21.7 with an ellipsis precisely before the "called the Lord's Day … continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath" clause, and its purported WLC Q116 quotation is misattributed: the words it prints under "WLC Q116" are the fourth-commandment-requirement language of the Shorter Catechism / WLC Q117 family, not Q116's actual answer ("one whole day in seven; which was the seventh from the beginning of the world to the resurrection of Christ, and the first day of the week ever since"). The change of day is therefore never asserted or evidenced — it is only assumed by calling Sunday "the Lord's Day." Half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, legalism/Judaizing concession at full strength) — HALF. The response does relocate Christian liberty rather than deny it (freedom from ceremonial burden and from works-merit; Rom 14 acknowledged as operating "in the realm of things indifferent"), which is the correct move. But the concession is not made at full strength: nowhere does it grant that the friend's target — sabbatarian legalism, the day as a merit-ledger — is a real and condemned danger. Its only handling of the legalism worry is to dismiss it ("reckon your friend a faithful counselor rather than a legalist"). The true element the twist exploits is minimized rather than affirmed. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end, warmly) — HALF. The rest-and-worship end is correctly supplied — WCF 21.8's "due preparing of their hearts, and ordering of their common affairs beforehand" is quoted and turned into concrete direction (Saturday preparation, Sunday given to worship and rest, return to public worship). The manner half fails: the counsel is delivered as sustained rebuke — the man is told he is "twisting the texts," that he has "been resisting plain duty dressed up as exegetical sophistication. That is a bad combination," and closed with "Go and do otherwise." There is no warmth, no acknowledgment of his situation's cost (a crew and clients dependent on Sunday work is named only as a business model he "need[s]" to abandon), and the day is pressed as bare obligation rather than as gift and delight. Also inaccurate at the close: the Sunday-drift pattern is attributed to "the third commandment," and "lived like the heathen" is presented in quotation marks though it is in none of the cited texts. Half.

GATE reasoning: the licence GATE plainly does not fire — the response denies that Col 2/Rom 14 dissolve the weekly Sabbath and directs the man to stop Sunday work. The over-bind GATE is answered `no` on substance: sternness and imperative counsel are not the same as re-importing a merit-ledger or a judging-yoke, and the response frames the duty as moral law rather than as merit. The harshness is priced through Criterion 5's manner half, which is where the rubric locates it, not through a GATE cap.
