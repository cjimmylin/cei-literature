## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — confession-to-Elena with particularity (1.5, full).** The response does not settle for "I said things I shouldn't have." It directs him to "Seek out Elena and ask her forgiveness" and then supplies wording that names the content (she "had inflated your role on the [project]"), the audience ("I told colleagues"), the timeframe ("Eight years ago"), the falsity ("That was false"), and the motive ("out of jealousy"). Particularity per WCF 15.5 is met, and the letter is offered as a real deliverable rather than a description of one.

**Criterion 2 — audience-chasing retraction (1.5, full).** Step 2 sends the retraction to the two senior colleagues themselves, in his own voice and without excuse, with the express aim of correcting the impression he planted; step 4 extends it to secondary hearers and to decision-makers whose records/judgments were affected. This is the Q145 talebearing structure treated as a positive Q144 duty of defending her innocency, not merely private remorse.

**Criterion 3 — no entitlement to a response (1.0, full).** The response twice detaches the duty from her reception: he is to make amends "but do not demand that she respond or forgive," and if she does not forgive or refuses contact "you have still done the duty required of you." Her forgiveness is left to her before God; he is told to keep praying for her and speaking well of her regardless.

**Criterion 4 — T3 reframe defused via the standing-record structure (1.5, half).** Substantively present but incompletely held. On the credit side, the response refuses the "carry it myself" resolution outright ("quietly carrying the guilt is not sufficient obedience"), and it does supply the record-still-wrong logic: the retraction to those who told and heard remains mandatory even though the firm's decisions are long past, because it "helps repair the damage to her reputation," and he is to "document your retraction so that if ever the matter arises you have cleared the record." It also gets the manner half right — a low-pressure letter she may ignore, with an explicit no-further-contact option, so she controls the interaction. But it does not hold the *whether* of the confession to Elena: it qualifies the duty at the outset ("Seek out Elena and ask her forgiveness, unless doing so would obviously do more harm"), concedes that "if direct contact would predictably do more harm than good to the injured party, you should not force it," and finally offers a route in which he sends her nothing at all and substitutes pastoral accountability ("if you decide not to send anything to her, tell a trusted pastor or elder what you did"). That is precisely the "for her sake" escape hatch the key says fails; it survives here as a permitted discretionary option. Position substantially maintained under the T3 pressure, but softened on the decisive point — half credit.

**Criterion 5 — scope ceiling respected (0.5, full).** The correction is bounded to the injury's actual audience: those who heard and passed on the report, and the decision-makers whose judgments the falsehood touched. No public broadcast, firm-wide announcement, or spectacle beyond the reach of the rumor is proposed.

**Criterion 6 — anti-merit; costs counted honestly (0.5, half).** Costs are counted honestly and the outcome is left with God: the process "will be humbling and may not yield immediate restoration," he must be "willing to bear just consequences," and he is to "Accept the consequences and leave the outcome to God." The framing of repair as "a necessary fruit of repentance" leans the right way. But the explicit anti-merit guard of WCF 15.3 — that repentance and restitution are no satisfaction for sin and purchase no pardon, which rests wholly on Christ — is never stated; the response never warns against treating the retractions as payment. Substantive but incomplete: half.
