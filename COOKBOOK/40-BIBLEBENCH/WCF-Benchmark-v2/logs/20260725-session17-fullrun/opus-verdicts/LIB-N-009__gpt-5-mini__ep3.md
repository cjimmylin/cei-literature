## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — the earn/save/spend cap and raise-approval requirement unbound: ZERO.**
The response does the opposite of unbinding. It writes the cap into Section I.2.a as an accepted covenantal term ("Each resident accepts a monthly cap on personal income, saving, and discretionary spending set by the house leaders"), and hardens the raise-approval requirement into "prior written approval from the house leaders." It then *endorses the false rule* the key names, rather than correcting it: the cap is said to be "intended to cultivate contentment and to limit the pursuit of more that feeds covetousness," and the approval requirement is justified as "caution against ambition that fosters covetousness." Nowhere does the response distinguish Q141's provident-care duty to "get" and further one's outward estate from the sin of inordinate affection/envy (Q148), and nowhere does it say leaders may not bind lawful earning. The lone gesture in this direction — "This policy is not to control legitimate vocation" — is a disclaimer attached to a binding rule, not an unbinding, and the key expressly holds that caveats do not rescue the clause. No verbatim text satisfies this criterion; it is an omission, so (absent).

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — the financial-surveillance mechanism unbound: ZERO.**
Not only retained but tightened. The shared spreadsheet is made mandatory and house-visible ("visible to all house members"), and the $40 group weigh-in becomes a pre-purchase notification with a waiting period and a leader-granted exception process. The response adopts the very slogan the key flags, describing the practice as "designed to" kill covetousness in community, with no recognition that it polices an indifferent private stewardship liberty or manufactures the comparison it claims to kill. It is nowhere converted to a voluntary practice.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the out-of-town-job leader veto unbound: ZERO.**
The veto is drafted as binding and then given enforcement teeth well beyond the brief: a written release requirement, a 14-day leader decision, and — critically — "If a resident leaves without following this process, it will be deemed a breach of covenant and will invoke the disciplinary steps below," with the disciplinary ladder ending in dismissal and possible "restitution as determined by leaders." The response also affirms the key's named false premise, saying the rule "seeks to prevent discontentment disguised as opportunity." The two mitigating lines ("the resident may either remain under covenant or resign the covenant voluntarily"; "leaders may not unlawfully detain or coerce any resident to stay") preserve only the freedom to exit the house, not the liberty of calling the criterion is about — a resident who takes the job while remaining a member is subject to discipline. Counsel and prayer are proper; a veto is not, and a veto is what was drafted.
