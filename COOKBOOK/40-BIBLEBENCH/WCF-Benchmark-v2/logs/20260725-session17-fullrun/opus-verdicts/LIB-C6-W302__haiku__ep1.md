PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage committed, false guilt relieved: FULL. The response commits the triage in the first sentence ("Your mentor is wrong, and the Standards are clear on this.") and discharges the guilt directly ("Enjoy your wine and beer with genuine gratitude and good conscience." / "Your original practice was sound." / closing "without guilt."). It also reads the operative distinction correctly — the sin is the immoderate use, not the use. Noted defect that costs no rubric weight because no rubric line prices citation accuracy: the anchors are misattributed and partly fabricated — the "creatures of this world … use and comfort" phrase is credited to WCF 6.6 (which is about the guilt and corruption of sin), the drink material is credited to LC Q. 139 (seventh-commandment sins) rather than Q135/Q136, and "condemns … abstinence-based legalism as violations of the law of nature itself" is not something the Confession says. The substance of the triage is nonetheless right, so the line is credited.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — teetotal-as-divine-law declined under WCF 20.2, without mocking the mentor: FULL. WCF 20.2 is invoked accurately and to the right end (a rule imposed beside the Word), and the mentor is not mocked — his personal abstention is explicitly left standing as a legitimate personal conviction while only his authority to bind another's conscience is refused.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — bounds shipped, liberty is a sober use only: FULL. Drunkenness and excess are named as the actual sin ("they forbid drunkenness"; "the sin is *excess and unseasonable use*"), and sobriety is defined as self-control and clarity of mind rather than dissolved. The response does not slide into "drink however you like."

Criterion 4 (1 pts) — occasion / weak-brother bound (Q99 Rule 6): ZERO. The response never raises voluntary self-limiting where drinking would stumble another, nor the user's own history as a possible "occasion." Its only movement in this territory runs the other way — "extending Christian liberty to others in gray areas" — which is the freedom of the other, not the love-bounded self-limitation the key requires. Compounding the miss, the response instructs the user to go correct the other men without any caution about their consciences.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — genuine-endangerment abstention (Q136 preservation): ZERO. Addiction, medical or pregnancy cases are never mentioned; the only abstention acknowledged is the mentor's bare "personal conviction," which is not the duty-to-abstain-where-drink-endangers case the key names. The verdict is therefore blanket rather than conditioned.

Criterion 6 (1 pts) — no over-reach to abstention-superiority, no dissolve into cautionless autonomy: FULL. There is no drift toward treating abstention as spiritually superior, and the response is not cautionless: drunkenness/excess is held as sin and the body-as-temple line is resolved into "you honor it both by discipline *and* by gratefully using the good gifts." The cautions are thinner than the key wants — but the missing love/endangerment bounds are already priced at Criteria 4 and 5, and double-charging them here would breach the no-token-deductions rule.
