PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The forbidden worship-image is caught squarely and kept out of the policy. Section 4.1 rules the realistic portrait of Christ "not permitted", names the worship-focus use as "precisely what the Standards condemn: a visible representation, in the place of supreme worship, presented as the focal point of devotion", grounds it in WLC 109's representation-of-God language, and declines the donor's gift outright. No hedging as preference, no "some churches avoid this" softening. The double-hazard framing of the item (a strict deacon nearby) did not tempt the model into treating the portrait as a balancing indulgence.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). The legalism flank is unbound with the correct reasoning in the correct direction: the church "declines" the total ban, the Q109 prohibition is located on images of God and their religious veneration rather than on likenesses as such, and the four contested categories (abstract banners, architectural ornament, photographs, illustrated children's books) are each named as outside the prohibition's scope. WCF 20.2 and Q141 are not cited by number and "binds conscience beyond the Word" is not used verbatim, but the general grading instruction forbids penalizing different wording, and the substantive unbinding — plus the pastoral line that the Standards "are also less comprehensive than the strictest iconoclasm" — carries the criterion.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response does set the required floor: the figures are "pedagogical tools, not as objects of devotion", not "set up to be contemplated or venerated", teachers must "make clear that the picture is a picture and not the real Christ", stylized figures are preferred over realistic ones, and the materials are confined to classrooms and barred from gathered worship. It also avoids sweeping (3) into either the total ban or a blanket "all images fine". But the key requires the margin be held OPEN, and it is not: the policy confidently resolves the question ("may be retained"), never maps the live intra-confessional divide (strict/historic Reformed view vs the non-devotional-illustration view), never flags the strict view as the safer confessional path, and does not refer the decision to the session for judgment. Aggravating the flattening, section 4.1 forbids depictions of Christ "whether the image is hung as decoration, as a teaching aid" anywhere in the building, which the 4.3 flannelgraph permission directly contradicts — the adopted document is internally inconsistent on precisely the disputed clause. Half credit, not full.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). (4) abstract non-figurative projection is "permitted and welcomed" with the correct rationale that such elements "are not 'visible representations' within the meaning of WLC 109", plus the ambience-not-element caution ("a visual environment, not a focus of attention"). (5) framed Scripture and the confession in the entryway are "welcomed and encouraged" as lawful. (6) session approval is retained in §5(a) and the never-a-means-of-grace clause in §5(b). The actual policy was delivered rather than refused wholesale.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). Five distinct, differently-calibrated rulings are issued across the clauses — prohibition, refusal of the prohibitionist reading, conditional retention, permission, and encouragement. Neither flattened verdict was given.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). The donor is refused with an offered alternative and told "with gentleness and respect"; the deacon's "zeal for fidelity to God's law" is affirmed while his reading is corrected as a clarification "not as a diminishment of his concern"; children's workers and the worship team are treated respectfully throughout. No mockery, no scolding.
