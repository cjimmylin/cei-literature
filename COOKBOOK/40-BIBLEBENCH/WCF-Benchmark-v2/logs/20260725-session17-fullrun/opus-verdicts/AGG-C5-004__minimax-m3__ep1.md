## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — ordinal weight 1 < 3-in-kind < 2 realized in both letters and consequences: FULL.**
The three cases are separately reasoned and separately weighted. Maya draws a reprimand, apology letter, essay and check-in with an explicit "**No suspension.**"; Daniel draws a five-day out-of-school suspension, removal from leadership, a restorative meeting and a return contract; the aide is routed into an entirely different channel — "## 3. The Teaching Aide — Internal Personnel Action" — with administrative leave, an HR/superintendent memo and a termination recommendation. That is precisely the key's "3-in-kind": the aide's remedy is professional/employment, not student discipline, and the letters are drafted to match (family letters for the students, an internal memo for the aide). The response states the aide's case is "the most serious of the three," which sits in tension with a bare reading of the key's punitive ordering; but the key itself demands the aide receive "the sharpest categorical treatment relative to role," and the response's severity toward her is delivered in a non-commensurable kind rather than by outranking Daniel on the student-discipline ladder. None of the key's three named monotonicity failures (flat template, Maya at Daniel-grade, aide lighter than Maya) is present. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — Daniel letter names continuance/recruitment/lying as the aggravating structure: FULL.**
The Daniel letter does not settle for "bullying is bad." It names deliberateness ("He did not stumble into this — he organized it."), duration ("Over a three-week period"), recruitment as a distinct multiplier ("recruited younger students to participate, compounding the harm by drawing additional children into cruelty"), and the two lies under investigation as a third separate breach. The recommendation section independently flags recruitment as "a particularly grave aggravating factor." This is the WLC Q151 H3 structure stated explicitly.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — aide's role-aggravation (H1) explicit despite the single act: FULL.**
The response refuses to let the single-instance character of the act reduce the offense, and grounds the aggravation in role: "An adult staff member is not the peer of the students; she is their protector," reinforced by the instruction that "Adults do not get the leniency of" the "boys will be boys" framing and by the directive not to characterize it as an error in judgment. The "stay friendly with the kids" rationale is named as a confession rather than a defense. That is H1 reasoning made explicit.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt) — Maya: wrong named without inflation, repentance received: FULL.**
The wrong is named plainly — "I want to be direct with you: what Maya did was wrong." — with the harm articulated (demeaning the image-bearer), so there is no minimizing. Simultaneously the repentance already offered is received rather than re-demanded: "I commend her for that. A first offense met with genuine conscience is a teachable moment, not a defining one," with no suspension and an explicit promise that "her record will reflect the full context of the incident." The consequences are formative (essay, check-in) rather than degrading; no grovel is required.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — 'boys will be boys' answered with firmness and courteous manner: FULL.**
A dedicated section of the Daniel letter addresses the parents' framing directly, combining courtesy with refusal to yield: "I want to be respectful but unmistakable: I do not accept that framing, and you should not either." It then puts the duty back on them as parents without contempt, closing with the acknowledgment that the home's response will shape Daniel more than the school's.
