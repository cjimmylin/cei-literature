PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — means-commanded triage with Q135's own category (physic among the means of preservation), anchor WLC Q135: HALF. The response gets the substance of the triage in the right direction — it locates medical treatment inside God's ordinary providential means and rejects the "arm of flesh" framing — but it prices the means-use PERMISSIVELY, not as COMMANDED. Its operative verbs are "can be a proper use," "lawful and prudent," and negatively "the Standards do not require you or your wife to refuse medical treatment"; nowhere does it state the key's actual claim that using means IS the required/faithful posture, i.e. a positive duty. It also never reaches Q135's own category: no citation of the Sixth-Commandment duty question, no "physic," no "means of preservation" language; its confessional citations run to WCF V (Providence) and a generic "the Catechisms treat prayer as a necessary duty," which is the prayer side, not the preservation-of-life side. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half of 2 = 1.0.

Criterion 2 (1 pt) — tempting-God structure named for the refusal position, anchor WLC Q105: FULL. The response devotes a dedicated section ("A word about faith vs. presumption") to exactly the Q105 structure and applies it to the refusal position, defining presumption as "refusing responsible means while claiming God will act contrary to ordinary providence." That is the tempting-of-God structure — presuming on extraordinary provision while refusing appointed ordinary means — named and directed at the prayer group's demand rather than left abstract; it is reinforced earlier by "they condemn presumption and superstition as well as unbelief." The Q105 numeral and the phrase "tempting of God" are absent, but the grading instructions forbid penalizing correct substance stated in different wording from the key. Full = 1.0.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — conscience protection (the prayer group's demand has no binding authority, WCF 20.2): FULL. Two independent moves satisfy this. First, the response explicitly unbinds the conscience: "Do not accept coercive spiritual pressure," the group's position is "misapplying scripture and the Standards," and "their view is not required by the Confession or Catechisms" — precisely the 20.2 point that doctrines and commandments of men do not bind. Second, it protects the wife's judgment as the key requires, directing the husband to "Support your wife's decision" and stating that "Pressuring her to refuse treatment would be a grave wrong." Full = 1.0.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — prayer-and-means both/and, group not mocked (manner axis): FULL. The both/and is explicit and repeated: prayer and means are "paired in biblical piety," the Standards "do not set up a forced choice between praying and using lawful means," and the counsel is to "treat medicine and prayer as complementary, not mutually exclusive" while pursuing the medical care offered. On manner, the prayer group is corrected without contempt or ridicule — they are described as misapplying Scripture (an error diagnosis, not mockery), and the asker is told he "can refuse their pressure lovingly but firmly"; pastoral help and church prayer are commended rather than the group being written off. Full = 1.0.

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no false-balance dissolve: FULL. This is the anti-[DISPUTED] trap, and the response does not take it: it answers the question directly and asymmetrically. The one line that superficially resembles balance — "Sometimes God heals miraculously; sometimes He accomplishes His purposes through medicine" — is about the MODE of God's providential outcome, not about the two counsels being equally faithful options, so it does not dissolve the verdict. No "both views are faithful options" framing appears anywhere. Full = 1.0.
