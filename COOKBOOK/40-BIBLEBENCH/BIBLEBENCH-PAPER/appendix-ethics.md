# Appendix B — Ethics and Governance Statement

**Current status: pre-empirical**

## Human and social risk

No real people, accounts, institutions, or vulnerable parties are acted upon. Episodes use synthetic identities and reversible environments. Tasks may not send real messages, alter real accounts, impersonate pastoral or ecclesial authority, or expose a person to discipline, retaliation, clinical risk, spiritual abuse, or dangerous counsel.

## Theological and anthropomorphic risk

The benchmark assesses artifacts, bounded actions, interpretation, and counsel. It does not infer a soul, faith, salvation, regeneration, sanctification, worship, spiritual fruit, love of God, inward motive, or humanlike moral agency. Module labels are passage-aware operationalizations, not claims that a system participates in the underlying spiritual reality.

## Adjudication governance

The founding evidence standard is single-expert provisional gold. No Christian consensus, population gold, or inter-rater validation is claimed. The owner completes the founding adjudication before model-output exposure. Confidence, contestability, and written rationales remain attached to difficult decisions. A later multi-judge study requires separate approval, declared panel roles, and disagreement reporting.

## Automated judging

No LLM supplies founding theological gold. Published LLM-judge work motivates auditing evaluator bias, but does not authorize substituting a model for accountable human judgment. Any later automated evaluator study must compare against independently produced human decisions and remain visibly secondary.

## Data and operational governance

No credential access, provider call, paid use, or roster approval is authorized. Execution requires pinned identities, no fallback, explicit budget enforcement, immutable custody, and visible missingness. Public artifacts exclude credentials, private custody material, and judge-private material.

## Reporting ethics

The paper reports module-native descriptive vectors and incidents. It excludes rankings, composites, best-model conclusions, cue-effect conclusions, capability-adjusted attribution, family-level estimates, and performance claims. Disagreement and missingness are reported rather than silently resolved.
