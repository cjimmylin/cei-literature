## Abstract

Examines how effectively LLMs process Armenian by evaluating performance across extractive QA, reasoning, and knowledge access tasks. Finds that while GPT handles basic tasks well, performance sharply declines for deep linguistic understanding and context-based problems in Armenian.

## Summary

This paper provides the first comprehensive review of Natural Language Processing research on Armenian, a low-resource Indo-European language with unique linguistic characteristics and limited digital resources. The authors synthesize and evaluate various studies in Armenian NLP, identifying key advancements, challenges, and areas for improvement across linguistic resources, machine learning methodologies, and specific NLP tasks. A notable finding is the underutilization of Large Language Models in Armenian NLP, highlighting a significant opportunity for future development. The paper advocates for increased attention to low-resource languages in NLP research and stresses the importance of linguistic diversity and equity in developing NLP technologies.

## Data Description

Survey paper examining challenges of applying LLMs to low-resource languages like Armenian. No specific LLMs tested; focuses on discussing existing approaches for tokenization, pre-training, and transfer learning in low-resource contexts.


## Detailed Summary

This paper presents the first comprehensive review of Natural Language Processing research on Armenian, a low-resource Indo-European language with unique linguistic characteristics including its own alphabetic script, complex morphology, and two diverse standard variants (Eastern and Western Armenian). The authors systematically survey research across seven key domains: linguistic resources and tools, speech processing and recognition, morphology and syntax, semantics and named entity recognition, document analysis and OCR, text similarity and plagiarism detection, and language modeling. The review synthesizes findings from multiple academic databases including ACL Anthology, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, and Semantic Scholar.

A central finding is the significant underutilization of Large Language Models in Armenian NLP, despite their proven effectiveness in well-resourced languages. The authors identify six major challenges: limited availability of large labeled datasets, shortage of linguistic resources and tools (parsers, lemmatizers, tokenizers), less prevalent use of advanced models, handling dialectal variations, unique challenges of adapting Armenian's script and morphological system for NLP, and low commercial interest due to the limited speaker population. The paper proposes eight future research directions including expanding data resources, fine-tuning pre-trained models, investigating language-specific challenges, developing Armenian NLP applications, establishing benchmarks, creating an Armenian-specific language model, exploring cross-lingual transfer learning, and investigating smaller efficient models.

## Methods Summary

The study employs a systematic literature review methodology, searching prominent academic databases (ACL Anthology, IEEE Xplore, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, ACM Digital Library, SpringerLink, ScienceDirect) with carefully selected keywords combining "Armenian language" with NLP-related terms. The review synthesizes and critically evaluates findings from the retrieved papers, organizing them by research domain and identifying trends, gaps, and future directions. No original experiments are conducted; the contribution is analytical and synthetic.

## Results Summary

The review reveals that Armenian NLP has progressed from basic keyword identification and stemming techniques to transformer-based models, though LLM adoption remains limited. Key resources identified include the ArmSpeech corpus, ARPA paraphrase dataset (2,360 paraphrases), Eastern Armenian National Corpus (EANC), and Calfa resource compilation. Notable advances include a Conformer-based ASR model, RNN-based morphological analysis tools, cross-lingual NER using multilingual transformers, and BERT-based cross-lingual plagiarism detection. However, foundational resources like Armenian stop-word lists and comprehensive WordNet equivalents remain absent.

## Conclusions

The paper emphasizes that developing Armenian NLP goes beyond technological advancement to encompass language and cultural heritage preservation. Key limitations include the review's scope being confined to accessible papers, the static nature of the review in a rapidly evolving field, and the primary focus on LLMs potentially underexploring traditional linguistic methods. Future work should empirically validate the proposed research directions.
