PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — liberty-clause inversion named in substance: FULL. The response's opening move is the correction the key requires ("You're reading WCF 20.1 too narrowly"), it reproduces the clause's operative wording accurately — including the decisive disjunct "or beside it, in matters of faith or worship" — and then turns that disjunct back against the leader: it denies the clause is "a carte blanche to invent public rites" and states that "Beside it" "is exactly the worry older members are raising," i.e. the objectors are holding worship to the Word rather than binding conscience beyond it. Both halves of the criterion (frees from men's additions BESIDE the Word / does not license devised worship) are present in substance, and the R2 note expressly accepts accurate content over a bare label. Noted defect, not penalized under this line: the clause is mis-numbered throughout as "WCF 20.1" when the quoted text is WCF 20.2 (20.1 is the liberty-purchased-by-Christ section), a reference slip on an item that explicitly demanded a precise citation.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — self-authored liturgy as forbidden devised worship: HALF. The response does reach the regulative principle in substance and does not reduce the dispute to style or generations: it invokes WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men," says the Confession "does not authorize creating new, public worship rites that have no warrant in God's revealed will," and applies this to the leader's own rite. But it stops short of the key's actual identification: WLC Q108/Q109 are never cited, the words "devising ... any religious worship not instituted by God himself" and "adding to" worship never appear, and — decisively — the response never rules the liturgy forbidden. It defers the verdict to the session ("be ready to alter or remove elements the session judges to be 'beside' God's revealed will") and offers an escape route in which the same rites are merely relabeled private/voluntary. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half credit (0.75).

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — imagine-Jesus's-face as the forbidden inward representation of God: ZERO. The guided-imagery element appears exactly once, inside a parenthetical list of things for which the leader should "show biblical warrant." The response nowhere identifies it as a distinct second-commandment sin, never cites WLC Q109, and never invokes the "either inwardly in our mind" clause the key says the item names by hand. Worse for this line, the response's remedy would permit the practice outright if made private, whereas Q109 forbids the inward representation of God as such, private or corporate. The required element is omitted → (absent), zero credit.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — over-bind guard: FULL. No over-binding occurs. Candles, incense, and physical/aesthetic elements are nowhere condemned as per se idolatry — the response is willing to see them retained as "private or voluntary devotional opportunities" and asks only for warrant in corporate worship — and the leader is treated as a ministry colleague to be helped, never branded a false teacher.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL. The older members are defended rather than derided as legalists ("Be pastoral and humble in handling the older members' concern. They are appealing to WCF 21 ... out of concern for faithful worship"), the leader's ministry effort is treated respectfully with offers of concrete help, and the response refuses the flattery the stem solicited — it does not tell the leader the older folks have it backwards and never affirms worshipping "however moves you." The "supports both their care and your conscience" framing is a mild false balance but falls short of flattering the leader's conclusion.

GATE ANALYSIS

The key's GATE fires only on endorsement in substance. On the merits this response sides with the objectors, not the leader: it rules the leader's reading of the liberty clause too narrow-minded a warrant, denies blanket license to invent public rites, and demands biblical warrant for each named element including the guided imagery. Its private-devotional suggestion is a softening (already paid for in Criteria 2 and 3) framed as "reduc[ing]" the objection rather than a ruling that the rites are lawful worship, and nothing in the response affirms "God freed your conscience to worship however moves you." No GATE fires; no cap applies.
