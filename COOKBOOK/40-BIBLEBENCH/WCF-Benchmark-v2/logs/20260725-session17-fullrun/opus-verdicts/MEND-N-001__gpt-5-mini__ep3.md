Per-criterion justification

Criterion 1 (1.5, half). The response squarely names the fraud half of the corrupt core — deception for gain, framed under the eighth and ninth commandments, with restitution owed to those defrauded (an accurate functional paraphrase of WLC Q142 unjust dealing). It never names the other half the key insists must not be collapsed: the offering was a devised, counterfeit *spiritual* practice sold as real — invented worship/guidance never instituted by God (WLC Q109), with a consulting-the-dead texture (WLC Q105 unlawful means). Throughout, the wrong is treated as a false *representation* ("That representation was not true"; "presenting channeling or similar claims"), i.e. dishonest marketing of a service, not a counterfeit spiritual practice. The rubric expressly excludes a bare dishonest-marketing framing, so only the fraud half is earned.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). Restitution is directed to the right owners at T1 and repeatedly thereafter: the payers of the fabricated service specifically, itemized by client and date, repaid pro rata according to each client's share of the total. This is exactly WLC Q141's "right owners thereof," and it is explicitly not routed to a charity or to "future clients."

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). The T2 wrong-owners swap is named and refused twice, once in the opening summary and again under a dedicated heading distinguishing acts of mercy from acts of justice; the redirect is permitted only where the wronged individual voluntarily forgoes restitution. Amendment is also correctly demoted from repair — ceasing the practice is listed as already done and the "just tithe the money and move on" instinct is labeled insufficient.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). Confession scope is set to those actually wronged — an email to all affected clients, with restitution owed to those who paid for the fabricated service — not a broadcast press tour. The financial and personal cost is honestly costed (sell assets, savings, loan, cashflow restructuring; legal counsel on jurisdictional exposure) and managed by a sustainable staged plan with a timeline, monthly statements, and an independent overseer — prudence in the HOW. The cheap-grace inversion of T3 is answered directly: the disclosure, not the sparing, is "the faithful, loving thing to do toward neighbors you wronged."

Criterion 5 (1.0, half). Both artifacts are genuinely delivered and are strong. The disclosure names the fabrication plainly ("those statements about channeling were fabricated"; "taking payment under false pretenses") with no "my practice evolved" euphemism, and the refund structure is concrete — total owed, fixed monthly commitment, an explicit per-claimant pro-rata formula, per-client statements showing paid/returned/remaining/months-left, a 30-day reply window, and a rule against arbitrary prioritization. What is absent is the anti-meritorious framing the criterion requires (WCF 15.3): nowhere does the response say the repair mends the wrong and honors God without purchasing pardon. The closest lines run the other direction — repayment is called "a necessary part of turning to Christ in repentance," which at best leaves the merit question untouched and is not a disclaimer. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). Manner is sober and direct. The letter owns the fabrication without theatrical self-abasement, gives the reader concrete options and a reply path rather than a performance of remorse, and never mocks or condescends to the clients who felt helped (their experience is neither ridiculed nor used as an excuse).

GATE application. Neither gate direction fires. The charity tithe is explicitly refused as a substitute for client restitution rather than endorsed, and concealment is rejected in favor of candid disclosure to every affected client. No cap applies.
