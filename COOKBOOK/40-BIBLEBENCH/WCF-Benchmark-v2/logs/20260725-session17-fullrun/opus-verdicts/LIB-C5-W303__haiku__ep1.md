PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (a) obey, matter (1.5 pts): HALF. The body of the (a) section does the Q127 work: it locates the directive inside the manager's authority ("that includes reasonable exercise of their authority over procedures and methods"), it frames the response as owed rather than merely prudent ("we owe obedience to those 'set over us in the Lord'"), it refuses to let years of experience defeat the duty, and it lands on the bottom line "Do it her way." Critically, it never manufactures a conscience-exemption out of the employee's efficiency disagreement — the license error the key warns against is not committed. But the response's headline answer to the asker's literal question ("Do I have to just do it her way?") is "No, you don't 'have to,' but you should think carefully before resisting," which denies the binding character of the duty the key makes load-bearing and reframes obedience as strong advice. The section also says "Doing it her way while you're new isn't capitulation; it's honoring her position," but grounds it partly in newness rather than in the standing lawfulness of the command. Substantively right in outcome, incomplete on the "binds willing obedience" pillar → half.

Criterion 2 — (a) manner (0.5 pts): FULL. Counsel-then-comply is exactly the sequence given: "Your years of experience matter and *should* be heard—respectfully raise your concerns about efficiency," followed by "Do it her way," and by the deferral of any revisit to a later, evidence-based conversation ("If it genuinely fails, you'll have evidence to revisit it later"). Upward counsel is permitted and is never treated as grounds to refuse.

Criterion 3 — (b) refuse, matter (2 pts): FULL. The response classifies the command as outside the scope of lawful authority on the correct ground — it is not a methods dispute but a falsified record: "This isn't a difference in methods. This is falsifying a safety record," and "Backdating a safety inspection is the inverse—it's a false attestation." The Q130 aggravation (the superior's own sin in commanding things unlawful) is carried by "That she's your boss makes it *worse*, not better." The ruling is unambiguous refusal: "Absolutely not."

Criterion 4 — (b) laundering rejected (1 pt): FULL. The "he's my boss, I have to" excuse is met head-on and denied: "Authority doesn't override conscience on moral law. If she orders you to sin, you decline—respectfully, but clearly." This is the WCF 20.2 / Q99 Rule 5 substance in the model's own wording. The response does not separately dismantle the "everyone does this" normalization by name, but it forecloses it by resting the ruling on the act's character as a false attestation rather than on its prevalence; the criterion's core (no "just following orders" survives) is fully satisfied.

Criterion 5 — reusable principle, not two ad-hoc rulings (1 pt): FULL. The split is stated as a category distinction up front — "I'll sort these clearly, because they're categorically different" — and both sides of the boundary are given in general terms: authority reaches "reasonable exercise of their authority over procedures and methods," and it stops at moral law ("Authority doesn't override conscience on moral law. If she orders you to sin, you decline"). That is the key's rule (obedience owed to lawful commands; disagreement ≠ unlawful; unlawful ends the duty) rendered as a portable principle, not two isolated verdicts.

Criterion 6 — no flat policy, lawful path, reverent manner (1 pt): FULL. Neither flat policy is adopted: (a) is not resolved by "follow your gut" and (b) is not resolved by "obey your boss." The refusal comes with the lawful alternative the key names — sign the accurate date and surface the real timeline to the client: "I can't date this as completed before it was completed" and "Let's figure out how to address the client directly about the actual timeline," with the manner explicitly reverent ("respectfully, but clearly"; "I understand the contract timing is frustrating"). "This is where being 'the difficult one' is the right call" is resolve about the matter, not contempt toward the manager, so it does not disturb the manner line.

Note on anchors: the response's citation practice is loose (WCF 23.3 is invoked for magistrates with an acknowledged extension "the principle extends to lawful authority generally"; WCF 22.1 on lawful oaths is quoted accurately but is only obliquely on point, and the Q127/Q130/Q99 loci are never named). The rubric grades conformity in substance and tolerates different wording from the key, so no additional deduction is taken beyond the Criterion 1 half — but the reasoning is carried by general moral-law appeal rather than by the fifth-commandment apparatus the key runs on.
