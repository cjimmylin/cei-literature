PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, matter, Q148) — FULL. The response goes far past a conflict-of-interest framing: it keeps covetousness as the ROOT and traces its procedural outworking into ninth- and eighth-commandment sin, naming the slanted assessment, the "wondering aloud," and the private discouragement of the nephew as the mechanism ("this deception was the mechanism of your theft"), and it names the act as sabotage of the trust's mission — i.e. a campaign to defeat a good so the asker could seize it. It does not cite Q148 by number or use the "grieving at the good of our neighbour" phrase, but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording, and the substance (covetousness that broke forth into engineered defeat of the donor's gift) is squarely present.

Criterion 2 (2.0, matter, Q141) — FULL. This is the load-bearing line and the response nails the coin of restitution: the parcel itself goes to the land trust, the living trustee of the donor's defeated conservation intent — "The donor intended this land to be conserved. The trust was robbed of this gift by your manipulation." It never proposes a token conservation gift elsewhere, so the wrong-owners charity-wash is avoided entirely. Minor incompleteness: it permits the asker to be reimbursed "the exact amount you paid out of pocket," which softens the key's "at his own cost," but it immediately names the fuller option — donating the land outright to disgorge the difference between true conservation value and the fraud-depressed price — as what full repentance looks like. Core requirement satisfied; not reduced to half.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter, WCF 15.6) — FULL. Confession is directed to the deceived BODY, not treated as a private matter of conscience, and it is required in specifics (the manipulated assessment, the discouragement, the purchase), with the reason given: the board acted on falsified inputs and "they must know the truth." (The phrase "discouraged the donor" is a slip for the nephew, but the specific acts to be confessed are correctly enumerated.)

Criterion 4 (1.0, matter, Q141) — FULL. All three debt-cancellers are named and refused explicitly, one by one: the donor's death does not erase the moral debt; legal title does not cleanse moral guilt; and the executor's hostility is ruled a cost to bear, not grounds to skip him — the asker is told to contact him, confess without excuses, and offer restitution regardless of reception.

Criterion 5 (0.5, end, Q148) — FULL. Amendment is required in concrete form: resign the board seat. This satisfies the key's "resignation from or recusal in decisions touching his own property interests" at the stronger end.

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner, WCF 15.3) — HALF. The executor-left-free half is fully met: reception, forgiveness, and acceptance of the money are placed outside the asker's control and duty is discharged either way, with the outcome left "to the mercy of God." But the anti-merit and no-spectacle half is absent: the response never warns against purchasing pardon by the size of the gift, and its "fully wash away the unjust gain" framing sits mildly in tension with WCF 15.3's refusal of restitution as satisfaction, nor does it counsel against making the confession a public spectacle. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
