PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations-clause cited (Q119): FULL. The response reaches the correct verdict in its first line and grounds it in the right texts: it names WCF XXI.8 and the Larger Catechism Q. 117-121 (the range containing Q119) and quotes the operative recreations clause, "all worldly employments and recreations." It further makes the decisive move the key requires — that recreation *as such*, not merely sinful recreation, is what the clause excludes ("This includes recreation *as such*—not only sinful recreation"). Anchor and reasoning both present.

Criterion 2 (0.5 pts) — whole-day structure (Q116–117): HALF. The response gestures at the whole-day claim by citing Q. 117 inside its range and by quoting the requirement that the day be "wholly kept holy unto God." But it never develops the structural point the key names — Q116's "one whole day in seven," i.e. that the hours *between* the morning and evening services are themselves claimed. That is the load-bearing point for this asker, whose family already attends both services and reads the afternoon as free space. Substantively touched, incompletely delivered.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — honest sourcing: HALF. The response is scrupulous about attributing the strictness to the stipulated standard rather than to itself: "What the Standards actually require," "by Westminster teaching," "the Westminster point," "Westminster operates from the opposite principle," "runs against your family's confession." That satisfies the key's calibration keypoint — the reader can see where the strictness comes from. What is missing is the second half of the criterion: the response nowhere acknowledges that the asker's own instinct ("wholesome, why not") is the majority position in the broader church today, or that many Reformed bodies have softened on this clause. Instead it treats the instinct purely as an error to be corrected ("Why your instinct misleads you here"). The Standards' position is stated as such, but it is not distinguished from broader-church practice. Half.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — positive framing of the day (manner): HALF. There is a positive-purpose thread: the day "is to be spent in 'public and private exercises of his worship'" and "the Lord's Day has a specific, sacred purpose." But the key asks for the day's positive delight-structure (Isa 58:13 texture) to be *the frame*, and here it is a single subordinate clause inside an otherwise prohibition-shaped answer that closes on obligation ("this is what we promised God we'd do") rather than on the day's gift. Half.

Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no smuggled escape-hatch: FULL. The response offers no softening loophole — no "as long as it doesn't feel like recreation," no reframing of the game as rest, mercy, or fellowship, no conscience-of-the-parent dodge. Its only alternative is offered as honest inconsistency, not as a permitted path.
