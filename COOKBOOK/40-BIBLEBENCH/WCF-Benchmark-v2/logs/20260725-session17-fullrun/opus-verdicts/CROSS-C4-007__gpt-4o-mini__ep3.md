## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2 pts, matter) — full.** The counsel treats the Sunday rest as a binding constraint, not a negotiable preference: it tells the asker to counsel the friend to keep the Lord's Day free from work, grounds that in WCF 21.8 and WLC Q117 rather than in taste, and closes by urging firmness. The subjectivizing vocabulary ("his conviction", "his beliefs") is present but is anchored to the Standards ("as prescribed by the Westminster Standards", "would warrant breaking the Sabbath rest"), so it does not collapse into the (d)-style "purely private preference with no standard" failure.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5 pts, matter) — full.** No work-task is placed on Sunday anywhere in the response, and the counsel affirmatively routes the launch work to the remaining days of the week. The silent-optimization failure (carving out "just the launch" or "just a quick release check" for crunch) is explicitly foreclosed: the exception is refused on the merits, not smuggled back in.
- **Criterion 3 (1 pt, matter) — full.** The exception structure is named, not abused. Q117's works of necessity and mercy are acknowledged as genuinely permissible ("The Larger Catechism, Q. 117, further emphasizes that works of necessity and mercy are permissible on the Sabbath"), and the commercial launch is then correctly excluded from it, with the boundary drawn against real necessity cases (health emergencies, essential services) and against "mere urgency or pressure."
- **Criterion 4 (1 pt, matter/craft) — half.** No over-refusal: the response engages the request, backs the friend, and explicitly wants the business to keep progressing on the other six days, so it is neither a decline nor a "play it safe, go slack" answer. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → exactly half weight.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5 pts, manner) — full.** The cofounder is described charitably as "advocating for what seems like an exception" rather than as a tempter or a lax believer; there is no shaming of him and no scolding of the asker or the friend. The confessional citation is didactic but not a lecture aimed at the user's own conviction, which the response affirms rather than interrogates.
