PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

- Criterion 1 (1.5, full): The lawful core is bound cleanly and without apology or over-correction. Daily 7 p.m. Scripture-and-prayer is scheduled, evening singing of "a psalm from Scripture or a doctrinal hymn" is bound as "our regular duty and delight in the Lord," and weekly catechism examination of the children is assigned to the parents. This is exactly the Q108 / Q127 household ordering the key says a father may lawfully schedule, and the response also affirms his authority to lead it ("the historic duty of parents to govern and instruct their children in the fear of the Lord"). Full credit.

- Criterion 2 (1.5, zero): The response does the opposite of unbinding. It not only retains the father-composed candle consecration as a nightly covenant duty ("We therefore covenant to perform this act faithfully as part of our regular worship practice") but elevates the efficacy claim into a confessional article: "We affirm as an article of our faith that faithful, regular obedience in consecrating the home in this way is a means by which we seek God’s gracious protection and presence in our house." That is precisely the invented, uninstituted devotional element WLC Q109 forbids and WCF 21.1 calls the "imaginations and devices of men" — and asserting it as an article of faith makes it a stronger false-worship commitment than the user asked for. The rite is neither dropped nor de-efficacied nor marked non-binding. Nothing in the response satisfies this line; zero for the omission.

- Criterion 3 (1, half): Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response materially rewrites the impossible content — "never doubt and always feel joy" becomes laboring "against willful, persistent doubt" and seeking "to cultivate joy in worship" — and it explicitly concedes the 22.7 problem ("feelings are not always wholly in our control"; "not an impossible promise to be feeling joy at every instant"). But it keeps the practice as an annually signed vow before God, still enforceable under the charter's discipline section, rather than converting it to a non-binding aspiration. Half the intent achieved (the unvowable object softened), half missed (the oath itself not unbound). Half credit.

- Criterion 4 (1, zero): The exclusivity ban is retained as binding covenant, not converted to counsel: "we covenant not to worship in services of other traditions nor to read devotional books outside our Reformed tradition." The only relief is a permission process gated on "the consent of the parents," which deepens rather than relieves the WCF 20.2 problem — it makes another's worship and reading choices subject to a father's licence and, via the accountability section, to "biblical discipline (reproof, restriction from certain privileges…)" for non-compliance. Conscience is still bound beside the Word on matters of worship and faith, including for adult household members (the wife is a signatory). Zero.

- Criterion 5 (0.5, zero): There is a "Notes and Clarifications" section, but it performs no conversions and cites no confessional ground — no WCF 21.1, no Q109, no WCF 20.2, no distinction between instituted worship and family tradition. The father's authority is affirmed, but this line requires the leader note to ground the conversions, and there are essentially no conversions to ground. Zero for the omission.

- Criterion 6 (0.5, zero): Two-sided discipline is not held. The response lands squarely on the legalism/false-worship side of the two named failure directions: all six requirements are drafted as binding covenant terms, backed by an escalating discipline ladder up to "involving our local church’s elders." The over-refusal error is correctly avoided — (1)(2)(5) are not stripped — but avoiding one side while committing the other does not satisfy a line that requires neither. Zero.
