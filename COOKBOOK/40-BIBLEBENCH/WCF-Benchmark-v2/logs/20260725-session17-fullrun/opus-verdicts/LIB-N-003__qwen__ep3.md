PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response identifies precisely the briefed proposal — teaching the days as "spiritually obligatory seasons for growth" — as the out-of-bounds element, and grounds it in the correct confessional material: WCF 20.2 on conscience not bound beside the Word ("God alone is Lord of the conscience"), WCF 21.1 on the regulative principle, and LC 109 on devised worship "under the title of antiquity, custom, devotion, good intent, or any other pretence whatsoever." This is not a bare "second commandment" gesture; it is an accurate paraphrase plus citation of the may-not-bind-uncommanded-worship content. Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.0, full). The lawful floor is stated correctly and is not dissolved: the Lord's Day is named as the ordinary weekly holy day (WCF 21.7–8), and occasional/extraordinary fasts and thanksgivings are affirmed as confessionally provided for and lawful ("The Standards do provide for fasting and thanksgiving, but they strictly define them as occasional and providential, not fixed"), with the WCF 21.5 language quoted. Ground 2 of the ruling restates the floor accurately. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The response does gesture at the margin — it concedes that "some in the Reformed tradition permit" a voluntary focus on the life of Christ as a matter of Christian prudence — and it clearly notes the historic Presbyterian lean against stated holy days (the Directory replacing the Book of Common Prayer "precisely to abolish the fixed calendar"). But it does not map the question as a live intra-confessional dispute; it resolves it confidently in one direction, declaring at the outset that "Elder B is right, and Elder A is in error" and ruling flatly that "The church may not institute fixed, recurring holy days that are not found in Scripture." The key expressly warns against fake-resolving this margin. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, half). A definite, usable ruling IS delivered — a verdict, four numbered grounds, and an instruction to vote against the proposal; the response does not duck the ranking or the counsel, so this is nowhere near zero. But the ruling's substance departs from the key's on two of the three specified contents: it does not hold that neither elder is wholly right as stated (it ranks Elder B simply right and Elder A simply in error), and it never singles out the imposition of ashes and the saints' commemorations as the elements pressing hardest on Q109 that should be dropped — it condemns the whole package uniformly instead. Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, half). The response stops short of the popery-style blanket condemnation the key warns about: it explicitly leaves room for a voluntary, non-statutory seasonal focus permitted "as a matter of Christian prudence under WCF 1.6." But it also tightens past what the Standards fix, ruling categorically that the church "may not institute fixed, recurring holy days that are not found in Scripture" and that a fixed calendar per se "is a substantive addition to the elements and institutions of worship" — i.e. treating voluntary non-binding calendar observance as forbidden rather than as disputed liberty. Partial over-restriction: half.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). Manner is respectful throughout. The asker is addressed collegially ("Fellow elder"), Elder A's argument is engaged on its merits rather than mocked (his position is called an error and a misunderstanding, which is a substantive judgment, not contempt), and the closing exhortation to the ordinary means of grace is brief and pertinent to the ruling rather than an extended sermon. Full credit.
