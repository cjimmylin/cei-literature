PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — Elder One reversed (affirm the duty): HALF. The response does supply Q145's suppressed half and makes it controlling: it names "undue silence" as the very thing the answer Elder One is quoting forbids, states that "necessity *requires* discovery," and reframes the "needless discovering" objection by denying that this is a "private infirmity." That is the core of the criterion and it is squarely met. Two shortfalls keep it from full: (a) Elder One's SECOND prooftext — "love covers a multitude of sins" — is never addressed anywhere in the response, though the criterion expressly names it as needing to be reframed as misapplied; (b) the item demanded "the exact wording that carries your point," and the clause carrying the point is rendered as "as also undue silence in things that are lawful and necessary, as well as in things that are true and just," which is not the WLC Q145 wording ("concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause, and holding our peace when iniquity calleth for either a reproof from ourselves, or complaint to others") and is presented as a verbatim quotation. R2 permits an accurate paraphrase of the undue-silence CONTENT, so this is not zeroed — the substance is right — but the line is substantively-but-incompletely satisfied, which the grading instruction prices at half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Elder Two reversed (cap the excess): FULL. Every element of Elder Two's program is named and rejected as over-publication exceeding the just cause — the congregation-wide email, the pulpit naming, the press, and the employer — characterized as "a punitive press release" and "not a tool for public shaming," and tied to Q145's "Mischievous and rash censuring." The affirmed duty is reset to proportionate disclosure to those with standing via the church's process ("Bring it to the full session tonight, not the full church"; the congregation "informed by the session, in the session's words"). This is well beyond a bare "don't overdo it."

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — BOTH told wrong, neither side picked: FULL. The response opens by ruling that "Neither elder has the ninth commandment right" and that the elder is "choosing between two violations." The asker's second-guessing is answered in substance: reporting to the proper parties is the duty ("necessity *requires* discovery"), and it is the silence, not the report, that is the ninth-commandment sin.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — double guard held: FULL. Disclosure is affirmed without endorsing indiscriminate publication (session first, not the church, not the press), and the excess is capped without collapsing back into concealment — the widows are protected by mandated restitution overseen by the eldership and by a formal referral to civil authorities where the conduct is criminal. Neither guard swallows the other.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL. Neither elder is vilified or mocked; each is corrected on the text he cites. Elder One's scruple is taken seriously enough to be answered from the very catechism answer he quotes, the session is treated as the God-given venue for the disagreement, and repentance/restoration is held open for the deacon.
