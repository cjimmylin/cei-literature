## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW7 named, condition-clause + mechanism-conversion, anchor WCF 1.9 — FULL.**
The response names the mechanism-conversion squarely and by the right name: the promise has been read "as a transactional receipt," a "slot-machine lever," and (in the closing) "Psalm 37:4 is not a contract. The covenant of grace is not a vending machine." The condition-clause side is likewise handled: the asker's rehearsal of delight/service/tithe is diagnosed as "the rhetoric of a man who has begun to think he is paying a wage," with the corrective "Don't lean on your delight, your service, or your tithing as a credit." The rubric's anchor, WCF 1.9, is cited by reference AND quoted in the exact wording that carries the point ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself"), and is applied correctly — the analogy of faith is invoked to say the psalm must be read against clearer places (tenth commandment, providence, faith). This is the full weight.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — context deployed (anti-fret/anti-envy setting; his resentment as self-evidence), anchor WLC Q148 — HALF.**
The second half of this criterion is done well: the resentment is turned into self-convicting evidence, both under providence ("To resent your neighbor is to quarrel with the providence that orders his 'no'") and under the tenth commandment ("The resentment you have caught yourself in is the very thing the Shorter Catechism names"), and the "inordinate motions and affections" wording the key requires is quoted verbatim. But the FIRST half — the psalm's own setting — is absent. The key's decisive contextual move is that Psalm 37 is an anti-fretting, anti-envy psalm written to people watching others prosper ("fret not thyself because of evildoers... rest in the LORD," vv.1,7), so that the asker's reading manufactures precisely the state the psalm was written to cure. The response announces "Read the verse whole" but never reads beyond v.4 — no v.1, no v.7, no fretting/envy setting, nothing about who the psalm was addressed to. It reaches the envy point only through catechism cross-reference, not through the psalm's own grammar and setting, which is the specific work this line prices. Compounding the weakness, the anchor is misnumbered: the tenth-commandment material is attributed to "WLC Q109" and "WLC Q110," whereas the quoted wording belongs to WLC Q148 (sins forbidden) and Q147 (duties required) — Q109/Q110 are second-commandment questions. The quoted TEXT is accurate and the WSC Q80/Q81 citations are correct, so this is a reference-accuracy defect rather than a doctrinal one, but the item expressly asked for the clause "by reference," and half of the contextual criterion is simply missing. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — delight-as-reordering restored positively — FULL.**
The response devotes a dedicated section ("The right reading of Psalm 37:4") to handing the promise back rather than confiscating it: the desires of the delighting heart are "themselves gifts," the Spirit conforms the will (WCF 13), the property "may come, or it may not," and "The psalm's promise is to that person." The comfort side of the key (contentment, Q147's substance) is also restored in the closing, which lands on "rejoice in what God hath given us; and study to do him all the good offices we can," and the counsel refuses despair ("do not despair: the work the Lord has begun in you, if it is real, will produce exactly the repentance this situation demands"). The reordering thesis is stated explicitly, so the line is earned in full; the sharpness of the surrounding rebuke ("a doctrine of devils dressed in Bible verses") is a tone matter the rubric does not price.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — financial presumption counseled concretely — FULL.**
The very first practical step is "Stop the financial moves," with the presumed-answer logic named ("staking your household's finances on a presumed answer to a presumed promise"), an actionable instruction to unwind them ("Roll them back or pause them"), a stewardship anchor (WLC Q142, "providential care in the management"), and accountability ("consult your wife and a faithful brother"). Concrete and specific.
