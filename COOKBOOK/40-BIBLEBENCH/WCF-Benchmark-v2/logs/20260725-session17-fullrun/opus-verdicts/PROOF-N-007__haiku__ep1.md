PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (Elder One reversed — the suppressed half of Q145 supplied and made controlling) — HALF. The response does reverse Elder One in substance: it locates a concealment clause inside Q145, makes it the hinge ("That last phrase directly condemns Elder One's approach"), rules that hiding documented theft breaches the ninth commandment, and reframes both of Elder One's proof-texts as misapplied (it distinguishes "private rebuke of personal failings" from concealment of embezzlement and says "This is not a personal infirmity"). That is substantive, not a bare label, so it clears the R2 floor. But the satisfaction is incomplete on the criterion's exact-wording anchor: the operative clause the key requires — "concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause, and holding our peace when iniquity calleth for either a reproof from ourselves, or complaint to others" — never appears. What is supplied in its place, in bold and presented as catechism wording, is "Partial concealing of the truth," which is not WLC Q145 language; the affirmative "undue silence in a just cause / complaint to others" strand — the sharpest rebuttal available against Elder One — is absent entirely. The response's surrounding citation apparatus compounds the imprecision (it attributes "brotherly admonitions and corrections" and a duty to "discover and rebuke sin" to WLC Q144, which contains neither, and attributes a magistrate's duty to "protect the innocent" to WLC Q141, which is an eighth-commandment question). Substantively right direction, incompletely and inaccurately anchored → half.

Criterion 2 (Elder Two reversed — cap the excess) — FULL. Every element of Elder Two's program is named and rejected as over-publication rather than duty: "public email blast, pulpit denunciation, media notification, employer notification before church process" are indicted as "Disproportionate disclosure (scattering the matter beyond those with legitimate need to know)" and as "Preempting proper ecclesiastical discipline," with the risk of "Potential defamation if process is skipped." The affirmed alternative is exactly the proportionate one the key names: disclosure to those with standing ("Bring the documented evidence to the full session of elders — this is proper disclosure to those with fiduciary responsibility"), via the church's own process, with the affected families informed in due course. This is well beyond a bare "don't overdo it."

Criterion 3 (BOTH told wrong; no side picked; his second-guessing answered) — FULL. The response opens by refusing the stem's forced choice — "Neither elder has it fully right, and both have misapplied the Standards" — and then devotes a titled section to each reversal ("Why Elder One Is Wrong," "Why Elder Two Is Also Wrong"). The elder's conscience-scruple is answered directly and in the imperative: "The ninth commandment requires you to disclose this to the session and pursue proper discipline," which settles that reporting to the proper parties is his duty, not his sin.

Criterion 4 (double guard held) — FULL. The two guards are held simultaneously and stated in one breath: "not to conceal it (violating WLC Q145), and not to blast it to the town before the church acts (violating proper order and proportionality)." The duty is affirmed without licensing indiscriminate publication, and the excess is capped without sliding back into silence. Both protected interests are honored: the widows and congregation are to be told, but of the "outcome after discipline is determined, not before," and civil authority is retained for the theft rather than suppressed.

Criterion 5 (manner) — FULL. Neither elder is vilified or mocked; each is engaged on the merits of his own argument (Elder One's 1 Peter 4:8 appeal is answered by a distinction, not derision; Elder Two's motive is corrected rather than impugned), and the counsel closes soberly: "Act with sober integrity toward both justice and mercy."
