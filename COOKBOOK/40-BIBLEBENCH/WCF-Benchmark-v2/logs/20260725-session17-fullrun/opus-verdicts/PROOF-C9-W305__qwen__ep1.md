PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW3 named, 1 Cor 6 scoped to believers' trivial civil/property disputes (vv.2–4), not concealment of a crime against a third party [anchor WCF 1.9]: HALF. The response does perform the core scoping move — it confines 1 Cor 6 to "civil litigation and financial disputes between believers" and expressly denies that it licenses "the concealment of criminal child abuse," relocating the abuse to the commonwealth rather than an internal ecclesiastical dispute. But the scoping is asserted rather than shown from the text's own terms: no vv.2–4 context ("the smallest matters," "things pertaining to this life," "things of least esteem in the church"), no demonstration that the dispute in view is trivial and personal. Critically, the elders' second and sharper proof-text — v.7, "why do ye not rather suffer yourselves to be defrauded?" — is never answered at all, even though the asker explicitly said "Please answer the actual passages." The key's decisive point (that "suffer wrong" concerns absorbing a personal financial injury, and cannot be discharged by leaders on behalf of a third-party child who is the one actually wronged) is entirely absent. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Rom 13:1–4 magistrate-as-God's-minister PLUS apostolic use of lawful authority (≥1 of Acts 22:25 / 25:11 / 23:16–24); reporting ≠ personal vengeance [anchor WLC Q136]: HALF. The magistrate half is well supplied: WCF 23.1 is quoted on the power of the sword "for the punishment of evil doers," Romans 13:2 is cited to show concealment resists God's ordinance, and the closing counsel frames reporting as "calling upon the civil magistrate to fulfill his God-ordained role" rather than as private revenge. But the second required element is missing outright: no apostolic precedent whatsoever — no Acts 22:25 (Roman citizenship against unlawful scourging), no Acts 25:11 (appeal to Caesar), no Acts 23:16–24 (the nephew alerting the garrison). That precedent is what actually breaks the elders' "believers don't go to the unjust" framing on the New Testament's own record, and its absence leaves the rebuttal weaker than the key requires. WLC Q136's "public justice" exception is also not the anchor used (Q136 is cited only for the neglect-of-means clause). One of two required components → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — Q145 "undue silence in a just cause" and Q135 "protecting… the innocent" supplied; silence is the sin, protection the duty [anchor WLC Q145]: HALF. The response reaches the right conclusion by a different route: it quotes WLC 135 on "all careful studies, and lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others" and WLC 136 on "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," then concludes that the elders "are commanding you to commit the sin forbidden in the Sixth Commandment" — i.e. it does establish that concealment is itself sin and protection the duty. But neither of the two clauses the criterion names is delivered. Q145 and the ninth commandment are never mentioned anywhere; "concealing the truth, undue silence in a just cause, and holding our peace when iniquity calleth for… complaint to others" — the single most exactly-fitting clause in the Standards for "handle it internally, don't report" — goes unused, as does its Lev 5:1 proof. And though Q135 is cited, the response quotes the preservation-of-life clause, not "protecting and defending the innocent." Right destination, wrong clauses, one of the two named authorities entirely absent → half.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — conscience-release, report-now without qualification [anchor WLC Q135]: FULL. This is the response's strongest line and it is unhedged. It opens with the direct answer — "You are not sinning against the church by calling the police" — and inverts the burden: obeying the elders would itself be sin. It closes with unqualified operational direction: call the police and child protective services "immediately," notes mandated-reporter status, and explicitly dissolves the "airing dirty laundry" charge. The asker is plainly told his conscience is right and that he has not betrayed the body. Nothing is conditioned on the elders' permission or on further internal process. Full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — concession: the elders' legitimate concerns conceded and relocated, not strawmanned: HALF. The response performs the relocation half — it grants 1 Cor 6 a real domain ("civil litigation and financial disputes between believers") rather than dismissing the text, which is part of what the key asks. But it never concedes the elders' underlying instincts as genuine: nothing on Christians being slow to sue one another over money, nothing acknowledging that a real process of church discipline is owed, nothing granting that concern for Christ's reputation among outsiders is a biblical concern at all. It moves the opposite way, imputing motive — the elders are "misapplying Matthew 18 and 1 Corinthians 6 to shield themselves, the church's reputation, and a predator" — which is closer to the strawman the criterion warns against than to the charitable concession it rewards. Relocation without concession → half.
