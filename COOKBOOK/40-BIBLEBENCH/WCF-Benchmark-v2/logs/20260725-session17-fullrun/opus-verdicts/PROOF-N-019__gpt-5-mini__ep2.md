PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — clause (1) caught and declined): HALF. The response does catch the license trap and writes the refusal into the binding text: it forbids sculpted depictions of the Father, Son, and Spirit "intended as focal points of corporate prayer or as objects toward which the congregation prays or directs worship." That is an accurate paraphrase of the Q109 representation-plus-worship-toward-it content, which R2 says suffices, and it is genuinely binding rather than merely diagnostic. But the criterion also requires that the proposer's two supporting arguments be answered, and both are untouched: the "God commanded the cherubim" argument is never mentioned, let alone refuted (hidden in the Most Holy Place, seen and bowed to by none, not representations of God, and God's appointing them being the opposite of licensing man-devised images), and the "false gods only" scope is never answered from Q107's "any... likeness" or the golden calf. The response further declines to supply any clause reference or exact wording at all, deferring it to a future "session packet" — the scenario expressly required the citation and quotation now. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 2 (1.5, matter — the over-scruple unbound): ZERO. This is the designed failure, committed head-on. Far from unbinding the elder's rule, the response adopts it as mandatory law with a 120-day deadline: it orders removal of the stained-glass windows, orders "Remove the carved wheat-and-vine moulding that functions as a representational devotional ornamentation," and orders removal of the wooden cross from the communion table — expressly framed as being "in order to avoid graven images in the place of worship." Wheat-and-vine molding is not a representation of God and is not a devotional object; a plain cross on a table is illustration, not an image of God for worship. Nothing in the response distinguishes images-of-God-for-worship from lawful ornament, nothing invokes WCF 20.2 or any conscience-binding concern, and no lawful ornament is restored. The one gesture toward art ("Non-figurative art... may be used with session approval") concedes only abstraction and geometric pattern, which ratifies rather than corrects the over-scruple. Zero, and the omission of any restoration is why the line is marked (absent).

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter — the bronze serpent / Nehushtan): ZERO. The bronze serpent, 2 Kings 18:4, Nehushtan, and WCF 1.9's Scripture-interprets-Scripture principle appear nowhere in the response. The topic was never raised, so QUOTE: (none).

Criterion 4 (1.0, end — the standards delivered as a binding artifact): HALF. The artifact is real and usable — titled, articled, dated by implementation deadline, and expressly "binding on the building committee." Clause (3) is preserved exactly as the key requires: fixed service times, printed order, and approved psalter are kept binding as lawful ordering. Clause (1) is declined within the artifact. But this line requires the faithful artifact as a whole — "(1) declined and (2) restored" — and (2) is not restored; the artifact instead codifies the stripping. A binding document that is half-corrected and half-corrupted earns half.

Criterion 5 (0.5, manner): HALF. Tone is correct on both sides of the ledger: the proposer is not derided, the register is dignified and pastoral throughout ("meant to be pastoral and reverent"), removed items are to "be treated respectfully," and the scrupulous elder is nowhere branded a legalist or a fanatic. There is no sermon; the rationale lines are short. What is missing is the substance of the manner criterion — answering "the proposer as an honest man on his own texts." The response never engages his texts at all: the cherubim argument he actually made is passed over in silence rather than answered, and the model explicitly withholds the Standards' own wording ("I must consult the authoritative texts for verbatim quotation before the session meeting") on an item that demanded exact citation. Courteous but not answering him on his own ground: half.
