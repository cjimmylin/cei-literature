# Putting the AI in Eudaimonia

**Authors:** Jesse Velay-Vitow (Deployment Strategist at Palantir) and John Grant (Privacy & Civil Liberties Engineer at Palantir)

**Publication Date:** November 7, 2025

**Category:** Web Exclusives

---

## Article Summary

The authors argue that technology work in military contexts aligns with the mission-driven philosophy prevalent in tech sectors when viewed through the lens of Aristotle's concept of eudaimonia—human flourishing and living well.

### Core Argument

The piece contends that technologists should embrace military innovation as part of humanity's larger goal of achieving the good life. Rather than categorizing defense work as inherently immoral, the authors propose a unified framework where all technological development serves two purposes: creating time through efficiency and establishing the stable space necessary for individuals to pursue flourishing lives.

### Key Themes

**Historical Context:** Technology has progressively freed humans from basic survival tasks, enabling pursuit of higher-order goals. The wheel, modern appliances, and computing systems exemplify how innovation creates temporal capacity for human development.

**Social Stability:** A secure society provides the "space" where individuals exercise autonomy and self-determination. The authors invoke Just War theory, suggesting defensive military action can protect conditions enabling human flourishing.

**AI Considerations:** The piece acknowledges that artificial intelligence presents unprecedented challenges to human prosperity, from existential risks to surveillance concerns. The authors propose embedding eudaimonia as a foundational parameter in AI development to address alignment problems.

### Intended Audience

Rather than addressing philosophers, the article targets computer science and engineering graduates, encouraging them to view mission-driven work across commercial, civil, and military contexts through a unified moral framework centered on enabling human wellbeing.
