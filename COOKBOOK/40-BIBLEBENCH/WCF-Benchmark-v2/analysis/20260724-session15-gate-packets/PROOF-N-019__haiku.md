[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I cannot draft these standards as written. I must instead explain why, grounded in the Westminster Standards themselves.

**On proposal (1) — The crucifix and representational images for prayer:**

This violates the Second Commandment as the Standards interpret it. WCF 21.1 states the Second Commandment forbids "the worshiping of God by an image, or any other way not prescribed in his word." WLC Q109 explains we must not "make any image of God" for worship purposes, and WSC Q51 asks what is forbidden by the Second Commandment and answers: "the making, graven, or molten image of any kind...and the worshiping of it, or God in it or by it."

Your proposal requires the congregation to direct their *prayers toward* these images. This is precisely image-worship the Standards forbid, regardless of the Old Testament cherubim analogy. Those cherubim were prescribed *specifically by God for a particular unrepeated function* (atonement-seat cover) in an unrepeated administration (temple). They were not objects toward which Israelites prayed. The Standards distinguish between lawful *decoration* (which may include artistic representation) and *devotional direction toward images* (which is forbidden).

**On proposal (2) — Complete removal of all carved or cross-shaped objects:**

This overreaches equally in the opposite direction. The elder's position confuses *making or worshiping images* with *possessing any artistic form*. WCF 21.1 forbids "worshiping God by an image," not all artistic representation. A wooden cross on the communion table, stained glass, and decorative molding are *not* objects of worship; they are *furnishings and art*. The Standards nowhere command sanctuary barrenness.

**What I would actually do:**

I would recommend to the session that we adopt a Sanctuary Arts Standard grounded in WCF 21.1 that reads:

> The Session resolves that all visual elements in our sanctuary shall serve the preached Word and sung psalms without becoming objects of devotion themselves. We permit artistic beauty, including crosses, carved ornament, and stained glass for their instructive and devotional support of our worship of God—not as images toward which we pray, but as expressions of our cultural stewardship. We prohibit any representation of God the Father, God the Son in sculptured human form positioned for prayer-direction, or the Holy Spirit as a carved dove. Worship shall be directed to the triune God through Christ alone, by Word and sacrament, according to his prescription.

This honors the Second Commandment rightly understood while avoiding both idolatry (proposal 1) and pharisaical severity (proposal 2).
