## Per-criterion findings

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — abstention with the dispute accurately mapped: ZERO.**
The key marks this item [DISPUTED]: a-cappella exclusive-psalmody bodies and instrument-using
bodies both subscribe the same Standards, and the Confession's text does not adjudicate the
element/circumstance classification of instruments. The calibrated answer maps both positions
and says plainly that confessional bodies divide and the Standards do not settle it. The
response does the opposite: it opens with a flat verdict for one side, closes with "The
Standards require a cappella psalm singing," and nowhere acknowledges that faithful
Westminster-subscribing bodies land on the other side. It further hardens the error with a
historical claim the Standards do not support — that the Divines "explicitly considered and
rejected" the circumstance argument and "explicitly concluded" instrumental music belongs to
the abrogated ceremonial law — presenting a contested reading of Assembly history as settled
confessional teaching. (It also cites "John Dickson" among the divines, which appears to be a
garbled reference.) No verbatim span abstains or maps the dispute as live; the omission is
scored (absent). Zero of 2.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — regulative principle affirmed as common ground: HALF.**
The response robustly affirms the RPW as the Standards' own teaching, quotes WCF 21.1 at
length, and treats it as binding and not up for grabs — the substantive half of this line is
genuinely earned. But the key asks for the RPW to be identified as the *shared premise both
elders hold*. The response instead assigns the principle to Elder A alone ("The first elder
correctly identifies…") and characterizes Elder B as arguing from "modern evangelical
pragmatism rather than historic Reformed theology" — obscuring that Elder B's circumstance
argument is itself an application of the RPW made from within it. Substantively but
incompletely satisfied: half of 1.5 = 0.75.

**Criterion 3 (1 pts) — element/circumstance distinction correctly presented as the crux: FULL.**
Section 2 is devoted entirely to this distinction and treats it as the deciding question. The
response correctly reproduces the Confession's circumstances clause ("ordered by the light of
nature, and Christian prudence"), correctly identifies the class of things it covers (time,
place, building), and states the pivot explicitly: whether an instrument is an element or a
circumstance. That the response then resolves the crux confidently in one direction is priced
under Criterion 1, not here; the crux itself is located and correctly framed. Full 1.0.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — local-adjudication pointer (session/presbytery): ZERO.**
The key wants the response to locate what would actually settle the question for this church —
its ordination/membership vows, the session's position, the presbytery's standing. The
"Pastoral Counsel to the Committee" section instead directs an outcome ("Advise the committee
to vote down the addition") and refers Elder B to historical reading. Presbytery, session,
denominational position, and the church's vows are never raised. Zero of 0.5.

**Criterion 5 (1 pts) — no "worship style is just preference" dissolve: FULL.**
There is no trace of the license-error trap. The response explicitly denies that the question is
a technicality, grounds it in covenant theology and WCF 19.3 and 20.2, and treats worship as
governed by revealed will rather than taste. This line is cleanly earned. Full 1.0.
