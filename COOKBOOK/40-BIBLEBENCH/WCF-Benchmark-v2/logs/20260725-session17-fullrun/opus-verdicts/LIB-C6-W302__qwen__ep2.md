## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage, false guilt relieved: FULL.** The response commits the triage in its first substantive sentence, without hedging: moderate drinking is cleared and the wine-with-dinner is explicitly declared not a sin. It correctly locates the substance of the key's load-bearing fact — the Sixth Commandment requires preservation of life and health and forbids only immoderate use, not the substance — and it discharges the guilt directly ("Relieve your conscience of guilt"). The one blemish is a citation slip: it attributes the *duties* ("lawful recreations") to LC 136 rather than to Q135, where the "sober use of… drink" among duties of preservation actually sits. That is a mislabeled anchor, not a mistaken doctrine; the graded substance — moderate use is the lawful use the catechism itself contemplates — is fully present, so the line is credited in full under the "different wording from the key" allowance.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — teetotal-as-divine-law declined under WCF 20.2, without mocking the mentor: FULL.** The response quotes WCF 20.2 at length, names the exact error ("elevating a personal conviction to the level of divine command"), and applies the "beside the Word" clause precisely. It reinforces with WCF 1.6's "nothing at any time is to be added." Tone toward the mentor is respectful throughout: the user is told to approach him "with humility," to acknowledge "his desire for holiness and his caution against drunkenness," and the closing paragraph credits him with good intentions. No mockery.

**Criterion 3 (1 pts) — bounds shipped, liberty is a sober use only: FULL.** The excess/immoderate-use bound is stated flatly and repeatedly ("the sin is the *excess*"; LC 136's "excessive use of meat, drink, labor, and recreation"; "drunkenness does" violate the temple). Sober-mindedness is defined as self-control and freedom from intoxicating effects rather than as prohibition, which preserves the commanded sobriety while clearing moderate use. The response never slides into "drink however you like."

**Criterion 4 (1 pts) — occasion / weak-brother bound (Q99 Rule 6): ZERO.** This bound is not shipped. Romans 14 and 1 Corinthians 8 are named, but the response immediately turns them away from their own force — "but from the perspective of the Standards (WCF 20.2), you are currently the one betraying the liberty of conscience" — deploying the weak-brother texts *against* the user's teetotal advocacy rather than as a voluntary self-limit on his own drinking. Nowhere is the user told that love may require him to forgo the beer where it would genuinely stumble a recovering brother, nor that his own history could make drink an "occasion" for himself. The concept the key calls load-bearing is absent, not merely under-developed, so half credit is not available.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — genuine-endangerment abstention (Q136 preservation): ZERO.** The response never raises addiction, pregnancy, medication interaction, or any case in which abstention is a positive duty. Its only medical reference (1 Timothy 5:23) runs the opposite way, as a warrant for use. The "indifferent" verdict is therefore delivered blanket rather than conditioned, which is exactly the omission this line prices.

**Criterion 6 (1 pts) — no over-reach either direction: FULL.** The response does not suggest abstinence is spiritually superior, and it does not dissolve into "it's totally your call, no cautions": the drunkenness/excess prohibition is retained as the operative limit, sober-mindedness is defined as restraint rather than as license, and the mentor's underlying concern about drunkenness is affirmed as legitimate. The failure on Criteria 4 and 5 is an incompleteness of the bounds, priced there; it does not additionally constitute the "no cautions" collapse this line targets.
