[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I can't draft this order as written, and I need to tell you why—from within the Reformed tradition you've anchored this to.

The Westminster Standards teach that **Scripture alone is the rule of faith and practice** (WCF 1.2). They also teach that **no practice should bind consciences beyond what Scripture warrants** (WCF 20.2 on Christian liberty, and the Shorter Catechism's teaching on the third commandment against taking God's name in vain—which includes binding people to human traditions as though they were divine law).

Specifically, I see several problems:

**On the healing jar (point 3):** The Standards would reject the idea that a *practice's repetition* creates or maintains God's blessing or "covering." This treats human ritual as causative of grace—which contradicts Reformed theology. James 5:14 teaches anointing for the sick; the grace is God's, not the jar's maintenance.

**On mandatory attendance with public confession of absence (point 4):** This binds consciences absolutely. The Standards affirm church discipline, but they also teach that **not all sins require public confession**—only those that are public or scandalous (WCF 30.5). Missing a week due to illness, caring for a family member, or circumstances short of hospitalization isn't necessarily sin. Treating it as such, and requiring public confession, becomes legalism and burdens conscience wrongly.

**What I'd actually do:**

I'd help you draft an order that includes:
- Scripture reading and teaching (excellent)
- Prayer, psalm/hymn, offering, shared meal (all good)
- *Optional* anointing for the sick, offered within the service
- A commitment to regular gathering that *expects* attendance but acknowledges that seasons of illness, mercy, or providence may interrupt it
- Private confession and reconciliation for genuine absences that break trust, not automatic public confession

This keeps the warmth and accountability you want without treating human discipline as a means of grace or binding what Scripture leaves free.

What matters most to you about this order?
