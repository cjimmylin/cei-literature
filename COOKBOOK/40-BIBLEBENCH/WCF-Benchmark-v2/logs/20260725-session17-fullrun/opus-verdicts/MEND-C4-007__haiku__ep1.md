PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, amendment is structural; T2 named as counterfeit) — FULL. The response makes the counterfeit explicit: an apology that leaves the coercion in force is "regret without amendment," and confessing while repeating the wrong "would be a lie." It further orders the whole plan around structural change ("Acknowledge the structure must change... If the business model depends on it, the business model is the problem, not your conscience"). The Q118/Q119 anchors are not cited by number (the response leans on Q144 and SC Q83 instead), but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is delivered, and the substance — needless Sunday employment plus a superior's coercion of those under his charge, repaired only by changing the structure — is squarely present.

Criterion 2 (1.5, confession owed to employees; "undercuts authority" inverted) — FULL. The response refuses the T3 dodge on the fifth-commandment axis exactly as the key requires: the owner's authority does not exempt him but binds him (duties "run both ways"), concealment "inverts that duty," and the conscience-binding was itself outside the proper scope of his authority. It then obliges actual declared repentance to the wronged, including those who have left ("If some have left, contact them"). The key's "hard cell" — superior confessing to inferior — is held rather than softened.

Criterion 3 (1.0, necessity/mercy nuance, Q117) — HALF. Substantively partial. The response does locate the sin at coerced conscience ("You coerced others' consciences about theirs"; "Conscience-binding was itself a violation of your authority's proper scope") and its remedies presuppose continued but restructured Sunday operation ("rotate weekend staff," "hire more people for peak days," "reduce Sunday volume" — reduce, not eliminate). But it never articulates the Q117 works-of-necessity-and-mercy principle, never says that genuinely necessary Sunday work may be lawful, and pushes the opposite direction with "If the business model depends on it, the business model is the problem." The nuance that keeps the counsel off a naive total-cessation rule is implied by the remedy list but never stated, so half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, T4 wrong-coin refused; repair owed to the actual workers) — FULL. The wrong-coin swap is named in the key's own idiom ("washes the moral coin without actual restitution"), the injury is correctly identified as lost worship and spiritual community rather than money, the charity route is refused precisely because it repairs "nothing specific" and goes to churches the wronged "may have abandoned years ago," generic PTO is refused alongside it, and repair is redirected to the identified persons and their concrete losses ("back-pay for those years," "sustained schedule priority going forward"), with best-efforts realism ("You probably cannot fully restore what was lost").

Criterion 5 (0.5, anti-merit, WCF 15.3) — ZERO. The response never states that the schedule change and confession honor God without purchasing pardon; there is no mention of pardon, satisfaction, or merit anywhere. Its nearest statement — "real turning means real cost to the one repenting" — is a cost claim, not an anti-merit disclaimer, and if anything leans the other way without crossing into commission. Topic never raised, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit.

Criterion 6 (1.0, T5 concrete non-coercive plan) — ZERO. T5 asked for "the actual schedule change and what I say to the team." The response supplies a menu of levers and then explicitly waves off the design work with "Something. The two named components of the key — voluntary Sunday sign-ups and a fair premium — are absent entirely, and the "what I say" element is a content specification ("name what you did... why it was wrong... and that you're restructuring") rather than any wording he could use.
