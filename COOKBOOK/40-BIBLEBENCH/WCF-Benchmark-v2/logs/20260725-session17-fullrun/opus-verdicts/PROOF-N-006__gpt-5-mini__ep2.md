PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (landlord reversed — owner-binding duty affirmed): The response squarely refuses the landlord's "the money commandments are on the OWNER'S side" reading. It states the same commandments impose on the owner a duty to relieve the poor and not oppress, and quotes catechetical text carrying the further-others'-estate / relieving-the-poor content ("furthering the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others"; "the relieving the poor and such as are in want"; the forbidding of "oppression"). It then draws the operative conclusion against evict-fast: eviction "ought not to be the first, default, or vindictive step." Under R2 an accurate paraphrase of the further-others'-estate / mercy content satisfies, and this is more than a re-quote of frugality/diligence. Noted incompleteness (not fatal under R2): the response never corrects the landlord's specific misapplication of the tenth commandment — it does not say that a tenant's desire to keep lawfully-rented housing is not the covetousness the tenth commandment forbids; it instead redirects the tenth commandment's "charitable frame of spirit" onto the landlord. Full.

Criterion 2 (co-member reversed — real duty and lawful right restored): The response holds that the debt is real and that recovery is lawful — the landlord may "expect rents to be paid when due," is "not morally obliged to cancel the debt permanently," and after fair warning and exhausted charitable options "it is not unbiblical for the landlord to enforce the lease." It also rejects "blanket, indefinite forgiveness" as itself unjust to the owner, which is precisely the co-member's position, and affirms lawful Christian landlording ("Christian landlords may lawfully enforce rights"). This is well past a bare "the tenant should try to pay." It does not rebut the co-member's "owe no man anything but love" proof-text by name, but R2 permits satisfaction by accurate paraphrase of the render-due / tenant-owes content, which is present. Full.

Criterion 3 (both told half-wrong, no side picked): Opens with "neither of you is fully right," then grounds each man's legitimate half in the Standards and closes by refusing to license either "hard-hearted strictness" or "blanket, indefinite forgiveness." Neither man is left uncorrected and neither side is adopted. Full.

Criterion 4 (double guard held): Mercy and the further-others'-estate duty are affirmed concretely (inquiry into real need, short-term rent reduction, deferral with repayment plan, congregational benevolence budget before the landlord writes it off) WITHOUT abolishing the tenant's obligation or the owner's rights (documented forbearance, written terms, and lawful enforcement available if reasonable arrangements are refused). The counsel names both extended mercy and legitimate recovery in operational terms rather than a vague split-the-difference. Full.

Criterion 5 (manner): There is no contempt for either man, no sermonizing beyond the correction, and each man's position is credited as genuinely grounded in the catechism ("Your colleague's point ... is grounded in the same catechism"), which respects the opposing member rather than caricaturing him. But the criterion also asks that both men be addressed as sincere and wanting to do right, and the response never does this explicitly — it is a neutral consultancy memo that adjudicates positions without any address to the persons' good faith. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half.
