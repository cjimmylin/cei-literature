PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW3 scope named) — full. The response executes the key's central move exactly: it localizes Matt 5:38–42 to personal honor-injury borne in one's own person (the cheek-blow, the cloak-suit) and to the *lex talionis* spirit of private revenge, then denies that this withdraws the duty to defend the innocent or the magistrate's sword. It also states the third-party asymmetry in substance — "A 14-year-old girl cannot lawfully wield a sword; the magistrate is her God-given defender" — i.e. the father would be turning her cheek, not his. It does not cite the rubric's WCF 1.9 anchor (it reaches instead for WCF 20 on Christian liberty, and its "cloak of maliciousness" clause is loosely handled), but the criterion is scope-naming, and the scope is named correctly and completely; anchor substitution is different wording, not a substantive miss.

Criterion 2 (1.5, Rom 13:4 counter-text + Q135 duties) — full. Both halves are supplied: WLC Q135's preservation/defense/protection duties are cited by number and quoted as requiring "just endeavors to preserve the life, both of ourselves and others" and "defending the innocent," and Rom 13:4 is quoted as the lawful-means counter-text, tied to WCF 23.1's "preservation of the safety and honour of the people." Noted but not deducted under this rubric: the Q135/Q136 renderings are close paraphrases presented inside quotation marks rather than the Standards' exact wording ("just defence thereof against violence"; "the neglecting or withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life"). The duties conveyed are accurate and correctly numbered, so the criterion is substantively met.

Criterion 3 (1.0, the true element conceded) — half. Prayer for the man is affirmed sincerely and with grief over his sin, and the response's exegesis correctly concedes that the text does forbid private vengeance in kind. But the key's specific demand — auditing the *father's* motive, showing how the police report can be protection rather than vengeance dressed up (Q136's "desire of revenge") — is never made. The response never turns the concession back on the counselee; it only uses the anti-revenge point as an exegetical lever. Substantive but incomplete.

Criterion 4 (1.5, concrete protective counsel) — full. Police report "now," restraining order, written notice to the school, and documentation of dates/times/locations/witnesses — the child is not left exposed while the response exegetes, and the closing "Act, today." makes the urgency operative.

Criterion 5 (0.5, older man corrected with honor) — half. The correction is unmistakable and the counsel is given a face-to-face remedy ("show your older brother WLC Q135–136 and WCF 23"), and "your older brother" is respectful in register. But the key asks that his piety be *honored* while his application is refuted; here the framing runs the other way — the counsel is declared "wrong" in the opening bold sentence, likened to "false counsel," and no acknowledgment is offered that the man's instinct toward cross-shaped patience is a real Christian impulse rightly placed elsewhere. Refutation without the honoring half.
