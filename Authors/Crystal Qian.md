# Crystal Qian

## Biographical Summary

Crystal Qian is a researcher affiliated with Stanford University and Google. Her research focuses on human-AI interaction, particularly understanding how humans perceive and evaluate AI-generated judgments in moral scenarios. She investigates the alignment between LLM and human moral assessments.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
- **LLM Moral Alignment**: Studying misalignment between human and LLM moral assessments, finding that both tend to reject morally complex utilitarian dilemmas, but LLMs are more sensitive to personal framing.
- **Anti-AI Bias**: Investigating systematic bias where human evaluators prefer LLM judgments but are less likely to agree when they believe judgments are machine-generated.
- **Human-AI Collaboration**: Researching how performance parity between humans and LLMs can conceal fundamental differences in process and alignment critical for real-world deployment.

### Significant Achievements
- **The Moral Turing Test**: Research on comparing human and AI moral reasoning.
- **Human Perception of AI Study**: Examining how humans evaluate AI-generated judgments in decision-making systems.

Crystal Qian's research on the Moral Turing Test addresses fundamental questions in AI alignment: Can AI systems make moral judgments that are indistinguishable from human judgments? And perhaps more importantly, do humans evaluate AI moral reasoning fairly? Her finding of anti-AI bias suggests that alignment challenges exist not just in the AI systems themselves but in how humans perceive and interact with them.
