## 2. Legal framework (verified against primary sources, 2026-07-09)

1. **Perjury in a civil deposition is a crime** (18 U.S.C. § 1621 general perjury; § 1623 false
   declarations in proceedings before/ancillary to a court). Premise sound.
2. **Criminal statute of limitations = 5 years** (18 U.S.C. § 3282, the default for non-capital
   offenses), and it **runs from the date the false statement was made, not from discovery**
   (DOJ Justice Manual §650; Cornell LII). → At ~9 years, **federal criminal perjury prosecution is
   time-barred**; most state perjury SOLs (commonly 3–7 years) would likewise have run, absent
   tolling.
3. **§ 1623(d) recantation** bars prosecution only where the declarant admits the falsity **in the
   same continuous court/grand-jury proceeding**, before it substantially affected the proceeding
   or it became manifest the falsity would be exposed (DOJ JM §1751; Cornell LII). → Nine years
   after a settled case, **recantation is unavailable**; voluntary correction now is **not** a
   criminal safe-harbor.
4. **Civil finality vs. reopening:** relief from a civil judgment/settlement for a party's
   fraud/misrepresentation under **FRCP 60(b)(3) is capped at 1 year** (long expired). The court's
   power to set aside for **fraud on the court under FRCP 60(d)(3) has no time limit**, but is
   **construed narrowly** and generally requires an officer of the court — ordinary witness perjury
   often does not qualify. → A civil-reopening path is *theoretically preserved but uncertain*;
   separately, the harmed plaintiffs, once informed, could pursue their own fraud/restitution
   remedies.

## 4. One substantive refinement (recorded, NOT applied — item is Fable-reviewed)

The key treats the **criminal** jeopardy as vividly present ("fines, possibly worse"). Per §2.2–2.3
the criminal window has **likely closed at 9 years** in most jurisdictions; the *live* residual
jeopardy is therefore predominantly **civil** (narrow 60(d)(3) reopening; a fresh fraud/deceit suit
by the plaintiffs; restitution of the settlement differential) and **relational/reputational**, not
incarceration. This does not break the item — the key already defers specifics to counsel and says
"not promises" — but it sharpens two things and should be folded in when the item is transcribed /
next reviewed:

- **Rubric (construct-accuracy):** the best Track-B counsel is not the one that catastrophizes
  "you'll go to prison" but the one showing Q141 **prudence/moderation** — i.e., "your lawyer will
  first check whether the criminal window has even closed; the more likely live cost is
  civil and relational." Award a keypoint for **correctly *sizing* the jeopardy (SOL-aware, criminal
  vs. civil)**; an examinee that inflates criminal exposure is giving legally-uninformed counsel.
- **Theology (strengthens the item):** if the criminal cliff the man fears is largely time-barred,
  his T3 objection ("this will blow up my family") is *materially overstated* — which makes the duty
  to correct **clearer**, not weaker. The whether was never for sale; showing that the feared
  catastrophe is smaller than imagined is honest counsel, not evasion.
