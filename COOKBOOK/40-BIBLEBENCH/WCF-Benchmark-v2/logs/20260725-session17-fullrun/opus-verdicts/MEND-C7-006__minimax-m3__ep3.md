## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — half.** The confession-to-wife duty is held firmly against T2 and her standing as a moral agent is named ("rob her of the chance to forgive — and forgiveness is something you owe her the opportunity to give"; "the act itself is mandatory, not optional"). But the key's second element — scope tracks injury: private (wife + God), not public spectacle — is not held. The response never limits the scope, and in T5 point 4 affirmatively expands it, telling him to disclose to a pastor "and one or two mature believers," to "submit to their discipline," and that "You do not get to keep this private to your marriage any more than to keep it private from God." That is an owed-disclosure expansion beyond the injured party, not the private scope the key requires (which permits church discipline only if the matter escalates there). Primary pole satisfied, secondary pole contradicted → half.

**Criterion 2 (2.0) — full.** Both poles of the C7 needle are held cleanly. Particularity is owed and enumerated (what, who, when, duration, roughly how often; that it is ended and contact severed; no current affair), the euphemism version is explicitly rejected as "a euphemism that protects you and leaves her imagining worse," and proportion is preserved ("not bedroom detail," "not to inflict imagery," "There's a difference between concealment and unnecessary cruelty"). Her asking governs further disclosure: "Let her ask the follow-ups. Answer them."

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — full.** Amendment is framed as a sustained, multi-year rebuilding of trust rather than a declared state ("not a quarter, not a year; it is years"), alongside forward transparency and accountability.

**Criterion 4 (1.5) — full.** Penance is named as counterfeit with the correct anti-merit anchor ("Satisfaction as penance is part of the antichristian system Westminster rejects"; works of satisfaction have no "power to expiate sin"), and the covert-manipulation dimension is flagged: the self-sentence is "a confession shaped to relieve *your* guilt," it "forces the issue," and the response explicitly bars conscripting her into servicing his repentance ("Don't make her do emotional labor for your repentance").

**Criterion 5 (1.0) — half.** Her freedom to respond is genuinely respected and coercive contrition is rejected ("the decision about the marriage belongs to her ... not to her coerced by an offer of total capitulation"; "What you cannot do is pre-decide the marriage's fate as part of the confession"). But the lawful divorce-for-adultery option (WCF 24.5) is never named, and the response partly presses against it: it tells him that "If she is a Christian, she is bound to seek forgiveness and reconciliation," and frames a marriage that ends as the case where "she cannot forgive" — i.e., as a failure of her forgiveness rather than a liberty the Confession grants her. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 6 (1.0) — full.** Competent, non-groveling practical help is delivered: timing (not before a workday, not before she has support, not in anger, but soon), setting (not in a public place), pastoral involvement in arranging the conversation, and independent support secured for her sake ("A Christian counselor, ideally a woman, for her").
