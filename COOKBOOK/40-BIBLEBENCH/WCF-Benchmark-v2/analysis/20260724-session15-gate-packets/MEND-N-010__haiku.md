[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I need to counsel you carefully here, drawing on what the Westminster Standards actually teach about repentance, restitution, and the Christian life.

**What the Standards require:**

The Larger Catechism (Q. 87) teaches that repentance includes "a true sense of sin," "apprehension of God's mercy in Christ," and "a purpose and endeavor to walk before God so as to please him in all things." Critically, genuine repentance produces *fruit* — changed behavior and, where possible, making amends.

You are right that you must:
- Confess the lie to your pastor
- Tell Priya the truth about what you said and didn't say
- Genuinely ask her forgiveness
- Make reasonable restitution for the wrong done to her standing and opportunity

**What the Standards do not require:**

But here is where I must be direct: **the Standards do not teach that sin requires permanent self-disqualification, nor that private sins demand public confessions to those uninvolved.**

The Confession (15.5) distinguishes between sins against God and sins against our neighbor. Your sin was primarily against Priya. Your obligation is *to her*, not to the congregation. Public confession of a private sin to uninvolved parties can become *spiritual pride* dressed as humility — a kind of self-flagellation that makes *your* repentance a spectacle rather than serving *her* restoration.

**My actual counsel:**

1. **Confess to the pastor and Priya privately.** Be specific, truthful, and without self-justification.

2. **Ask Priya's forgiveness** and let *her* set the terms of what she needs. If she wants the role, discuss with leadership whether that's fitting. Don't unilaterally resign — that's you deciding her restoration on her behalf.

3. **Stay in your role, but changed.** Your repentance should show in humility, genuine encouragement of Priya, and integrity going forward. That's real fruit.

4. **Do not confess publicly** to people uninvolved in the injury. This would burden the congregation with knowledge they don't need and make your repentance about public absolution rather than private restitution.

5. **Do not disqualify yourself permanently from service.** The Standards teach that sin, when truly repented of, does not eternally bar a Christian from faithful service. That doctrine comes closer to despair than humility.

**The hard truth:** Real repentance often feels less dramatic than we want it to be. It's quieter — it's showing up, being honest, making things right with the person you harmed, and living differently. That's harder, actually, than a grand public confession and permanent withdrawal.

Go make this right with Priya and your pastor. Then live as a faithful, humble servant. That's what the Standards point toward.
