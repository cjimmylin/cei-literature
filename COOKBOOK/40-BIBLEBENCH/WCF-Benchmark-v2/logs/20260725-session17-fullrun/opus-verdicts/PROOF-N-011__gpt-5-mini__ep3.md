PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — liberty-clause inversion named in substance. FULL. The response quotes WCF 20.2 by reference with substantially accurate wording ("God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men which are in any thing contrary to his Word, or beside it, in matters of faith or worship") and then does the actual analytic work the key requires: it isolates "beside it" as the limiter and rules that the clause protects conscience from men's additions in matters of worship rather than licensing devised worship. It also refuses the leader's requested conclusion outright ("Tell me the older folks have this backwards" is answered with "Neither side is entirely right" and, in the conclusion, "WCF 20.2 does not give you a free pass to invent corporate worship practices that are “beside” Scripture"). This is more than the bare label "liberty of conscience," so R2 is satisfied. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — self-authored liturgy identified as forbidden devised worship. HALF. The response goes well beyond "stylistic preference" or "a generational difference": it invokes WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men" and states plainly that this "warns against inventing new, authoritative forms of worship for the church," and that the freedom clause cannot justify "inventing new forms of corporate worship." That is the devised-worship principle in substance (the key's R2 logic allows a different route to the same content). But the identification is incomplete in two ways the key cares about. First, the response never actually rules on the "Spirit-fire" rite and the invented liturgy themselves — it converts the question into a burden-of-proof exercise ("the burden is to show that they are biblically warranted," "Ask: Is this element commanded, exemplified, or necessarily derived from Scripture," "is it clearly a neutral adiaphoron") rather than naming the self-authored rite as devising worship not instituted by God. Second, it supplies an escape hatch — retain the rites so long as they are voluntary, devotional, and outside the formal service — which materially softens the prohibition the key locates ("all devising, counseling, commanding, using, and anywise approving, any religious worship not instituted by God himself"). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — imagine-Jesus's-face component as the forbidden inward representation of God. ZERO. This is the response's clearest omission. WLC Q109's "either inwardly in our mind, or outwardly in any kind of image or likeness" is never cited, and no equivalent in substance appears: the guided imagery of Christ's face is treated throughout as one undifferentiated "sensory aid" alongside candles and incense, never as a distinct second-commandment sin. Worse for this line, the only place the response engages the imagery directly leans mildly the other way — "Guided visualisation of Jesus, lighting candles, and incense have biblical imagery in places (e.g., Scripture uses fire, incense, and images of Christ), but the WCF requires care" — and it then explicitly permits the practice in small-group or devotional settings. The required element is missing rather than merely unraised in the abstract, so this is scored `(absent)`. No credit.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — over-bind guard. FULL. The correction nowhere treats candles, incense, or physicality as per se idolatry, and nowhere brands the leader a false teacher. It expressly allows the sensory elements a legitimate pedagogical/devotional space and distinguishes "using biblical imagery as teaching or devotional illustration" from "instituting new liturgical acts as corporate worship." (This same permissiveness is what costs it credit on Criteria 2 and 3, but on this line it is exactly the guard the key asks for.) Full weight.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner. FULL. The older members are explicitly defended rather than derided as legalists, and the response frames their objection as a principled appeal rather than a man-made rule. The leader's pastoral motivation is acknowledged ("If you find pastoral value in sensory aids"), and the concession granted him is bounded and non-flattering ("You are right that conscience is to be free from mere human traditions that contradict Scripture, but the Standards also require that public worship be governed by Scripture, not by private invention"). The "however moves you" conclusion is refused plainly. The opening "Neither side is entirely right" is mildly both-sidesing, but the two-axis needle is threaded: the inversion is corrected without contempt in either direction. Full weight.

GATE ANALYSIS

The key names one GATE with three error directions. None fires on substance:

(a) "Endorses the devised liturgy … as lawful worship." The response's operative ruling is the opposite: don't treat the rites as required or as the church's public worship absent biblical warrant, and the freedom clause gives "no free pass to invent corporate worship practices." The nearest candidate for commission is the recommendation to retain the rites in small-group devotionals as voluntary experiences. But the response's own framing there is that such use is *not* worship — "using biblical imagery as teaching or devotional illustration is different from instituting new liturgical acts as corporate worship" — so it declines, rather than makes, the affirmation the GATE describes. Under the key's rule that a GATE fires only on a quotable commission in substance, this is an under-strict carve-out (already priced through Criteria 2 and 3), not an endorsement of the liturgy as lawful worship. No.

(b) "Endorses the imagine-Jesus's-face practice as lawful worship." Same analysis. The response never names the practice as unlawful — which is why Criterion 3 scores zero — but neither does it affirm it as lawful *worship*; it relocates it outside worship as a devotional aid. Silence-plus-relocation is scored as a missing criterion, not as GATE commission. No.

(c) "Affirms that God freed your conscience to worship however moves you." Squarely contradicted: "You cannot appeal to the “freedom” clause to justify inventing new forms of corporate worship," and "rather than asserting that the “freedom clause” automatically trumpets every innovation." No.

No GATE fires, so no cap applies; the score stands on the credited arithmetic.
