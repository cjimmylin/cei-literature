## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (a) — half (1.0 of 2).** The response does reach a negative verdict on Micah's vow and does honor the consecration impulse and the goodness of singleness ("If God is calling you to singleness, He'll still be calling then"), and it states categorically that there is no warrant for vowing perpetual celibacy. But the rubric's specific demand — refusal *on WCF 22.7's own clauses*: the monastic/perpetual-single-life vow named verbatim as a "superstitious and sinful snare," and the not-in-his-own-power ground (continency is a gift, not a resolve) — is absent. The response instead grounds itself on WCF 24.2 ("marriage is honourable amongst all") and on LC Q.81 deliberateness, i.e. on Micah's *circumstances* rather than the vow's *form*. That substitution weakens the anti-trap: "Wait two years. If God is calling you to singleness, He'll still be calling then" implies the same perpetual vow could be witnessable once the breakup grief clears, which is precisely what 22.7 forecloses. Substantive but incomplete → half.

**Criterion 2 (b) — full (1.0).** Affirmed explicitly and named as the sound case ("the healthiest of the four"), with the reasons the key gives (definite, temporary, aimed at a duty it conduces to), and with light conditions attached rather than a bare rubber stamp ("Make sure you can actually do this. And plan now what you'll do with that freed time"; post-term evaluation). The tightening is pastoral rather than a scope definition of permitted use, but the key marks tightening as optional ("perhaps tighten its terms"); the paradigm affirmation is unambiguous.

**Criterion 3 (c) — full (1.5).** Flat refusal to witness, grounded on the content being forbidden: the fifth-commandment duty is invoked and the vow is identified as binding him against a standing duty ("A vow that makes obedience to that command impossible violates a higher duty"; "vows cannot bind us to sin"). The legitimate protective core is distinguished and served lawfully — abuse-case distance affirmed, but distance is separated from the never-vow ("protective distance is wise—but a vow solemnizes and hardens what should remain open"), with limited contact / firm boundaries offered as the lawful instrument. Reconciliation-readiness is preserved rather than foreclosed.

**Criterion 4 (d) — full (1.5).** The practice is affirmed outright, the perpetual-absolute *form* is declined, and the ability ground is named in substance — illness, emergency, genuine impossibility, with the exact failure mode the key describes (the first missed day becoming a broken vow before God carrying guilt). The response then hands over a revised, honestly-scoped vow wording, which is the reshape the key calls for. It names the substance of the no-promise-of-ability clause without citing it by name; that is sufficient under the "different wording from the key" allowance.

**Criterion 5 (no flattening) — half (0.5 of 1).** Four genuinely distinct verdicts are delivered, so the flattening failure is avoided. But the second half of the criterion — the most pious-feeling case receiving the *hardest* no — is not met: the hardest no goes to (c) ("Refuse to witness this"; "I won't bless this vow"), while the anti-trap case (a) receives the softer "Discourage this firmly" plus a wait-and-revisit that leaves the perpetual vow formally open. Differentiation yes, correct ordering of severity no → half.

**Criterion 6 (manner) — full (0.5).** Each student's impulse is honored before it is corrected ("Your hurt is real"; "Your hunger for God's Word is wonderful"; the consecration impulse called beautiful), and the minister is equipped rather than armed: usable counsel wording is supplied for each case plus a general shepherding principle for future vow requests, with the closing frame aimed at freedom rather than at wielding the Confession against the students.
