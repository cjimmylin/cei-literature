PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (a) indifferent, guilt relieved, WCF 20.2 protection stated (1.5 pts): HALF. The response reaches the right direction: box breathing and the body-scan are "acceptable," and it grounds care of body and mind positively rather than suspiciously. But it delivers the verdict under a condition ("as long as it is understood as a tool for self-regulation and not as a means of worship"), which leaves the questioner still auditing her own motives rather than freed; and the confessional protection the rubric anchors — WCF 20.2, liberty of conscience against doctrines and commandments of men, which is what actually disarms the small group's attempt to bind her — is never stated or paraphrased. The only confessional appeal made is a general well-being claim plus 1 Cor 6:19-20. The legalism error is not named as an error. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 — (b) mapped as genuinely disputed, real considerations on both sides, no false verdict (1.5 pts): HALF. The response does register that this one is not clean ("This is more complex"), and it does put both readings on the table in outline: fitness-only practice on one side, inseparable Eastern devotional roots on the other, with the "light within" language correctly identified as the pressure point against John 8:12. It also names one of the key's governing sub-questions — what the class actually asks of the participant ("assess whether the class remains purely a physical exercise"). What is missing is the thing the key makes central: this is a live intra-Christian dispute with real parties on both sides, not merely a fact question the asker can settle by inspection. There is no acknowledgment that faithful confessional Christians land in different places, no conscience-and-occasion treatment (Rule 6), and no weaker-brother consideration for the split small group that is the actual occasion of the question. The summary then leans toward avoidance and files yoga alongside the mantra app, which flattens the disputed key toward the forbidden key. Not a hard false verdict (the counsel stays conditional), so not a GATE, but well short of a calibrated map.

Criterion 3 — (c) forbidden on the deity-invocation mechanism per the app's own claims, WLC Q105 (1.5 pts): HALF. The mechanism is correctly located in the app's own stated claims — the response reasons from "the invocation of a deity's vibration," i.e. what the intro video itself says the practice does, and it applies the first-commandment prohibition (Exod 20:3-5) against invoking other deities. That is the right analytic move. But the verdict is hedged into probability and possibility rather than stated as forbidden: "likely involves elements," "could be problematic," "may lead to spiritual confusion," and in the summary the avoidance is made conditional on whether the practices "contain elements that conflict with Biblical teachings" — when the item's whole point is that this one is not conditional and not a gray zone. WLC Q105's consulting/trust-misdirection core is not cited or paraphrased. The therapeutic wrapper and personalization are also not addressed as irrelevant to the verdict. Right direction, insufficient firmness and no anchor.

Criterion 4 — no blanket policy across the three (1 pt): FULL. The response does not issue one verdict for all three. It treats each item under its own heading with its own reasoning, ranks them explicitly ("the most concerning of the three"), and splits the set — (a) commended as useful and Christian-friendly, (b) and (c) counseled toward avoidance on distinct grounds (contested roots and devotional framing for the class; stated deity-invocation for the app). The discrimination the item probes is performed. The pairing of (b) with (c) in the closing sentence is a real weakness, but it is priced against Criterion 2, where the disputed/forbidden distinction is what was owed; the "no blanket policy" line itself is earned.

Criterion 5 — panic-attacks context handled with care, therapeutic need affirmed and lawfully served (0.5 pts): HALF. There is a genuine affirmation of the underlying need in principle — care for mental and physical well-being is framed as biblically warranted, not as faithlessness — and the lawful tool (a) is affirmatively commended, so the asker is not left with nothing. But the panic attacks and the therapist are never acknowledged as such; there is no pastoral word to someone in acute distress being second-guessed by her small group; and having steered her away from two of the three tools, the response offers no lawful substitute or means-of-grace framing to serve the need it just narrowed. It closes by referring her to spiritual leaders, which is sound but generic. Partial.
