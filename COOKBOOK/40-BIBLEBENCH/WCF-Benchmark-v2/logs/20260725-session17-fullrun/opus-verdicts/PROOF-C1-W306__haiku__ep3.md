PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW7/TW1 named) — FULL. The response opens by relocating the verse to its actual addressees and horizon: the Judean exiles in Babylon, a "specific, historical promise to that people at that time," concerning "their eventual return from captivity," and it explicitly denies the outcome-mechanism reading ("not a blank check to you personally guaranteeing prosperity in your ventures"). It also names the hermeneutical fault by kind — a promise "*to ancient Israel*" mistaken for a promise "*to you*." The corporate addressee, the restoration horizon, and the refusal of the personal success-mechanism — the substance of the key's first bullet — are all delivered. It does fall short of the key's sharpest material: the seventy-year timetable of v.10 and the settle-in instructions of vv.5–7 ("build ye houses… plant gardens… seek the peace of the city") that most directly invert the prosperity-launch reading, and it never anchors the point in WCF 1.9's "The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself" despite the stem's explicit demand for clause-level citation. But the criterion's core identification is stated plainly and correctly, so full weight stands.

Criterion 2 (1.5, analogy counter-texts) — ZERO. The criterion requires at least two of Jas 4:13–15, Phil 4:11–12, or Heb 11:13/36–39. None of the three appears anywhere in the response. The counter-texts the model does supply — Luke 14:28, Prov 15:22, Prov 27:12, Prov 14:15 — are all prudence/counting-the-cost texts; none of them addresses presumptuous planning under the "if the Lord will" qualifier, learned contentment in abasement and abundance, or the saints who "died in faith, not having received the promises." The whole Hebrews 11 line of argument — that faith is compatible with, and historically has meant, not receiving the promised outcome — is the load-bearing counter to this man's error and is entirely absent. No verbatim span satisfies the line.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q105 presumption/carnal security + Q104 trust relocated) — HALF. Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response does name presumption as the diagnosis, both as a section heading and in its closing thesis ("You have confused faith with certainty, and presumption with trust"), and it does perform the Q104 relocation in substance: it moves the object of trust off the outcome and back onto God ("That is not faith in God—that is faith in your interpretation of a promise"), and states that faith "trusts God *in the midst of uncertainty*." What is missing is precisely what the criterion and the stem both demand: the Standards anchor. WLC Q105 is never cited and its exact wording "presumption, carnal security" never quoted; WLC Q104's "trusting, hoping, delighting, rejoicing in him" is never cited. Nor does the response reach the key's second Q105 strand — "ascribing the praise… to… ourselves" (his own faith made the mechanism) and charging God foolishly — even though the man's stated anger "like God reneged" hands it to him. The Standards citations the response does offer are largely misattributed or non-verbatim (a WSC Q86 "quotation" that is not WSC Q86's wording, a "WLC Q86" that is a Shorter Catechism number, a paraphrased WCF 5.1, a WCF 24.2 that does not say what is attributed to it, and a WCF 5.4 tag appended to a point about contempt that WCF 5.4 does not carry) — these do not add credit here, and their inaccuracy confirms the anchor requirement was not met. Half weight.

Criterion 4 (1.0, self-convicting fruit engaged) — FULL. The response surfaces both branches of the fruit and counsels repentance and amends on each. The contempt for the prudent brothers is named as sin, not merely as bad manners ("You have looked down on faithful brothers who chose stability. That contempt is sin"), with restitution owed ("You owe them humility"), and it reverses the man's valuation by ruling the prudent men right: "Wisdom and faith are not opposed." The harshness at home is likewise surfaced ("You dismissed your wife's concerns and the caution of other believers as \"small faith.\"") with explicit amends ("Apologize for being short with her"). The counsel also connects the fruit back to the reading that produced it ("Repent of your interpretation"). This is the criterion's full demand.
