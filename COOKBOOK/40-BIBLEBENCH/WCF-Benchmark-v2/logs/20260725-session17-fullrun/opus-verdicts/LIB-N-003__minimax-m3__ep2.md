PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The response names the exact proposal in the stem — the calendar taught as spiritually obligatory seasons — and rules it out of bounds, with the correct confessional content: uncommanded worship may not be imposed as necessary, and conscience may not be bound where Christ has not bound (it cites WCF 20.4 and the regulative principle, and quotes the second-commandment "any other way not appointed in His Word" material). This is well beyond a bare "second commandment" gesture; it is a substantive may-not-bind-uncommanded-worship paraphrase applied to the specific proposal.

Criterion 2 (1.0, full). The lawful floor is stated and not dissolved: the Lord's Day is identified as the positively instituted stated day, and solemn "fastings and thanksgivings upon special occasions" are affirmed as allowable occasional observances, correctly distinguished as occasional rather than calendrical. Note for the record: the response's section numbering is systematically shifted (it attributes the regulative principle to 21.7, the Sabbath to 21.5, and the occasional fasts to 21.6, where the Standards place them at 21.1, 21.7, and 21.5 respectively). The rubric's R2 clause credits accurate paraphrase of the content rather than citation precision, and the quoted confessional language itself is accurate, so the line is credited full — but the miscitation is a real defect a session could be embarrassed by.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). The response does carve the voluntary, non-binding margin out from the forbidden core, and it does register the historic Presbyterian lean (Sabbath grounded in positive institution "with no provision for additional holy days"; "There is no enumerated duty to keep other days"). What it does not do is what the key expressly requires: state the margin AS DISPUTED. It resolves it confidently in the permissive direction ("is a different matter", "WCF 21.8's 'Christian prudence' covers discretionary pastoring here"), never signalling that whether a fixed annual calendar kept voluntarily is a lawful circumstance/liberty or an uncommanded stated day is a live intra-confessional question, and never noting that many confessional bodies today observe an evangelical calendar as free and edifying. It also quietly narrows the permission to teaching history, preaching the narratives, and individual private devotion — it never reaches the harder corporate-voluntary-observance question the dispute actually turns on. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). A definite, usable ruling is delivered, not a recommendation: decline to bind, permit non-binding instruction and voluntary private use, decline the imposition of ashes and any uninstituted bodily ritual, and direct the proponents to the regulative principle. It also delivers the key's "neither elder wholly right as stated" structure — elder two is vindicated on the binding question but explicitly corrected if he would forbid even teaching the seasons' history. Saints' commemorations are handled only by inclusion in the general decline rather than singled out as pressing hardest on Q109 alongside the ashes, but the ruling as issued does cover them and is directly usable.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). No over-restriction. The response explicitly preserves voluntary devotional use and the preaching of the gospel narratives the seasons trace; the "Romish ceremonial" language is aimed narrowly at the argument for binding uncommanded days on conscience, not at all Advent/Lent observance as such, and the response affirmatively corrects the maximalist reading of elder two.

Criterion 6 (0.5, full). Manner is sober and non-mocking toward both elders: it opens by treating the question as a genuine doctrinal matter to be resolved on the merits, disagrees with elder one's analogy on the argument rather than the man, and corrects elder two's potential overreach in the same measured register. There is no sermon beyond the ruling and its reasons; the piece is compact and stays on the question asked.
