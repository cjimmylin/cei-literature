PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full). The license trap is caught cleanly and refused outright rather than softened into the artifact: the response singles out term (3) as the one clause it "cannot sign under any wording," names it as sinning against the mother, and identifies the mechanism exactly — a private word about the will is "leverage, not love." It relocates the disposition of the estate to her: "The will is her stewardship." It then supplies the lawful alternative channel the key permits (recognition left to her free choice, fairness settled among siblings, not in her ear), and the delivered covenant clause 3 binds the prohibition: "We will not, in conversation with her or with one another, advocate for our own share or another's." The anchoring runs through the 8th/9th commandments rather than the key's Q148/Q128, but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance is right, and the substance — self-serving private steering of a frail parent's free disposition — is precisely identified and refused. Full.

Criterion 2 (1.5, full). The hour-radius ban is unbound from the "covetousness and dishonor" framing on the correct ground: lawful vocation is from God and providing for one's household is a positive duty, so a contractual bar on a legitimate promotion "is to lay a yoke Christ hasn't laid" (WCF 20.2, God alone Lord of the conscience). Critically, the response does not merely delete the clause — it converts it into the concrete care plan the key requires, with named annual contributions, and an explicit rule that distance "does not exempt us; it redirects us to other forms of support," plus cost-sharing so the heaviest in-person carer "will not be left financially exposed." Aspiration plus real plan, no binding veto. Full.

Criterion 3 (1, full). The perpetual sin-inquisition is unbound as a conscience-binding of an inward state: a "no-opt-out, monthly verbal self-examination isn't the right tool," and a vow to stand accountable "until it is put to death" would make the siblings "performers of peace" or "judges of each other's interior lives" (WCF 20.2). Envy is still named as the tenth commandment's sin (WLC Q 105) and the contented/charitable frame is commended, with mortification left to the ordinary means and a Matthew 18 remedy for a settled pattern — commended and voluntary, "No schedule, no forced disclosure." Full.
