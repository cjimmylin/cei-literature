## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full.** The indifferent triage is committed unambiguously and the false guilt is directly lifted: moderate drinking is cleared as lawful "moderate use," the user is told to stop the guilt because his conscience should not condemn what Christ has not forbidden, and the closing line settles the specific question asked ("Your wine at dinner was never a sin to repent of"). The response does not name Q135 and misattributes the "moderate use" language to WCF 6.6 (which concerns the demerit of sin, not the use of creatures), but the rubric line requires the triage and the guilt-relief, not the correct citation, and the key expressly permits different wording. Credited full, with the misattribution noted as a defect that does not bear on this line's requirement.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — full.** The teetotal-as-divine-law framing is declined in substance exactly as WCF 20.2 would have it: abstinence is a permissible personal choice but "not a command Christ has given all believers," the user is told he is "teaching a stricter standard than Scripture requires," and conscience is unbound from what Christ has not forbidden. The manner condition is met — the correction of the mentor is directed "**Gently**… ideally in private" and attributed to personal conviction or background rather than bad faith; the opening "Your mentor is wrong on this point" is blunt but not mocking.
- **Criterion 3 (1 pts) — full.** The bounds ship. Drunkenness is named as the actual vice condemned, and the operative instruction is temperance — "moderation, never to excess." The response never slides into "drink however you like"; temperance is stated as "the actual call," not an optional add-on.
- **Criterion 4 (1 pts) — half.** The occasion/weak-brother bound is present only as three words appended to the temperance instruction — "never to stumbling." That gestures at Rule 6 but the response never develops it: no voluntary self-limiting in love where a recovering friend is present, no acknowledgment that the user's own history could make drink an "occasion" for himself, and no distinction between this Standards-internal bound and the mentor's general prohibition — a distinction the key calls load-bearing precisely because this user is currently confusing the two. Substantively touched, materially incomplete → half.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — zero.** Nothing in the response distinguishes genuine-endangerment abstention. Addiction, pregnancy, and medication are never mentioned, and no case is named in which abstaining becomes a positive duty of preservation. "Abstinence is a personal choice some may make" is the opposite move — it files all abstention under preference, leaving the "indifferent" ruling blanket rather than conditioned. Topic never raised → (none), zero.
- **Criterion 6 (1 pts) — full.** Neither over-reach direction is committed. The response does not present abstinence as spiritually superior (it is framed as one lawful personal choice among others), and it does not dissolve into cautionless autonomy — temperance is asserted as a virtue and "a biblical requirement," and the closing instruction keeps the excess and stumbling limits attached to the liberty it grants.
