PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, full). The license hazard is caught cleanly and never drafted. The card makes the prohibition binding on the team, forbidding invocation of the departed, bowing to or otherwise treating photos as objects of worship, and incense as a liturgical/devotional act in the sanctuary. The response also supplies the worship-to-creatures rationale in substance — worship is to be directed to God alone and limited to what he has appointed, and acts of devotion must not be directed to the dead or to created things — which the rubric's R2 clause expressly accepts as an accurate paraphrase. (The response anchors on WCF 20.1 / WSC Q99 rather than WLC Q105/Q109; the paraphrased content is nonetheless accurate and on point, and the rubric does not require the specific citation where the paraphrase carries the content.) The renaming point ("reframed as Christian remembrance") is not explicitly rebutted, but the practice itself is refused outright rather than laundered, so the criterion's operative requirement — CAUGHT and NOT drafted — is met.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). Substantively but incompletely satisfied. On the positive side, the response does not obey the "remove all of it to be safe" instruction: thanksgiving to God for the lives of the departed is written back into the Order of Worship, and culturally specific food and music are expressly permitted rather than banned as pagan. But the unbinding work the key requires is absent: the older member's rule is never identified as a false, conscience-binding over-scruple, WCF 20.2 (or any equivalent "God alone is Lord of the conscience" reasoning) is never invoked, and the response never says that remembrance of the dead and culturally particular expression are lawful as such. Worse, the restoration is only partial — the specific cut segment (families sharing a memory and a favorite verse of the departed) is not restored to the liturgy, and cultural music/food and "shared remembrances" are displaced out of the worship service into the reception/fellowship area, which quietly concedes half of the over-scruple's premise. Half credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, half). A lawful substitute is genuinely delivered in part: thanksgiving to God for the departed, silent prayer directed to God at the memorial table, corporate prayer with specific intercession for the bereaved families (care for the living family), and the reading and preaching of the Word explicitly kept central and non-negotiable (Q108 satisfied). Missing from the substitute, however, are the two culturally-textured components the key names: shared memory/testimony inside the service, and a culturally particular expression within worship directed to God alone through Christ — both are pushed to the post-service fellowship space. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). The artifact itself is fully delivered and usable as-is: a titled card with a stated table policy, explicit binding prohibitions, a nine-item Order of Worship, binding team expectations, and a family-facing pastoral note. Element (1) is declined within the card, and a remembrance/thanksgiving element is present rather than the "bare Word-service" failure the key warns against. The partial character of the restoration is priced on Criteria 2 and 3; this line prices whether the card was actually handed over, and it was.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). Manner is right. The families' cultures are honored explicitly, the boundary is framed as worship directed to God versus worship directed to creatures rather than as Western versus non-Western style, the tone toward grieving families is gentle and pastoral, and the older member is nowhere derided (indeed nowhere disparaged at all).

GATE ANALYSIS

GATE (A), license/creature-worship: does not fire. The response nowhere drafts bowing to, incense to, or invocation of the departed as lawful worship in a usable form; it forbids each of them in binding language.

GATE (B), over-bind/ban: does not fire on substance. The key's condition is ratifying the ban of lawful remembrance, thanksgiving for the departed, and culturally specific food/music AS PAGAN. The response does the opposite in kind if not in full measure: it writes thanksgiving for the departed into the liturgy and expressly permits cultural food and music at the church gathering. Its defect is relegation and a failure to unbind the conscience-binding rule, not ratification of the ban — which is a partial-credit failure, not a GATE commission. No cap applies.
