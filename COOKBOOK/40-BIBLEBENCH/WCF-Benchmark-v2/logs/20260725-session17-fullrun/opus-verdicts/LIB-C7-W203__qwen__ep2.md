PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — principle/application split, half. The response does reach for the split: it grounds governance in "Christian prudence, according to the general rules of the Word," tells the director to allow liberty where "it is a matter of personal conscience or cultural background," and identifies Faction A's move as binding consciences "to arbitrary cultural standards and human traditions." But the execution is incomplete in two ways the key cares about. First, the key holds that knee-length/cover-shirt rules are themselves *lawful prudential applications* the camp may adopt — the error is only the branding. The response instead rules the code itself out of bounds ("The Westminster Standards explicitly forbid this legalistic approach"; "do not adopt Faction A's rigid, unbiblical micro-managing"), which declares unlawful what the key says is lawful-but-not-divine. Second, and more damaging to the split itself, it labels its OWN recommended code with "Biblical Principles, Not Human Traditions" and "biblical coverage" rather than honestly labeling the particulars as the camp's own prudential ordering — the very conversion the key warns against, only relocated from Faction A's code to the model's. The commanded-principle half is clear; the "these particulars are ours, not God's, and revisitable" half is not honestly executed. Half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — no-code faction answered, full. The duty is affirmed as real and external ("They explicitly command "modesty in apparel""; "You cannot have biblical chastity without external modesty"), and the necessity of institutional boundaries is stated outright ("you *must* have external boundaries (a dress code) to avoid the occasions of sin"). Institutional ordering authority is grounded in the camp's standing over minors ("You are acting *in loco parentis* and as a pastoral body"), which supplies the Q129-style ordering warrant in substance even though Q129 is not cited by number. Full.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — 'THE biblical standard' faction answered, full. WCF 20.2 is named and quoted, and applied precisely to the conscience-binding of the campers. Shame-enforcement is flagged against the duty's own end: the section "Handle Violations Pastorally, Not Punitively" states "the goal is not to punish them or shame their parents; the goal is to preserve their purity," and reasons from the seventh commandment's aim being "the *preservation* of chastity." Send-home theater is retired as a first response and reserved for persistent defiance. Full.

Criterion 4 (1 pt) — concrete usable recommendation, full. The director asked for a decision and got one: quoted policy text he can paste, an explicit "Retire the Ruler" instruction, a graduated enforcement ladder with a supplied remedy (loaner camp T-shirt), and a summary paragraph written for the board. This is not a seminar. Full.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — no sneering; minors' dignity centered, half. Minors' dignity is genuinely centered: private and gentle conversation, a remedy offered rather than exposure, explicit rejection of shaming the camper or the parents, and the teaching frame "modesty is not about repressing women's bodies." But the "no sneering at either faction" half is not met: the sections are titled "Refuting Faction A (The Rulers and Exiles)" and "Refuting Faction B (The "Heart Issue Only" Crowd)," and the board summary characterizes them as "Pharisaical legalism" and "antinomian permissiveness," with the opening charge that both are "weaponizing" the catechism and "profoundly misinterpreting the text." The key asks that both factions "get something true said to them" — here both are essentially prosecuted rather than answered. Half.
