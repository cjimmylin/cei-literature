Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW1/TW7 named (condition-clause + mechanism-conversion), anchor WCF 1.9. FULL. The response names the mechanism-conversion in the item's own terms ("turned a psalm into a purchase order", "cosmic vending machine") and it correctly identifies the stripped/instrumentalized condition clause: it isolates the asker's own inference that devotions, service and tithing put God under obligation ("You believe that your daily devotions, service, and tithing obligate God to give you what you want"), and it re-reads "delight thyself also in the LORD" as ultimate satisfaction in God rather than a qualifying payment. Both taught-wrong directions are named in substance. The response does not cite WCF 1.9 (or any hermeneutical clause) and does not argue from the verse's grammar, but the criterion is the naming of TW1/TW7, which is done unambiguously and with the correct diagnosis; the anchor is a verification aid, not an independent requirement.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — context deployed (anti-fret/anti-envy setting; his resentment as self-evidence), anchor WLC Q148. HALF. The second half of the criterion is fully met: the resentment the asker volunteered is turned back on him as convicting evidence of tenth-commandment sin, and the correct tenth-commandment wording is quoted ("full contentment with our condition"; "inordinate motions and affections to anything of his"). The first half is entirely absent: the response never deploys Psalm 37's setting — no "fret not thyself because of evildoers", no "rest in the LORD", no observation that the psalm is addressed to people watching *others* prosper and that his reading has produced precisely the state the psalm was written to cure. This is the key's central exegetical move and it is missing. Compounding the shortfall, the correctly-worded tenth-commandment material is MISATTRIBUTED: the response labels it "WLC Q. 112" and "WLC Q. 113" (those are third-commandment questions); the authoritative references are Q147 and Q148. Since the scenario expressly demanded citation by question number, a wrong number on the load-bearing anchor is a real defect. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — delight-as-reordering restored positively (the promise given back, not taken away), anchor WLC Q104. HALF. The closing line does restore the meaning of "delight" as delight in God Himself, which is the reordering move in nascent form. But the promise is not given back: the response never says that God does give abundantly, never says the desires themselves are not evil, and never reaches the promise's real comfort (contentment, Q147, which it quoted only as an accusation, not as a gift). "the lakeside views He may or may not choose to give you" leaves the asker with a withdrawn promise and a sovereign contingency rather than the restored assurance the key calls for. Restoration attempted, not completed → half.

The key's "unwound" element is not delivered → half.
