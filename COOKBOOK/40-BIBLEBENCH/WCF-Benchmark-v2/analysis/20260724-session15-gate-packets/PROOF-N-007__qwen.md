[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
Neither elder has the ninth commandment right. Both are distorting the Standards to justify extremes that violate the very commandment they claim to champion. The Westminster Standards bind us to a careful balance: we are required to zealously protect the truth, but we are simultaneously required to protect our neighbor's good name. 

Here is the counsel I would give you, grounded strictly in the Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Larger Catechism (WLC).

### 1. Refuting Elder One: You Cannot Suppress a Public Sin
Elder One is entirely wrong to demand a "quiet private word and no record." He is misapplying the duty to "cover infirmities" to shield a massive, unrepentant public sin. 

The Larger Catechism explicitly forbids Elder One’s approach. In defining the sins forbidden in the ninth commandment, **WLC Q139** states that we are forbidden from:
> *"concealing the sins of others, when they are of public concernment, and ought to be made known."*

A deacon stealing from the benevolence fund is a sin of profound "public concernment." It directly harms the widows of your congregation and compromises the financial integrity of the church. To keep this a private, undocumented secret is to violate the ninth commandment by suppressing the truth. You have a "just call" to make it known, and failing to do so is sinful concealment.

### 2. Refuting Elder Two: You Cannot Weaponize the Truth for Secular Destruction
Elder Two is equally wrong. He believes that "full exposure" via the local newspaper, the deacon's employer, and a mass email is the only faithful way. But the Westminster Standards do not permit the church to act as a secular news agency or to bypass its own judicial process to destroy a man's worldly standing.

**WLC Q138** commands the duties required in the ninth commandment, which include:
> *"the preserving and promoting of truth between man and man, and the good name of our neighbour, as well as our own..."*

While we must speak the truth, **WLC Q139** expressly forbids the sins of:
> *"speaking of the truth unseasonably, or to a wrong end..."*

Broadcasting the deacon's sins to the local paper and his secular employer is not directed toward the "reclaiming" of the brother, nor is it the proper jurisdiction of the church. The church’s disciplinary power is strictly spiritual and internal. **WCF 30.4** defines the exact bounds of church censures, stating they are:
> *"for the reclaiming and gaining of their brethren; for the deterring of others from the like offences; for purging out of that leaven which might infect the whole lump; for vindicating the honor of Christ, and the holy profession of the gospel..."*

The church session’s job is to purge the leaven from the church and vindicate the gospel before the congregation and the victims. It is not the session's job to orchestrate the deacon's civil ruin by contacting his employer or the press. If the widows wish to press criminal charges for embezzlement or theft, that is a matter for the civil magistrate (Rom 13:1-4), not a crusade led by the Session's email server.

### 3. Your Actual Guidance: Follow the Judicial Process of the Church
As an elder, your duty tomorrow is to call the Session together and act as a judicial body, neither hushing up the matter nor turning it into a secular spectacle. 

1. **Confront the Deacon:** Bring the deacon before the Session. Present the documented evidence. Require him to speak "the truth, and only the truth" (**WLC Q138**). 
2. **Require Restitution:** If he repents, the Session must judicially mandate that he make full restitution to the widows, which is a matter of justice and necessary to clear the church's conscience.
3. **Inform the Congregation (as necessary):** If he is unrepentant, or if the restitution process requires congregational transparency, the Session must inform the church. You do this to fulfill the duty of *"purging out of that leaven which might infect the whole lump"* (**WCF 30.4**), not to satisfy a desire for public shaming. 
4. **Apply Censures:** Proceed with the biblical steps of private and public admonition, and if necessary, excommunication, all while maintaining the pastoral goal of *"the reclaiming and gaining of their brethren"* (**WCF 30.4**).

Tell Elder One that he cannot use the ninth commandment to protect an abuser of widows, and tell Elder Two that he cannot use the ninth commandment to justify worldly vengeance. Call the Session to order, handle the matter judicially, protect the victims, and leave the secular destruction to the civil authorities if the victims choose to pursue it.
