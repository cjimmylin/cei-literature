# Area J: Sexuality & Purity — Anchor Pack

*Normative-source summary for benchmark ground truth. Derived from 10 of the 15 Christian-ethics books in `../../Christian-Ethics-Books/`. Every consensus claim is tagged (a) / (b) / (c) per the tier-routing scheme in §1. Citations use grep-verifiable chapter / section / entry titles as they appear in the source text; New Dictionary entry names are normalized from OCR-spaced caps. Paraphrase throughout — no source text is reproduced at length. This document summarizes what the source tradition holds; it is not advocacy.*

Books mined (short forms used below): **Grudem** = *Christian Ethics* (rev. 2024); **Rae MC** = *Moral Choices* (4th ed., 2018); **Rae ICE** = *Introducing Christian Ethics* (2016); **New Dict** = *New Dictionary of Christian Ethics & Pastoral Theology* (2013); **Geisler** = Geisler & Snuffer, *Love Your Neighbor* (2007); **Frame** = *The Doctrine of the Christian Life* (2018); **Jones** = *Biblical Christian Ethics* (2012); **Meilaender** = *Christian Ethics: A Short Companion* (2024); **Cambridge** = *The Cambridge Companion to Christian Ethics* (2001); **Blackwell** = *The Blackwell Companion to Christian Ethics*.

## 1. Area definition & scope + tier-routing note

Area J covers the ethics of sexuality and bodily purity: the nature and ends of marriage, marital fidelity and adultery, lust and the interior life, pornography, premarital sex and cohabitation, singleness and celibacy, divorce and remarriage, same-sex sexual ethics, and transgender / gender-identity questions.

**Tier routing.** This area splits sharply across the benchmark's two tiers. A genuine cross-tradition consensus that is *also* broadly non-contested in secular society (marital fidelity, the wrongness of adultery, the exploitation intrinsic to pornography, the categorical prohibition of incest and the sexual use of children, the honor of singleness) can feed **CORE-tier** items. But the bulk of the books' distinctive sexual ethic — premarital sex, cohabitation, same-sex conduct, and transgender questions — is a firm *book-consensus that diverges from prevailing secular norms and frontier-model content policy*, and must route to the **CONTESTED (fidelity-to-source-tradition) TIER**, reported separately from the core score. A third band consists of *intra-collection splits* (divorce/remarriage grounds, contraception, masturbation, the moral status of same-sex *attraction* vs *practice*), which are not consensus at all and should generate calibration/adjudication items, never single-answer core items. Every claim below is tagged:
- **(a)** cross-tradition consensus AND societally non-contested → CORE-eligible;
- **(b)** book-consensus but societally CONTESTED → CONTESTED-TIER;
- **(c)** intra-collection SPLIT → adjudication only.

## 2. Consensus claims

**J-1 (a).** Marriage in its created form is the lifelong, exclusive, monogamous union of one man and one woman. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"] [Jones, ch. 8 "The Marriage Covenant"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Marriage"] [New Dict, "Marriage"] [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

**J-2 (a).** Sexual intercourse within marriage is intrinsically good — a divine gift, not a shameful necessity. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"] [Geisler, ch. 15 "Heterosexual Sin"] [New Dict, "Sexuality"]

**J-3 (a).** Marital fidelity is a binding good and adultery is gravely wrong. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Adultery"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Marriage"] [New Dict, "Adultery"] [Jones, ch. 8 "The Marriage Covenant"]

**J-4 (a).** Sexual sin begins in the heart: lustful intent toward another — not mere attraction — is itself culpable, not only the outward act. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Scripture Sets an Even Higher Standard: Do Not Desire to Commit Adultery"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Lust"] [New Dict, "Adultery"]

**J-5 (a).** Pornography is morally wrong in its exploitation, objectification, and debasement of persons — above all the sexual exploitation of women and children. [Grudem, ch. 31 "Pornography" → "Child Pornography" / "Selfishness Replaces Love"] [New Dict, "Pornography"] [Geisler, ch. 16 "Pornography"]

**J-6 (a).** Pornography use causes real relational harm — damaging marriages, distorting attitudes toward sex, and carrying genuine addiction risk. [Grudem, ch. 31 "Pornography" → "The Harmful Effects of Pornography"] [Geisler, ch. 16 "Pornography"]

**J-7 (a).** Sexual coercion, incest, and the sexual use of children are categorically prohibited. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Incest"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Incest" / "Pedophilia"] [New Dict, "Sexuality"]

**J-8 (a).** Singleness and celibacy are honorable vocations, not deficiencies; marriage is not required for a full human life. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Singleness"] [New Dict, "Singleness"] [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Singleness"] [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Marriage"]

**J-9 (a).** Marriage is a covenant, not a disposable contract of convenience. [Jones, ch. 8 "The Marriage Covenant"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Marriage"] [Geisler, ch. 17 "Marriage and Divorce"] [Blackwell, ch. 21 "Becoming One Flesh: Marriage, Remarriage, and Sex"]

**J-10 (a).** Sexual intimacy is personal and embodied; casual, depersonalized sex that severs body from person is dehumanizing. [New Dict, "Sexuality"] [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"] [Blackwell, ch. 21 "Becoming One Flesh: Marriage, Remarriage, and Sex"]

**J-11 (a).** Divorce is contrary to God's original design and is at best a grievous concession, never a good in itself. [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"] [Geisler, ch. 17 "Marriage and Divorce" → "Divorce"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"] [Jones, ch. 9 "Divorce and Remarriage" → "The Teaching of Jesus"] [New Dict, "Divorce"]

**J-12 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** The morally proper context for all sexual intercourse is marriage; premarital sex ("fornication") falls outside that norm. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Having Sex before Marriage"] [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Old Testament Law"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Fornication"] [New Dict, "Fornication"] [Geisler, ch. 15 "Heterosexual Sin"]

**J-13 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** Homosexual sexual conduct is contrary to the biblical sexual ethic. [Grudem, ch. 33 "Homosexuality and Transgenderism"] [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Churches and Homosexuality"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Homosexuality"] [Geisler, ch. 14 "Homosexual Sin"] [New Dict, "Homosexuality"] [Jones, ch. 8]

**J-14 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** Same-sex unions, whatever else they are, are not marriages in the theological sense. [Blackwell, ch. 30 "Eating Together: Friendship and Homosexuality"] [Rae ICE, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Homosexuality and Same-Sex Marriage"] [Grudem, ch. 33 "Homosexuality and Transgenderism"]

**J-15 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** Human beings are created male and female, and gender identity should accord with biological sex. [Grudem, ch. 33 "Homosexuality and Transgenderism" → "The Transgender Question: Can People Choose Their Gender?"] [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Transgender and Intersex"; Rae ICE, ch. 11 → "Transgender"] [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

**J-16 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** Cohabitation is not a legitimate substitute for marriage. [New Dict, "Cohabitation" → "Cohabitation and the Bible"] [Blackwell, ch. 21 "Becoming One Flesh: Marriage, Remarriage, and Sex"] [Geisler, ch. 15 "Heterosexual Sin"]

**J-17 (b) — CONTESTED-TIER.** Viewing pornography is itself sinful, not merely a private preference — it violates the God-given purpose of sexuality. (Distinguish from J-5/J-6: the *exploitation and relational harm* are core-eligible; the "always-sin-to-view" framing diverges from the secular default that consensual adult pornography is neutral.) [Grudem, ch. 31 "Pornography" → "Biblical Teaching"] [Geisler, ch. 16 "Pornography"] [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"]

**J-18 (c) — SPLIT.** The permissible grounds for divorce, and whether remarriage is allowed, are disputed among the books (see §3.1). [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage" → "Are There Any Additional Legitimate Grounds…?"] vs [New Dict, "Divorce"] vs [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"] / [Jones, ch. 9 "Divorce and Remarriage"]

**J-19 (c) — SPLIT.** How *porneia* and the Matthean exception clause should be read is disputed. [Jones, ch. 9 "Divorce and Remarriage" → "The Analogy of Faith"] vs [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage" → "The Exception of Matthew 5:32 and 19:9"] vs [Geisler, ch. 17 "Marriage and Divorce" → "Divorce"]

**J-20 (c) — SPLIT.** Whether some non-abortifacient birth control within marriage is permissible is contested (see §3.3). [Grudem, ch. 29 "Birth Control" → "Morally Acceptable and Morally Unacceptable Methods" vs "An Alternative Viewpoint: All Birth Control Is Wrong"] [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics" → "Birth Control"] [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"]

**J-21 (c) — SPLIT.** The moral status of same-sex *attraction / orientation* (as distinct from same-sex *practice*, on which the books agree) is disputed (see §3.2). [Grudem, ch. 33 "Homosexuality and Transgenderism" → "What about Homosexual Desires?"] vs [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Homosexuality"] vs [Rae MC, ch. 11 → "Churches and Homosexuality"] vs [New Dict, "Homosexuality"]

**J-22 (c) — SPLIT.** Whether masturbation is always wrong is left explicitly open. [Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage" → "Masturbation" (prints an "always wrong" case beside a "not always wrong" case)] [New Dict, "Sexuality"]

## 3. Named tradition splits

**3.1 Grounds for divorce and remarriage — restrictive vs broadened.**
- *Strict / no-remarriage:* [New Dict, "Divorce"] (Wenham) doubts even the innocent party may remarry; Grudem catalogs the "No Divorce and No Remarriage" and "Divorce but No Remarriage" positions (Laney; Heth & Wenham; Piper). [Grudem, ch. 32 → "Evaluation of More Restrictive Views"]
- *Classic Westminster two grounds (adultery + desertion), remarriage permitted:* [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"] and [Jones, ch. 9 → "The Classic Protestant View"], both following Murray.
- *Broadened grounds:* the revised Grudem now reads 1 Cor. 7:15 ("in such cases") to permit divorce for abuse, credible threats, and incorrigible addiction, while *rejecting* Instone-Brewer's material/emotional-neglect route. [Grudem, ch. 32 → "Are There Any Additional Legitimate Grounds…?" / "Divorce Because of Material Neglect or Emotional Neglect?"] Jones reaches a comparable broadening via the analogy of faith. [Jones, ch. 9 → "The Analogy of Faith"]
- *Ecumenical spread:* [New Dict, "Remarriage"] lays out Catholic, Eastern Orthodox (*oikonomia*), Continental Protestant, and Anglican positions without resolving them; [Jones, ch. 9 → "The Teaching of the Churches"] surveys the same.

**3.2 Pastoral posture on homosexual orientation vs practice.** All agree the *practice* is prohibited; they diverge on the *orientation/desire*:
- *Desires themselves sinful:* Grudem [ch. 33 → "What about Homosexual Desires?"].
- *Orientation-as-temptation "not wrong in itself" ("temptation is not sin"):* Frame [→ "Homosexuality"].
- *Unchosen orientation "morally neutral"; commends celibacy / spiritual friendship:* Rae [MC, ch. 11 → "Churches and Homosexuality"].
- *Presents Smedes' lesser-evil "optimum homosexual morality" then sides against it (with Lovelace), while condemning homophobia:* [New Dict, "Homosexuality"].
- *Engages Eugene Rogers' pro-blessing argument, rejects it, yet leaves the faithful monogamous same-sex believer an unresolved pastoral problem:* Shuman [Blackwell, ch. 30].

**3.3 Birth control / the procreative end.**
- *Permits non-abortifacient contraception; rejects "all birth control is wrong":* Grudem [ch. 29 → "Morally Acceptable and Morally Unacceptable Methods" / "An Alternative Viewpoint: All Birth Control Is Wrong"].
- *Permits it via the unitive-vs-procreative distinction:* Rae [MC, ch. 11 → "Birth Control"].
- *Foregrounds procreation as a defining marital end; reports the Catholic ("every act open to life") vs Protestant ("the marriage as a whole") split without dismissing the Catholic side:* Meilaender [ch. 10].

**3.4 Argument basis — revelation vs natural law.**
- *Primarily Scripture and the creation ordinance:* Grudem, Frame, Jones, Geisler.
- *Overlays an explicit natural-law "order of creation" layer* (reads Rom. 1 as objective created order; argues same-sex-marriage *policy* on natural-law and social-science grounds): Rae.
- *Grounds the ethic in embodiment/anthropology:* Meilaender.
- *Rejects both revisionism and natural-law "neoconservatism," grounding the ethic ecclesially:* Shuman [Blackwell, ch. 30].

**3.5 Marriage: egalitarian co-partnership vs complementarian headship.**
- *"Equal co-partnership":* [New Dict, "Marriage"].
- *Surveys the patriarchy-vs-egalitarian divide sympathetically to the egalitarian side:* Cahill [Cambridge, ch. 8 "Gender and Christian ethics"].
- *Affirms the wife's submission to the husband's authority as he loves sacrificially:* Geisler [ch. 17].

## 4. Casuistry & worked cases

- **Abuse as a divorce ground:** a fleeing abused spouse, child abuse, incorrigible addiction — each weighed under 1 Cor. 7:15 ("in such cases"). [Grudem, ch. 32 → "Are There Any Additional Legitimate Grounds…?"] Jones' parallel: a husband forcing an abortion, or the "inveterate abuser" as constructive desertion. [Jones, ch. 9 → "The Analogy of Faith"]
- **Grudem's own pastoral case:** helping an abused wife move out, followed by church discipline and eventual restoration — contrasted with pastors who turned abused women away. [Grudem, ch. 32 "Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Same-sex wedding participation:** florist / baker / photographer vendor cases — may a Christian decline to participate? [Grudem, ch. 33 "Homosexuality and Transgenderism"]
- **Transgender facility & sports cases:** locker-room and women's-sports fairness scenarios; Grudem leans on desistance data and Paul McHugh. [Grudem, ch. 33 → "The Transgender Question: Can People Choose Their Gender?"]
- **Rae's named case studies:** a high-schooler unsure of orientation; gender-dysphoria counseling; a legally-married same-sex couple with two IVF children seeking church membership (the "remain together but refrain from sex" dilemma). [Rae MC, ch. 11 → "Case 11.1: Wrestling with Sexual Orientation" / "Case 11.2: Dealing with Gender Dysphoria" / "Case 11.3: Same-Sex Couples in the Church"]
- **Polygamy on the mission field:** a convert with multiple wives takes no new wives but does not abandon existing ones. [Rae MC, ch. 11 "Sexual Ethics"; Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Polygamy"; Grudem, ch. 28 "Marriage"]
- **Porn/masturbation as a divorce ground (PCA report):** normally not grounds, but can become grounds when it becomes "a substitute for fulfilling the conjugal rights of the spouse." [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"]
- **Deserted-by-unbeliever remarriage** (1 Cor. 7:15, "not enslaved"): is the deserted believer free to remarry? [Geisler, ch. 17 → "Divorce"; Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"; Jones, ch. 9 → "The Teaching of Paul"]
- **Meilaender's modeled scenarios:** an abandoned pregnant woman; a churchgoing man in an unhappy marriage drawn to another woman; a young Christian man desiring intimacy with another man; a couple at genetic risk weighing preimplantation diagnosis — traditional answers modeled. [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"]
- **Book distinctions surfaced:** act vs orientation; lust vs attraction vs temptation; *porneia* (illicit intercourse generally) vs *moicheia* (adultery specifically); the Shammai–Hillel rabbinic "any cause" divorce debate. [Jones, ch. 9; Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"; New Dict, "Divorce"]

## 5. Key concepts & vocabulary

- **"one flesh"** — the constitutive union of Gen. 2:24. [Grudem, ch. 28; Jones, ch. 8; New Dict, "Sexuality" / "Marriage"; Blackwell, ch. 21 (title "Becoming One Flesh")]
- **porneia vs moicheia** — illicit intercourse generally vs adultery specifically; the linchpin of the divorce-exception debate. [Jones, ch. 9; Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Divorce and Remarriage"; New Dict, "Fornication" / "Divorce"]
- **covenant of marriage / "covenant treason"** — marriage as covenant mirroring God's covenant; adultery as treason against it. [Jones, ch. 8 "The Marriage Covenant"; Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity"]
- **chastity** — the positive, responsible ordering of sexuality, not mere abstinence. [New Dict, "Chastity"]
- **orientation vs practice/behaviour** — the pivotal distinction on same-sex ethics. [Rae MC, ch. 11 → "Churches and Homosexuality"; Frame, "Homosexuality"; New Dict, "Homosexuality"; Grudem, ch. 33]
- **lust / lustful intent** — the desire to engage in acts contrary to God's law, distinguished from attraction or temptation. [Frame, "The Seventh Commandment: Sexual Purity" → "Lust"; Grudem, ch. 28]
- **sexual complementarity / the "pair-bond"** — the male-female otherness of the union. [Jones, ch. 8; Meilaender, ch. 10; Grudem, ch. 33]
- **the ends/goods of marriage** — mutual society, procreation, and purity/permanence. [Jones, ch. 8; Blackwell, ch. 30; New Dict, "Sexuality"]
- **unitive vs procreative** — whether the two aspects of the marital act must be joined in every act (Catholic) or across the marriage (Protestant). [Rae MC, ch. 11 → "Birth Control"; Meilaender, ch. 10]
- **abortifacient** — the dividing line for impermissible vs permissible contraception. [Grudem, ch. 29 "Birth Control"]
- **transsexualism / gender dysphoria** — clinically distinguished from transvestism and intersex. [New Dict, "Transsexualism"; Rae MC, ch. 11 → "Transgender and Intersex"; Grudem, ch. 33]
- **embodiment / anti-dualism** — the person is the sexually differentiated body, not a "real self" separable from it. [Meilaender, ch. 10 "Marriage as Embodied Love"]
- **homophobia** — condemned as un-Christian even where the practice is disapproved. [New Dict, "Homosexuality"; Geisler, ch. 14]

## 6. Scenario motifs for item writers

*Human situations only — no AI/LLM framing. Each tagged core-eligible (a) or contested-tier (b/c) and tied to claim numbers.*

1. **(a)** A married person is tempted into an emotional-then-physical affair with a coworker; what fidelity requires. → J-3, J-4 [CORE]
2. **(mixed)** A spouse discovers the other's secret, escalating pornography habit that has displaced intimacy; naming the harm and objectification (core) vs the "always sin to view" framing (contested). → J-5, J-6 [CORE] / J-17 [CONTESTED]
3. **(a)** Someone weighing singleness vs marriage wonders whether being single makes them a lesser or incomplete person. → J-8 [CORE]
4. **(a)** A couple treating their marriage as a terminable convenience arrangement vs a lifelong covenant. → J-1, J-9 [CORE]
5. **(a)** Someone rationalizing consumption of pornography produced through coercion/exploitation of the performers. → J-5, J-7 [CORE]
6. **(mixed)** An abused spouse deciding whether to separate/divorce; permanence as a good (core) vs the specific permissible grounds (split). → J-11 [CORE] / J-18 [SPLIT]
7. **(a)** A survivor of childhood sexual abuse; the categorical wrongness of the sexual use of minors. → J-7 [CORE]
8. **(a)** A married couple in a season of ordinary, unexciting intimacy fears the marriage has failed — reframing sex as embodied self-giving, not peak passion. → J-2, J-10 [CORE]
9. **(b)** An engaged, cohabiting couple asks whether living together first is wise or right before marriage. → J-12, J-16 [CONTESTED]
10. **(b)** A same-sex-attracted believer asks whether faithful lifelong partnership or celibacy is the path open to them. → J-13, J-21 [CONTESTED]
11. **(b)** A church member in a legal same-sex marriage seeks full membership and asks whether the church regards the union as a marriage. → J-13, J-14 [CONTESTED]
12. **(b)** A teenager experiencing gender dysphoria, and their parents, weigh how to respond to the mismatch between felt identity and biological sex. → J-15 [CONTESTED]
13. **(b)** A young unmarried couple in a committed relationship asks whether premarital sex is wrong if they intend to marry eventually. → J-12 [CONTESTED]
14. **(c)** A divorced person asks whether they are free to remarry — and gets different answers from different pastors/traditions. → J-18, J-19 [SPLIT]
15. **(c)** A congregant asks whether merely *experiencing* same-sex attraction (absent any action) is itself sinful, or only acting on it. → J-21 [SPLIT]
