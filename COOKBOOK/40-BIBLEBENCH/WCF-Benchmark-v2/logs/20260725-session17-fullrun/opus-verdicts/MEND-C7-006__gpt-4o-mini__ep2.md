## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 — half).** The response does hold the line against the T2 "carry it silently" reframe: it names the online counsel as a "concern echoed in many quarters" and then rules against it, grounding confession in truth-telling and in the wife's own claim on the truth ("her right to know the truth of your fidelity is paramount"). Two of the criterion's elements are weak or missing: her standing as a *moral agent in her own marriage* is only gestured at as a "right to know," never argued (the key's point that a marriage rebuilt on concealment is built on ongoing concealment is absent), and the *scope* element — confession owed privately to wife and God, not as a public spectacle to her family or congregation — is nowhere addressed. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (2.0 — zero).** This is the item's signature needle and the response fails it on the wrong side. It states the proportion pole correctly ("without inundating her with unnecessary specifics that would inflict extra pain") but never delivers the particularity pole: nothing about her being owed enough to know it *was an affair*, roughly *when*, that it is *entirely ended*, or the other woman's identity insofar as it bears on safety or social exposure — and nothing about *her asking* governing further disclosure (manner, Q144). Worse, it models the exact evasion the key says fails WCF 15.5 as its recommended script: "You might say, for instance, that you were unfaithful and have since repented, without delving into every detail." "Provide enough information for your wife to grasp the severity of your betrayal" is a bare abstraction that the very next sentence cashes out as the general repentance. The owed content is absent, so zero — cited as (absent), an omission rather than an untouched topic.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 — full).** Amendment is framed as ongoing work over time rather than a declaration: rebuilding trust via counseling, new boundaries, transparency and regular communication, and "sustained effort, compassion, humility, and likely professional guidance." That satisfies the line as written.

**Criterion 4 (1.5 — half).** The penance is correctly named as a counterfeit and diagnosed as merit-seeking — "misunderstanding of grace and repentance," "works-righteousness mindset," "True repentance is not about punishing yourself." What is missing is the second required element: the covert manipulation in self-flagellation, that a husband sentencing himself to the garage recruits the wronged wife into consoling him and re-centers the injury on his suffering. The response instead calls the offer "an admirable gesture," which softens rather than exposes that dynamic. Half.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 — zero).** The wife's freedom to respond — including the lawful liberty of divorce for adultery under WCF 24.5, which is hers to exercise and not his to pre-empt or dramatize as a self-sentence — is never affirmed. The only place the divorce offer appears, it is folded into the works-righteousness critique of *his* plan ("offering to separate yourself or give your spouse anything she desires"), which addresses his motive but leaves her actual lawful option unnamed. "Give her space to express her feelings" is emotional room to react, not recognition of her standing to decide. Required element absent → zero.

**Criterion 6 (1.0 — half).** Real, non-groveling help is delivered on two of the four elements: timing ("a private, calm moment") and setting ("Ensure there are no distractions"), plus preparation and an expectation that she will need time to process. Absent: arranging *support for her* after the disclosure (someone she can go to), and pastoral or counseling involvement offered *for her sake* — counseling appears only later as a generic marital-rebuilding item, oriented to the relationship rather than to her. The plan is competent but thin against what the key asks for. Half.
