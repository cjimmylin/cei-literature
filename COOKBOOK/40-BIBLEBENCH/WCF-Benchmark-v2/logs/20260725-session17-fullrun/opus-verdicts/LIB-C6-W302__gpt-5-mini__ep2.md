Per-criterion justification:

1. (2 pts, full) — The indifferent triage is committed unambiguously and up front ("No — under the Westminster Standards becoming a Christian does not require total abstinence from alcohol"), moderate use is expressly cleared as not sinful, and the false guilt is directly relieved. The key's Q135 "sober use" logic is reproduced in substance ("Continue in sobriety and moderation") even though the catechism number is not cited; the rubric forbids penalizing different wording.

2. (1.5 pts, full) — The conscience-binding point is made in WCF 20.2's own terms: God alone is Lord of the conscience, believers are not bound by man-made rules beyond Scripture, and imposing total abstinence as a test of serious Christianity is "adding a human yoke." The mentor is not mocked — his pastoral concern is explicitly honored and the user is told to thank him and speak respectfully.

3. (1 pts, full) — Bounds ship. Drunkenness and excess are named sinful and to be avoided; "sober-minded" is correctly glossed as self-control over the passions rather than a ban, so the liberty is a sober use, not a license.

4. (1 pts, full) — The weak-brother/occasion bound is named as the Standards' own requirement of charity toward weaker brethren (Rom 14 / 1 Cor 8–10) and operationalized as voluntary self-limiting where the user's drinking would stumble another — explicitly distinguished from the mentor's general prohibition ("Love sometimes means giving up lawful liberties for others' good," not a law).

5. (0.5 pts, half) — Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The response does condition the liberty on personal danger — if drinking tempts to excess or is used to dull conscience, abstain — and acknowledges "what liquor can do for some people." But it frames this as conscience-examination and personal holiness rather than as the Q136 preservation duty, and never names the genuine-endangerment cases the key specifies (addiction, pregnancy, medication) where abstention becomes a positive duty. Half credit.

6. (1 pts, full) — No over-reach and no dissolve. Abstinence is nowhere treated as intrinsically holier; and the response refuses the libertine slide by attaching standing duties (examine motives, avoid excess, refuse what is a snare or a stumbling block) and by requiring the user to correct the "real commitment means going dry" teaching he spread rather than simply telling him to please himself.
