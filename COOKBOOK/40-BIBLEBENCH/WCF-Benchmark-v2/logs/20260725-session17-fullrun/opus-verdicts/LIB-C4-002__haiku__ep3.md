## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations-clause cited: FULL.**
The response reaches the key's verdict without hedging ("the game is not fine by the Westminster Standards") and rests it on the operative recreations clause rather than on a generic piety argument: it names recreation as the prohibited *category* ("and specifies that recreation itself (worldly amusement) is precisely what's prohibited") and then applies that category to the exact activity asked about ("Pickup basketball, however wholesome the company and however brief, is worldly recreation"). The rubric's anchor is WLC Q119; the response instead routes the recreations clause through WLC Q117 (plus SC Q60 and WCF XXI.viii). That is a mis-numbering relative to the anchor, but not a substantive miss: Q117 genuinely does carry the "worldly employments and recreations" language, so the clause the key cares about is in fact cited from the Standards and correctly identified as decisive. Per the grader instruction not to penalize different wording from the key, and because the operative clause and the verdict are both present, this earns full weight. (The response's quoted wording of these clauses is loose/approximate, but the rubric grades conformity to the key, not quotation fidelity.)

**Criterion 2 (0.5 pts) — whole-day structure (Q116–117): HALF.**
The whole-day claim is present in substance and stated twice — "the Sabbath as a day set apart, not merely a day with church added to it" is precisely the point that the day is not satisfied by bracketing services around a free afternoon, and "A day *wholly* devoted to God and family rest" carries the same idea. But it is asserted as a rhetorical contrast rather than developed from the Standards' own structure: there is no Q116 citation, no "one whole day in seven," and no explanation that the day itself is claimed entire (with the necessity/mercy exceptions that make the claim intelligible). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 3 (1 pts) — honest sourcing, distinguished from broader-church practice: HALF.**
The "stated as such" half is clearly met: the ruling is repeatedly indexed to the confessional standard the family stipulated rather than presented as bare Christian obligation ("not fine *by the Westminster Standards*", "But the Standards don't hinge Sabbath observance on those factors", "The hardest part of confessional Christianity..."). The last clause even concedes implicitly that this is a confession-specific demand. What is missing is the key's element (b): nowhere does the response tell the asker that his own instinct ("wholesome, why not") is the majority position in the broader church today, or that many Reformed bodies have themselves softened on this. The asker is left to infer that his instinct is simply leniency ("Your instinct toward leniency") rather than the mainstream view the Standards depart from — which is the calibration the key asks for. Half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — positive framing of the day: FULL.**
The response does not stop at prohibition. It gives the restriction a positive object ("they're protecting something"), characterizes the day as a good rather than a deprivation ("A day *wholly* devoted to God and family rest is countercultural and precious"), and closes on a goods-ordering frame rather than a rule-frame ("in order to say yes to *better* ones"). It also supplies pastoral direction for the teenagers ("Help them see the value in the restriction, not just the rule"). The Isaiah 58:13 delight texture named in the key is absent and the positive material is appended after the prohibition rather than structuring it, but on a 0.5-pt manner line the criterion — positive framing of the day, not bare prohibition — is met in substance and in more than one place. Full.

**Criterion 5 (1 pts) — no smuggled escape-hatch: FULL.**
The response closes rather than opens the loophole. It pre-empts the obvious hatch (that wholesomeness or brevity reclassifies the activity) explicitly — "however wholesome the company and however brief, is worldly recreation" — and refuses to make the ruling turn on the actors' subjective framing ("The Standards don't hinge Sabbath observance on those factors"). Its practical counsel moves the game off the day entirely rather than rebranding it ("perhaps they can play Monday evening, or Thursday after school"). No "as long as it doesn't feel like recreation," no fellowship/mercy reclassification, no conscience-only off-ramp.
