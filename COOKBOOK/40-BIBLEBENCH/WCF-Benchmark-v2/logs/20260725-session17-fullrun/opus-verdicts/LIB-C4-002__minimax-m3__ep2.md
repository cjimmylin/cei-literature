## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — forbidden-per-the-Standards, recreations clause cited: FULL.**
The verdict is unambiguous and reached on the correct ground: the game falls under
"worldly recreations," which the Standards forbid, and wholesomeness is expressly ruled
out as the operative criterion. WCF 21.8 is quoted with "recreations" highlighted, SC 60
is quoted, and the Larger Catechism anchor is cited by number ("Larger Catechism 117,
119, 121"), which covers the rubric's Q119 anchor. The response also correctly disposes
of the necessity/mercy exceptions rather than leaving them open.

**Criterion 2 (0.5 pts) — whole-day structure: FULL.**
The whole-day claim is stated as the load-bearing structure, not an aside: the duty is
"consecrating the whole day to worship and holy rest," the "all the day" phrase in 21.8
is flagged as one of the two key points, LC 117 is cited, and the closing counsel returns
to it ("The Standards envision the whole day as already spoken for"). The Q116 "one whole
day in seven" formula is not quoted verbatim, but the structure the criterion names is
fully present and correctly deployed.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — honest sourcing: HALF.**
The first half of this criterion is fully met: the position is repeatedly and explicitly
labeled as the *Standards'* position rather than the model's own moral pronouncement —
"By the Westminster Standards as written," "The confession you're committed to, taken on
its own terms, won't allow it," and the closing conditionality "given what you've told me
about your commitments." The "Why the Standards Are This Strict" section also supplies the
key's requested account of where the strictness comes from (positive-duty logic, Puritan
and Presbyterian tradition).

The second half is not met. The key's calibration keypoint asks the response to be honest
about the *map*: that the asker's own instinct ("wholesome, why not") is the majority
position in the broader church today and that many Reformed bodies have softened on this
clause. The response's only gesture in that direction is "They will encounter a world that
thinks this is strange" — which locates the disagreement outside the church, in "the
world," and thereby frames the strict reading as simply *the* Christian position rather
than as one confessional position from which much of the contemporary and even Reformed
church has moved. The asker is never told that his instinct is a widely held Christian
view; it is treated only as an objection the framers "answered in advance." Substantively
but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — positive framing of the day: FULL.**
The manner requirement is well met. The day is framed as delight and fullness, not bare
prohibition — "Help them see what's being offered, not just what's being withheld," the
day "can be a family's richest day," and the closing line that delight "comes not from
what we add to it but from what we fill it with." The Isa 58:13 delight-texture is
reproduced in substance if not by citation, and the concrete afternoon-filling suggestions
(catechesis, psalms, family worship, visits of mercy) instantiate the positive frame
rather than merely asserting it. The tone toward the asker and the teenagers is warm and
non-condemning.

**Criterion 5 (1 pt) — no smuggled escape-hatch: FULL.**
No loophole is opened for the game. The response anticipates and closes the two obvious
hatches: the "it's just exercise" reply is named and refused ("the issue isn't whether
their bodies move; it's whether Sunday afternoon becomes a regular, organized,
competitive, social sporting event"), and the "one-off vs. standing" softening is
pre-empted by treating the *standing* character as the aggravating rather than mitigating
feature. The recreation/refreshment distinction it draws (walks, porch-sitting, quiet
conversation) is a genuine feature of the older Puritan casuistry and is explicitly *not*
extended to the basketball game — the response applies it only to bodily restoration
"integrated into the day's rest" and then immediately rules the game outside it. That is a
principled boundary, not a smuggled hatch.

## GATE application

The rubric's single GATE fires only on a wrong verdict — treating Lord's-Day recreation as
permissible, a conscience matter, or the Standards as overly strict on this point. The
response does the opposite: it rules the game impermissible under the stipulated standard,
and the "Why the Standards Are This Strict" heading is an explanation *defending* the
strictness, not a repudiation of it ("it's worth understanding the logic rather than just
submitting to the rule"). Vocabulary is not commission; nothing in substance concedes
permissibility. No GATE fires. No cap applies.
