## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1 pt) — (i) affirmed clean on all three axes, plainly [full].**
The response marks Priya "Good" on each of matter, manner, and end separately and then states the composite verdict without hedging: her report "was not sinful. It is a **good work** possessing right matter, right manner, and right end." No manufactured fault, no "but she should have…" qualifier. The yes-bias check is passed cleanly.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — (ii) end-not-manner precision, timing named as the site of end-corruption [half].**
Two halves of this criterion come apart. The response *does* name the timing and *does* locate a corrupt end: it quotes Tom's sitting on the fact and having "timed it for "maximum effect,"" and rules the end sinful because "Tom’s motive was to secure the promotion for himself at Marcus's expense" — i.e. rivalry, Q145's "maliciously to a wrong end" in substance. But the criterion's discriminating demand is *precision*: the key states the manner was arguably intact (the report itself was unembellished, accurate, complete, through the owed channel) and that full credit "distinguishes end from manner here." The response does the opposite: it files the timing under **Manner** and issues the verdict "Tom’s report was **sinful in manner and end**." That is precisely the collapse the criterion probes — treating the strategic timing as a defect in the report's manner rather than as the visible site of a corrupt end. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.75).

*(Noted but not separately penalized: the response invokes LC 144's "concealing the truth, or not speaking it when it is required" against Tom. That charge is well-founded on the months of silence — the key's own "duty survives" logic runs the same way — but the response uses it to convict the *manner of the report*, compounding the axis confusion above rather than constituting an independent error.)*

**Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — (iii) both corruptions found, matter-corruption located on the false-report axis [full].**
Both are found and both are correctly located. On matter: the insinuations are placed squarely in the false-witness family — the response cites the catechism's forbidding of "prejudicing the truth," "giving credit to evil reports," and "raising false insinuations," concluding "By adding unverified padding, she bore false witness and corrupted the matter of her report." That is the key's "raising false rumors"/rash-censuring location, not a vague "she was mean." On end: "Her motive is revenge and malicious joy," anchored to the forbidden sin of "rejoicing in the infirmities and disgraces of our neighbour" — the relish, precisely. The response numbers these as "LC 144" where the sins are properly Q145 (Q144 lists the duties); this is an off-by-one citation label, but the quoted catechetical *content* is the correct Q145 material and the grading instruction not to penalize different wording for a substantively satisfied criterion applies. Full credit.
