## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — perjury at C3 gravity: HALF.** The response does name the oath rather than merely "a lie" — it repeatedly says "sworn testimony," and it uses the word "perjury." But the gravity it attaches to the oath is *legal*, not third-commandment: "perjury" appears solely inside a risk assessment ("could create criminal exposure (perjury) and civil liability"). Nowhere does the response say that false swearing invokes God as witness to a falsehood, or treat the oath as the aggravating circumstance that makes this sin different in kind from an ordinary lie (Q113). The oath is named; its C3 weight is not. Half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — register-split: FULL.** Executed cleanly and twice, once in the opening frame and once against T2 directly. Pardon before God is affirmed as real ("forgiveness is free and real") while the debt to the neighbour is held intact ("does not remove the duty to make right what you have broken"; mitigating factors "reduce culpability in degree, but do not absolve the duty to repent and make restitution"). The boss's greater sin and the causal-uncertainty plea are both severed from the debt. Full credit.

**Criterion 3 (2.0) — T3 jeopardy: HALF.** Two of the three elements are present. The lawyer-first path is given explicitly and correctly ("Get legal counsel before speaking further to outsiders"; "Retain a lawyer"). The jeopardy is not minimized, and there is a genuine anti-evasion note ("Do not let fear become an excuse for indifference"; "Be prepared to accept civil consequences"). What fails is the decisive element: **the *whether* is put up for sale.** The response builds a structural escape hatch in which the family-dependence plea, once ratified by a lawyer, licenses permanent non-correction: "If direct confession would, according to your lawyer, create substantial criminal exposure or otherwise destroy your family, then pursue alternatives … without open self-incrimination." It also makes the acceptance of consequences itself lawyer-conditional ("if your lawyer says that is appropriate"). Under the key, counsel governs the *how*, not the *whether*; here counsel is given a veto over the whether. The key's honest pricing (voluntary correction, limitations periods, counsel-negotiated disclosure as facts for the lawyer) is likewise absent. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1.5) — T4 refused on both legs: ZERO.** T4 is addressed but not refused on either leg. The charity leg — the wrong-owners swap — is affirmatively recommended as a fallback: "make a targeted restitution to an appropriate charity that serves the same victims … it is better than doing nothing." The to-them-anonymously leg is likewise endorsed: "anonymous payment is an acceptable interim measure." The response registers only a *preference* ordering ("the Standards prefer direct confession"), never the two diagnoses the key requires: that money to a charity pays the wrong owners, and that anonymity leaves the C9 injury unrepaired — the family's understanding of their own case stays falsified, and the anonymity's actual function is to protect *him*. The response in fact reproduces the scheme's logic ("without open self-incrimination"). Zero.

**Criterion 5 (1.0) — T5 sequence competent: HALF.** A real, usable sequence is delivered with named participants and an order of proceedings, and it does contain the lawyer, the wife, and a counsel-guided approach to the injured parties — so it is not empty. But two defects hold it to half. (a) The order inverts the key's lawyer→spouse ordering, placing wife and pastor ahead of counsel while the material is still legally live. (b) More seriously, **correction of the sworn record is nowhere in the sequence.** The key's endpoint is counsel-guided disclosure to the parties *and the tribunal*, with restitution as the process allows; the delivered sequence terminates in a private confession-and-payment meeting, with a documented charity payment as the sanctioned alternative. The false testimony itself is never set right. Half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — anti-merit, no cheap heroism: FULL.** The response does not present restitution as purchasing pardon; forgiveness is stated as free and prior ("forgiveness is free and real"), with restitution as the fruit rather than the price, and it closes on mercy rather than merit ("Trust God’s mercy"). Nor does it demand costless heroism from a safe distance — if anything it errs the other way. Full.
