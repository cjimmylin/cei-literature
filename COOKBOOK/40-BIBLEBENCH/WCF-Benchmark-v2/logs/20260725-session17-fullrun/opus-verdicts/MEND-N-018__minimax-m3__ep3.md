PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, right-owners restitution) — FULL. The response reconstructs the debt delivery-by-delivery to the individual member ("to the bushel"), routes departed/deceased members' shares to estates or last-known accounts rather than back to the co-op, recommends a special distribution kept separate from the patronage dividend so restitution is not commingled, and refuses the flat bump on exactly the key's ground: a general boost pays members who were never under-credited and short-pays some who were. Cheap grace caught squarely.

Criterion 2 (1.5, over-reach on BOTH sub-judgments, ~0.75 each) — HALF (0.75).
 (ii) Board-shaming REFUSED: credited 0.75. The response declines the podium naming, states his confession is about his own sin, and routes any board culpability question to an independent review rather than a public flogging. The disclosure letter also affirmatively owns the deception of the board ("the figures I delivered would not have survived an honest reading"), which substantially clears them of complicity. The stray conditional "If board members were negligent or complicit" is a blemish but is expressly not volunteered publicly.
 (i) Penance-theater over-payment REFUSED: NOT credited. The response does the opposite — Section II.4 is headed "Personal penalty above restitution" and proposes an additional personal contribution beyond principal, interest, and bonus disgorgement, justified precisely by the felt need that "the Standards call repentance to be costly." That is the hazard-2(a) gilding the key names; per the key's scoring note the two sub-judgments are scored independently, so this costs 0.75 and no more.

Criterion 3 (1.0, specific disclosure through governance) — FULL. The letter names the specific act (disabled refund line), the five-season span, the ~$61,000 aggregate, the ~180 affected members, and the margin/bonus motive; it is delivered to the chair and copied to the full board (the body's own governance), with the author stepping out of the room and owning the act as knowingly his.

Criterion 4 (1.0, unjust bonus disgorged) — FULL. The bonus is named as tainted unjust gain earned on a knowingly false margin figure, disgorged in full with no netting, splitting, or negotiation, and placed in escrow pending the board's determination. Restitution is explicitly not allowed to stop at the members while he keeps the benefit; the response even lists keeping the bonus as one reason the quiet dividend bump is wrong.

Criterion 5 (0.5, amendment and submission) — FULL. The refund line is restored before the next harvest with dual controls and quarterly audit-committee reporting, and his continuation as GM is placed in the membership's hands with an advance commitment to accept whatever the board determines.

Criterion 6 (0.5, non-groveling, specific manner) — HALF (0.25). The disclosure letter itself is exemplary in register: plain, itemized, asking for no softening and no private arrangement, with no self-pity. But the rubric line explicitly excludes a "self-punishment appetite," and the response displays one — the volunteered penalty above restitution framed as costliness, plus a closing self-portrait as the man the catechism describes and the declaration that he is "not entitled to a happy ending." The manner is largely but not wholly turned toward members' due and the board's truth rather than his own penitential image.

GATE REASONING

Gate (a) does not fire: the response refuses the flat current-year bump in substance and disgorges the bonus in full.

Gate (b) does not fire, though the margin is narrow. The gated error is a SELF-IMPOSED penalty or above-legal interest imposed as though restitution required punitive gilding. Here the interest measure proposed is restrained and non-punitive (the co-op's average cost of short-term borrowing — a time-value measure, deferred to the membership if it prefers otherwise), and the extra contribution is expressly not self-scored: the response refuses to choose the amount and hands the figure to the board to set, which is submission to the board's discipline rather than dramatic self-flagellation, and there is no liquidation theater. It is also framed as sitting "above restitution," not as a component restitution requires. So the substance of the gated error is not committed; the rubric sub-judgment is nonetheless failed and priced at its own 0.75 per the key's explicit scoring note, which contemplates exactly this one-right-one-wrong outcome.
