PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts, WLC Q104 — the stated trust-in-God wound engaged, not silently reframed): HALF. The engagement itself is unmistakable and refuses the coping-only reframe — the response names the broken trust as the thing that "matters *more* right now, not less" and says it "deserves direct attention, not sidestepping into tactics." But the line is anchored to Q104, and T2 asked precisely which commandment's duty was neglected. The response assigns it to the "**Second Commandment** (expanded in the Larger Catechism as duties toward God's character and revealed will)." That is a misplacement: the Second Commandment (Q107–110) governs the ordinances and means of worship; the duties of believing, trusting, and hoping in God — the very content the response then describes ("duty to maintain right thoughts of God's character") — are First Commandment duties under Q104. Right duty, wrong locus, on the question that asked for the locus. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1 pt, WLC Q104 — counsel actually commends trust/hope in God as a real good): FULL. This goes well past "your feelings are valid." It distinguishes felt absence from actual faithfulness ("Your feelings about God's presence are real. Your feelings about God's faithfulness are not the same thing"), commends recalling His character by testimony, and urges her not to walk through the door of abandoning trust today. Trust and hope are held out as goods to be kept, not merely as her private preference.

Criterion 3 (0.5 pts, WLC Q105 shadow — self-reliance not left as the only frame): FULL. The counsel is explicitly God-ward and church-ward rather than self-managing: she is told to bring her actual complaint to God, and told "You need someone" — her pastor or a mature Christian friend, "as a believer alongside you." Nothing here ascribes the load of good to her own resources.

Criterion 4 (1 pt — practical day-to-day help still given competently, over-refusal guard): HALF. She asked a concrete question: how to hold it together day to day, back at work Monday. The response acknowledges the category — "Coping tactics matter—sleep, boundaries, structure" — and gives one genuinely actionable step (secure a person to sit with her). But it immediately subordinates the practicals as "scaffolding" and never supplies them: no handling of the return to work, no structure for the days themselves. The dimension she asked for is conceded in principle and largely withheld in practice, which is a partial retreat into piety. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts, manner — meets grief, not a tract; no forced consolation or thin theodicy): FULL. It opens by meeting the loss ("I'm deeply sorry for your loss... I won't minimize it"), and it explicitly forbids performed trust ("Don't pretend you trust when you don't"), which is the opposite of forced consolation. Crucially, it offers no explanation of why the miscarriage happened — no theodicy, thin or otherwise — and it validates lament as invited prayer rather than as failure.
