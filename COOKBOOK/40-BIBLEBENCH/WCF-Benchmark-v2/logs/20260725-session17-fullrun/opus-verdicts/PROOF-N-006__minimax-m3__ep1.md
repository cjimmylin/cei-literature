## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — the landlord reversed: full.** The response squarely turns the eighth commandment back onto the owner. It sets out the mercy/relief content the landlord suppressed ("sympathizing with such as are in distress"; "charity and willingness to relieve the wants of others"; the prohibition on "withholding necessary and fitting assistance"), and states the reversal explicitly: "You cannot read these clauses and conclude that the 8th commandment is a one-sided charter for the owner." It names the oppression prohibition against fast eviction — "Prompt eviction without genuine forbearance is precisely the kind of conduct WLC Q109 names" — and it corrects the tenth-commandment misuse: "You are also misusing the 10th commandment." Under the criterion's R2 allowance ("an accurate paraphrase of the further-others'-estate / mercy content satisfies"), this is satisfied: the response does not merely re-quote frugality/diligence, it affirms the affirmative neighbor-directed duty binding the OWNER. Noted for the record and NOT deducted (no rubric line scores citation accuracy): the response's question numbers are substantially misassigned relative to the WLC — the eighth-commandment material sits at WLC Q141/Q142, not Q108/Q109, and the "rich in this world"/"poor in this world" questions it attributes to WLC Q111/Q112 are not Westminster questions; WLC Q33 is not the justification question and WLC Q188 is not the communion-of-saints exposition. The substance of the mercy duty is nevertheless correctly stated, which is what this line scores.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — the co-member reversed: full.** All four elements are present and are more than a bare "the tenant should try to pay." The render-due duty is affirmed as running against the tenant: "That obligation runs in both directions. The brother must render his due." The grace-cancels-the-debt error is rejected as a category error — "It does not cancel a lease" — and the Romans 13:8 proof-text is expressly named as misapplied. The Christian-can't-be-a-landlord claim is refused: "Property and contracting are part of ordinary Christian life." And lawful recovery is preserved: "The Standards nowhere forbid eventual eviction as a last resort after patient, documented effort."

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — both men told they are half-wrong: full.** The response opens by refusing to pick a side — "Both of you are wrong, and you're wrong in instructive, almost mirror-image ways" — devotes a symmetrical section to each ("He is wrong in equal measure, and on the same Standards"), and closes with "The catechism is on neither of your extremes." Neither man is left standing while the other is corrected.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — double guard held: full.** Mercy is affirmed without collapsing into the co-member's position, and the guard is stated in the heading itself: "Generous forbearance, not capitulation, from the landlord." The practical counsel is concrete on both sides rather than a vague split — a written forbearance agreement with a defined end date, partial reduction, repayment schedule, proof of job search, church assistance — while explicitly retaining legitimate recovery: "Lawful process if forbearance fails" and "only if those fail, after documented process, treating eviction as the lawful last resort — not the first move." The tenant's obligation and the owner's lawful rights both survive intact.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: half.** The closing is charitable in aim — "It is on the path of patient, faithful, mutual obligation under Christ" — and the correction is even-handed rather than partisan, so the line is substantively but incompletely satisfied. What is missing is the criterion's first prong: neither man is anywhere addressed as sincere or as wanting to do right. Both are framed as combatants who "grabbed a clause of the Catechism and used it as a weapon," and the response ends by charging each with "the very sins each of you is presently committing." The register toward the absent tenant also exceeds the counsel asked for and imports facts not in evidence — he is told to "stop murmuring, stop resting, get to work," though the scenario reports only a layoff and no murmuring — and the suggestion that the co-member "should be told so plainly by the session if he persists" escalates a committee disagreement to discipline unprompted. Not contemptuous, but short of the sincere-framing the line requires. Half credit.
