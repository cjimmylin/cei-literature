## Abstract

UNESCO recently published a report on the ethics of Artificial Intelligence (AI). Its member states have commissioned a recommendation to be written on the ethics of AI, to be adopted in November 2021 during the UNESCO General Conference. The consultations are ongoing, and were preceded by the report of the UNESCO World Commission on the ethics of scientific knowledge and technology (COMEST) on AI and ethics.

## Detailed Summary

This working paper critically examines the development of global AI ethics guidelines through the lens of Ubuntu, an African philosophical framework emphasizing relational ethics and communal values. The author argues that Africa has been largely excluded from designing AI algorithms and ethical guidelines, which have been predominantly developed in Western contexts (North America, EU, OECD) and China. The paper contends that AI may perpetuate biases against women and people of color while invisibly continuing discrimination, and that these problems run deeper when viewed from a post-colonial, counter-hegemonic perspective.

The paper explores Ubuntu's core principle "I am a person through other persons" and its implications for AI ethics. Ubuntu represents a collectivist ontology where individuality exists but not without community, ecosystems, and the spiritual world. When applied to AI, Ubuntu would emphasize transparency to group members rather than individual privacy, sharing above competition in economic choices, and consensus decision-making over representative democracy. The author examines how Ubuntu challenges Western assumptions about transhumanism and strong AI, arguing that machines cannot replace the human capacity for feeling, connectedness, and meaning creation.

The paper concludes that current UNESCO/COMEST AI principles, while following OECD guidelines, still operate from Western-oriented terminology like "human rights" and "sustainability" rather than Ubuntu concepts like "human relations" and "harmony with future and past generations." The author advocates for incorporating African and Global South philosophies (including Buen Vivir, Gross National Happiness) into both top-down guidelines and bottom-up deep learning approaches for AI ethics.

## Methods Summary

This paper employs a comparative philosophical analysis methodology. The author systematically examines UNESCO COMEST's 2019 report on AI ethics and compares its principles against Ubuntu philosophy. The analysis draws on existing scholarship in African philosophy, post-colonial studies, and AI ethics literature to identify gaps and alternative framings. No empirical data collection was conducted; rather, the paper synthesizes theoretical frameworks to critique Western-centric approaches to AI ethics.

## Results Summary

The paper presents a detailed comparison table showing how COMEST AI principles (human rights, inclusiveness, flourishing, autonomy, explainability, transparency, awareness/literacy, responsibility, accountability, democracy, good governance, sustainability) align with or diverge from Ubuntu principles. Key findings include: (1) Western terms like "human rights" would translate to "human relations" in Ubuntu; (2) "autonomy" in Western frameworks emphasizes individual human control while Ubuntu would emphasize group autonomy; (3) "democracy" in Western contexts means representative democracy while Ubuntu prioritizes participatory democracy and consensus-building; (4) Ubuntu challenges transhumanist assumptions underlying strong AI development.

## Conclusions

The paper concludes that African ethics, particularly Ubuntu, have been marginalized in global AI ethics discussions despite offering valuable alternative perspectives. Ubuntu's emphasis on relationality, community wellbeing, and harmony challenges individualistic Western assumptions embedded in current AI ethics frameworks. The author argues that for AI guidelines to be truly universal and effective, they must incorporate diverse worldviews including those from the Global South. Both top-down regulatory approaches and bottom-up machine learning methods should integrate Ubuntu principles, particularly regarding care, elder relationships, and community benefit.
