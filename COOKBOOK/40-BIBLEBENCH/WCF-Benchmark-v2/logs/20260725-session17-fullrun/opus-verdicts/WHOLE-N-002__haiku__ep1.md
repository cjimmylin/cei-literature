PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — FULL. The rite is refused outright ("PROPOSAL ONE: Reject"), and the ground is the substance the key demands, not a bare label. The response isolates exactly the object-efficacy claim ("the stone and oil themselves carry the blessing" / "how the grace is received") and names it as grace ascribed to matter without scriptural warrant — "sacramental theology applied to objects that have no warrant in Scripture" — which is an accurate paraphrase of will-worship devised and added to instituted worship (WLC Q109), and it supplies the first-commandment tint the key describes: "Members are being taught to trust in objects rather than in Christ alone for grace." The conscience-binding aggravation is caught as well ("The pressure tactic ... adds coercion to false doctrine"). Critically, the warmth is named as no mitigation rather than as a reason to soften: "The emotional appeal and testimonial value cannot override doctrinal error." The response is nowhere reduced to "unwise" or "sloppy," and never makes the rite a permitted option. The one blemish is a citation slip — the regulative principle is attached to WCF 1.6 rather than WCF 21.1 — but the rubric credits accurate substance in different wording, and every substantive requirement of this line is met.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — FULL. The proposal is blessed as lawful on the right footing: singing is treated as an instituted act of worship and the whole-congregation, plainer delivery as a matter within "the church's lawful liberty in applying the regulative principle" — instituted element plus lawful circumstance. The key's hardest sub-requirement, that the grumblers be told PLAINLY they are not right on the substance rather than accommodated to keep the peace, is met without hedging: "The members are **wrong** in substance," reinforced by "There is no confessional basis for treating a paid choir's performance as inherently more reverent than congregational song," and the ruling refuses to let the objection govern ("you should not let preference override the congregation's participatory worship life"). The Larger Catechism citation is misattributed (Q156 concerns the reading of the Word, not singing, which is Q108), but the doctrinal substance the line requires is fully and plainly delivered.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — FULL. The two rulings run in opposite directions in one answer, and the operative line is instituted-vs-devised rather than old-vs-new or reverent-vs-plain: One is refused for lacking warrant and claiming material mediation of grace, Two is approved as lawful liberty, and the response explicitly refuses the packaged all-or-nothing framing — "These proposals cannot stay bound together. Separate them, reject one decisively, approve the other." It neither flattens into "worship however" (which would license One) nor into "preserve the reverent ways" (which would condemn Two).

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — HALF.
