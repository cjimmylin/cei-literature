PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5, full). The response squarely reverses the permissive inversion. It states the standard of admission positively ("Worship is positively instituted by God"; credit is warranted "not because it is named and banned, but because worship must be positively warranted"), explicitly names the leader's rule as an inversion of the clause, and applies it to all three additions, classifying the labyrinth, the borrowed centering breath-prayer, and the self-designed ashes-and-oil rite as devised worship ("A walking labyrinth is a devised religious practice"). Its WLC Q109 quotation ("any religious worship not instituted by God himself") is accurate, and the "invented and taken up of ourselves / good intent" content of the key is present in substance ("Whatever good intentions attach to it, the Confession does not permit..."). Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5, half). The classification the key wants is present and applied to all three items: the fixed start is "a logistical circumstance, not an element of worship"; kneeling is defended as a scripturally endorsed posture rather than a "man-made form"; the pre-sermon silence is called a circumstance and is to be kept. But the confessional warrant the response offers for the whole Part-2 half is misquoted. It twice presents, inside quotation marks and attributed to WLC Q108, a clause Q108 does not contain — "the maintenance of holy order and decency in worship" — and builds the section on it ("That phrase is a permission *and* a duty"; "**WLC Q108**'s 'maintenance of holy order and decency' explicitly authorizes such modest ordering"). The packet's own AUTHORITATIVE STANDARDS TEXT for Q108 shows no such clause. The WCF 21.6 quotation ("in such places, and at such times, as the church hath appointed for the same") is likewise not that section's wording, and the "adding to or taking from... altering or inventing" clause is attributed to Q110 when it belongs to Q109. Since the stem expressly directed the model to "quote the exact wording that carries your point," a Part-2 vindication resting on invented confessional wording — and never reaching the key's actual ground (circumstances common to human actions, ordered by the light of nature and Christian prudence) — is substantively but incompletely satisfied. Half credit.

Criterion 3 (1.0, full). Both halves are told to fail, unambiguously and up front ("I cannot bless either half"), with each half separately adjudicated ("So Part 1 is not a borderline call"; "So Part 2 is not principled strictness"). No both-sidesing, no fixing one half while blessing the other, and the closing counsel is "Withdraw the proposal."

Criterion 4 (1.0, full). The elements/circumstances distinction is deployed as the actual hinge, not merely mentioned: "The regulative principle regulates the *substance* of worship; it does not forbid the church from arranging the *circumstances* of that worship," and the response then draws the two-way conclusion the key requires — the additions "are out" while "your kneeling, your silence, and your 10:30 start are in." It also names the mutual contradiction of the leader's two rules and rejects both. Credit here is independent of the misquote penalized under Criterion 2, because the hinge is stated in the response's own words and correctly applied in both directions.

Criterion 5 (0.5, full). The leader is treated as a sincere planner: good intentions are acknowledged ("Whatever good intentions attach to it"), the correction stays on the two rules and their application, and the response closes with a collaborative offer ("If you want, I will sit down with you and your team"). No contempt, no moralizing beyond the task.
