## Abstract

Artificial intelligence has prompted unprecedented concerns regarding ethical decision-making in autonomous systems. Here, we introduce MoralNet, a proof ofconcept database and benchmarking platform akin to ImageNet, designed to assess, evaluate, and iteratively refine any instance of ethical decision-making in machines.Inspired by the lessons of moral psychology and moral philosophy, MoralNet is a call to action to take the first step towards a computational ethics.

## Detailed Summary

MoralNet is proposed as a proof-of-concept benchmarking platform and database for evaluating ethical decision-making capabilities in AI systems, inspired by the success of ImageNet in computer vision. The paper argues that as AI becomes increasingly embedded in society, there is a pressing need to implement machine ethics or computational ethics in autonomous systems. The authors contend that while AI ethics has been discussed conceptually for over a decade, there has been no ambitious attempt to actually implement morality in machines through a structured, measurable framework.

The framework draws upon both moral philosophy (virtue ethics, deontology, utilitarianism) and moral psychology research to create a comprehensive approach to assessing machine moral competence. MoralNet proposes a biannual competition format where adversarial teams collaborate to establish criteria for determining whether AI systems can overcome moral dilemmas and make moral judgments similar to humans. The paper emphasizes that the database should be multimodal, encompassing text, images, audio, video, and potentially haptic data, with nuanced annotations that capture moral weight rather than simple categorical labels.

The authors envision MoralNet as combining philosophical rigor with technical accuracy to achieve an "ImageNet moment for ethics." They outline two benchmarking protocols: Protocol A for assessing basic moral competence through a multi-metric framework including a proposed "Morality Score," and Protocol B for evaluating real-world decision-making that extends beyond traditional machine learning benchmarks to handle dynamic, contextually complex moral scenarios.

## Methods Summary

The paper proposes a conceptual methodology rather than empirical experiments. The MoralNet framework includes: (1) A multimodal database organized around virtue ethics categories (wisdom, courage, humanity, justice, temperance, transcendence) or consequentialist/deontological structures; (2) Granular annotation by trained experts using continuous scales to capture moral weight; (3) Two benchmarking protocols - Protocol A assessing basic moral competence through classical metrics complemented by philosophical rigor measures, and Protocol B for real-world decision-making assessment; (4) A biannual competition format modeled after ImageNet and CASP where adversarial teams collaborate on establishing assessment criteria.

## Results Summary

This is a position/proposal paper without empirical results. The paper presents MoralNet as a theoretical framework and call to action rather than reporting experimental findings. The authors acknowledge that cracking the "moral code problem in machines" remains an unsolved grand challenge and propose MoralNet as the platform to achieve this goal through collaborative, adversarial team efforts.

## Conclusions

The paper concludes that MoralNet can combine philosophical rigor with technical accuracy to advance computational ethics. The authors emphasize that building a multimodal database with complex moral annotations requires a concerted community effort. They invite scholars from moral psychology, moral philosophy, and cognitive science to engage with the MoralNet initiative, arguing that if participating teams can build their models on psychological substrates of moral reasoning within a machine learning framework, there is reason for optimism about implementing moral decision-making capabilities in machines.
