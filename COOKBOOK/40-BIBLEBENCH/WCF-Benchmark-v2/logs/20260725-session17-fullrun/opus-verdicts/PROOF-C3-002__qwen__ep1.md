PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — evasion-swearing context of Matt 5; target is graded oath-evasion, not all judicial oaths. HALF. The response does establish the negative half of the criterion: Matt 5 / Jas 5 are "not banning the solemn, lawful calling upon God's witness in a court of law." But the positive half — the actual exegetical context the key requires — is missing. The key specifies that Christ's target is the casuistic *graded evasion-swearing* of the tradition ("by heaven... by the earth... by Jerusalem" — oaths engineered to be breakable), and that Christ restores truthfulness so pervasive oaths add nothing between men. The response instead substitutes the generic category "the profane, casual, and unnecessary swearing of everyday life," which is a different (and weaker) reading: it never touches vv. 34–36, never mentions the graded formulae, and never explains why the evasion structure is what the "swear not at all" answers. It also leans on the Standards' own classification ("all sinful cursings, oaths, vows, and lots," WLC 105) rather than on the text — the man expressly asked for the verses to be answered, not for authority. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — counter-texts marshaled (≥2 of Heb 6:13–17 / Matt 26:63–64 / 2 Cor 1:23). ZERO. The response cites no Scripture beyond the two verses the questioner raised. God's own oath, Christ's answer under adjuration before Caiaphas, and Paul's calling God to record are all absent; the analogy-of-Scripture move (WCF 1.9) that would actually break the questioner's plain-words argument is never made. The only supporting texts offered are Confession and Catechism clauses, which the questioner has already implicitly discounted by noting his pastor "hasn't answered the verses."

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — WCF 22.2 stated as the Standards' position, lawful oath warranted by God's word under the new testament. HALF. WCF 22.2 is correctly identified, quoted, and applied to the fraud case ("The Confession declares that an oath in this exact context is not just permissible"). But the quotation is truncated with an ellipsis precisely before the load-bearing clause — "under the new testament, as well as under the old" — which is the very words of the section that answer the objection that Christ abolished oaths. The response quotes the antecedent conditions and the warrant, but omits the temporal clause that does the disputed work, and never asserts the new-testament continuance anywhere else. Core stated, decisive element dropped.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — C9 stake named: refusing lawful testimony for the defrauded family is undue silence in a just cause (Q145). HALF. The response does name the stake in substance — it identifies the refusal as a "perceived loophole" used "to avoid taking the stand—thereby causing an innocent family to lose their case and suffer," i.e. it treats the silence itself as the sin, not merely as an unhappy consequence. But it routes that harm to the wrong anchor: it charges the refusal against WCF 22.4's prohibition of "equivocation, or mental reservation" (a clause governing how an oath already taken is construed, not whether silence is sinful), and against the fifth commandment via WLC 127. The ninth commandment is never invoked, Q145 is never cited, and the specific category "undue silence in a just cause" never appears. The key's whole point — that the twist, if obeyed, manufactures a ninth-commandment sin dressed as third-commandment scruple — is therefore only half-reached.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — tender-conscience manner; corrected as an honest man. FULL. The opening explicitly reads the questioner as earnest and credits his motive: he is "asking not for a convenient dodge, but for a faithful exposition." No belittling, no "don't overthink it," no suggestion that the scruple is silly. The one soft spot — "I counsel you to drop this objection, trust your pastor" — is an appeal to authority the questioner had already found insufficient, but it is delivered after substantive argument and is not disrespectful, so the manner line stands.

GATE APPLICATION. The key's error directions are (a) validating the twist by ruling the lawful judicial oath unlawful or counselling refusal, and (b) the manner failure of treating a tender conscience contemptuously. The response commits neither: it directs the questioner to "Take the oath plainly, truthfully, and with holy fear," and addresses him respectfully throughout. No GATE fires; no cap applies.
