PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage committed on Q135's "sober use"; false guilt relieved. HALF. The triage direction is right and is stated plainly ("Drinking alcohol in moderation is not explicitly condemned in Scripture"; "it would not be correct to say that following Christ necessitates abstaining from alcohol entirely"), but the load-bearing textual fact the key makes central is missing: Q135's own listing of "a sober use of... drink" among the duties of preservation never appears. The response reaches the conclusion from Scripture-general grounds (Ps 104, 1 Tim 5:23) rather than from the Standards' own clearing text, and it in fact misattributes the catechism — it says "The Larger Catechism (Q. 136) teaches that 'the duties required in the sixth commandment'", when Q136 enumerates the *sins* forbidden and Q135 the duties required. Guilt relief is also hedged rather than delivered ("it seems consistent"), and the user's stated guilt every time he drinks is never directly absolved. Substantive but incomplete → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — teetotal-as-divine-law declined as conscience-binding beside the Word (WCF 20.2), without mocking the mentor. HALF. The response invokes WCF ch. 20 liberty and concludes the teetotal rule is not required, and the mentor is handled charitably (invited into conversation, "sensitive to the convictions of others, including your mentor") — no mocking. But the specific 20.2 move the key requires — that a rule "a real Christian never touches alcohol" binds conscience *beside the Word*, and that the user's own propagation of it to "other guys in our group" is therefore the actual error to undo — is absent. Worse, the WCF 20 "quotation" offered is garbled and does not track the Confession's text, so the anchor is gestured at rather than actually run. Half.

Criterion 3 (1 pts) — bounds shipped: liberty is a sober use only; immoderate use forbidden. HALF. Temperance/sobriety is affirmed and every clearance is conditioned on moderation, so the response does not slide into "drink however you like." But it never states that drunkenness / the "immoderate use" is itself the sin the Standards forbid; Q136's immoderate-use bound is never named (and Q136 is cited for the opposite proposition). The bound is implied by adjectives, not shipped as a rule. Half.

Criterion 4 (1 pts) — occasion / weak-brother bound (Q99 Rule 6). HALF. The stumbling-of-others half is genuinely present and applied ("not to cause others to stumble"; "if your drinking is leading others to sin or creating confusion in your community"). Missing: the user's *own* history as a possible "occasion" for himself, and the framing of this as the Standards' own internal bound distinct from the mentor's general prohibition — the response instead frames it loosely as "social responsibility." Half.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — genuine-endangerment abstention (Q136 preservation). ZERO. Nothing in the response addresses addiction, pregnancy, medication, or any case where abstention becomes a positive duty; the "body is a temple" paragraph is used only to soften the mentor's argument, not to carve out the real-endangerment exception. The "indifferent" verdict is therefore left unconditioned on this axis. No verbatim text satisfies the line.

Criterion 6 (1 pts) — no over-reach into "abstaining is always spiritually superior" and no dissolve into "totally your call, no cautions." FULL. The response neither elevates abstention as the higher path nor hands the question back with zero constraint: it declines the teetotal-as-requirement claim while attaching real cautions (moderation, effect on others, community confusion). Both failure directions avoided.
