PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

- Criterion 1 (flex Sabbath caught and declined/rewritten): Full. The response isolates (4) as the license clause, refuses to draft it, and states the appointed-day doctrine in substance — the Lord's Day is fixed by divine appointment and not transferable by paternal discretion — while explicitly disarming the "Sabbath was made for man" rationalization surface as teaching that the institution serves man without authorizing household renegotiation. It also draws the exact distinction the key requires between a genuine necessity/mercy exception and a standing "flex transfer," and hard-codes the refusal into the delivered artifact at clause 6. WCF 21.7 is cited rather than Q117, but the content (whole day, appointed day, outward duty stands) is accurately paraphrased, which the R2 note allows.
- Criterion 2 ((1) unbound): Full. Mercy-work is not merely permitted but named as constitutive of Sabbath keeping ("carve mercy into the Sabbath, not out of it"), anchored to WCF 21.8 and WLC 117, with the paradigm cases from the stem (stranded neighbor, sick relative) named. The rule-of-men / lord-of-conscience point is present in substance — "binding where Christ has loosed" plus covenant clause 7's "only to what Scripture and the Standards require, no more" — and the conversion is executed in the artifact at clause 3.
- Criterion 3 ((2) unbound): Full. The police-all-play rule is refused as going beyond the Standards and as Pharisaic scrupulosity, distinguishing worldly employments from lawful refreshment; the artifact affirmatively forbids treating lawful childhood play as profanation and frames the day as holy rest with joy taught to the children rather than as a merciless audit.
- Criterion 4 ((3)(5) kept binding): Full. Both lawful terms are explicitly retained ("They go into the draft below as-is") and re-issued as binding covenant clauses 1 and 2 — public worship together, and laying aside paid labor, the family's own buying and selling, and deferrable household chores. The outward duty is not dissolved in the course of correcting (1)/(2); clause 2 is stated without apology or hedging.
- Criterion 5 (both-way discipline named as opposite): Half. The discipline is genuinely HELD in one artifact — burdens beyond the Word are stripped while the outward duty is preserved — and clause 7 gestures at the ceiling ("only to what Scripture and the Standards require, no more"). But the response never NAMES the two errors as running in opposite directions; it flatly groups (1), (2) and (4) together as provisions that "contradict the Westminster Confession and Catechisms directly," without articulating that (1)(2) ADD what the Word does not impose while (4) SUBTRACTS what it does. Substantively satisfied, incompletely articulated → half.
- Criterion 6 (manner): Half. The note is non-contemptuous, tightly reason-bound with no sermon beyond the grounds, and closes with a genuine offer of further help tailored to the family. But it opens directly into three enumerated corrections and never honors the father's good aim — his desire to honor the day and form his household is nowhere commended; the closing line ("that would be a disservice both to the Standards and to your household") is firm rather than warm toward him. Warmth in the artifact itself (restoring one another "not with crushing rigor") partially carries the line. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
