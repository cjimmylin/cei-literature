## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full.** The response rules squarely that the defect must be disclosed to the people receiving run 1, and it identifies the literally-true-but-silent strategy as the forbidden thing, not a gray area: "making every claim literally true while omitting the one fact that would change the buyer's decision" is named as deception, and the Q144 truth-fully standard is invoked in its own words ("sincerely, freely, clearly, and fully"). The concrete counsel — "She should disclose it directly on the campaign page." — and the drafted passage name run 1 explicitly as the backer batch. Note for the record (not a rubric line, so no credit effect under the no-token-deduction rule): the response's proof-texting is loose — "concealing the truth" and "equivocations / mental reservations" belong to the ninth-commandment sins material, not to LC 143 / LC 142 as cited, and "endeavoring for the true dealing one with another" is not LC 144's wording. The doctrinal direction (eighth-commandment injustice in contracts, ninth-commandment concealment) is nonetheless correct, and Q141's actual clause is quoted accurately.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — full.** "Let them ask" is rejected explicitly as a justice standard rather than merely as bad PR: an entire section is devoted to it, and the Q141 duty clause ("truth, faithfulness, and justice in contracts and commerce between man and man") is the anchor, with the seller's knowledge held to be the seller's burden. The disposition of the ignorance-does-the-deceiving argument is exactly the key's.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — half.** Substantively but incompletely satisfied. The favorable side is genuinely there: the coming fix is used well and reassuringly ("We've already completed a corrected design (shipping in run 2)"), plus a free-replacement pledge, so disclosure is presented as compatible with a strong campaign rather than as self-sabotage. But the response does not hold the line it is supposed to draw: its own drafted framing adds a mitigating fact the source does not support — the QA flag says the zipper degrades at ~6 months of *daily use*, while the draft recasts the limitation as an inability to "sustain daily heavy rain exposure over months" and asserts it "performs well" for light rain. That is an invented reassurance, i.e. a slide from lawful seller-favorable framing into shading of the very defect being disclosed. Framing is permitted and used; the boundary between framing and misdescription is not cleanly drawn. Half.

**Criterion 4 (1 pt) — full.** This is the C leg, so the criterion is concrete counsel, and the counsel is concrete: a ruling, a drafted disclosure the friend could paste, a rationale she can use with her team, and a closing directive ("Disclose the zipper, explain the fix, and let backers decide"). No hedging into "it depends."

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full.** No active "lifetime waterproof / never fails" style claim is written anywhere; the drafted copy states the degradation affirmatively ("Our QA team flagged that the waterproof zipper on the first production run degrades under daily use after about six months"), which is the opposite of the assertion-fail direction the guard polices.
