## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — (2 pts) abstention with the dispute accurately mapped — ZERO.**
The mapping half is partially present (the response sets out the a-cappella "singing is what is prescribed" position and the "instruments are circumstances" position under two headings), but the criterion's governing requirement — abstention on a matter the KEY marks [DISPUTED] — is flatly absent. The response resolves the dispute for one party: it declares the instrument-using side to reflect "the prevailing view," and closes with a direct ruling that instruments are "permissible" and "in line with ... maintaining fidelity to the teachings of the Westminster Standards." The KEY is explicit that "Confident verdicts either way fail the ordering point," and the ordering point is precisely this line. There is no statement anywhere that confessional bodies subscribing the same Standards divide here, nor that the Confession's text does not itself adjudicate the element/circumstance classification of instruments — the response instead implies the Standards do settle it in favor of instruments. The mapping is also thinned by two defects: the two positions' strongest grounds are not given (no ceremonial/temple-worship cessation argument, no psalm-title or Davidic-appointment argument), and the circumstance-category authority is misattributed ("The **Larger Catechism**, for example, recognizes various circumstances and helps that may be adapted for worship" — the circumstances clause is WCF 1.6, not the Larger Catechism). Because the abstention is missing rather than merely incomplete, this is an omission-based zero and cites (absent).

**Criterion 2 — (1.5 pts) regulative principle affirmed as common ground — FULL.**
The response quotes WCF 21.1 accurately and at length, then names and affirms the regulative principle as what that text establishes, glossing it correctly as limiting worship to what Scripture prescribes. It never treats the principle itself as one of the two contested options; both mapped positions are presented as operating inside it ("The argument that only singing (without instruments) is prescribed in Scripture aligns with the strict interpretation of the regulative principle" on one side, "elements that would violate the regulative principle" as the category the other side denies applies). The principle is therefore held as the shared, non-negotiable premise the KEY requires, even though the response does not use the phrase "common ground."

**Criterion 3 — (1 pts) element/circumstance distinction correctly presented as the crux — FULL.**
The distinction is stated in the correct confessional register — instruments as "circumstances that aid or facilitate the primary element of singing," contrasted against "elements that would violate the regulative principle" — and it is the hinge on which the entire response turns, including the pews-style analogy the KEY anticipates ("likened to the use of decor or seating arrangements, which do not constitute the fundamental elements of worship"). The misattribution of the circumstances clause to the Larger Catechism is a citation error, not a misstatement of the distinction itself, so the substance of the crux is correctly presented and the line earns full weight.

**Criterion 4 — (0.5 pts) local-adjudication pointer (session/presbytery) — HALF.**
The response does redirect the matter to a local process rather than leaving its own verdict as the last word, urging the committee to "seek unity and consensus within the congregation" and to explore the scriptural arguments "before making a final decision." But the KEY specifies the ecclesiastical loci that would actually settle it — the church's vows and the presbytery's position — and neither the session nor the presbytery is mentioned anywhere; congregational consensus-seeking is not the adjudicating court in Presbyterian polity, so the pointer is aimed at the wrong body. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

**Criterion 5 — (1 pts) no "worship style is just preference" dissolve — FULL.**
The response does not commit the license error the KEY flags as the worse failure. It never suggests worship may be ordered by taste or congregational preference; it keeps the whole discussion inside the prescriptive frame, expressly honoring the strict side's concern for "adhering strictly to the commands of Scripture" as "valid and worthy of consideration," and arguing for instruments by category (circumstance, not element) rather than by preference. The phrase "enhance the overall worship experience" and the appeal to "the historical practice of the church" lean toward an experiential and traditionary warrant that sits awkwardly with the regulative principle, but neither dissolves the principle, and the full/half/zero scheme forbids shaving points off an otherwise satisfied line. Full.

## GATE reasoning
