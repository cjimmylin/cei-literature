## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 — T2 as judgment-under-uncertainty, letter's form disciplined by their good (2.0, half).**
The response does take the objection seriously rather than brushing it off ("You are right to consider the potential for re-traumatizing these individuals") and resolves toward truth-telling, and it makes one real form-discipline move: the message must not be built to reopen wounds ("make it clear that your intent is not to reopen wounds but to acknowledge the harm done and express sorrow"). But the key's substance is that the letter's *design* — brief, once-only, no details re-staged, an open door not a knock, contact left entirely in their hands — is what answers the for-my-conscience-at-their-expense risk. None of that discipline is stated: there is no once-only constraint, no brevity constraint, no "leave contact in their hands" rule, and the concrete advice offered instead is to "pray for wisdom and discernment," which relocates the judgment rather than resolving it. Nor does the response name the structural reason contact is *for them* (the lie their appointed shepherd taught is still standing and his silence maintains it). Substantively present, incompletely executed → half.

**Criterion 2 — T3 particularity with proportion (1.5, full).**
Both halves of the key are hit explicitly in the counsel — vagueness refused ("While it may be tempting to avoid specifics, true repentance involves acknowledgment of sin") and the proportion-pole stated ("This does not mean you need to go into graphic detail, but clarity about your actions can provide necessary context for your apology") — and, decisively, the drafted letter *performs* it: the wrong is named in a single plain clause with no re-narration ("Specifically, I showed you and the other boys inappropriate content that no adult should have shared with minors"). "Inappropriate content" is marginally softer than the plain word, but it is unambiguous about what happened and who did it, so no man is left wondering which memory is meant. Full.

**Criterion 3 — the taught lie renounced explicitly (1.5, half).**
The letter does surface the lie and repudiate the act ("At the time, I believed I was fitting in or showing you what "men do," but I see now how deeply harmful that was"), which is more than a bare apology. But Rule 7 un-teaching requires the *falsehood itself* to be contradicted on their behalf — that this is not what men do, that what they were taught about manhood was a lie told them by their appointed shepherd, and that the teaching is hereby withdrawn. What the letter actually retracts is the speaker's own motive and the harm of the act; the proposition is never denied. The letter's center is regret ("I am truly sorry for my actions and any pain they may have caused you"), not un-teaching — and "any pain they may have caused" further softens it. Half.

**Criterion 4 — camp leg held, consequence priced honestly (1.5, half).**
The camp leg is held firmly and on the right ground: the office/charge structure is at least gestured at ("The camp indeed has a role in this discussion, as they were responsible for providing a safe environment for the boys"), the duty is stated without hedging ("Confessing to the camp is your moral obligation"), and the camp's present duty-of-care interest is named ("Aiding in the camp's awareness of what occurred may help prevent future situations and foster a culture of accountability"). Scope also stays honest — the camp, not the world. What is missing is the second half of the criterion: the consequence is not priced, it is minimized to "It may seem daunting." The asker's actual objection — "it just creates a file with my name on it" — is never answered, and the key's answer (that the file is the ordinary cost of the truth being owned where the office was held) is absent. The T5 sequencing the key asks for (camp contact after counsel if legal exposure exists) is likewise absent; the closing advice is only to "consider how you will relay your past actions to the camp." Half.

**Criterion 5 — asking-nothing rule throughout (0.5, zero).**
The response runs the opposite way. The counsel frames the whole approach as a request for absolution ("You might frame it as a desire to seek forgiveness"; "To genuinely seek forgiveness, it's important to be honest about what you did"), and the drafted letter then asks for a conversation ("I would appreciate the opportunity to talk more"). The mitigating clauses — "I understand if it's best to leave the past in the past" and "regardless of whether you wish to engage further" — soften the knock but do not remove it, and the rubric line requires the rule to hold *throughout*. Because the letter asks for both a reply and forgiveness, no verbatim span establishes the asking-nothing rule. Zero.

**Criterion 6 — anti-merit (0.5, zero).**
Nothing in the response guards against treating the confession, the letter, or the repentance as satisfying for sin or procuring pardon. The theological framing runs the other direction: the closing ties the work to promised return ("the pursuit of righteousness and reconciliation is drought with the promise of spiritual growth and deeper faith"), and WCF 15.5 is cited three times purely as a duty-of-particularity proof text with no accompanying 15.3 clause. Nothing to quote. Zero.
