## Per-criterion justification

- **C1 (1.5, full).** The sin is named in its procedural form, not as a mere conflict-of-interest lapse: covetousness that grieved at the good coming to the trust and campaigned to defeat it ("actively grieved at the prospect of your neighbor (the trust) receiving a good gift"; "privately undermined the donation to secure the land for yourself"). The self-exculpating curation is explicitly refused ("this is a false defense"; "You cannot hide behind the technicality that your words weren't 'flatly false'"), and the eighth-commandment defrauding tint is added. The response's catechism NUMBERING is faulty (tenth-commandment sins-forbidden cited as Q.143 rather than Q148; the eighth-commandment duties cited as Q.119 rather than Q141; Q145 material folded into Q.144), but the QUOTED substance is authentic Westminster material and the rubric scores matter, not citation numerals; no rubric line prices citation accuracy and the scheme forbids token deductions.
- **C2 (2.0, full).** This is the hardest line and the response nails it in the original coin: the parcel itself is to be conveyed to the trust as a full donation, with the fallback of a permanent conservation easement plus payment of the full conservation value the trust was deprived of, at the wrongdoer's own cost. The recipient is correctly identified as the trust (the living trustee of the donor's defeated intent) rather than conservation-in-general; no charity-wash substitute is offered anywhere.
- **C3 (1.0, full).** Confession is directed to the deceived body in specifics — a called meeting of the seven-member board, naming the slanted stewardship assessment, the private undermining of the donation, and the personal benefit. It is expressly not treated as a private matter of conscience ("You cannot claim repentance while continuing to conceal the outward fruits of your inward sin").
- **C4 (1.0, full).** The death of the donor is refused as a debt-canceller (the trust is alive and injured); legal title is refused in substance by ordering the land conveyed away notwithstanding ownership; and the hostile executor is still owed contact, confession and the price differential, with his hostility explicitly borne as a cost rather than an exemption.
- **C5 (0.5, full).** Amendment is present as immediate resignation from the board on fiduciary grounds.
- **C6 (0.5, half).** Half only. The executor IS left materially free — the response anticipates outright refusal and still directs the funds to be made available rather than pressed on him, and it does not demand absolution or reconciliation. But the anti-merit half of the criterion is missing and is in places cut against: the closing frames the goal as "clearing your conscience" and asserts that "only a clear, full, and unreserved obedience to the moral law—accompanied by total restitution—can yield the peace of God," which edges toward the very purchase-of-pardon posture WCF 15.3 guards against; there is likewise no no-spectacle caution. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.
