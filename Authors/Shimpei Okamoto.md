# Shimpei Okamoto

## Research Summary

Shimpei Okamoto is affiliated with the Graduate School of Humanities and Social Sciences at Hiroshima University in Higashi-Hiroshima, Japan. His research focuses on virtue ethics, medical ethics education, and the ethical implications of AI and robotics.

His notable recent work, "AI-based medical ethics education: examining the potential of large language models as a tool for virtue cultivation" (BMC Medical Education, February 2025), examines the potential of Large Language Models to foster virtues in medical ethics education. Drawing on neo-Aristotelian philosophy, the paper proposes guiding LLMs with "v-rules" (virtue-based instructions such as "act compassionately" or "act with courage") to enable them to model virtuous behaviors in ethical discussions and scenarios.

The study explores how LLMs can serve as tools for cultivating practical wisdom (phronesis), empathy, and other core virtues in medical students and practitioners, thereby contributing to the development of moral character in healthcare professionals. This work positions LLMs not merely as information sources but as pedagogical tools for moral education and character development.

His broader research interests include British philosophy (particularly J.S. Mill), food ethics, and robot ethics. He published "Clockwork Courage: A Defense of Virtuous Robots" in the Journal of Philosophy of Life (2022) and has written on topics such as whether we should eat human-pig chimeras.

His research is supported by funding from the Japan Society for the Promotion of Science (JSPS) KAKENHI [Grant No. 21K12908] and the Uehiro Foundation on Ethics and Education [Grant No. UEHIRO2023-0111].
