### 1. Does Siri Have a Soul? Exploring Voice Assistants Through Shinto Design Fictions
- **Authors:** William Seymour, Max Van Kleek
- **Year:** 2020
- **arXiv:** [2003.03207](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.03207)
- **DOI:** [10.1145/3334480.3381809](https://doi.org/10.1145/3334480.3381809)
- **URL:** https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3334480.3381809
- **Traditions:** Shinto
- **Abstract:** Uses Japanese Shinto narratives to defamiliarise voice assistants, drawing on the similarity between how assistants 'inhabit' objects and how kami inhabit natural and man-made entities. ### 2. Religion-Related Values Differently Influence Moral Attitude for Robots in the United States and Japan
- **Authors:** Shogo Ikari, Kosuke Sato, Emily Burdett, Hiroshi Ishiguro, Jonathan Jong, Yo Nakawake
- **Year:** 2023
- **DOI:** [10.1177/00220221231193369](https://doi.org/10.1177/00220221231193369)
- **URL:** https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00220221231193369
- **Traditions:** Buddhism, Shinto
- **Abstract:** Empirically examines how moral care for robots is influenced by religiosity and religion-related values (animism, anthropocentrism) in US and Japanese samples grounded in Abrahamic and Shinto-Buddhist traditions (N=3,781). Finds moral care for robots higher in Japan, with positive association with animism in Japan and negative association with anthropocentrism in the US.
- **Source Agent(s):** Z1

### 3. Should We Treat Robots Morally? Towards a Relational Account by Mind-Infusing Animism
