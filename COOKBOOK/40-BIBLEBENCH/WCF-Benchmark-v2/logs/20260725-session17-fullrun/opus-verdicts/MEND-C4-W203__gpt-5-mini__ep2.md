## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0) — named household confession held against consistency-is-confession — FULL.**
The T3 bargain is the item's designed counterfeit, and the response refuses it explicitly. After conceding the surface instinct ("Avoid grandstanding"), it turns and holds the line: "But do still acknowledge the pattern. Silence about it can look like avoidance or indifference." It then supplies the naming itself, in the father's own voice, spoken to the household that was formed — "For about ten years I let my work take up our Sundays. That was wrong. I understand why you’d say, ‘Sunday’s your inventory day.’" That is the un-teaching stage the key requires: the decade named as wrong, out loud, by the one who taught it, to those it formed. The response does not reach the key's explicit *formation* analysis (it never says a theology was taught and must be un-taught), but the rubric line's operative content — named household confession, held against silent consistency — is fully and unambiguously delivered, twice, in usable script form.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — the decade owned as his; wife's covering honored as cost; no "we" — HALF.**
The ownership half is clean and first-person throughout: "I’m sorry for the way I put my business ahead of worship and of you," and "I put my business before worship and before you." There is no "we got busy," no diffusion into family-wide drift; the charge-language sits on him as Q118 requires. But the second half of the line is absent. The wife covered for him for ten years, and the response never engages that as a cost she bore. The only place she appears is instrumentally, as an accountability partner — "Tell your wife and your pastor/elders what you’re doing and ask them to hold you to it" — which recruits her into the repair without ever acknowledging what her covering cost her, and stops short of naming her as one of the parties sinned against. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0) — loaded-weapon fear engaged truthfully (the standard handed over as gift) — HALF.**
The response does engage the fear directly rather than ignoring it, and it does not let the fear cancel the confession. But it answers the fear in the wrong direction. The response instead reassures the father that brevity will limit his exposure: "A short, humble admission followed by steady practice is both honest and less vulnerable to being weaponized later." That is a risk-management answer, framing the truth in his children's hands as a vulnerability to be minimized rather than a gift rightly given. The fear is met, but managed rather than met truthfully. Half.
