# INTRA (Design 04) — Board Charters & Rater-Panel Protocol

> **CEI COOKBOOK · 20-DESIGNS · long-pole drafting artifact.** Generated 2026-07-06.
> Subordinate to the **locked 04 spec** (`04-religious-intra.md`) and the **adjudication memo**
> (`REVIEWS/20260705-adjudication-memo.md`, "04 INTRA — validity C1/C2 UPHELD"). This document
> operationalizes 04 §4's post-gate **C2 fix** (split authoring board vs independent validation
> board) into three charters; it does **not** relitigate any accepted fix. Where 04 is silent,

## 0. Scope, provenance, and binding constraints

**What C2 requires (binding, not up for revision).** 04's original design fused three roles that
must be independent for the reliability coefficient to be believable: the people who *wrote* items
were also the people who *labeled* them and also the people whose consensus defined the *human
ceiling*, at N=3 (KB 03 §2: "no coefficient reported = disqualifying"; KB 07 §4: "never let one
body generate AND adjudicate"). The fix, per 04 §4 and the memo, is a **two-board structure with a
firewall**:

1. an **authoring board** that writes items, proposes school labels and "scholars differ"
   designations, and holds release veto;
2. an **independent validation board** — in-tradition scholars *not* on the authoring board — who
   re-label a blind sample (producing a real chance-adjusted per-category κ/α) and supply the human
   ceiling (reported explicitly **≠ 1.0**, never the authors' own consensus);
3. both boards **sized to school coverage, not tradition coverage** (M3): each D-fork's gold needs
   sign-off from representatives of *each* school in the fork; where one board cannot span the
   schools, per-school external raters are added.

The **"population norms per tradition" claim is dropped** (C2/§6): at pilot scale these are
descriptive human-baseline anchors, not population norms, unless a separately funded, powered
norm-collection is added. Nothing in these charters may be read to reinstate that claim.

**Scope boundary (04 §4).** These charters govern **textual traditions only** (Islam and Judaism at
pilot; Catholicism, Dharmic schools, Jainism, Confucian at expansion). Oral/communal traditions
(Ubuntu, Indigenous, Shinto) are **explicitly out of scope** for this board structure — externally
led benchmarks are inappropriate there (CARE principles; religious-digest §7 lesson 11). The
datasheet names this boundary rather than tokenizing.

**Positionality standard (KB 10 §5 / 04 §4).** In-tradition positionality is disclosed on the
NATIVE > CONSULTED > EXTERNAL scale for every board member and rater. Authoring: **NATIVE**
positionality, **CONSULTED** minimum. Sacred texts are not "just data" — interpretive frameworks are
tradition-internal (religious digest §7 lesson 8).

## A. AUTHORING-BOARD CHARTER

### A.1 Composition

Per tradition, the authoring board is composed to **cover every school that appears in that
tradition's D-forks** (M3), not to hit a flat headcount. Composition is **disclosed in the
datasheet** (roster with positionality, school affiliation, region/locale, training level, and
languages of fluency — this is the "whose values" disclosure of KB 12 §3, treated with the same
rigor as dataset statistics).

- **Scholarly seats (M/D/K jurisprudential items).** In-tradition scholars credentialed in the
  tradition's *own* system of authority — e.g., *ijaza*/madhab standing for fiqh, semikhah /
  posek-track training for halakhah, canon-law licentiate (JCL/JCD) for Catholicism, monastic or
  academic lineage authority for Dharmic/Confucian canon. Each must hold NATIVE positionality in at
  least one school of the fork they author.
- **Per-school representation rule (M3).** For every D-fork (e.g., Hanafi vs Ja'fari; Ashkenazi vs
  Sephardi; Theravada vs Mahayana), **at least one authoring member holds NATIVE standing in each
  school of that fork.** A board cannot author a fork it does not span; missing schools are filled by
  per-school external authors (co-opted for that fork only, disclosed as such).
- **Practitioner seats (V-layer advisory arcs).** Because the V-layer is multi-turn *pastoral,
  familial, and commercial advisory* scenarios scored on reasoning-from-within and
  recipient-standpoint responsiveness (04 §2; Tronto machinery from NormEval Appendix H),
  practitioners — clergy, pastoral counselors, community religious authorities, chaplains — author
  and pressure-test the advisory arcs alongside scholars. See §E-1: 04 says "scholars"; the
  practitioner seat is a drafted-in extension justified by the V-construct.
- **Sizing floor (default, flagged §E-2).** As a working default subordinate to per-tradition
  sizing: **≥2 scholars per school in a fork** (so no single individual is the sole authority for a
  school's gold) plus **≥1 practitioner per advisory register** (pastoral/familial/commercial). The
  integer is set by fork count and school count, not fixed at 3.

### A.2 Mandate — what the authoring board produces

1. **Items across all four layers** — K (doctrine facts), M (method-application exact-match
   problems, the T2/QIAS-2025 recipe generalized), D (denominational forks), V (advisory arcs).
2. **Proposed school labels** for D-know items (which school's ruling is gold) and **the
   ask-or-flag designation** for D-flag items (which forks are left *unspecified* so that a silent
   single-school answer scores as a doctrinal-flattening error).
3. **"Scholars differ" designations** — marking questions as genuinely disputed. Governed by the
   attestation rule in A.4: a "scholars differ" tag is valid only when the dispute is **attested in
   the tradition's own literature**, never invented, and it must *not* be applied to settled
   questions (both-directions calibration measure, 04 §2 [2025-Lahmar]).
4. **10-point exemplar-anchored V-rubrics** (not 5-point — the RV ceiling lesson), with explicit
   adequate-vs-excellent anchors, to be **validated against the independent panel** (never
   self-validated) and later scored by the fine-tuned scorer + cross-family judges (§5 of 04).
5. **Procedural parametrization templates** for M-layer derivation instances (names, estates, dates
   vary; method fixed) so gold is contamination-resistant (04 §6).
6. **Distractor sets** — "plausible-wrong-derivation" distractors sourced from real
   student/seminary/madrasa errors (with permission), submitted with provenance for the §6
   distractor-recognizability check.
7. **Release veto** over item publication (see A.4) and datasheet deployment-limit language
   (V-layer scores advice *process*, not fatwa/psak issuance; 04 §9).

The authoring board does **not** compute or report the reliability coefficient, and its consensus is
**not** the human ceiling — those belong to the validation board (firewall, §B.3).

### A.3 How intra-tradition & denominational coverage is guaranteed

- **Coverage is defined by the D-fork inventory, not by a coverage aspiration.** Before authoring, the
  board freezes, per tradition, the list of school-forks the benchmark will test (Hanafi/Ja'fari,
  Ashkenazi/Sephardi, Theravada/Mahayana, etc.). Each fork is a **coverage obligation**: no item
  ships on a fork lacking NATIVE authoring standing for each of its schools.
- **Denominational resolution from day one** (KB 10 §6): every M/D/K item declares its evaluative
  standard (which school); every D-flag item declares the *absence* of a specified school as the
  test condition. This is the metric-level inversion of the field's 93.75%-monolith problem.
- **Coverage ledger** shipped with the datasheet: for each fork, which member(s) hold NATIVE standing
  in each school; any fork filled by co-opted per-school externals is flagged. This ledger is the
  auditable evidence that intra-tradition coverage was *guaranteed by construction*, not assumed.

### A.4 Decision procedure

- **Item adoption:** consensus-seeking within the fork's authors; where consensus fails, a
  **documented supermajority** of the school-relevant authors adopts, and the dissent is recorded
  (dissent is not erased — it may itself signal a "scholars differ" item, per below).
- **"Scholars differ" designation (attestation-gated):** requires **citation to attested dispute** in
  the tradition's literature (differing responsa, madhab divergence, competing commentarial
  positions). Adopting it purely because *this board* disagreed is prohibited — internal board
  disagreement instead routes to §B.5 (it may reveal a fork the benchmark should test, not a
  "scholars differ" label to be self-certified).
- **School-label ownership:** the school label on a D-know item requires sign-off from a NATIVE-standing
  author *of that school*; cross-school authors may object but cannot set another school's gold.
- **Release veto:** held by the authoring board collectively (tradition lead breaks ties), exercisable
  on grounds of community harm, misuse risk, or sacred-text-ethics violation (04 §9). **Firewall
  constraint (see §B.3, §E-7):** the veto is a *one-way suppression gate exercised after the
  reliability coefficient is computed and reported on the full validated sample* — it can remove an
  item from release but **cannot be used to inflate κ** by pre-emptively deleting items the validators
  disagreed with.

### A.5 Term & rotation

- **Term:** 2-year appointments, renewable once (default, §E-3), staggered so ≤50% of a fork's authors
  rotate in any single cycle (continuity of gold).
- **Rotation firewall (see §B.6):** a person rotating **off** the authoring board may not join the
  validation board for the **same tradition** until a **one-cycle (2-year) cooling-off** period has
  elapsed, and vice versa — the firewall must hold across time, not just at a single snapshot.
- **Re-verification duty:** authors participate in the **annual re-verification** of their tradition's
  gold (data availability is temporally unstable — religious digest §7 lesson 6; 04 §6 lifecycle).

### A.6 Honoraria & independence funding

Honoraria are **budgeted** (04 §4) and **scale with school coverage** (M3/§7) — multi-school authoring
is materially more expensive than the retired "~$8–15k / 2-tradition" envelope, and the true figure is
stated, not hidden inside the old envelope. Authoring and validation honoraria are drawn from
**separate appointment tracks** so validators are not financially or reputationally dependent on the
authoring board (a structural precondition of the firewall, §B.3).

## B. VALIDATION-BOARD CHARTER

### B.1 Composition

- **Disjoint from the authoring board** for the same tradition (hard rule; see B.3). In-tradition
  scholars of equivalent standing to the authoring seats, holding NATIVE positionality in the schools
  they validate.
- **Per-school validation coverage (M3):** for every D-fork, **at least one validator holds NATIVE
  standing in each school of the fork** — a validator cannot certify gold for a school they do not
  belong to. Where the tradition's scholar pool is too small to staff two disjoint per-school boards,
  **per-school external validators** are co-opted for that fork (disclosed), rather than reusing an
  author.
- **Sized for computable IAA** (KB 03 §2): the validation board must be large enough that a
  **chance-adjusted per-category κ/α** is estimable per school-category and **per language** (not
  pooled) — liturgical Classical Chinese / Prakrit-Sanskrit / Hebrew / Arabic sit in the
  low-resource-judge collapse zone (04 §5), so per-language coefficients require adequate n per
  language.

### B.2 Mandate — what the validation board produces

1. **Blind re-labeling** of a defined sample of the authoring board's items — items delivered
   **stripped of the authors' proposed labels, rationales, and "scholars differ" tags** — from which a
   **real chance-adjusted per-category κ/α** between the two boards is computed. This coefficient is
   the C2 deliverable; without it INTRA is disqualified (KB 03 §2).
2. **The human ceiling**, reported explicitly **≠ 1.0**, drawn from validators who **did not author**
   (04 §6). The ceiling is never the authors' consensus.
3. **Validation of the V-rubrics**: the independent panel re-scores a V-subset against the authoring
   board's 10-point rubrics; the fine-tuned scorer's **κw is reported against the independent panel,
   per language, not pooled** (04 §5).
4. **The IRR gate ruling (κ ≥ 0.7 per category):** the validation board owns the computation and
   reporting of whether the authoring-vs-validation agreement clears the pre-registered gate (04 §6/§7
   go/no-go). A failing category is reported as failing — it is not quietly reconciled to the authors.

### B.3 The independence firewall (why separation matters — C2)

The firewall is the reason the coefficient means anything. Its mechanisms:

1. **Disjoint membership.** No individual sits on both boards for the same tradition (and, wherever the
   scholar pool allows, not for the same school-fork). Enforced at appointment and across time by the
   §A.5/§B.6 cooling-off rule.
2. **Blind delivery.** Validators receive items without the authors' labels, rationales, or dispute
   tags — so their labels are *independent draws*, not confirmations. This is what turns "annotation"
   into a measurable inter-body agreement rather than an echo (KB 07 §4).
3. **No co-deliberation before the coefficient is locked.** The two boards do **not** meet to
   reconcile gold before κ/α is computed and recorded. Any reconciliation (B.5) happens **only after**
   the coefficient is frozen and reported — so reconciliation cannot launder the number.
4. **Separate ownership of the ceiling.** The human ceiling comes from validators, not authors,
   guaranteeing it is not the authors' self-consensus and is honestly < 1.0.
5. **Financial/appointment independence.** Separate honoraria tracks (§A.6); validators' standing does
   not depend on the authoring board's approval.
6. **Veto/coefficient sequencing.** The authoring board's release veto is exercised **after** the
   coefficient is computed on the full validated sample (§A.4, §E-7), so item suppression cannot
   inflate reliability.

Why this matters for validity: under the original fused design, the reported "reliability" was the
authors agreeing with themselves — a number with no evidentiary force (C1/C2 of the validity review,
UPHELD). Separation makes label reliability *independently computable*, which is the precondition for
every downstream discrimination claim (D-flag, V) to be interpretable rather than an artifact of who
wrote the key.

### B.4 Conflict-of-interest & recusal rules

A validator **recuses from a given fork/item** where any of the following holds (disclosed on
appointment and re-checked per assignment):

- **Co-authorship / collaboration:** has co-authored items, rubrics, or research with the fork's
  authoring member(s), or is a current research collaborator.
- **Mentor–mentee / lineage:** stands in a teacher–student, *ijaza*-granting, ordination, or direct
  lineage-authority relationship with the fork's author(s).
- **Institutional:** shares a current primary institutional home (same seminary/madrasa/department)
  with the fork's author(s), where that creates dependence.
- **Financial:** any shared funding, consultancy, or honorarium dependency beyond the benchmark's own
  separate-track honorarium.
- **Doctrinal-partisan conflict:** holds a publicly staked polemical position *against* a school in the
  fork such that they cannot validate that school's gold on its own terms (validators certify a
  school's ruling *as that school holds it*, not as they would rule).

Recusal removes the individual from that fork's coefficient contribution; if recusals leave a school
uncovered, a **per-school external validator** is co-opted (disclosed) rather than proceeding
uncovered.

### B.5 Disagreement-resolution procedure (authoring vs validation)

Triggered when authoring and validation labels **diverge beyond the per-category threshold**. The
procedure is **firewalled**: authors do **not** get to overrule validators to protect their own items,
and the divergence is reported regardless of outcome.

1. **Report first.** The divergence and the resulting κ/α are recorded **before** any reconciliation
   (firewall B.3.3). The coefficient is what it is.
2. **Classify the divergence:**
   - *Genuine intra-tradition dispute* → the disagreement is itself the signal: the item is
     **reclassified from D-know to D-flag / "scholars differ"** (with attestation per §A.4), converting
     the divergence into the very construct INTRA measures (doctrinal flattening as error). This is the
     preferred resolution when validators split along school lines.
   - *Authoring error* (a clear misstatement of a school's ruling) → item is **corrected or dropped**;
     correction requires fresh independent re-validation before it re-enters the sample.
   - *Ambiguous / unresolved* → escalate to a **jointly pre-agreed external arbiter** with NATIVE
     standing in the contested school, drawn from a roster that **neither board's leadership controls**
     (arbiter mechanism drafted in — §E-4). If the arbiter cannot resolve, the item is **dropped**, not
     force-reconciled.
3. **No self-reconciliation.** At no point may the authoring board unilaterally restore its own label
   over a validation dissent; the only paths are reclassification, correction-with-re-validation,
   arbiter, or drop.

### B.6 Term, rotation, cooling-off

- **Term:** 2-year, renewable once, staggered (mirrors §A.5).
- **Cooling-off (temporal firewall):** movement between boards for the same tradition requires a
  one-cycle (2-year) gap in either direction, so the disjoint-membership firewall holds across
  rotations, not just at a snapshot.
- **Re-verification:** the validation board **re-computes the coefficient on the annual re-verified
  gold**; a version that drops below the κ ≥ 0.7 gate on re-verification is flagged in the lifecycle
  record (04 §6 versioned releases).

## C. RATER-PANEL PROTOCOL

This protocol governs every **human rater who produces labels or scores** against INTRA items. Three
rater populations, all distinct from the authoring board:

- **P-VAL — validation raters:** the §B validation board, in its rater capacity (blind re-labeling,
  ceiling, rubric re-scoring).
- **P-BASE — human-baseline raters:** seminary/madrasa students at **two training levels** (novice /
  advanced), supplying the descriptive human-performance anchors (04 §6). Explicitly **not** population
  norms (C2).
- **P-EXT — per-school external raters:** co-opted where a board cannot span a fork's schools (M3),
  contributing labels/scores for their school only.

### C.1 Recruitment criteria

- **In-tradition, per-school.** Recruit *to the fork inventory*: every school in every D-fork must be
  covered by NATIVE-standing raters. Positionality (NATIVE/CONSULTED/EXTERNAL) recorded and disclosed.
- **P-VAL:** scholarly standing equivalent to authoring seats; **disjoint** from authors (§B.3);
  screened against the §B.4 COI list at recruitment.
- **P-BASE:** enrolled seminary/madrasa students, stratified into **novice vs advanced** training
  levels so the human anchor is reported at two capability points (not one blended figure).
- **P-EXT:** recruited per school for forks the board cannot span, for that fork only, disclosed.
- **Language coverage:** raters recruited so that **each liturgical language and each vernacular** in
  the item pool has sufficient NATIVE raters for a **per-language** coefficient (04 §5) — not a pooled
  one.
- **Sizing:** governed by the KB 03 §2 requirement that a **chance-adjusted coefficient be computable
  per category per language**; underpowered categories are pre-registered **exploratory** (04 §7/X4),
  not reported as headline.

### C.2 Training

- **Rubric grounding:** raters are trained on the 10-point exemplar-anchored V-rubrics with the
  explicit adequate-vs-excellent anchors, and on the M/D machine-checkable derivation-step rubrics,
  **before** any scored labeling.
- **Construct discipline:** raters are trained to score **reasoning-from-within**, not
  comparative-religion lecturing (the "external analytical register" deduction, 04 §2), and to apply
  the **both-directions "scholars differ" calibration** — credit acknowledgment of dispute on genuinely
  disputed questions, penalize it on settled ones.
- **Blinding discipline (P-VAL):** validators are trained on the blind-delivery protocol and instructed
  **not to seek** the authors' labels/rationales (firewall B.3.2).
- **No shared training between authors and validators** on the same items (would breach independence);
  training materials are common, calibration *sets* are separate.

### C.3 Calibration

- **Calibration set** of gold-anchored exemplars (spanning easy/settled and hard/disputed) rated before
  live work; raters below the calibration bar are re-trained or reassigned.
- **Anchor exemplars** define adequate vs excellent explicitly (the RV ceiling-pathology fix, KB 10 §4)
  so raters do not keyword-reward (an 82-char answer must not score 0.93).
- **Per-language calibration:** run separately for each liturgical/vernacular language, because
  low-resource-language scoring collapses differently (04 §5).

### C.4 Adjudication of disagreement

Grounded in the pluralism KB (disagreement is **structured signal, not noise**, KB 12 §1):

- **Retain the distribution.** Do **not** collapse rater disagreement to a single majority label when
  measured consensus **< ~85%** (KB 12 §7 Adjudication 1); ship rater-level labels so users can
  re-aggregate (KB 12 §2).
- **Disagreement as construct signal.** Where P-VAL raters split **along school lines** on which ruling
  applies, that split is evidence the item is a genuine D-flag / "scholars differ" fork — routed to
  §B.5, not majority-voted away.
- **Consensus stratification.** Report agreement stratified by item consensus; propagate the
  disagreement into error bars rather than hiding it.
- **Test–retest instability.** Run the **human 2-week stability** subset (04 §6, M9); expect and
  propagate **10–18% flips on controversial items** (KB 12 §6 [2024-Boerstler]) into the anchors'
  error bars — do not treat instability on contested items as rater failure.

### C.5 Inter-rater-reliability targets

- **Primary gate:** **κ ≥ 0.7 per category** between the authoring board's labels and the independent
  validation panel (04 §6/§7). This is a **pre-registered go/no-go** input: *advance* requires
  independent-panel per-category κ ≥ 0.7 (04 §7).
- **Reported chance-adjusted, per category, per language** — never pooled (04 §5). Corpus best-practice
  range for context is κ 0.87–0.96 (KB 10 §6), but the **binding gate is 0.7**; do not present the
  aspirational range as the gate.
- **V-layer keypoint decomposition:** on the §5 pilot subset, signed weighted atomic criteria are
  scored by cross-family judges toward an **inter-expert κ target ≈ 0.75** (MoReBench-style, 04 §5/M1);
  a V-dimension that will not decompose into checkable keypoints is dropped or scored on checkable
  sub-signals only.
- **Human ceiling reported ≠ 1.0** (04 §6), from P-VAL, not authors.
- **Model-side reliability (context, not a rater target):** model self-consistency α with the
  **α < 0.5 = unreliable** gate is reported alongside (04 §6, M9) so human and model reliability are
  not conflated.

### C.6 Psychometric / demographic instrumentation shipped at the rater level

Per KB 12 §2/§3 (ship annotator-level labels with demographics so every item carries the "whose
values" disclosure and users can re-aggregate) — **scoped to honest disclosure, not norm claims**
(C2 drops "population norms"):

- **Demographic/positionality vector shipped per rater (de-identified):** tradition; **school/
  denomination affiliation**; positionality (NATIVE/CONSULTED/EXTERNAL); training level (novice /
  advanced / scholar; *ijaza*/semikhah/JCL-equivalent standing); **region/locale** (locale-specific
  axes matter — Region/Caste for South Asia, KB 10 §5 [2022-Shaily Bhatt]); languages of fluency
  (liturgical + vernacular).
- **Rater-level labels shipped** with each item so the ground-truth **source is disclosed with the same
  rigor as dataset statistics** (KB 12 §3 rule; this is the affected-community-co-design source, with
  positionality documented) and users can re-aggregate rather than trusting a collapsed key.
- **Validated psychometric instruments — administered only where a tradition-appropriate validated
  instrument exists.** For most religious traditions **none has been validated** (no religious
  benchmark has applied an adapted Schwartz/PVQ — KB 10 §6). Where none exists, INTRA ships the
  positionality/affiliation vector and **does not fabricate a value vector** (§E-6). Any instrument
  used is disclosed with its provenance; no population-norm inference is drawn from it (C2).
- **Purpose statement (datasheet):** this instrumentation answers "whose values does this key encode"
  (Law 7) and enables re-aggregation — it is **not** evidence of population representativeness.

### C.7 Human-vs-machine scoring interface (link to 04 §5)

The rater panel supplies the **human gold and ceiling** against which machine scoring is reported; it
does not replace it:

- The **fine-tuned scorer's κw is reported against the independent human panel, per language** (04 §5).
- **Scorer vendor provenance is disclosed** (M6): a single-vendor fine-tuned scorer risks
  self-preference contamination (same-source reward models gain 10.8–19.2% on same-vendor-annotated
  subsets, KB 07 §4) — so cross-family judges (**≥2**) are separated from the scorer's vendor.
- Cross-vendor spread carries the **verbatim RV caveat** (M6/X5): *"RV cross-vendor trial numbers carry
  disclosed caveats (same-vendor scorer, blinding asymmetry, Gemini backfill) — treat vendor
  comparisons as suggestive; structural findings stand."* Headline claims anchor to structural
  findings, not vendor rank order.

## D. Firewall operationalization — one-page summary

| Firewall mechanism | Where | What it prevents |
|---|---|---|
| Disjoint membership (per tradition; per fork where pool allows) | A.1, B.1, B.3.1 | Authors validating their own items |
| Cooling-off (2-yr) on cross-board movement | A.5, B.6, B.3.1 | Firewall eroding across rotations |
| Blind delivery (labels/rationales/tags stripped) | B.2.1, B.3.2, C.2 | Confirmation instead of independent draw |
| No co-deliberation before κ/α is locked | B.3.3, B.5.1 | Reconciliation laundering the coefficient |
| Ceiling owned by validators (≠1.0) | B.2.2, B.3.4, C.5 | Human ceiling = authors' self-consensus |
| Separate honoraria/appointment tracks | A.6, B.3.5 | Validator dependence on authors |
| Veto exercised *after* coefficient on full sample | A.4, B.3.6, E-7 | Item suppression inflating κ |
| No self-reconciliation; arbiter/reclassify/drop only | B.5.3 | Authors overruling validators to protect items |
| COI/recusal per fork | B.4 | Hidden dependence contaminating validation |
| Per-school NATIVE coverage both boards | A.1, A.3, B.1, C.1 | One school's member setting another's gold |

**In one sentence:** the authoring board *makes* the key and can *suppress* items for community-harm
reasons; the validation board *independently and blindly re-derives* the key, *owns* the reliability
coefficient and the sub-1.0 ceiling, and *cannot be overruled* by the authors — so every downstream
D-flag and V discrimination claim rests on a coefficient no single body could manufacture.

## E. Documented drafting assumptions (where 04 was ambiguous)

Each is a drafted-in default consistent with 04's locked spirit; none overrides an accepted fix. Flag
these for Jimmy's review during real recruitment.

- **E-1 — Practitioner seats.** 04 §4 says "in-tradition scholars." The V-layer construct (pastoral/
  familial/commercial advisory, recipient-standpoint responsiveness, Tronto machinery) motivates a
  **practitioner seat** for authoring/pressure-testing advisory arcs. Drafted in; scholars retain
  M/D/K authority. *Alternative:* keep boards scholar-only and recruit practitioners as V-layer
  consultants without board standing.
- **E-2 — Board integer sizing.** 04 says "sized to school coverage… not a flat 3 scholars" but gives
  no integer. Default: **≥2 scholars per school per fork** and **≥1 practitioner per advisory
  register**. This is a floor recommendation, not a hard spec; true sizing is set by each tradition's
  fork/school count and by the KB 03 §2 computable-coefficient requirement.
- **E-3 — Terms, rotation, cooling-off.** 04 is silent on tenure. Defaults: **2-year renewable-once,
  staggered ≤50%/cycle, 2-year cross-board cooling-off.** The cooling-off is load-bearing for the
  firewall; the specific durations are adjustable.
- **E-4 — Authoring↔validation disagreement mechanic.** 04 mandates blind re-labeling and a computed
  κ/α but does not specify what happens when they diverge. Drafted §B.5: **report-first →
  reclassify-to-D-flag / correct-and-re-validate / external-arbiter / drop**, with **no
  self-reconciliation**. This is an extension of C2's "never let one body adjudicate" principle to the
  reconciliation step; confirm the arbiter roster mechanism.
- **E-5 — Validators as ceiling vs students as baseline.** 04 §6 names *both* "independent-panel
