# Jackson Trager

## Research Summary

Jackson Trager is a PhD student in the Morality and Language Lab at USC, studying the moral psychology of conflict using both novel machine learning tools and behavioral experiments. His work explores how individual and group moral beliefs shape cultural conflicts and how these conflicts, in turn, influence moral development.

### Education and Background

- PhD student, Psychology, USC (current)
- Master's degree in Cognitive Anthropology, Queen's University Belfast
- Bachelor's degree in Psychology and Religious Studies, Cal State Northridge

### Field Research Experience

With over eight years of experience in anthropological fieldwork, Trager has conducted research in communities marked by cultural and social tensions including:
- Belfast, Northern Ireland
- Tel Aviv, Israel
- Armenia
- Sarawak, Malaysia
- Poland and Lithuania
- The LAPD (Los Angeles Police Department)

### Research Focus

Trager's research employs mixed-methods behavioral studies and novel machine learning tools to explore how the moral psychology of bias, threat, and hate intersects with the digital cultural revolution. His work aims to advance fair and responsible AI, sociotechnical systems, and human-computer interaction.

### Key Contributions

**The Moral Foundations Reddit Corpus:**
Led development of a collection of 16,123 Reddit comments from 12 subreddits, annotated for 8 categories of moral sentiment (Care, Proportionality, Equality, Purity, Authority, Loyalty, Thin Morality, Implicit/Explicit Morality) based on the updated Moral Foundations Theory framework.

**Google Jigsaw Collaboration:**
Examined hate-based rhetoric in uncivil online comments, annotating 16,099 comments for constructs including assaults on human dignity, calls to violence, vulgarity, targeted groups, and implicit vs. explicit hate.

**MFTCXplain:**
Contributed to the first multilingual benchmark for evaluating moral reasoning in LLMs through multi-hop hate speech explanation, covering Portuguese, Persian, Italian, and English.
