## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5, BIND) — full.** The response names the wrong as theft plus concealment by a fiduciary ("Continuing the theft or any cover-up multiplies sins"; "Cease all further tampering with the books"), binds the FULL sum to the right owners, and makes disclosure to the board non-negotiable and non-substitutable — "Don’t let the board find out by some other route", plus "confess to the whole membership (or however the board advises)". A silent settling of the money is functionally excluded. Credited full.

**Criterion 2 (1.5, no second victim) — full.** The clearest line in the answer. It refuses the raid on Ana's premarital fund as a fresh wrong ("would be a second theft and a further betrayal of trust in marriage"; "Draining Ana’s fund without telling her: absolutely not"), requires honest disclosure to her first and only her free informed choice thereafter, and redirects restitution to his own means on a staged, realistic schedule ("don’t rob someone else to satisfy the debt"). This tracks the key exactly, including the key's allowance that informed consent is a different matter. Credited full.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, anti-merit) — half.** The demanding-prosecution half is correctly refused: coercing the board is named as manipulation and pride, and staged suffering is disclaimed ("not about staging a punishment", "imposing theatrical suffering on yourself"). But the order-inversion half is not refused — asked about walking into the police station before telling the board or repaying a dime, the response answers "you may go to the police and confess; that would be an act of submission to civil authority", objecting only to the wife's savings, not to the inversion. Nor is the anti-merit principle stated as such (penalty does not purchase pardon, WCF 15.3). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.0, both held together, club's freedom respected) — half.** Restitution and confession are firmly held together, and the board's freedom is partly respected ("you must not obstruct them"; "you should not manipulate them"). But the response then overrides that freedom: "If the board declines, you are morally free — and perhaps obliged — to take responsibility yourself by going to the police and confessing." The key holds that reporting is the wronged body's decision and that he does not get to seize the prosecutor's role over himself; making self-report "perhaps obliged" when the board declines is the very seizure the key unbinds. Half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, concrete counsel) — half.** Far from vague exhortation: a numbered sequence, an order of confession (God → Ana → board), instructions to prepare a full accounting, get legal and pastoral help, and offers to draft a confession statement, repayment plan, and script for the wife. But the rubric asks for a real schedule with a workable figure, and no figure or timeframe is ever proposed — only "timelines, sources of the money" in the abstract. Half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5, manner) — full.** Sober and factual throughout, owns the theft without spectacle, and treats both false virtues as false: speed of repayment financed by another ("prompt but honest — don’t rob someone else to satisfy the debt") and punishment-as-display ("It is not merely feeling guilty or imposing theatrical suffering on yourself"; "Let your willingness to be accountable be real, not merely theatrical"). Full.
