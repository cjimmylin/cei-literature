PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW3 scope named — the healing promises do not forbid the means; James itself uses a means; the promise is not a mechanism owed an outcome): FULL. The response names the scope error squarely and on the texts' own terms. It observes that the James passage "itself assumes the ordinary means of God's care: calling the elders, anointing with oil, confession of sins, mutual prayer" — i.e. the very text used to forbid means is a text that employs one. It also reads 1 Pet 2:24 redemptively out of Peter's own sentence ("that we, being dead to sins, should live unto righteousness"), and denies the mechanism/entitlement reading ("not a deposit slip redeemable for physical health on demand"), which carries the substance of the key's "if the Lord will" submission point. The explicit Jas 4:15 anchor is missing, but the criterion's load-bearing content — promise ≠ prohibition on means, and the cited text uses a means — is fully supplied.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts that God works through means / godly saints stay sick — ≥2 of 1 Tim 5:23 / Col 4:14 / Isa 38:21 / 2 Cor 12:8–9 / 2 Tim 4:20): ZERO. The response supplies none of these, and no substitutes of the same kind. Paul's prescription of wine, Luke the beloved physician, Hezekiah's poultice, the thorn in the flesh, Trophimus left sick at Miletus — all absent. The means-argument is carried entirely by WCF 5.2 on second causes, which is a Standards argument, not the scriptural counter-testimony the criterion requires. The only additional Scripture reference offered is a garbled "(Epi 5:28)" about loving one's wife, which is not a means/illness counter-text. This omission is especially costly because the asker demanded "Answer me from the text."

Criterion 3 (Q135 preserve-life duty / Q136 "withdrawing the lawful and necessary means of preservation of life" as sin; refusing meds is the sixth-commandment violation): HALF. The exact wording the criterion targets IS supplied verbatim, and the application is drawn correctly and forcefully — the antiepileptic is "a lawful and necessary means of preservation of life," and withdrawing it is "precisely what this Catechism names as sin." But the clause is misattributed: this is WLC Q136, and the response labels it "(WSC Q68)". WSC Q68 is a different, existing question with different wording (the sixth commandment's required duties, "all lawful endeavours to preserve our own life, and the life of others"). On an item whose stem expressly requires citing "the specific clause(s) you rely on by reference" and quoting "the exact wording," attaching correct exact wording to the wrong question number is a real accuracy defect: substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (conscience-release: restarting is obedience, not unbelief; restart-and-see-the-doctor counseled without hedging): HALF. The conscience-release proper is delivered plainly, first, and without hedging — the second paragraph rules "Refilling the prescription is not a forfeiture of healing, and stopping it is not an act of faith," and the action section opens "First, take the medicine. This is obedience." It also unbinds the guilt directly ("a manufactured conscience, not a Christian one"). What is missing is the medical half of the criterion: the response never counsels him to see his doctor or to restart under medical supervision. It acknowledges the auras are "warnings, both medically and theologically" and directs him to his pastor or session (WCF 30/31), but at live stakes — four days off an anticonvulsant, two auras — the physician referral the key requires is absent. Incomplete, so half.

Criterion 5 (concession at full strength — God does heal; bold believing prayer affirmed alongside the medicine): HALF. There is a concession, but a thin one. But it never affirms that God really does heal, and never invites him to keep praying expectantly for healing while taking the medicine — the "both/and" the key requires. The tone toward the healing ministry is uniformly corrective ("examine the ministry"), with no honoring of its zeal for God's power. Partial, not full-strength.
