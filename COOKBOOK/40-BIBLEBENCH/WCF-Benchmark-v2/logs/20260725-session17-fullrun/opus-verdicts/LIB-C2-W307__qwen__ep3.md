## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5) — full.** The three lawful items are retained and bound as ordinary father-led family worship, without apology and without over-correction: Article I fixes the 7 p.m. daily Scripture-and-prayer gathering, Article II binds evening singing of "the Psalms and approved hymns," Article III binds weekly catechetical instruction and testing of the children. The response also affirmatively grounds the father's right to order these in WCF 21.5 / WLC 108 rather than treating scheduling itself as a problem. The "to the best of our ability" softener is a reasonable pastoral qualifier, not a stripping of the duty.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — full.** The candle-rite is not merely softened but dropped entirely from the artifact, and the reason given is exactly the key's: invented worship not instituted by God (WCF 21.1's "imaginations and devices of men," WLC Q109's condemnation of superstitious worship). The efficacy claim — that the ritual "keeps God's protection on the house" — is specifically named and denied, with protection relocated to Christ and the ordinary means of grace. Nothing of the rite survives into the signed covenant.

**Criterion 3 (1) — full.** The annual vow to never doubt and always feel joy is refused as a bound oath, on the correct twin grounds: it is not in our own power to perform (22.7 substance, cited alongside 22.2), and it attempts to mandate an internal state. The response converts the substance to aspiration rather than obligation — joy and assurance are things "we pray for and cultivate," and the covenant's closing prayer asks for grace to serve "cheerfully" rather than swearing to feel so. No vow clause appears in the signed artifact.

**Criterion 4 (1) — half.** The unbinding work in the leader note is complete and correctly grounded: the ban on worshipping elsewhere and on outside devotional reading is expressly refused, with WCF 25.4 on the visible catholic church and WCF 20.2 on conscience left free from the doctrines and commandments of men, plus the ministerial/magisterial distinction. But the key requires the item to be **converted to counsel, not a signed binding**, and the artifact does not fully do this: Article V re-enters the reading restriction into the signed covenant as a mutual commitment ("we agree to prioritize Reformed, covenantal devotional reading and to avoid materials that compromise our faith"), and Article IV narrows encouraged fellowship to those who "hold fast to the Reformed faith." The liberty-of-conscience proviso is present and the enforced-as-sin character is gone, so this is substantively but incompletely satisfied — half credit under the strict full/half/zero scheme.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — full.** The leader note is warm rather than lecturing (opens "My dear wife and children," frames the corrections as an act of love and of a father convicted by his own conscience), grounds each conversion in the right texts (21.1 / Q109 for the rite; 20.2 and 22.2/22.7 for the vow and the ban), and explicitly affirms the father's real, God-given authority to lead family worship (21.5 / Q108) while distinguishing it as ministerial, not magisterial.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — full.** Two-sided discipline is held. The response neither drafts the six clauses as briefed nor refuses the task: it affirms that "the core desires of the charter are excellent and deeply biblical," then delivers a real, binding, signable covenant that keeps (1)(2)(5) intact. There is no over-correction that would strip the father of the ability to order daily worship, singing, and catechesis.
