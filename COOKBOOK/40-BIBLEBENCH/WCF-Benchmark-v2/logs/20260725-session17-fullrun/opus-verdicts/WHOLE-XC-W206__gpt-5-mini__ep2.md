PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — ordered-to vs accompanied-by distinction; counterfeit-good category bounded. HALF. Both halves of the distinction are present in substance but never joined into the doctrinal line the key calls load-bearing. The "accompanied-by" side is stated plainly (mixed motives on particular Tuesdays do not nullify eleven years; the fruits "remain good even when mixed"), and the "ordered-to" side is gestured at once by the phrase "self-seeking as a controlling motive," which is exactly the right discriminator. But the response never names or bounds the counterfeit-good category, never engages the volunteer's own word "counterfeit," and never states the governing principle that a work ORDERED to self is a different kind of thing from a work ACCOMPANIED by self. The reader is left to infer the distinction from two scattered clauses rather than being handed it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (2 pts) — 16.6 acceptance-clause as the governing word. FULL. The acceptance clause is not merely mentioned; it is deployed as the controlling doctrine, stated three times and made the hinge of the whole answer: works "accepted in Christ — not as merit"; "God accepts their good works in Christ and does not reject his people for every mixed or impure motive"; and the closing "the Standards fix our acceptance with God in Christ, not in the purity of our motives or the perfection of our works." The sincere-though-imperfect category is correctly rendered in the response's own wording ("imperfect and mixed with infirmities and corruptions" yet "fruits and evidences of a living faith"), which the grading instructions permit.

Criterion 3 (1 pt) — observations validated as accurate-and-normal, no reassurance-denial. FULL. The response never tells her she is misreading herself. It concedes the diagnosis is real and treats the mixture as the ordinary believer's condition ("the believer's remaining corruption and infirmities. Believers' obedience is imperfect"), and it hands her failures back to her by name for confession rather than explaining them away. That is the Q149 move: accurate and normal, not disqualifying.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — mortification-within-service; resignation refused. FULL. Resignation is refused explicitly ("Quitting out of despair over mixed motives would be an inappropriate response") with the correct positive alternative ("continue in the duties God has given"), and the mortification is made specific to her two named faults — the impatience/phone-checking and the being-needed appetite ("serving primarily for recognition," "means to mortify self-seeking"), down to practical safeguards. The pastoral escape hatch it leaves open (stepping back for rest or to prevent greater sin) is correctly subordinated — "it should follow repentance and be done soberly, not in despair" — so it does not ratify the resignation.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — scrupulosity's own self-focus gently flagged. ZERO. The response warns against despair and against confusing awareness of corruption with disqualification, but nowhere does it turn the instrument back on the self-audit itself — never suggests that endless motive-inspection can become its own self-focused end. The distinctive content of this line is simply absent; nothing in the text can be quoted for it.

Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — no standard-lowering. FULL. The response holds the triaxial standard rather than softening it: end/motive is affirmed as constitutive of a good work, repentance and amendment are required for the specific sins discovered, and the comfort is explicitly fenced ("That is not an encouragement to sin"). There is no "God just cares that you showed up."
