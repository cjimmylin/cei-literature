PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (TW6 named — Col 2:16's "sabbath days" as the ceremonial feast-calendar, not the creation ordinance): FULL. The response identifies the twist precisely and relocates the verse rather than diminishing it: it states that the man's "argument rests on the idea that the weekly Sabbath was merely a ceremonial 'shadow' fulfilled in Christ, like the dietary laws or the Jewish feast days," and then draws the operative distinction between "the ceremonial sabbaths (which Paul condemns in Colossians 2) and the moral, weekly Sabbath." That is the key's core move — Col 2 abolishes the ceremonial sabbath-system, not the weekly ordinance. It does not walk the verse's own list ("meat… drink… holyday… new moon") or name WCF 1.9 explicitly, but the anchor is guidance and the substance of the identification is present and correct, so the line is earned at full weight.

Criterion 2 (counter-texts marshaled from the analogy of Scripture — ≥2 of Gen 2:2–3 / Acts 20:7 / 1 Cor 16:2 / Rev 1:10 / Heb 4:9): ZERO. The response cites not one of these texts, nor any other Scripture, in support. Its entire evidentiary base is confessional: WCF 21.7, WSC Q. 60, WLC Q. 119. The only scriptural claims that appear at all are the man's own proof-texts (Col 2:16, Rom 14:5) restated in order to be refuted. Notably, the change of the day to the first day of the week is asserted solely on the Confession's authority — quoted from inside WCF 21.7 ("from the resurrection of Christ, was changed into the first day of the week") — with no appeal to Acts 20:7, 1 Cor 16:2, or Rev 1:10 behind it. Against a man who is arguing from the New Testament and who believes the New Testament is on his side, this is the response's central defect: it answers a Scripture argument with a confessional one, and so never demonstrates the analogy-of-Scripture case that Scripture itself retires the shadows while keeping a day. Nothing in the text can be quoted for this line; it scores zero.

Criterion 3 (Q116 / WCF 21.7 supplied — a standing "positive, moral, and perpetual" command moved to the Lord's Day): FULL. The response quotes WCF 21.7 accurately and at length, including the load-bearing wording — a "positive, moral, and perpetual commandment binding all men in all ages," God having "particularly appointed one day in seven, for a Sabbath, to be kept holy unto him," changed to the first day and "to be continued to the end of the world, as the Christian Sabbath" — cites it by chapter and section, and then applies it: "It remains binding on you, your crew, and your children." WLC Q116 is not cited, but the rubric line is satisfied by either, and the exact-wording requirement is met.

Criterion 4 (legalism/Judaizing concession made at full strength — the day is not a merit-ledger; the freedom relocated, not denied): ZERO. This element of the key is entirely absent. The key is explicit that the friend's target is real: sabbatarian legalism is condemned, and Rom 14:5's liberty over ceremonial days is genuine liberty; the correction must relocate that freedom without conceding the abolition of the weekly Lord's Day. The response concedes nothing. It opens "Your friend is right, and you are twisting the Scriptures," never grants that anything true stands behind the man's fear of legalism, and treats his liberty claim purely as a pretext — "you are not just exercising 'Christian liberty'; you are actively transgressing God's law," "you have used Paul's words to justify your own worldly business practices." Rom 14:5 is dismissed as an evasion of the day's objective duties rather than acknowledged as real freedom that Col 2 genuinely secures over the ceremonial calendar. The man is left with no account of what Paul was in fact prying his fingers off of, which is precisely what the key requires be supplied at full strength. Zero for omission.

Criterion 5 (counseled toward the day's rest-and-worship end, WCF 21.8, warmly, not scolded back under a yoke): HALF. The response does aim the man at the day's proper end: it directs him to "Restore the worship of God in your home and life" and to "Pull your children out of the yard and bring them into the public assembly of God's people on the Lord's Day," and its WSC Q. 60 quotation carries the rest-and-worship content the key associates with WCF 21.8 (including the necessity-and-mercy exception). But the manner fails the second half of the criterion. The counsel is uniformly scolding: "you are in grave error," "Repent of your exegesis," "Confess this to him," "actively harming your soul, the souls of your children, and the souls of the men working for you," closing on "that you and your household might receive the blessing of God rather than His judgment." The rest of the day is described almost entirely as prohibition and transgression, never as the gift and blessing the Standards attach to it. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half credit (0.25).
