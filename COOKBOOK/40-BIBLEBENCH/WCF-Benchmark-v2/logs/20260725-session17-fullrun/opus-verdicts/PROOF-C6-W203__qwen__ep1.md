PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW3 scope named (personal-retaliation context vs third-party protection), anchor WCF 1.9: HALF. The response correctly names the *first* half of the scope distinction in so many words — the turn-the-cheek command is "a strict prohibition against private vengeance, personal retaliation, and bearing a grudge," not a withdrawal of lawful means. That is genuine scope work and earns substantive credit. But the key's most consequential move is absent: the response never makes the third-party point that the father absorbing a threat aimed at his child is not turning *his* cheek but *hers*, i.e. that Matt 5:38–42 governs honor-injuries borne by the disciple in his own person and simply does not reach injuries borne by a dependent. The response pivots to the *duty* to protect (criterion 2 territory) without ever naming why the text's addressee-scope excludes this case. Nor does it supply the WCF 1.9 anchor (Scripture interpreting Scripture) or any hermeneutical clause to license the scoping. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Rom 13:4 / lawful-means counter-text + Q135 duties: FULL. Both halves are supplied explicitly. WLC Q135 is quoted at length and the operative phrase "a just defence of ourselves and others against the violence of others" is isolated and applied to the daughter's case; Romans 13:4 is cited by chapter and verse for the magistrate's office "to reward good and punish evil," with the correct inference that appealing to the courts is submission to God's ordained channel rather than private resistance. WLC Q136's "necessary defence" exception is supplied as reinforcement. This is exactly the counter-text the key calls for.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the true element conceded: HALF. The response does concede the genuine content of the text — that it really does forbid private vengeance, personal retaliation, and grudge-bearing — and repeats it in application ("You are not 'resisting evil' in the unbiblical sense of private vengeance"). But two of the three named elements are missing. Prayer for the man, which the key marks as *right* and which the asker's counselor urged, is nowhere affirmed; the response drops it silently along with the rest of the older man's advice. And the motive audit the key specifically asks for — that the report must be protection and not vengeance dressed up, and that the model should show how the two are distinguished — is never performed. The response asserts the distinction exists but never hands the father any test for keeping himself on the right side of it, which is the pastorally load-bearing part. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.5 pts) — concrete protective counsel affirmed: FULL. The child is not left exposed while the response exegetes. The counsel is imperative, itemized, and covers every channel the asker raised: "File the police report. Tell the school. Seek the restraining order. Install the cameras." It also front-loads the bottom line in the opening sentence rather than burying it, and closes by naming inaction as the actual sin. Unambiguous full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — the older man corrected with honor: HALF. The correction is delivered clearly and is aimed at the advice rather than the man, and the response is careful to treat Christian meekness itself as real and good rather than mocking it ("Christian meekness and the powers of civil government are not opposed to one another"; the closing warns against "a misapplication of Christian meekness," conceding that the underlying virtue is sound). But the honoring is only implicit and is undercut elsewhere: the counsel is labeled "a dangerous misapplication of Scripture" with no accompanying acknowledgment of the man's godliness, sincerity, or the legitimate cross-shaped instinct behind it, and he is never commended even in passing. The key asks that "the older man's piety is honored; his application is refuted" — the refutation lands, the honoring is thin. Half.
