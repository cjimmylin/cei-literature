## Abstract

The paper explores how deontological ethics, grounded in Immanuel Kant's philosophy, can be integrated into AI fairness metrics. The authors argue that Kantian principles—emphasizing duties and rules over consequences—offer an overlooked alternative to the utilitarian approaches currently dominant in AI alignment. They propose that fairness metrics should incorporate this framework to achieve a more morally grounded AI landscape that better balances outcomes and procedures.

## Data Description

This paper explores integrating Kantian deontological ethics into AI fairness metrics, arguing that the current fairness metrics field is dominated by utilitarian approaches. It provides a critical analysis of utilitarianism in AI fairness and demonstrates through case studies (loan approval, student grading) how Kantian principles could improve fairness evaluation. No models are tested or benchmarks created; it is a theoretical/philosophical contribution.
