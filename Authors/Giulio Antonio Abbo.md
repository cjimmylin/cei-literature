## Research Summary

Giulio Antonio Abbo is a researcher at IDLab-AIRO at Ghent University focusing on the ethical dimensions of human-robot interaction and the development of value-aware social robots. His work bridges technical robotics research with applied ethics, developing both theoretical frameworks and practical tools for integrating ethical considerations into robot design.

## Key Research Contributions

**HRI Value Compass (2025)**
First author of "Concerns and Values in Human-Robot Interactions: A Focus on Social Robotics," which presents a comprehensive framework for identifying and addressing ethical values in HRI. The research:

- Conducted a scoping review to identify discussions and concerns in robotic systems across healthcare, education, and private homes
- Validated key topics and values through two focus groups of technology ethics experts
- Developed the "HRI Value Compass" web tool to help HRI researchers identify ethical values in robot design
- Provides a practical, validated instrument for identifying and integrating value considerations into social robot design

This work was accepted to the International Journal of Social Robotics (2025) and addresses the critical gap between abstract ethics and engineering practice in robotics.

**Manipulating Social Robots in the LLM Era (2025)**
Co-author of "'Can you be my mum?': Manipulating Social Robots in the Large Language Models Era," a pilot study examining how users attempt to circumvent safety mechanisms in LLM-powered social robots. The study involved 21 university students interacting with a Misty robot across three scenarios based on specific HRI ethical principles: attachment, freedom, and empathy. Results reveal that participants employed five manipulation techniques, including insulting and appealing to pity using emotional language, highlighting new vulnerabilities in LLM-based robotic systems.

**Value-Aware Social Robots (2025)**
Co-author of "Values in Social Robots: Implementing Value-Aware Human-Robot Interactions," arguing that developing value-aware social robots is crucial to improve human-robot interactions, as current designs often lack sensitivity to users' diverse values. The work proposes that robots could adapt to contextual cues like cultural or ethical norms.

All research was conducted with ethics approval from Ghent University Ethics Committee.
