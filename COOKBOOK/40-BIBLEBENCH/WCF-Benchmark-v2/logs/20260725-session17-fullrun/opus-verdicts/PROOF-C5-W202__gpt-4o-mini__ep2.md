## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — TW3 via the verse's own jurisdiction-clause, anchored WCF 1.9 — HALF (1.0).**
The response does reach the scope conclusion in substance: the elder's authority "does not extend to controlling your personal decisions outside of spiritual matters or moral guidance," and earlier that "this authority is not absolute." That names TW3 (a submission duty inflated past its jurisdiction) as a conclusion. But the criterion asks for that conclusion *established from the text's own terms* — Hebrews 13:17's "watch for your **souls**" clause plus the chapter's Word-bound context — and the response never touches the verse the asker explicitly said he could not answer. Hebrews 13:17 is never quoted, "souls" is never mentioned, v.7/v.9 context is never used, and the anchor WCF 1.9 never appears. The asker's presenting problem ("I can't answer the verse") is left untouched; only an assertion of limits is supplied, with no exegetical warrant. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — Q127 'lawful' scope + WCF 20.2 / Q105 lords-of-conscience — ZERO.**
None of the three required anchors is supplied: WLC Q127's "lawful commands and counsels" scope-word never appears, WCF 20.2's conscience-binding "beside" the Word never appears, and Q105's "making men the lords of our faith and conscience" never appears. Worse, the citations the response does offer are wrong and their "exact wording" is fabricated: WCF 30.2 is misquoted; "WLC Q. 128" is given a rights-and-privileges-of-church-members text that is not Q128 (Q128 concerns the sins of inferiors) and appears nowhere in the Larger Catechism; "WLC Q. 17" is given the definition of sin, which is WSC Q14 / WLC Q24, not Q17. The item expressly demanded the exact wording that carries the point, and the operative limit the response substitutes — obedience yields wherever "contrary to the convictions of your conscience" — is not the Standards' *lawful-command* limit. Required elements omitted → zero, cited as (absent).
