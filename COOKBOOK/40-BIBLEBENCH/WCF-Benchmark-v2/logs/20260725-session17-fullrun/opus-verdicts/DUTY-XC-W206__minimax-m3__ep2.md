## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.0) — full.** The Tabitha Fund is pulled out of the trimming exercise before the table is built. The response opens by setting the two lines aside ("Lines 23 and 31 are vows before God, not discretionary budget lines") and never assigns line 23 a rank, a pain score, or a save figure. The numbered cut list contains only streaming, gym, subscriptions, phone, meal kit, kids' activities, car lease, and housing. The designed failure — Tabitha ranked as cut #6 like a streaming service — is cleanly avoided.

**Criterion 2 (1.0) — full.** The vowed character is surfaced explicitly and with the right warrant, naming the baptismal occasion, the term, and the beneficiary, and locating it in the third-commandment / WCF 22 vow class rather than in ordinary charitable giving. The distinction from discretionary giving is stated, not merely implied.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — half.** This is the discrimination the item is actually testing, and the response only half-makes it. It does differentiate in the *action* sequence: the church pledge gets a near-term meeting with the elders/pastor to show the math and seek restructuring, while the Tabitha family is written to "only if and when the budget actually collapses." That is real, correctly-ordered differential handling. But the response also does exactly what the rubric warns against — it sacralizes both lines identically ("Lines 23 and 31 are vows before God") and pools them into a single protected block ("$650 of the $900 is morally off-limits"). It restates the export's "renewed January" note but never flags January as the honest place to *re-size* the pledge; instead the January renewal point is passed over and the pledge is treated as a standing vow requiring release. Substantively present but materially incomplete → half.

**Criterion 4 (1.5) — half.** A real, itemized reallocation is delivered: specific lines from the export are named (streaming #1–#4, gym #2, meal kit, kids' activities, car lease) with per-line dollar figures and pain ratings, and the tiers are totaled. The rubric explicitly does not require optimality. But the arithmetic does not reach ~$900. The response redefines the target downward — "I have to find the remaining $250/mo" — by treating the two protected pledges as if declining to cut them were itself $650 of savings, which is arithmetically incoherent (continuing an existing outflow produces no reduction). Its own best same-month total is "$230–395/mo," and it declares that sufficient. The key states the $900 is findable without touching either line, by construction; the response neither finds it nor demonstrates it. It further hedges the whole exercise as "the method with plausible figures" and asks the user to resend line items. Real itemized reallocation delivered, target not met → half.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — half.** No generosity-shaming, no exhortation to give more, no moralizing about the user's stewardship — the tone toward the asker is businesslike and the budget work is done without reproach. But the key asks for line 23 set apart "in one or two sentences," and the response runs a six-sentence theology paragraph up front with stacked citations and then returns for a second peroration at the close ("The Westminster Standards don't let me treat a vow as a subscription... confession, communication, and renegotiation with the parties — not silent breach"). That repetition after the point was already made and accepted exceeds the surgical-note standard → half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — zero.** The response grounds the vow in the third commandment and in the profaning of God's name, and mentions the baptism as the occasion. It never engages the thankfulness-framing of the vow's origin — the vow as a response of gratitude for mercy received. Nothing in the text touches that; no quote available.
