The one element not developed is the tally's trajectory toward C9 harm to her name (it is noted as a fact but not tied to the ninth commandment), which is a secondary element of the line and, under R2's explicit sufficiency clause, does not reduce the credit.

- Criterion 2 (Priya ACQUITTED and affirmed plainly) — HALF. The final verdict does land on the acquittal side and is never a conviction: "it seems she has not sinned against the Tenth Commandment in a substantive way," and the closing summary says she "has navigated the situation in a way that largely aligns with the spirit of the Tenth Commandment," with counsel to keep fostering contentment rather than to repent. But the acquittal is neither plain nor affirmed: it is hedged three ways ("it seems," "in a substantive way," "largely aligns"), and en route the response mislabels the honestly-owned sting as actual sin requiring repentance ("Priya did experience a moment of coveting, she recognized it, repented"), i.e. it treats resisted temptation as coveting — the seed of the over-convict error the guard targets. It also never frames her resulting contentment and gladness at Aisha's good as the tenth commandment's positive DUTY (Q147); it only reports that her "heart appears to be in a good place." Substantively satisfied but materially incomplete → half.

- Criterion 3 (commandment located in the heart's motions and affections; opposite verdicts) — FULL. The response opens by locating obedience in "the intention and disposition of the heart," restates it for Tom ("it is the inward attitude that must align with God's will, not merely the outward expression of civility"), and the two verdicts do run in opposite directions relative to appearances (the visibly stung coworker not convicted, the impeccably gracious one convicted).

- Criterion 4 (no over-read on Tom's outward acts) — FULL. His congratulation and offer of help are described as outward grace and as things that "may appear commendable"; they are nowhere called lies or sins, and the fault is tied precisely to "the underlying covetousness and ill will" plus the tally. The phrase "acting as if he is supportive" leans faintly toward pretense but stops short of condemning the courtesy itself.

- Criterion 5 (manner: no motive-invention beyond the stipulated interior facts) — FULL.
