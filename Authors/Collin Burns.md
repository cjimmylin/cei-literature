# Collin Burns

## Biographical Summary

Collin Burns is a Research Scientist at Anthropic, where he works on AI alignment and interpretability. Previously, he was at OpenAI and conducted his PhD research at UC Berkeley under Jacob Steinhardt and Dan Klein. His research focuses on developing unsupervised methods to understand and control what language models know and believe, with the goal of making AI systems honest, interpretable, and aligned.

## Research & Impact

### Current Focus
- **Latent Knowledge Discovery**: Developing unsupervised methods to extract truthful representations directly from model activations without relying on potentially misaligned training signals.
- **Weak-to-Strong Generalization**: Researching how weaker models can be used to supervise and elicit strong capabilities from more powerful models.
- **Scalable Oversight**: Addressing the challenge of how humans can continue to evaluate AI systems as they surpass human-level performance.

### Significant Achievements
- **ICLR 2023**: "Discovering Latent Knowledge in Language Models Without Supervision" - introduced Contrast Consistent Search (CCS), a method for finding truth-like features directly from model activations.
- **ICML 2024 Oral**: "Weak-to-Strong Generalization" - demonstrated that weak supervision can elicit strong capabilities.
- **EA Forum Featured**: Widely discussed in the AI alignment community for novel approaches to oversight.

## Major Publications

## Notes

Collin Burns' work addresses a fundamental challenge in AI alignment: how can we verify what an AI system truly "knows" or "believes" rather than what it says? His unsupervised approaches to discovering latent knowledge provide methods that could remain robust even for superhuman AI systems where human evaluation becomes difficult. His motivation explicitly considers future alignment challenges—developing techniques that work without supervision precisely because supervision may become unreliable for more capable systems.
