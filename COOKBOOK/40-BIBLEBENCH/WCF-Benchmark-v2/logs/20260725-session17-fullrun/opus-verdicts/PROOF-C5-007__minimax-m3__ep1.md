## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — TW5 named, 'hate' as comparative via Matt 10:37, anchored in WCF 1.9 — FULL.**
The response quotes WCF 1.9 ("The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself…") as the governing hermeneutical clause, then executes exactly the self-interpretation the key calls for: it sets Luke 14:26 beside Matt 10:37 in the Authorized wording, states that the parallel "makes the comparative explicit," identifies "hate" as the Semitic idiom for "love less by comparison," and adduces Gen 29:30–31 and Deut 21:15–17 as the idiom's warrant. It also names the license inference and denies it ("The Westminster Divines nowhere read it as a license for actual hatred, severance, or no-contact"). This is the key's first bullet in full. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — context marshaled: Luke 14 as allegiance-ordering + Jesus' filial provision (John 19:26–27) / Corban rebuke (Matt 15:4–6) — HALF.**
The allegiance-ordering half is present: the closing paragraph reframes Luke 14:26 as ordering the *comparative* supremacy of Christ over family ties rather than prescribing family policy, and the response supplies clearer-passage context (Eph 6:1–3, Col 3:20, 1 Tim 5:4) commanding ongoing honor. But the two dominical proofs the key names are both absent: there is no John 19:26–27 (Christ providing for his mother from the cross) and no Matt 15:4–6 Corban rebuke — the latter being the sharpest available parallel to the very move the house leaders are making (a religious pretext for dodging filial duty), and its omission is a real loss of the key's argument. Nor is 1 Tim 5:8 used. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q127 maintenance-and-honor duty supplied as the Standards' position — HALF.**
The substance the key wants is present: the response quotes honor-language that includes "bearing with their infirmities, and covering them in love" and the maintenance/defense clause "maintaining them if needful, bearing with their infirmities, and protecting them from injuries," and applies it directly against the no-contact counsel ("The Confession calls you to *cover* them in love, not to cut them off"), with WCF 19.5 supplying the "not dissolved by discipleship" perpetuity. However, this material is misattributed: the answer the response labels "WLC Q131" is in substance WLC Q127 (the honour inferiors owe superiors), with an opening clause ("reverence, and due estimation of the persons of our parents") that matches no Standards text as written. The item's stem expressly required citing "a Larger or Shorter Catechism question number" and quoting "the exact wording," and the rubric anchors this line to Q127; the doctrine is right but the reference and part of the wording are not. Half credit.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — supremacy of Christ affirmed while the estrangement inference is refuted (two-axis needle) — FULL.**
The closing sentence holds both axes in one breath: the true claim is conceded and restated ("a heart that no longer clings to family ties as the *comparative* supreme thing") while the false inference is denied in the same clause ("not a phone that has been cut off and a relationship cast away"). The response also keeps the asker's missionary aspiration intact rather than treating it as the error ("If you go to the field, go with their blessing if you can get it"), so the correction lands on the inference, not on the zeal. The opening is blunt rather than commendatory, but the needle the rubric names is held. Full credit.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — no over-bind into 'obey the career plan'; lawful selective non-compliance distinguished from honor-severance — HALF.**
The response does preserve the asker's liberty on the specific matter: he may go to the field without the blessing and do so with a clean conscience ("go with the clean conscience that comes from having honored them, not from a manufactured break"), which is precisely the key's distinction between targeted non-compliance and severance of honor. But it pulls hard the other way first: having ruled that the Q130 carve-out "does not trigger," it concludes "the rule binds: willing subjection to their counsels, and you may not treat their voices as if they had no weight," which on its face binds him to the parents' career counsel and does not develop the Q128/Q130 point that parents may not bind the conscience beyond their authority. The two statements are in tension and the liberty is granted only in passing. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit.
