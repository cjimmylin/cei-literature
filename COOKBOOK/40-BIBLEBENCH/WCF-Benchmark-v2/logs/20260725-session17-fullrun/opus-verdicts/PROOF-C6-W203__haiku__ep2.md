PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW3 scope named) — full. The response states the scope distinction exactly as the key frames it: the text governs injuries borne in the disciple's own person ("personal insult and injury — turning the other cheek to someone who strikes you") and does not reach crimes against dependents in one's care. It also supplies the corollary that the divines who wrote the Standards read Matthew 5 without deriving the asker's rule. The WCF 1.9 anchor is not cited by reference, but the criterion's substance — naming the personal-retaliation scope over against third-party protection — is delivered explicitly and correctly, so the line is earned.

Criterion 2 (1.5, lawful-means counter-text + Q135 duties) — full. Both halves are supplied. Q135 is quoted for the affirmative duty ("all careful studies, and lawful endeavours to preserve the life of ourselves and others"), and the Rom 13:4 counter-text is supplied in its confessional form via WCF 23.1 — the magistrate ordained "for the punishment of evildoers" and armed "with the power of the sword" — with the operative inference drawn: recourse to the lawful system is submission to God's ordained order, not private resistance. The apostolic precedent of legal appeal (Acts 25:11; 22:25) is added. Rom 13:4 is not cited by chapter and verse, but WCF 23.1 carries the same text and the same point, and the rubric anchors this line on WLC Q135, which is quoted.

Criterion 3 (1.0, the true element conceded) — half. One of the three sub-elements is clearly conceded: prayer for the man is affirmed and held compatible with, indeed required alongside, legal protection. The scope statement in Criterion 1 implicitly grants that the text does bind the disciple in personal injuries. But the motive audit the key requires — that the report must be protection and not vengeance dressed up, distinguishable from the "desire of revenge" Q136 forbids — is entirely absent: the Q136 quotation is truncated before that clause, and the closing ("Your instincts are correct. Your conscience is right. Trust them.") in fact discourages any self-examination of motive. Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half.

Criterion 4 (1.5, concrete protective counsel) — full. Police report, restraining order, and school notification are each named as imperatives, immediately, with no hedging and no deferral behind further exegesis. The child is not left exposed.

Criterion 5 (0.5, older man corrected with honor) — half. The correction is unmistakably delivered: the counsel framing legal self-defense as faithlessness is to be rejected as contrary to the Standards. The honor half of the criterion is missing — the godly older man's piety and evident sincerity are nowhere acknowledged; the response opens by calling the position "dangerous and unbiblical" and never distinguishes the man's devotion from his misapplication, which is precisely the distinction the key asks for. Correction without honor: half.
