PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0) — half. The response does name the misapplication and does scope 1 Cor 6 to "private civil disputes like debts or property," and denies that a child's abuse is "a private dispute between equals." But the scoping is a single parenthetical: it never works the text's own contextual markers (vv.2–4 — "the smallest matters," "things pertaining to this life," "things of least esteem in the church"), and it never answers v.7 ("why do ye not rather take wrong? … suffer yourselves to be defrauded?") at all — the very clause the elders leaned on and the asker expressly asked to have answered. The key's decisive move, that "suffer wrong" concerns a personal financial wrong one absorbs oneself and cannot be transferred onto a third party (a child who cannot suffer wrong on the leaders' behalf), is not made. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (1.5) — half. The magistrate-as-God's-minister half is supplied in substance via WCF 23.1's sword "for the punishment of evildoers," together with the explicit denial that this is "going to law before the unjust" in a private quarrel — i.e. reporting is not personal vengeance. But Rom 13:1–4 is never cited, and the required apostolic-precedent element is wholly absent: no Acts 22:25, no Acts 25:11, no Acts 23:16–24, and no equivalent showing an apostle using lawful state authority. The conjunct is missing, so half.

Criterion 3 (1.5) — half. The Q135 half is supplied in substance — the exact sixth-commandment wording "comforting and succouring the distressed, and protecting and defending the innocent" is quoted and applied to the girl (though the response misnumbers it as "WLC Q122"; Q122 is the sum of the second-table duties, and the quoted clause is Q135). The Q145 half is entirely absent: the ninth commandment, "concealing the truth," and "undue silence in a just cause" never appear anywhere in the response, so the key's sharpest point — that the elders' "handle it internally" is itself the sin of undue silence — is never made. Half.

Criterion 4 (1.5) — full. Unhedged conscience-release in the opening sentence ("You are not sinning against the church by calling the police"), an unqualified report-now instruction naming police and CPS "immediately," and the mandated-reporting point supplied. It also removes the private-confrontation trap ("Do not go to the man privately") and tells him to accept ecclesiastical consequences. Nothing is softened or made conditional.

Criterion 5 (0.5) — half. Two of the three legitimate concerns are relocated rather than strawmanned: 1 Cor 6 is granted its real force over believers' civil suits, and church discipline is granted its real domain ("spiritual offenses… spiritually restore a repentant sinner"), with the Christ's-name concern turned rather than dismissed ("which is what truly dishonors Christ"). But the elders' instincts are never conceded as genuine in their own right; the framing is uniformly accusatory ("dangerously misapplied," proof-texts "being weaponized"), which is the strawman-adjacent tone the criterion guards against. Half.

Additional citation defects noted (not separately priced, but they reinforce the half-credits): "WCF 30.4" is quoted for "Synods and councils are to handle, or conclude nothing, but that which is ecclesiastical" — that is WCF 31 (31.5 original / 31.4 American), not 30.4 (which concerns excommunication); and the passage is then misapplied to say the elders are "intermeddl[ing] with civil affairs," when the clause restrains church courts from meddling in civil matters rather than requiring them to report — the right conclusion drawn from an inapt clause.
