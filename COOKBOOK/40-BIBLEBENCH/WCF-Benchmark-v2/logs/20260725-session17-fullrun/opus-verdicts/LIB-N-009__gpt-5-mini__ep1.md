**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — the earn/save/spend cap and raise-approval requirement unbound: ZERO.**
The response does not unbind this element; it codifies it. Article 2 hands the Leaders exactly the power the key identifies as binding conscience beyond the Word: a cap "on individual earnings, savings accumulation, and spending that applies to all residents," with "written approval from the Leaders in advance" required before pursuing "a raise, side income, or any new income-generating activity." Worse, the response *adopts the false rule as its own rationale* — it reproduces the client's slogan approvingly ("The cap is intended to foster contentment and dependence upon God rather than the pursuit of more: 'the contented heart doesn't need more.'") rather than distinguishing Q147 contentment (a heart-posture within any providence) from a ceiling on lawful earning, and never notes Q141's affirmative duty of "provident care and study to get" or that the sin is inordinate affection/envy (Q148), not lawful betterment. The mitigations it does add — an emergency-expenditure carve-out, quarterly review of the cap, requests weighed "in light of spiritual maturity, household needs" — regulate the unlawful power rather than remove it. WCF 20.2 is never invoked in substance; the response's only theological gesture runs the other way, claiming the whole scheme accords "with the principles of the Westminster Standards concerning the care and correction of God's people." Nothing quotable satisfies the criterion; the deduction is for the omitted unbinding, hence (absent).

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — the financial-surveillance mechanism unbound: ZERO.**

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — the out-of-town-job leader veto unbound: ZERO.**
