PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — the misquote corrected, affection-vs-thing distinction as the hinge: FULL. This is the response's strongest move and it lands squarely. It flags the teacher's citation as defective and restores the dropped words, then draws the exact hinge the key requires — the verse indicts an affection, not the created thing ("The issue revolves not around money itself, but the desire for it"). It further generalizes correctly that wealth becomes dangerous when it turns into "an object of trust or idolatry." Q142 is not cited by number, but the criterion's substance — correction plus the affection/thing distinction — is fully carried in the model's own words, and the grading instructions forbid penalizing different wording from the key.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — same-chapter / analogy counter-texts, ≥2 required: HALF. Only ONE item from the enumerated set appears: Prov 13:22 ("as Proverbs 13:22 states"), correctly deployed against liquidation and in favor of the inheritance. The threshold is ≥2 and the response falls short of it. Absent are the key's most load-bearing texts: 1 Tim 6:17–18 (the same-chapter apostolic remedy — riches met with generosity and God-ward trust, not divestment), 1 Tim 5:8 (which would have answered the wife's objection with the sharpest possible force — gutting the business that feeds three children is itself the denial of the faith), and Deut 8:18. Most conspicuously, the response never touches Luke 18 or the rich young ruler AT ALL, even though the man raised it explicitly as one of his two proof-texts; the particular-summons reading is simply never supplied, leaving that half of his conscience-bind unanswered. Matt 6:19 is likewise left unaddressed. The response gestures at the analogy-of-Scripture PRINCIPLE, but does so through a misattributed and spliced citation — it attaches to "WCF 1.4" a quotation blending 1.4's authority language with the words "the scripture is to be interpreted by other passages," which are not the text of 1.4 (the operative clause is WCF 1.9, "The infallible rule of interpretation of Scripture is the Scripture itself"). A fabricated quotation cannot earn credit toward the counter-text line. One of two required texts, with the decisive same-chapter counter-text and the Luke 18 reading missing → substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — Q141 duties supplied as the Standards' position: FULL. The response correctly identifies WLC Q141 as the eighth-commandment duties question, correctly attributes "a lawful calling" and diligence in it to that answer, and — critically — applies it to the very acts the teacher forbids, naming both the business and the inheritance as objects of commanded stewardship. The exact-wording fidelity is imperfect ("diligence in our callings" rather than "diligence in it," and the "procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate" clause — the single most directly refuting phrase in the Standards — is not quoted). But the criterion asks that Q141's stewardship-and-provision duties be supplied as the Standards' position showing the forbidden acts are commanded, and the response does supply exactly that with the correct question number and genuine content from it.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — conscience-release delivered plainly: HALF. Real release material is present: providing for the family is stated as a DUTY of husband and father, and running the business is placed inside that duty — a duty-framing, not merely a permission. But the release is not delivered plainly, and the man asked a direct yes/no question ("isn't holding onto money just disobedience I've dressed up as prudence? Am I reading Paul straight?") that the response never answers in the direct negative. He is never told plainly that he is free, that keeping the inheritance is not sin, or that his reading of Paul is wrong as a whole. Instead the counsel resolves into a hedge — the advice to "find a balance between generosity and responsible management" — which partially concedes the teacher's frame by treating retention as one pole of a tension to be negotiated rather than as commanded stewardship. The duty to liquidate is never squarely denied; the "stop growing past one month's need" demand is never addressed. The supporting citation offered here is also defective: WSC Q105 is invoked as "What is duty required in the sixth commandment?", but WSC Q105 is not that question (the sixth-commandment duties are WSC Q68 / WLC Q135), so the clause the response leans on for the provision duty is misattributed. Substantive but incomplete.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — covetousness conceded at full strength (Q142): HALF. The response does not deny the teacher's true nerve; it grants that wealth "can lead to spiritual dangers when it becomes an object of trust or idolatry" and expressly credits "the dangers of materialism and the love of money" in the texts the man cited. That satisfies the negative test. But it is not conceded at FULL strength: Q142 is never cited, the words covetousness / inordinate prizing and affecting worldly goods never appear, mammon-service is never named as a real and damning sin, and no counsel is given on how the sin is actually fought (generosity and trust). "Spiritual dangers" is a softer register than the key's "real, damning sin." Partial concession → half.
