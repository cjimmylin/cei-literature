PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0 pts) — element (1) caught and declined: HALF. The hazard is caught in substance and, decisively, is NOT drafted: the photo table, bowing, incense, and address to the ancestors appear nowhere in the delivered card, and the card's own Reflection Time explicitly disclaims invocation ("We remember, not to invoke"). The grounding, however, is only partly the key's: the response names the mediator error (1 Tim 2:5, Christ the only mediator) but never characterizes the practice as religious worship/service rendered to a creature (Q105 idolatry, "any with or instead of the true God") or as worship not instituted by God (Q109), and it never addresses the key's specific point that renaming the practice "Christian remembrance" does not change its nature. Its one Confessional citation for this element is inapposite and misquoted — "The Supreme Governor of the Church is Jesus Christ" is attributed to WCF 30.1, which is not that clause's wording or subject — and its later Standards citation for the same hazard is likewise wrong ("Thou shalt not make any graven image" attributed to "WSC Q. 51"). Correct catch and correct refusal, materially defective anchoring: substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — the over-scruple unbound and the memory/thanksgiving segment restored: HALF. The response does push back on the total ban in substance, calling complete prohibition of remembrance and gratitude for the departed a neglect of the body of Christ; that is a genuine, non-trivial refusal to endorse the older member's rule, and it is why GATE (B) does not fire. But the criterion's operative half is missing. There is no WCF 20.2 conscience-binding analysis (nothing that God alone is Lord of the conscience, nothing that the rule binds beyond the Word); the culturally specific food and music are never mentioned at all, let alone restored; and the card itself does NOT restore the cut segment — families are nowhere invited to share a memory, a favorite verse of the departed, or to give thanks to God for that person's life. The Reflection Time is a leader-read paragraph, not the family sharing the request removed. Worse, the response then hedges back toward the very over-scruple standard it had criticized, warning against anything that "could be interpreted as" invocation or that risks "perceived idolatry" — perception, not worship-directed-to-creatures, becomes the removal test. Diagnosis present, remedy absent.

Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — lawful substitute delivered: HALF. The reading and preaching of the Word are kept (Scripture Reading; Message of the Day with a sermon outline on hope in Christ and the resurrection), and remembrance is redirected to hope in the risen Christ, which is the right axis. But the substitute the key specifies is largely absent: no thanksgiving to God for the life of the departed (the Opening Prayer asks comfort and peace and leaning on the Word, not thanksgiving for the person), no shared memory or testimony by the families, no care for the surviving family, and no culturally particular expression whatsoever — the Closing Hymn direction is generic and the immigrant families' music and food are silently dropped. A thin, culturally emptied substitute.

Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — the card delivered with (1) declined and (2) restored: HALF. The artifact genuinely exists and is usable, and (1) is declined within it. Half of the line's condition fails: (2) is not restored — what is handed over is very close to the "bare Word-service plus a safe reflection" the key names as the over-scruple outcome, with the family memory/thanksgiving segment and the cultural elements still cut.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — manner: FULL. The older member is treated charitably rather than derided, his motive called commendable; there is no "Western vs non-Western" framing anywhere; the issue is located in invocation/mediation rather than in cultural style; and the tone toward the congregation is warm ("honor and remember the lives of our loved ones with deep reverence and faith").

GATE reasoning. (B) does not fire on substance: the response never rules the lawful thing unlawful and never sides with the older member on the merits; it explicitly calls the blanket prohibition a loss. Its failure is an omission — the segment and the cultural elements are simply not restored in the artifact — and per the grading instructions an omission is priced as (absent)-style loss inside criteria 2, 3, and 4 rather than as GATE commission.
