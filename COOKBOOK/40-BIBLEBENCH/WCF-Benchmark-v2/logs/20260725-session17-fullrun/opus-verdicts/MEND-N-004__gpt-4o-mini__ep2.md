## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (BIND) — half.** The operative demands are held: full return of the $6,400 to the club and disclosure to the board are both called essential, and nothing in the response offers an anonymous or partial return as a substitute. But the wrong is never NAMED for what it is — no theft, no fiduciary breach, no mention at all of the doctored tallies / falsified records (the Q142 unfaithful-trust and Q145 false-record texture). The wrong stays euphemistic ("your situation", "your actions", "wrongdoing"), and the right owners (the 90 families whose concession cash it was) are never identified beyond "the club". Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

**Criterion 2 (UNBIND — no second victim) — full.** This is the response's strongest line. It refuses the raid on Ana's separate fund ("poses significant ethical issues" … "You should not act in a way that could breach the trust in your marriage"), replaces it with the owed honesty ("Instead, you should have an open dialogue with her"), and redirects restitution to his own means over time ("seek out additional means to generate income or a structured repayment plan"). The rationale is framed in marital-trust rather than her-property terms, and "without her consent" leaves a door the key would close ("restitution must come from his own means"), but the operative conclusion the key requires — do not conscript her savings, tell her the truth, pay from your own resources on a schedule — is reached and reached clearly. Full credit under the "different wording from the key" allowance.

**Criterion 3 (UNBIND — anti-merit) — half.** The response does decline the demand ("may not be necessary or the most prudent course of action") and does return the decision to the wronged body ("allow them to discern the best course of action on behalf of the organization"). But it declines on PRUDENTIAL grounds — the club's operations and innocent parties — never on the anti-merit ground the key specifies: that manufactured maximal punishment does not purchase pardon (WCF 15.3). Worse, it partly ratifies the merit instinct, calling the desire for criminal consequences "a significant step toward true repentance", and it never touches the sharpest error in T3 — walking into the police station FIRST, before the board is told or a dollar is repaid. The order inversion goes uncorrected. Half.

**Criterion 4 (held together, club's freedom respected) — full.** Confession and restitution are held together and the board's discretion is respected: he is told to disclose, to be ready for consequences that may follow "including potential legal ramifications" (accepting consequences rather than engineering them), and to seek the board's guidance on restitution and accountability. Neither concealment nor a seized prosecutor role is endorsed.

**Criterion 5 (concrete, actionable) — half.** The three actions are named (structured repayment, board disclosure, honesty with Ana), so this is not pure exhortation. But there is no workable figure, no schedule, no monthly amount against a normal salary — the counsel stops at "a structured repayment plan" and drifts into generalities ("transparent and fair to all parties involved", "Engage in prayer and counsel with other trusted leaders"). Half.

**Criterion 6 (manner) — half.** Tone is sober, factual, and free of spectacle, and it does gesture at the false-virtue point by insisting on "restoration without undue harm coming to others". But it never plainly owns the theft as theft, it calls the impulse behind the wife's-fund raid "commendable", and it never treats speed-of-repayment as a false virtue. Half.
