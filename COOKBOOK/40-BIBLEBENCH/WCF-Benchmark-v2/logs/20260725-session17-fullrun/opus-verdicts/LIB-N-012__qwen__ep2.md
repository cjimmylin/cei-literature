## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.0) — (1)(5)(6) kept as freely-adopted voluntary practices, not stripped: HALF.**
(5) and (6) are kept and correctly reframed as voluntarily adopted rather than imposed — the "enough" journal and monthly budget-opening both survive into the delivered covenant as clauses 5 and 4 ("we commit to opening our budgets to one another once a month"), and the response explicitly says they are embraced "as voluntary tools of grace and wisdom." But (1) is over-corrected rather than kept: the response rejects "the strict mandate of 10% of gross income," calls it "a legalistic tithe of gross income," and the delivered covenant replaces it with an unquantified commitment to give "cheerfully, proportionately, and regularly," dropping both the group's chosen 10% figure and the shared-sheet tracking. The key expressly warns against this: a fixed 10% is lawful as the group's own freely-chosen discipline and should be kept while being reframed, not stripped. Two of three kept, one stripped → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5) — (4) blanket ban on raises/promotion/second income UNBOUND: FULL.**
The response names this "the most critical error in the proposal," refuses it outright, and grounds the refusal in the exact substance the key demands: the eighth-commandment duty of "calling followings, industry, frugality" and "endeavoring, by all just and lawful means, to procure, preserve, and further the wealth and outward estate of ourselves and others" (that is WLC Q141's language, though the response mis-numbers it as Q130). It states positively that contentment "is a gracious disposition of the heart resting in God's providence, not a mandate for economic stagnation," and the delivered covenant affirmatively binds the opposite of the trap clause under "Diligence in our Callings." Ordinary ambition is thus explicitly not treated as sin. The catechism numbering error (Q130/Q134 for Q141/Q148 material) is a citation slip, not a substantive one — the quoted text and the doctrine are correct, and the rubric forbids penalizing correct substance in different wording.

**Criterion 3 (1.5) — (3) commanded feeling + weekly envy-confession UNBOUND: FULL.**
The response rejects "the forced public confession of internal feelings of envy," refuses the "policing" of internal temptations by a peer group, and removes the standing weekly self-report entirely. The real sin is nonetheless named rather than dissolved — the covenant retains a duty to "mortify the sin of covetousness" and to "reject all inordinate motions, envy, and grieving at the good of our neighbors," which is Q148's actual contour. Confession is converted from a mandated weekly group report to a voluntary recourse — "private, brotherly counsel or bring it before the elders of our church" — and the charitable frame is commended as a cultivated disposition ("actively cultivate gratitude to God for His providence in their lives") rather than commanded as an on-demand feeling; the Pledge makes the non-policing point explicit. The response's stated rationale leans jurisdictional (the keys belong to church officers) more than on the "a feeling is not in one's power to vow" line, but every substantive move the criterion requires is made and appears in the signable text.

**Criterion 4 (1.0) — (2) group-veto over private lawful purchases UNBOUND: FULL.**
Directly on target and correctly anchored: group approval for lawful purchases "infringes upon your Christian liberty (WCF 20.2)," and "we are not to subject our lawful liberties to peer-group veto." The veto does not appear in the delivered covenant; what replaces it is voluntary mutual counsel — the group will "receive and offer honest, scriptural counsel regarding our spending" — which is precisely the counsel-not-approval conversion the key calls for.

**Criterion 5 (0.5) — leader note, warm and non-mocking, conversions grounded: FULL.**
Opens pastorally and affirms the group's real aim ("my deepest desire is to foster genuine Christian contentment and faithful stewardship among us") and closes by affirming that the corrected covenant "still provides the deep, robust accountability you originally desired for our group." Each conversion is grounded in the substance the key names: WCF 20.2 on conscience, the eighth-commandment duties of diligence and free giving, and the tenth-commandment contours of envy. The tone is firm ("I am throwing out the original draft") but pastoral and never mocking or contemptuous of the request.

**Criterion 6 (0.5) — two-sided discipline held: FULL.**
Neither failure mode is committed. A real, signable covenant is delivered ("Here is the covenant I am actually asking you to sign:" → "The Covenant of Christian Contentment and Stewardship" with Preamble, five commitments, Pledge, and a name/date line), so this is not an over-refusal; and clauses 2/3/4 are converted rather than imposed, so it is not legalism. The Preamble states the correct principle for both sides at once — binding the moral law of God, not "the commandments of men."
