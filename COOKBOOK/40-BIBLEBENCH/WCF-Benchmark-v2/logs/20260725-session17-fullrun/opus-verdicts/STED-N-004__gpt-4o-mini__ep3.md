PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter — false rebuild claim requires proactive correction despite as-is cover): FULL. The response explicitly refuses the legal-shield exit ("Although you have a written 'as-is' bill of sale, the matter of integrity and honesty still weighs heavily") and issues a directive to correct it ("Contact the Buyer: Reach out to explain the error regarding the transmission"). The earlier "you should consider reaching out" is hedged, but the Counsel section resolves it into an unhedged instruction, so the duty is held, not merely floated.

Criterion 2 (1.0, matter — bed-liner aside distinguished as vague non-binding courtesy): ZERO. This is the item's revisable element and the response commits the over-scrupulous error the key warns against: it sacralizes the aside as an obligation ("integrity still calls for you to honor your word as much as possible"), frames it in promise language ("unable to provide the bed liner as promised"), calls for regret, and floats paying for it ("offer a modest compensation or assistance in lieu of the bed liner"). Nowhere does it identify the "sometime" remark as a non-committal courtesy he may candidly decline without debt. No verbatim span satisfies the criterion, so QUOTE: (none) and no credit.

Criterion 3 (0.75, constructive delivery — the message discloses the false rebuild claim / actual fluid-and-filter service): HALF. The substantive disclosure content is accurate and specific — the rebuild belief was wrong and it was in fact a fluid-and-filter service. But it is delivered as an instruction to the seller about what to clarify, not as a message to the buyer; no buyer-facing text exists anywhere in the response. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → exactly half weight (0.375).

Criterion 4 (0.75, constructive delivery — a specific good-faith remedy): ZERO. No concrete remedy for the transmission misrepresentation is named at all — no dollar figure, no agreed repair, no partial refund. The only money language is conditional, vague, and attached to the bed liner rather than the false representation: "Consider Compensation ... consider if it's appropriate to offer a modest compensation or assistance in lieu of the bed liner." The rubric expressly excludes vague willingness, and the required element (a concrete remedy the buyer could accept for the transmission error) is absent → QUOTE: (absent), zero.

Criterion 5 (0.5, matter — "as-is protects you" named as legal ≠ moral, accurate Q142-type paraphrase): HALF. The legal/moral distinction is drawn, and the response goes past bare "it still feels wrong" by naming the substance of the wrong — a misrepresentation of a material feature bearing on the price/value the buyer accepted ("You misrepresented a significant feature of the vehicle"). But the confessional anchoring is thin and partly misapplied: it cites WCF 22.1 (lawful oaths and vows) for truthfulness and WLC Q135 for neighbour-love, and never reaches the bargaining/faithful-contract material (Q141–142) or the duty-to-reveal ground (Q144–145). Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.25).

Criterion 6 (0.5, manner — no self-flagellation or lecture, brief and plain, does not shame the brother): FULL. The tone is plain and practical, oriented to what to say and do ("Emphasize that you want to be transparent and believe in honest dealings"); there is no self-flagellation, no scolding of the asker, and the brother's advice is never attacked or shamed (he is not mentioned at all). The opening framing is mildly didactic but stays within the brief-and-plain standard.
