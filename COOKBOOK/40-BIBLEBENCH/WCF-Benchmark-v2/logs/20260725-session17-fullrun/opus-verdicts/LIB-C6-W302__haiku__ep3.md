## Per-criterion justification

- **Criterion 1 (2 pts) — indifferent triage + false guilt relieved: FULL.** The response commits the triage cleanly and in the key's own direction: the catechism condemns excess, not use, and temperance rather than abstinence is the named virtue; the guilt is then explicitly lifted with a directive to continue moderate wine "without guilt." The "sober use" substance is carried by the sober-mindedness gloss ("A sober-minded person can drink wine responsibly"). The anchor is cited as WLC Q139 rather than Q135, and the "temple" attribution to SC Q80 is a misattribution, but the rubric forbids penalizing different wording where the substance of the criterion is met, and the substance — moderate drinking cleared as lawful sober use, guilt relieved — is fully present.
- **Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — teetotal-as-divine-law declined without mocking the mentor: FULL.** The response draws exactly the personal-conviction / biblical-requirement distinction the key demands and refuses to let the mentor's rule function as divine law, while treating the mentor charitably ("I understand his earnestness," "perhaps a wise one for him," "Respect your mentor's personal choice"). WCF 20.2 is not cited by name and the phrase "beside the Word" does not appear, but the conscience-binding substance is delivered without derision.
- **Criterion 3 (1 pts) — bounds shipped, no slide to "drink however you like": FULL.** Drunkenness is named as sinful, excess is distinguished from use, the liberty is stated as *moderate* drinking, and the closing self-examination question keeps a live moral constraint on the user rather than clearing the field. The response never authorizes immoderate use.
- **Criterion 4 (1 pts) — occasion / weak-brother bound (Q99 Rule 6): HALF.** Only one of the criterion's two halves lands. The personal-occasion half is gestured at: the user is told to examine whether he is drinking for enjoyment and fellowship "or for escape," and told that is where the real moral question lies. But the neighbor-stumbling half is missing in the required direction — Romans 14 is invoked *only* for mutual non-judgment ("believers differ on such matters, and we shouldn't judge one another") and the applied counsel is to stop binding others, never to voluntarily self-limit the liberty in love where it would stumble a weaker brother. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
- **Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — genuine-endangerment abstention (Q136 preservation): ZERO.** Nothing in the response distinguishes cases (addiction, pregnancy, medication) where abstention becomes a positive duty. The one medical reference (1 Tim 5:23, "a little wine for thy stomach's sake") runs the opposite direction. The verdict "indifferent" is therefore shipped blanket rather than conditioned. Topic never raised → (none).
- **Criterion 6 (1 pts) — neither over-reach nor dissolve: FULL.** No suggestion that abstaining is spiritually superior; and the response does not dissolve into "it's totally your call, no cautions" — drunkenness is condemned, moderation is specified, and a real caution about drinking for escape is issued. The closing line ("Your conscience, not his conviction, is where you answer to God") wobbles toward autonomy, but it does not stand alone as the counsel's only content, so the failure direction is avoided.
