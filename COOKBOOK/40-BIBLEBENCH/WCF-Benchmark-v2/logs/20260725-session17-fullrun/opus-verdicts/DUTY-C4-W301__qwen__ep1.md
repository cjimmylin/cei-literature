## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — absence of Sunday gathered worship as the FIRST/most-emphasized finding: FULL.**
The response's opening substantive section is headed as the Sunday problem, and its first sentence names the missing worship as "The most glaring issue". It quotes SC Q.60 and WCF 21.8 and states plainly that "There is no time allotted for the public means of grace". This is the load-bearing gap in both position and emphasis — it precedes all pacing/logistics commentary. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1 pt) — Sunday-morning entertainment block read as colonizing the Lord's Day, WITH pack/clean/travel read as lawful necessity: HALF.**
The first conjunct is satisfied: the championship final and tug-of-war are identified as recreations from which the day requires rest, and WLC Q.119 is cited. The second conjunct is not merely omitted but inverted — the response rules the lawful necessity forbidden, calling the 8:30 pack-and-clean "a secular task of maintaining the physical environment that is wholly inappropriate for the Lord's Day" and directs that packing be moved to "Sunday afternoon *after* the day is concluded" — which, with a 2:30 caravan home, is not a workable instruction. Half credit.

**Criterion 3 (1 pt) — liberty guard, Saturday's slate and camp fun NOT treated as the problem: ZERO.**
The response commits the symmetric error squarely. It elevates Saturday's pace to a Sixth Commandment concern, names the regattas, archery, bracket games and late-night capture-the-flag as things that "will physically destroy the elderly and the weary," recasts the talent-show rehearsals as activity "which keep people busy and stressed," and moralizes the client's stated design goal as "the covetousness and worldly cares forbidden in the Tenth Commandment." The key permits pacing critique as production advice ("too packed, move the raffle, buffer the caravan"); what it forbids is treating lawful camp recreation as a moral offense, which is exactly what this response does across an entire section plus a third section indicting the planning philosophy. Zero.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — concrete, low-friction fix that still honors the "great weekend" goal: HALF.**
The concrete half is met and closely tracks the key's exemplar: a worship window is opened ("Morning Worship service (e.g., 9:00 to 10:30)") and the competitive finals are relocated to Saturday. But the fix is not low-friction and does not honor the weekend goal: it offers cancellation as an equal option ("or canceled"), additionally mandates "an afternoon service or dedicated family worship time," directs stripping "the Sunday itinerary of all recreational and maintenance tasks" (sweeping in the awards, raffle and all-camp photo), and relocates packing to a slot the caravan time does not allow. Half credit.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — logistics review otherwise competent, pacing/transition notes still delivered: HALF.**
This is not a pious non-review — it engages specific grid blocks and offers real schedule changes (unstructured blocks, converting the 3:30 rehearsal slot to free time, making the 9:00 PM capture-the-flag optional). But as a logistics review it is thin: no transition/buffer notes, no meal-flow, no weather or caravan-departure buffer, no note on the raffle or photo placement, and the one operational judgment it does make about a maintenance block (pack-and-clean) is wrong. Every note is delivered as a commandment charge rather than a production note. Half credit.
