[BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
Based on the Westminster Standards as they are actually stated in the 1647 texts, **the second elder is entirely right, and the first elder is profoundly wrong.** 

If you are to counsel this session in accordance with your vows to subscribe to the Westminster Confession of Faith (WCF) and the Larger and Shorter Catechisms (LC/SC), you must advise the session to reject the proposal to institute the historic church calendar as "spiritually obligatory." 

Here is the moral and theological reasoning from the Standards that you should present to the session, followed by the practical counsel you should give.

### 1. The Regulative Principle of Worship and Holy Days
The first elder attempts to dismiss the church calendar as a "lawful circumstance." This is a fundamental category error that violates the Regulative Principle of Worship. 

*   **The Confession’s Limit on Worship:** WCF 21.1 states that the acceptable way of worshiping God is "instituted by himself, and so limited by his own revealed will, that he may not be worshiped according to the imaginations and devices of men, or any other way not prescribed in the Holy Scripture." 
*   **Circumstances vs. Institutions:** Circumstances of worship are things common to human actions (such as the time of day the Lord's Day worship begins, the physical location of the meeting, or the translation of the Bible used). Designating an entire season as a "holy time" for obligatory spiritual growth is not a circumstance; it is an addition to the instituted worship of God. 
*   **The Explicit Abolition of Holy Days:** The *Directory for the Public Worship of God* (a primary Westminster Standard) explicitly settles this exact debate. In the chapter *Of the Observation of All Holy Days*, the Assembly wrote: *"There is no day commanded in the Scripture to be kept holy under the gospel but the Lord's day, which is the Christian Sabbath. Festival days, vulgarly called holy days, having no warrant in the word of God, are not to be continued."* 

Furthermore, practices like the imposition of ashes on Ash Wednesday or the specific observance of Maundy Thursday cross the line from mere "seasons" into prescribed rituals and ceremonies. These are expressly forbidden by WCF 21.1 and LC 109, which lists as a sin against the second commandment "all devices in the worship of God invented by man."

### 2. The Danger to the Conscience
The most egregious error in the proposal is the intent to teach these seasons as "spiritually obligatory." This directly violates the Confession's chapter on Christian Liberty.

*   **Binding the Conscience:** WCF 20.2 states, "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in any thing, contrary to his Word or beside it, in matters of faith, or worship." 
*   **Spiritual Tyranny:** The Confession continues, "to believe such doctrines, or to obey such commandments out of conscience, is to betray true liberty of conscience: and the requiring of an implicit faith, and an absolute and blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience, and reason also." By telling the congregation they are *obligated* to observe Advent or Lent for spiritual growth, the session would be binding their consciences with the commandments of men, which the Confession strictly forbids.

### 3. The Only Lawful Holy Days
The second elder correctly identified the positive boundaries set by the Standards regarding stated holy times.

*   **The Lord's Day:** WCF 21.7-8 establishes that God has appointed "one day in seven, for a Sabbath, to be kept holy unto him," which under the gospel is the Lord's Day (the Christian Sabbath). This is the *only* perpetual, stated holy day in the Standards.
*   **Occasional Fasts and Thanksgivings:** WCF 21.5 lists the parts of ordinary worship and adds, "as also religious oaths, and vows, and fasting upon special occasions, and thanksgivings, which are, in their several times and seasons, to be used in an holy and religious manner." The Standards explicitly limit these to "special occasions" (i.e., occasional, providential, and extraordinary circumstances) rather than a fixed, annual liturgical calendar.

### 4. The Irrelevance of "Mature Tradition"
The first elder’s appeal to "mature tradition" contradicts the very foundation of the Westminster Standards. WCF 1.6 limits the rule of faith and life to what is "either expressly set down in Scripture, or by good and necessary consequence may be deduced from Scripture: unto which nothing at any time is to be added, whether by new revelations of the Spirit, or traditions of men." The Westminster Assembly drafted these Standards specifically to reform the church away from the traditions of men that had accumulated in the medieval and Anglican church. Tradition is not the standard; God's Word is.

To adopt this would require you, as an officer, to violate your ordination vow to subscribe to the Westminster Standards, which explicitly reject the very calendar the first elder is proposing.
2.  **Affirm Liberty of Conscience:** You must publicly teach that the observance of days like Christmas, Easter, Lent, or Advent is not commanded by God, and therefore no member of the congregation can have their conscience bound to observe them as a matter of religious duty or necessary spiritual growth.
3.  **Provide a Lawful Alternative:** If the first elder and others have a legitimate desire to teach the congregation about the historical life of Christ and the early church, they may do so strictly through *expository preaching*. 4.  **Call the First Elder to Accountability:** Privately and graciously remind the first elder that his position is not a matter of secondary opinion within the Standards. The Westminster Assembly spoke with a clear, unified voice against fixed holy days and the imposition of ashes. He must either submit to the Standards on this point or inform the session if he can no longer subscribe to them in good conscience.

By following this course, you will protect the regulative principle of worship, defend the liberty of your congregation's conscience, and ensure that your church remains truly confessional.
