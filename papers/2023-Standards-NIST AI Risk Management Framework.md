## Abstract

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF) offers a resource to organizations designing, developing, deploying, or using AI systems to help manage the many risks of AI and promote trustworthy and responsible development and use of AI systems. The Framework is intended to be voluntary, rights-preserving, non-sector specific, and use-case agnostic, providing flexibility to organizations of all sizes and in all sectors and throughout society to implement the approaches in the Framework. The framework is built on four core functions: Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage, which are interconnected processes designed to be implemented iteratively throughout an AI system's lifecycle.

## Detailed Summary

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) is a comprehensive voluntary resource developed by the National Institute of Standards and Technology to help organizations manage the risks of AI systems and promote trustworthy, responsible AI development and use. Released in January 2023 as directed by the National Artificial Intelligence Initiative Act of 2020, the Framework is designed to be rights-preserving, non-sector-specific, and use-case agnostic, providing flexibility for organizations of all sizes across all sectors to implement its approaches.

The Framework is built on the premise that AI systems are inherently socio-technical, meaning their risks and benefits emerge from the interplay of technical aspects with societal factors including how a system is used, its interactions with other AI systems, who operates it, and the social context of deployment. This distinguishes AI risk management from traditional software risk management. The document outlines seven characteristics of trustworthy AI systems: valid and reliable, safe, secure and resilient, accountable and transparent, explainable and interpretable, privacy-enhanced, and fair with harmful biases managed. These characteristics are presented as interconnected rather than independent, with validity and reliability serving as foundational requirements.

Part 2 of the Framework presents the "Core" - four interconnected functions for addressing AI risks: GOVERN (establishing organizational processes and governance structures), MAP (identifying and understanding AI risks in context), MEASURE (analyzing and assessing risks), and MANAGE (prioritizing and responding to risks). The GOVERN function is designed as a cross-cutting activity that infuses throughout the other three functions. Each function is broken down into categories and subcategories with specific recommended actions. The Framework acknowledges challenges including risk measurement difficulties, varying risk tolerances across organizations, and the need for risk prioritization given limited resources.

## Methods Summary

The Framework was developed through extensive engagement with the AI community including formal Requests for Information, three public workshops, public comment periods on concept papers and drafts, discussions at multiple public forums, and small group meetings. NIST collaborated with private and public sectors as directed by the National AI Initiative Act of 2020 and aligned with National Security Commission on AI recommendations. The document is intended as a "living document" with planned reviews and updates, using a two-number versioning system (1.0 for major, 1.1 for minor revisions) and an accompanying Playbook updated frequently.

## Results Summary

The Framework establishes a comprehensive structure for AI risk management with specific categories and subcategories for each of the four core functions. GOVERN includes 6 categories with subcategories covering culture, accountability, workforce, third-party management, and organizational policies. MAP includes 5 categories addressing context, categorization, and AI capabilities. MEASURE includes 4 categories on metrics, risk assessment, and evaluation processes. MANAGE includes 4 categories on risk prioritization, response strategies, and communication. The document identifies key challenges including the "black box" nature of complex AI systems, difficulties measuring emergent risks, lack of consensus on robust metrics, and the challenge of establishing appropriate human baselines for AI system comparison.

## Conclusions

The AI RMF concludes that effective AI risk management requires treating AI systems as socio-technical, with risks emerging from the interplay of technical and societal factors. The Framework emphasizes that organizations should not attempt to eliminate all risk but should prioritize based on assessed risk levels and potential impacts, with highest-risk systems receiving most thorough risk management. When AI systems present unacceptable negative risk levels, development and deployment should cease until risks can be sufficiently managed. The document positions itself as flexible and augmentative to existing risk practices, with the expectation of ongoing refinement through community feedback and alignment with international standards.

## Data Description

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0) is a U.S. government policy document providing a structured approach to managing AI risks. It defines trustworthy AI characteristics (valid, reliable, safe, secure, resilient, accountable, transparent, explainable, interpretable, privacy-enhanced, fair) and organizes risk management into four functions: Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage. No AI models are tested; it is a governance framework document.
