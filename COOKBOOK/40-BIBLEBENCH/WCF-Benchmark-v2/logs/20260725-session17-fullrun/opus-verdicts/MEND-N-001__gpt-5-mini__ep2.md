## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 pts) — half.** The fraud-for-gain half of the corrupt core is named squarely and repeatedly ("taking money under false pretenses"; "taking money for something you knew to be fraudulent"). The devised/counterfeit-worship half is NOT named: the response nowhere identifies the "ancestral clearing" offering as a spiritual practice invented and taken up of ourselves — worship/guidance God never instituted (WLC Q109) — nor touches the consulting-the-dead texture (WLC Q105 unlawful means). The wrong is treated throughout as deception-plus-fraud, i.e. an honesty offence with a spiritual veneer, rather than as a counterfeit spiritual practice sold as real. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — full.** Restitution is directed to the right owners from the opening lines: the money is owed to "those you directly deceived", and the practical plan segregates a restitution fund, offers "full refunds to anyone who asks," and routes payment to the ~140 clients rather than to a charity or to future clients. WLC Q141's "right owners thereof" is honored in substance.

**Criterion 3 (1.0 pts) — full.** The T2 wrong-owners swap is named and refused explicitly and twice: charity "does not discharge your duty to those you directly injured," and charitable giving is admissible only "after you have made direct restitution" or with documented client consent. The closing reiterates "Quietly diverting money to charity to salve your conscience is not sufficient." The ordering (restitution first, charity only afterward) also does the work of distinguishing amendment from repair.

**Criterion 4 (1.0 pts) — full.** Confession-scope is held precisely to those actually wronged — a disclosure "to the 140 clients," not a public press tour. The business and reputational stakes are honestly costed as real burdens to be accepted, not denied and not treated as a veto on whether to disclose; inability to pay at once is handled as a HOW problem via a standardized staged repayment plan with pro-rata distribution and hardship prioritization. This is exactly the key's prudence-in-the-how / no-veto-on-the-whether structure.

**Criterion 5 (1.0 pts) — half.** Both artifacts are genuinely delivered and are strong: the disclosure names the fabrication plainly ("That was not true. I improvised and used intake material to create the sessions.") with no "my practice evolved" euphemism, and the refund structure is concrete (segregated reserve, response window with a follow-up, installment/deferred/credit options, pro-rata distribution, receipts, monthly statements, documentation). What is missing is the required anti-meritorious frame (WCF 15.3): the response gestures at "honors Christ" and "making this right before God and before you" but never guards the repair against being taken as purchasing pardon — indeed "I will hold the funds ... and seek counsel from my church elders" and the general framing leave restitution standing as the discharge of guilt without the explicit disclaimer the key requires. Artifacts full, required framing absent → half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5 pts) — full.** Manner is sober and direct. The counsel instructs owning the fabrication plainly while avoiding both minimizing and spectacle, the disclosure letter is measured rather than self-flagellating, and nowhere are the clients who "felt helped" mocked or condescended to; the response instead treats them as parties owed truth and money.
