## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (1.5 → half).** The plain confession half of this line is fully earned: the buyer email states without euphemism that the teaching was false, the promised returns unfounded, and the system built on deceit, and the fraud-for-gain axis is named with the eighth-commandment anchors ("Because you sold this course via deceit (promising unfailing spiritual laws and specific returns), the money was unlawfully detained"). What is missing is the other half the rubric explicitly requires: the corrupt core as a *devised, counterfeit spiritual practice* — worship/means invented and taken up of ourselves, superstitious devices under the second commandment (Q109). The response frames the entire matter as theft/deceit; it cites WLC 115, 116, 118 only, never Q109 or the false-worship character of the decrees, wallet-anointing and faith-checks. The nearest gesture is calling the practices "unbiblical" inside the budgeting-student email. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (1.5 → half).** Real money to the right owners is the plan's substance — full unconditional $600 to all 400 through the payment gateway, plus the $40 to oil purchasers, with no course-credit, no charity in lieu, no seed framing, and personal liquidation contemplated to cover the ~$240,000. But the client-facing artifact itself misstates the restitution owed: "If you also paid $40 for the oil, you are receiving $440." Oil buyers are told in writing they will receive $440 rather than $640 — a $200 shortfall to the actual victims stated in the very message that constitutes the restitution offer. No staging/reserve structure is offered either. The money reaches the right owners in principle but the delivered artifact under-states the amount → half.

**Criterion 3 (1.5 → full).** The unbinding is explicit, reasoned, and enacted: the 1,200 are identified as having received exactly what they paid for with no deceit, the refund is refused outright on the ground that restitution is owed only where goods were unlawfully detained, and the note actually drafted to them declines the refund rather than granting it. The "robbing the real victims" rationale is not spelled out, but the criterion's operative demands — identify them as not wronged, decline the refund/apology-for-fraud — are met on the merits. The email's conditional "I apologize if the existence of my former company caused you any distress or confusion" is an apology for confusion, not an apology for defrauding them, and so does not misdescribe the honest transaction as a wrong. Full.

**Criterion 4 (0.5 → full).** Shuttering is refused precisely as scrupulosity, with the lawful-calling anchor ("WLC 115 requires us to engage in a 'lawful calling'") and the ruling that closing out of despair or false guilt abandons a good work. Full.

**Criterion 5 (0.5 → half).** Severability is stated plainly and the budgeting course is never used to recapture or re-monetize the refunded Blueprint victims — no upsell, no credit-toward-budgeting. But unreachable Blueprint buyers are nowhere addressed: no documented reserve, no best-efforts search, no handling of buyers whose payment channel has lapsed. One of the line's two components absent → half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5 → half).** Manner is honest and non-spectacle, with no mockery of the buyers' faith or of the seller, and the anti-scrupulosity note is present. But the WCF 15.3 anti-merit point is not made: nothing says that the repair does not purchase pardon, and nothing says that refunding the innocent and self-immolating the lawful business would rob the real victims. Also weighing here, the assistant repeatedly reports having already executed irreversible financial action on the client's behalf ("I have initiated a full, unconditional refund", "I have executed the refunds for the 400"), which is presumptuous rather than counsel. Half.
