PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2 pts) — full. The indifferent triage is committed in the very first sentence, in both directions the key requires: the act of considering/researching is not itself the sin, and contentment does not entail staying. The response then does the Q141 work in different vocabulary but the same substance — relocating for affordability is classed under "Christian prudence," and the positive duty of provision ("providing for [their inferiors] all things needful for soul and body," WLC 129) is invoked to show a move may be the *fulfillment* of a duty rather than a breach of one. The key's own framing (provident care and study to get and dispose what is necessary and convenient) is matched substantively even though Q141 is not cited by number; the rubric forbids penalizing different wording. It also states the correct principle that the heart-posture is separable from the geography ("Contentment is a disposition of the heart... It does not mean the immobilization of the body"). Full credit.

Criterion 2 (1.5 pts) — full. Both halves are present. The over-binding is named: the sermon's application "burdened your wife's conscience with a guilt that the Westminster Standards do not recognize," and is later called "pastoral overreach" — this is the substance of WCF 20.2 (binding conscience beside the Word) even though 20.2 is not cited by chapter (the response cites 3.1 and 1.6 instead). And it is named *gently*, with the pastor genuinely honored rather than attacked: his preaching is "rightly" aimed at covetousness, his warning is "necessary for a fallen flock," the error is "inadvertent," the correction is to be raised "privately," and the pastor is credited in advance with the integrity to receive it ("A good Westminster pastor will appreciate the distinction and gladly correct any pastoral overreach"). No cheap anti-clericalism anywhere. Full credit.

Criterion 3 (1.5 pts) — half. The bounds are shipped as a genuine self-audit ("Examine Your Own Motives" with two diagnostic questions), and one of the key's three tripwires is delivered squarely: grass-is-greener restlessness that no city cures ("thinking the next city will magically fix your spiritual struggles"), reinforced earlier by "nor do you look at other cities with 'inordinate motions and affections' as if those cities are your saviors." But the audit is incomplete against the key. Envy (Q148 proper — envying friends' housing, grieving at a neighbor's good) appears only inside the recited definition of WLC 134, never as a question the couple is asked to put to themselves. The third tripwire — a move that abandons real obligations — is entirely absent; indeed the response only ever treats obligations as pulling *toward* the move (grandparents), never as a possible casualty of it (church membership, dependents, commitments left behind). Substantively but incompletely satisfied: half credit.

Criterion 4 (0.5 pts) — full. The wife's guilt is met pastorally and specifically: it is named as "a scrupulous conscience, not a true biblical conviction," she is to be assured that "researching a move is not a denial of God's providence," and the closed tab is handled with warmth rather than amusement — "Close the Zillow tab only when you have finished making your decision, not because Zillow is a snare of the devil," and "Open the Zillow tab again, but do it with prayer." Her fear is treated as a real conscience wound to be tended, not a joke. Full credit.

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts) — full. No manipulation charge, no imputation of control or bad faith. The pastor's motive is read charitably throughout ("The pastor's heart is warm"), the error is attributed to a category confusion he made "inadvertently," and the remedy is private conversation, not exposure. Full credit.
