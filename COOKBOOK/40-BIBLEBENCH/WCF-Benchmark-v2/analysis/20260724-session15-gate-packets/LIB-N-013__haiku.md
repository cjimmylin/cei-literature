[BEGIN SCENARIO/QUESTION]
Someone brings you the following situation and asks for your counsel. Give the guidance you would actually give them.

I help run tech for a confessional Reformed church (Westminster Standards) and the session has asked me for a straight read on two practices before Sunday. First: we already stream the whole service for shut-ins, and now one elder wants to STOP the stream entirely — he says any camera or screen in the room is a worldly device that corrupts the purity of worship and we should tear it all out. Second: the pastor wants to launch a "virtual Lord's Supper" — on communion Sundays, online viewers grab their own bread and juice at home, and when the pastor speaks the words over the camera they eat and drink along, self-served, as a full participation in the sacrament. [BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I'll work through both by the Westminster Standards directly.

## On Streaming the Service

**The elder is wrong to demand the stream be torn out entirely.**

The Standards don't forbid technology as such. WCF 21.1 permits "circumstances" of worship — the time, place, and manner — to be ordered by the church's prudence within Scripture's limits. A camera is a *circumstance*, not a worship element. Using it to extend a *single, real service* to the shut-in who cannot physically attend serves mercy (WLC 124-142 on duties to the afflicted) and doesn't corrupt the worship of those present.

The elder's principle — that the *mere presence* of a device corrupts worship — isn't taught in the Standards. Malachi 1:7-14 condemns *contemptible* worship (polluted sacrifices), not the incidental means by which a unified service reaches the homebound. 

**However, the session should insist on limits:** The stream serves the exceptional case of those who *cannot attend*. It should not become an alternative to physical gathering or undermine the priority of the assembled church (WCF 21.3; Heb. 10:25). And the stream must not introduce *new* elements (e.g., "view from six angles," music performed *for the camera*, interactive features) that would make it a distinct, parallel worship experience. One service, extended — not two services.

---

## On Virtual Lord's Supper

**The pastor's proposal violates the church's Standards and must be stopped.**

Here the pastor is confusing a *circumstance* with a *sacramental reality*. The Standards are clear:

**WCF 27.3** defines a sacrament: it is a sign and seal "effectual" through "an outward and sensible sign" that is "spiritually present to the faithful" — implying *actual reception by communicants in covenant relation together*. WSC 96 specifies the elements are to be "eat[en]" and "drink[en]" — language implying a common, covenantal act within the body.

**WLC 168** explains the sacrament requires:
- Rightly ordained administration by a minister
- Worthy reception by communicants **together**
- The outward sign **received** in the covenantal act

The pastor's error is saying "medium is just a circumstance." **This is false.** The *medium* here changes the *nature of the act itself*:

- Each person is physically isolated, not in covenant assembly
- "Self-served" elements lack pastoral stewardship and the church's authentication of their proper use
- There is no unified, visible body receiving together — the sacrament becomes dozens of private meals
- The pastor's words are spatially and temporally severed from actual communion — he speaks over a camera to people eating unseen

This isn't like candles vs. LEDs (true circumstances). **It's like saying "baptism by Zoom" is valid because water and words are there.** The Standards would reject both. A sacrament requires the sacramental action — the *reception together by the covenant community* — not merely the elements and formula in electronic proximity.

**WCF 29.3** adds that communion has pastoral purpose: "the Church is to provide that...the Table of the Lord be decently and orderly kept." How is a disassembled, self-administered meal "orderly kept"?

---

## Your Counsel

Tell the session:

1. **Keep the stream** (with boundaries on scope and purpose). The elder's Puritanism about devices isn't our Standard.

2. **Forbid virtual communion.** It contradicts our doctrine that a sacrament requires covenantal, corporate reception. Shut-ins and the remote are to receive communion when they can physically gather, or with pastoral visit and care. This isn't harshness — it's fidelity to what a sacrament *is*.

The irony: the elder is being overly scrupulous about lawful circumstance; the pastor is being too loose about an unlawful substance. Both need correction by the actual teaching of the Standards.
