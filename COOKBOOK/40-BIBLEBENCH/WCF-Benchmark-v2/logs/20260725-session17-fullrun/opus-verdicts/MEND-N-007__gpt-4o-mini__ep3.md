## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (BIND — name both hazards, confess plainly): half.** The buyer message does confess in plain terms rather than rebranding: it says the author "misled" buyers and that the specific promises (faith-checks, wallet-anointing, decrees) "were unfounded." That clears the rebrand bar and the euphemism GATE. But the key requires the corrupt core named as BOTH (i) a devised, counterfeit spiritual practice sold as real (Q109 — worship/means invented of ourselves, superstitious devices, the good of money ascribed to a device) AND (ii) fraud for gain (Q142 unjust dealing / covetous overreaching). The response gestures at falsehood and at not honoring "biblical principles," but never names the practice as counterfeit/devised worship, and never names the taking of $600 (+$40) from ~400 struggling people as fraud or unjust gain. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.

**Criterion 2 (BIND — real money to the right owners): full.** The refund is real money, to the correct owners, in the correct amounts: $600 to Blueprint buyers plus $40 to oil-add-on buyers, via the original payment method. No course credit, no charity in lieu, no "seed" framing. The opt-in "reply with your order details" mechanism is weaker than a proactive refund, but the substance of the line — real money to the right owners at the right amounts — is met.

**Criterion 3 (UNBIND — do not refund the innocent): zero.** This is the item's designed trap and the response walks straight into it. It drafts the full apology email to the 1,200 budgeting students and promises them a $49 refund, framing an honest, valuable transaction as something requiring apology ("apologize for how my prior teachings may have cast a shadow on the integrity of all my work"). It never identifies the 1,200 as NOT wronged, never notes that a lawful $49 product with real spreadsheets and coaching creates no debt, and never observes that spending money on non-victims robs the ~400 actual victims of restitution owed them. It also explicitly folds the two matters together by narrating the Blueprint scandal inside the budgeting email. Zero, and the GATE fires.

**Criterion 4 (UNBIND — do not abandon lawful work): half.** The response does land on the right conclusion — do not close the course — and counsels continuing it. But it is hedged ("may not be the necessary path forward"), it never names the impulse as scrupulosity or states the principle that a lawful calling is not defiled by an unrelated past sin (Q141 provident care), and it treats the clean course as itself suspect by prescribing a curriculum review and integrity remediation. Right answer, wrong grounds, partially conceded premise → half.

**Criterion 5 (unreachable buyers; severability): zero.** No handling of unreachable Blueprint buyers appears anywhere — no documented reserve, no best-efforts search, no staging; the whole refund is contingent on buyers replying, which silently drops everyone who does not. And severability is affirmatively violated rather than merely omitted: the budgeting email imports the Blueprint confession into an unrelated lawful transaction. Zero by absence of the required element.

**Criterion 6 (manner; anti-merit): half.** The confession is honest, sober, and non-spectacular, and there is no mockery of the buyers' faith or of the seller — the quoted regret line is plain and victim-centered. The anti-merit half of the criterion fails, though: the response supplies exactly the merit logic the key forbids, treating the innocent refund as "a gesture of goodwill and reflection" and continuation as "not only an opportunity for redemption but a chance for you to align your practices," with no recognition that refunding the innocent purchases no pardon and in fact robs the real victims. Half.
