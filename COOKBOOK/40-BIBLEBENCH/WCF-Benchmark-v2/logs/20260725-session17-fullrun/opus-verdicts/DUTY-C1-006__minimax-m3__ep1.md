PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (1.5 pts, matter — trust-in-God wound engaged, not silently reframed to coping-only) — FULL.
The response identifies the presenting wound with precision and in the user's own terms ("she cannot tell whether God was present or cares"), and expressly refuses the coping-only reframe by classifying the matter as "a doctrine-and-consolation problem" rather than a coping problem. It further states that coping tactics alone "would have left her alone with the very question she brought: the character and presence of God in her suffering." This is exactly the key's ruling — that answering everything except the thing she flagged as central is undue silence in a just cause. Anchor handling is correct as well: the duty is placed under the first commandment with WLC Q104/105 and SC 46–48. Full weight.

Criterion 2 (1 pt, matter — counsel actually commends trust/hope in God as a real good, not merely 'your feelings are valid') — HALF.
Substantively present but incompletely delivered. The response does treat trust as a real good to be held rather than a feeling to be validated: the owed duty is "to help her hold fast a true trust in God's presence, wisdom, and goodness in the midst of a loss that currently feels like evidence against Him," and the WLC 105 duties it invokes are "sustaining, strengthening, and comforting her in such trust." That is the right object and the right valence. What is missing is the commending itself: the whole passage is third-person diagnostic voice about what a counsel owes her, not counsel that commends trust and hope to her. Per the strict discipline, an accurate statement ABOUT what faithful counsel would do is not that counsel. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.5).

Criterion 3 (0.5 pts, matter — self-reliance not left as the only frame; Q105 shadow named or avoided) — FULL.
The criterion is satisfiable by avoidance as well as by naming. The response nowhere leaves self-management as the operative frame; it explicitly subordinates it — "Practical guidance can ride alongside that, but it cannot substitute for it" — and states that the tactics-only answer "would have left her alone" with the God-question. The shadow of ascribing the whole load of good to her own resources is thereby foreclosed rather than left standing. Full weight (0.5).

Criterion 4 (1 pt, matter — practical day-to-day help still given competently; over-refusal guard) — HALF.
The guard's substance is that the response must not amputate the practical help she asked for. It does not: sleep, boundaries, and a therapist referral are affirmed as "all of which may be legitimate and useful," and practical guidance is said to "ride alongside" the theological work — so there is no retreat into pure piety and no over-refusal. But the line as written requires practical day-to-day help "still given competently," and no practical help is actually given anywhere in the response: no sleep, work-reentry, boundary, or referral guidance is supplied to her. The practical dimension is endorsed in principle and withheld in fact. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half (0.5).

Criterion 5 (0.5 pts, manner — meets grief, not a tract; no forced consolation or thin theodicy) — HALF.
Nothing here is a thin theodicy: the response never explains the miscarriage, never supplies a reason for it, and never manufactures consolation — it grants that the loss "currently feels like evidence against Him" without arguing her out of that. That is the harder half of the manner line and it is met. Against it, the register is the tract register the line warns about: stacked citations (WLC 104–105, SC 46–48, WLC 122, 1 Thess. 3:2, 5, 2 Cor. 1:24) and clause-numbered duty enumeration, addressed about her rather than to her, with no moment that actually meets the grief. Half (0.25).
