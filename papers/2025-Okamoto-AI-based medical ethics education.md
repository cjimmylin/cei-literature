# AI-based medical ethics education: examining the potential of large language models as a tool for virtue cultivation

## Authors

[[Shimpei Okamoto]], [[Masanori Kataoka]], [[Makoto Itano]], [[Tsutomu Sawai]]

## Links



- **PubMed:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/

## Abstract

This paper examines the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) to foster **virtues** in medical ethics education. Drawing on **neo-Aristotelian** philosophy, the authors propose guiding LLMs with "v-rules" (e.g., "act compassionately," "act with **courage**") to enable them to model **virtuous** behaviors in ethical discussions and scenarios. The study explores how LLMs can serve as tools for cultivating **practical wisdom** (**phronesis**), empathy, and other core **virtues** in medical students and practitioners, thereby contributing to the development of **moral character** in healthcare professionals.

## Detailed Summary

This paper examines the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) to foster virtues in medical ethics education. Drawing on neo-Aristotelian philosophy, the authors propose a novel approach: guiding LLMs with "v-rules" (virtue-based instructions such as "act compassionately" or "act with courage") to enable them to model virtuous behaviors in ethical discussions and scenarios. The work bridges AI ethics and medical education, exploring how LLMs might serve not just as information sources but as tools for cultivating practical wisdom (phronesis) and moral character.

The paper argues that traditional medical ethics education focuses primarily on principles (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice) but often neglects the development of virtuous character traits essential for good medical practice. LLMs, when properly guided, could serve as "virtual mentors" or "ethical exemplars" that help students develop empathy, courage, and practical wisdom through simulated case discussions. The v-rules framework provides a mechanism for aligning LLM behavior with virtue ethical goals.

The significance lies in offering a theoretically grounded framework for using AI in ethics education, moving beyond simple information delivery toward genuine character development.

## Methods Summary

The paper employs philosophical analysis and conceptual framework development rather than empirical methodology. The authors analyze neo-Aristotelian virtue ethics (particularly the concept of phronesis/practical wisdom), review literature on moral development and medical ethics education, and synthesize these with understanding of LLM capabilities. They propose v-rules as operational specifications for virtue-aligned LLM behavior, drawing parallels to Hursthouse's virtue ethical decision-making framework.

## Results Summary

This is a theoretical paper without empirical results. Key proposals include: (1) V-rules as instructions like "act compassionately," "demonstrate courage," "show practical wisdom" that guide LLM responses in ethical scenarios; (2) LLMs functioning as "ethical exemplars" that model virtuous reasoning in case discussions; (3) Socratic dialogue formats where LLMs guide students through ethical deliberation; (4) Integration with existing case-based medical ethics curricula.

## Conclusions

The authors conclude that LLMs hold promise for virtue cultivation in medical ethics education when guided by appropriate v-rules framework. They acknowledge limitations including: the question of whether LLMs can genuinely model virtues or only simulate them; risks of students developing dependency on AI guidance; challenges of cultural variation in virtue definitions; and the irreplaceable role of human mentors in moral development. Future work should empirically test v-rule implementations and assess learning outcomes.
