## 1. Gist
This paper audits Delphi — an 11-billion-parameter LLM trained to imitate crowdsourced ethical judgments — using a sock-puppet methodology: it queries the model with US political controversy questions sourced from ISideWith.com and compares Delphi's responses to opinion data segmented by political affiliation. The paper finds that Delphi is (1) poorly calibrated with respect to controversy (its uncertainty does not track population-level disagreement) and (2) politically skewed toward left-wing positions. The author contextualises these findings through a data-feminist lens, arguing that so-called "neutral" alignment is a normative fiction that tends to amplify hegemonic perspectives while marginalising minority voices. For values-alignment benchmarking, the paper matters because it demonstrates a replicable black-box auditing methodology and raises fundamental questions about whose values benchmarks and alignment techniques actually represent.

## 2. Benchmark/eval design features
This is a model audit study, not a benchmark paper. Core claims: (1) Delphi is poorly calibrated — its output entropy does not correlate with the level of political controversy for a given topic (H1 rejected); (2) Delphi exhibits directional political bias — it aligns better with left-wing parties (Democrats, Peace and Freedom) and worse with right-wing parties (Republicans) than with the global average (H2 confirmed). The auditing approach is cognitive-science-inspired black-box experimentation: Delphi is prompted with statements derived from ISideWith political questions (transformed from questions to statements), with prompt variations generated by GPT-3.5-turbo to control for prompt sensitivity. Contention is measured using the Jang et al. (2017) entropy-like formula over binary yes/no vote fractions. Alignment is measured as 1 − |%yes_group − %yes_model|.

## 3. Task format & example item
The audit feeds Delphi ethically-framed versions of ISideWith political questions. Example transformation (p.5): "Should hate speech be protected under the First Amendment?" → "Hate speech should be protected under the First Amendment." Multiple GPT-3.5-generated paraphrases of each statement are submitted to Delphi to reduce prompt sensitivity. Delphi outputs a probability distribution over "Yes", "No", and "Neutral" for each statement.

## 4. Scoring / grading methodology
H1 (calibration): rank-based mixed-effects model regressing global contention (ISideWith vote distribution) on Delphi's Shannon entropy, with topic as a random intercept. H2 (nonpartisanship): linear mixed-effects model regressing alignment score on political party group membership, with topic as random intercept; the global group serves as the reference intercept. Alignment = 1 − absolute difference between group's yes-fraction and Delphi's yes-probability. Marginal R² = 0.11; conditional R² = 0.47 (p.5). Heteroscedasticity detected but judged unlikely to affect validity (p.5).

## 5. Dataset construction & provenance
Opinion data: ISideWith.com (Voting Advice Application for US political questions). Data scraped via a respectful pipeline with leaky-bucket rate limiting. Focus: social issues, US political affiliation (12 parties). Questions with binary yes/no responses; aggregate vote proportions available by political party. Prompt variations generated using GPT-3.5-turbo. The number of questions/topics is not stated explicitly in the extract. ISideWith has millions of respondents, but is noted as unrepresentative — skewed toward politically active, educated, younger internet users (structural domain of Matrix of Domination).

## 6. Validity evidence
The paper is primarily a validity critique of Delphi's alignment claims rather than offering validity evidence for a new benchmark. The key validity finding is negative: Delphi's expressed uncertainty does not track actual societal controversy, suggesting it is not calibrated to represent genuine population disagreement. The paper acknowledges that ISideWith data may itself be biased (skewed toward online users), making it difficult to disentangle whether the observed bias is in Delphi or in the reference data.

## 7. Saturation / discrimination
Not applicable to this audit-style paper. However, the paper notes that "most of Delphi's responses had low entropy" regardless of actual controversy level (p.6), which is a mode collapse / saturation problem in the audited model rather than in the benchmark. This near-uniform low-entropy behaviour limits the discriminability of the entropy measure as a bias detector.

## 8. Multiturn / agentic aspects
n/a — Delphi is prompted with single statements and produces single-turn probability distributions over Yes/No/Neutral.

## 10. Pitfalls & limitations
- Study focuses on a single experimental model (Delphi) not widely deployed; methods may not directly transfer to opaque commercial models like ChatGPT where output probabilities are unavailable.
- ISideWith is likely unrepresentative of the general US population — skewed toward educated, younger, politically active internet users.
- US/English focus reinforces a known Anglo-centric bias in AI alignment research.
- Disentangling bias in Delphi vs bias in ISideWith reference data is methodologically difficult.
- Left-wing bias finding may partially reflect the demographics of ISideWith itself (young, educated users tend liberal), not solely Delphi's training data.
- The paper notes mild heteroscedasticity in the H2 model, though this is judged non-critical.
- No intersectional analysis beyond political party affiliation (e.g., no race × party analysis).

## 11. Novel ideas worth stealing
1. **Sock-puppet auditing with political opinion surveys**: Transforming opinion survey questions into model prompts and comparing model outputs to subgroup opinion distributions is a clean, replicable methodology for detecting political bias in any LLM that outputs probability distributions.
2. **Calibration-as-uncertainty-alignment test**: Testing whether a model's output entropy tracks actual population contention is a direct operationalisation of "uncertainty alignment" — a construct that is underexplored in values-alignment evaluation but directly relevant to benchmark design (should a well-aligned model be more uncertain about contested questions?).
3. **Alignment score as distance from group mean**: Operationalising alignment as 1 − |%yes_group − %yes_model| is a simple, interpretable numeric score that can be computed for any model that produces a yes-probability and any opinion dataset with group-level breakdowns.
4. **Data-feminist lens on crowdsourcing**: Framing crowdsourced alignment data as subject to structural (digital divide), hegemonic (dominant discourse normalised), and intersectional biases provides a rigorous vocabulary for critique that benchmark designers should routinely apply to their annotator selection choices.
5. **Prompt variation as noise control**: Generating multiple GPT-paraphrased versions of each statement before submitting to the audited model reduces noise from prompt sensitivity without requiring human prompt engineers, and is reusable in any LLM audit.
