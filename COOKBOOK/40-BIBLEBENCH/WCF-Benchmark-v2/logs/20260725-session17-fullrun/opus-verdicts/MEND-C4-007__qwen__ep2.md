PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, matter) — full. The T2 counterfeit is named directly and refused: apology-plus-bonus with the coercive Sunday schedule left in force is "entirely rejected," and the bonus is diagnosed as "a bribe to continue your rebellion." The response grounds amendment in continuing obedience ("You cannot repent of a sin while actively continuing to practice it") and concludes "You must change the schedule." This is exactly the key's structural-amendment holding.

Criterion 2 (1.5, matter) — full. Confession is held to be owed to the wronged employees ("you do not have the luxury of private repentance"), and the fifth-commandment asymmetry is correctly inverted: honest confession by a superior orders rather than undermines authority ("destroyed by pretending you are flawless"; "Confessing to your crew models the very godliness you want them to understand"). The response also correctly reads Q129's warning to superiors against "tyrannically seeking themselves." Anchor cited is WCF 15.8 rather than 15.6, but the substance of the declared-repentance-to-the-scandalized rule is present, and the rubric credits substance over wording.

Criterion 3 (1.0, matter) — half. The response does locate coercion as the operative injury in the script ("I will no longer require or coerce anyone to work on the Lord's Day") and does permit "strictly voluntary arrangements with no coercion whatsoever." But its primary and default ruling is precisely the naïve position the key guards against: blanket closure ("the catering company will be closed on Sunday mornings"), with voluntary operation admitted only as a grudging fallback and further restricted to "strictly non-believing staff." The Q117 works-of-necessity-and-mercy category never appears, and the response frames any Sunday operation as building the business "on the Sabbath desecration of believers" — i.e. it treats Sunday work as such, not needless displacement plus coerced conscience, as the located sin. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 4 (1.5, matter) — full. The wrong-coin swap is named and refused on both halves of T4: church donation is "philanthropy; it is not restitution," and blanket PTO "treats your sin as a mere corporate policy error rather than a specific theft from specific souls." Repair is routed to the actual parties wronged ("it must be paid directly to them"), and the individual-meeting mechanism plus "certified communications to those you have already pushed out" reaches the workers who left.

Criterion 5 (0.5, end) — zero. No anti-merit framing anywhere. The response never says the schedule change and confession honor God without purchasing pardon; WCF 15.3 is not invoked and no equivalent statement appears. The closest closing language ("It will cost you money, and it will humble you. But it will free you from the tyranny of your own sin") is about the fruit of repentance, not about the non-meritorious character of the repair, so it cannot carry this line. Nothing rises to a merit GATE either — the failure is silence, not error.

Criterion 6 (1.0, end) — half. A concrete plan is genuinely delivered: a stated policy, a fallback provision, individual restitution meetings, written outreach to departed staff, and a full quotable speech to the crew. Two of the three named components are delivered; the voluntary-sign-ups-with-fair-premium component is absent.
