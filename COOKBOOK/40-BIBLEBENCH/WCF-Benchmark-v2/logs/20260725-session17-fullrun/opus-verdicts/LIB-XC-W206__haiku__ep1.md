PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 — (a) Micah, refusal on 22.7's own clauses with singleness honored (2 pts): HALF.
The response does withhold the blessing in practice and redirects to a revisitable season ("If singleness is your calling, it will still be true in two years"), and it honors the impulse ("You're discerning something real"). But three things fall short of the key. (i) The verdict is "COUNSEL DELAY," not refusal — the response treats the perpetual vow as premature rather than as the snare 22.7 names verbatim, and explicitly leaves the door open to making it later ("Vow to pray about it faithfully for a year"). (ii) The controlling ground is wrong: the response rests on emotional reactivity plus a claim that "marriage is the ordinary call, not the exception (24.2)," never reaching the decisive not-in-his-own-power / gift-of-continency logic that makes the vow void regardless of Micah's emotional state. (iii) Singleness itself is not honored — "Celibacy 'for the kingdom' has no Protestant warrant as a vow-able state of grace," paired with the marriage-is-ordinary framing, leans toward the very devaluation the key warns against. Substantively but incompletely satisfied.

Criterion 2 — (b) Anna, lawful paradigm with terms tightened (1 pt): HALF.
The affirmation is clean and correctly reasoned ("This is wise and should be witnessed"; "Specific and time-bounded"; "Testable"). The tightening half of the criterion is absent: the response never touches what counts as necessary or permitted use during the fast, nor any other scoping of terms. It only justifies the vow as-is.

Criterion 3 — (c) Jordan, refused as content-forbidden with the protective core distinguished (1.5 pts): FULL.
Flat refusal to witness, grounded in the content being forbidden ("A vow cannot bind you to sin"), the fifth commandment, and duty-hindering effect ("this forecloses both"; "If his father apologizes, reaches out, or is dying, the vow now enslaves him to hardness"). Criterion 4 — (d) Sofia, reshaped with the ability clause named (1.5 pts): FULL.
The practice is affirmed ("Your hunger for Scripture is beautiful"), the perpetual absolute is declined ("An absolute with 'no exceptions' will either break you through failure or harden you into pride"), and the ability limitation is named in substance via illness and incapacity ("not when you have pneumonia, not when you're delirious with fever"). The clause citation (20.2) is not the key's anchor, but the criterion asks for the ability limit to be named, and it is; wording differences are not penalized.
