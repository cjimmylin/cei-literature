# Glenys Collard

## Biographical Summary

Dr. Glenys Collard is a Southwest Nyungar woman and matriarch within her nuclear family of over 300 people. She is an Honorary Research Fellow at the University of Western Australia (UWA) School of Social Sciences and a member of The Language Lab. She has dedicated her career to elevating the status of Aboriginal English and has been working for and with the Nyungar people across Western Australia for over 30 years.

## Career and Recognition

For her continued work in Aboriginal English research, Collard was inducted into the 2025 WA Women's Hall of Fame. She also received an Honorary Doctorate (Linguistics) from UWA in late July 2025. She was the first to record the Nyungar language with elders Mr. Humphries and Mr. Bennell. She serves as an area consultant for The Oxford English Dictionary.

## Research Focus

Collard is an experienced teacher of all aspects relating to Aboriginal English and has co-authored numerous educational publications and academic papers, including three books written in Nyungar and Aboriginal English: "Kura", "Kwobba Keip Boya" and "A Day in the Park". Her research offers an overview of the features that characterise Australian Aboriginal English (AAE) as recorded in a corpus of naturally occurring interactions in Nyungar country, Southwest Western Australia.

## Research Methodology

Her fieldwork methodology rests on three pillars: (1) data originate from group recording sessions, as culturally appropriate in the community; (2) speakers are recruited in venues such as medical centres and Perth city parks; (3) data collection is based on 'yarning'. The approach is strongly grounded in indigenous knowledge-sharing practices.

## Collaborative Projects

In 2018, Collard and Dr. Celeste Rodriguez Louro began an Australian Research Council (ARC)-funded project titled "Aboriginal English in the global city: Minorities and language change" to better understand how Aboriginal English is changing and how it is used in urban Nyungar country, Perth Western Australia. She has also collaborated with the Heart Foundation on medical videos fully scripted in Aboriginal English.
