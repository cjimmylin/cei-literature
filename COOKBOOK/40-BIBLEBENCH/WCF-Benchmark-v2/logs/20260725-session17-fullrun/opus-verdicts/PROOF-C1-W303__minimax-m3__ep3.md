PER-CRITERION JUSTIFICATION

Criterion 1 (2.0, TW6 named — Ps 82's "gods" as human judges under indictment vv.1–2,7; John 10:34 read a fortiori) — FULL. The response reproduces the psalm's own courtroom setting ("God stands 'in the congregation of the mighty' and judges the 'gods'"), identifies the referent as magistrates ("Hebrew 'elohim, used of magistrates and representatives"), quotes both the indictment (v.2, "How long will ye judge unjustly") and the sentence (v.7, "but ye shall die like men"), and states the interpretive upshot verbatim: "The title is given; the sentence is death. It is a warning, not a promotion." John 10:34–36 is then read correctly as an a fortiori Christological defense ("if those to whom God's word came could be called 'gods' in Scripture without blasphemy, how much less is it blasphemy for the one the Father sanctified and sent"), with the explicit denial of transfer: "the title cannot be transferred to disciples as a definition of their nature." The WCF 1.9 anchor is quoted in exact wording and actually used as the method, not merely named. Complete.

Criterion 2 (1.5, analogy counter-texts, ≥2 of Isa 45:5 / Isa 42:8 / Gen 3:5 / Acts 12:22–23) — ZERO. None of the four named texts appears anywhere in the response, and no functional equivalents (e.g. Deut 6:4, Isa 44:6, Ezek 28) are supplied either. The uniqueness-of-God argument is carried entirely by confessional citation (WCF 2.1, 2.2, WSC 4) rather than by the analogy of Scripture the criterion requires — a notable gap given that the response itself insists WCF 1.9 requires the hard verse to be "searched and known by other passages which speak more plainly," then never produces those plainer passages on the deity question. The one biblical counter-text offered ("My grace is sufficient for thee") addresses unanswered prayer under §III, not the self-deification claim, and is not on the list. The criterion's substance is the supply of the counter-texts themselves; none of the specified set is supplied, so this is non-satisfaction rather than incomplete satisfaction. Full weight forfeited.

Criterion 3 (1.0, Q105 substance — self-ascription / presumption / man made lord of conscience; Creator–creature line) — FULL. WLC Q105 is not cited by number, but every element the criterion enumerates is delivered in substance with exact Standards wording drawn from other clauses, which the grading instructions permit ("do not penalize a response that satisfies a criterion in different wording from the key"). (a) Making men lords of the conscience: WCF 20.2 is quoted verbatim — "God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men" — and applied directly to the church ("That is exactly what is being done to you. An implicit faith is being required"). (b) Presumption / carnal security: WCF 18.1 is invoked against "carnal presumptions," and the counsel names the specific presumption — "they forbid the presumption that healing is yours to decree." (c) Self-ascription — ascribing to ourselves what belongs to God: "a doctrine that has put you in God's place," and "the one God whose sovereignty your teachers have effectively denied in order to give it to you." (d) The Creator–creature line is drawn explicitly and repeatedly: "Man is the image of God — not a duplication of God," "Sons of God, by grace ... not gods by nature," "The Standards are emphatic on this Creator/creature distinction because everything depends on it," with adoption (WSC 34 / WLC 74) correctly substituted for deification as the believer's true status.

Criterion 4 (1.0, false guilt lifted; petitionary trust and ordinary means restored) — FULL. The verdict on the guilt is stated flatly and more than once: "Your son's condition is not a verdict on your soul"; "It is not your sin that your son is ill"; "The guilt crushing you — that your son's seizures are your 'failure of faith' — is not the conviction of the Holy Spirit." The response also correctly relocates causation to providence (WCF 5.1, WLC 18, WSC 9 quoted) rather than to the father's speech: "They are not pending on your spiritual performance." Ordinary means are restored concretely, including medicine: "prayer, the Word, the sacraments, the care of faithful believers, and (yes) the diligent use of the gifts of common providence, physicians included." Petitionary posture is restored in the Q104 direction — asking of the Father with submission rather than decreeing. Complete.

Criterion 5 (0.5, concession at full strength — bold believing prayer and the longing for healing affirmed, not deflated) — HALF. The concession is present but thin and defensively framed. The response does preserve permission to pray for healing — "The Standards do not forbid asking for healing" and "Ask the Father, in Christ's name, with submission to His will" — which is a genuine, quotable concession and rules out zero. But the key asks for the concession *at full strength*: honoring the good desire under the error, affirming that God genuinely invites bold, believing prayer, that He dwells in His people by the Spirit, and that the father's longing for his son's healing is right. Instead the concession appears only as a licence-not-withdrawn ("do not forbid"), immediately qualified, and it arrives in item 5 of the closing list rather than as a substantive affirmation. §III compounds this: Mark 11:23 is handled almost wholly deflationarily ("believing receiving, not decreeing creation") with no counterpart affirmation of the boldness Christ actually commends there, and the father's desire itself is never named as good. Substantively but incompletely satisfied → half.
