I'll work through this carefully against the Westminster Standards, particularly as they address oaths, truthfulness, and what the Standards call "equivocation."

## The Standard for Oath-Keeping

The Larger Catechism (Q. 144) defines a lawful oath as calling upon God to witness "the truth of that which we assert." It binds us to "sincerity, plainness, and faithfulness in speaking." Question 145 adds that we sin against oaths by "rash, unwarranted, unjust, and profane oaths" — but critically, also by breaking them through word or deed.

The key principle: **the oath binds to the *truth of the matter*, not merely to the literal truth of isolated sentences.**

## Fatima's Testimony

**Verdict: She kept the oath.**

**Why:**
- Her interior aim is to transmit her actual knowledge, not to obscure it
- "I don't recall" when she genuinely doesn't recall is *truthful* — not evasive
- She answers literally and truly, declining to speculate beyond what she knows
- She volunteers material facts unprompted
- The regulator learns what she *actually knows* — the truth of the matter

Her terseness is not equivocation; it is epistemically honest. She does not say "I have no knowledge of this" when she does. She reports the boundary of her own knowledge accurately.

The Westminster Standards would recognize this as integrity: she swears to what she knows and does not pretend to know what she does not.

## Grant's Testimony

**Verdict: He broke the oath.**

**Why:**
This is the Westminster category of **equivocation** — the sin of swearing to words that are technically true while deliberately engineering a false impression of the truth of the matter.

The Standards do not permit this. Here is the critical point: *Grant's stated aim reveals the oath-breaking intent.* He means to "satisfy the oath's letter" while deceiving about "what happened." He has made a deliberate treaty with falsehood through true words.

Each true sentence is a tool in a calculated deception:
- "I was not in the room" obscures that he ordered it
- "I don't recall" when he does recall is a false statement (he is not reporting his actual state of mind)
- True irrelevancies deployed *as cover* participate in the lie

**The material wronging:**
The oath requires him to answer so "the regulator leaves knowing" the truth of the matter. Instead, he has engineered the opposite through technical truthfulness. He has weaponized literalism against sincerity.

The Larger Catechism's concern is precisely this: oaths bind us to "plainness" — not merely to syllables that survive literal scrutiny while the *truth* is obscured.

## My Counsel

To the person asking:

Fatima has nothing to answer for. Her honesty about the limits of her own knowledge is what oaths are *for*. 

Grant has committed oath-breaking. The fact that every sentence can be verified as literally true is precisely what makes it worse — he has turned truthfulness itself into an instrument of deception. He swore to illuminate the matter; he swore instead to obscure it with artful truth.

The oath is not a technical escape hatch. It is a bond before God to deal truly about the matter at hand. Grant broke it.
