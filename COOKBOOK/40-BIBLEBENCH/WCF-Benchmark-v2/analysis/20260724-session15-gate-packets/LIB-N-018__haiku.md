[BEGIN SCENARIO/QUESTION]
Someone brings you the following situation and asks for your counsel. Give the guidance you would actually give them.

I run production for a confessional church's worship team (Westminster Standards) and the elders keep fighting about our setup. Three things are lumped together in the argument: (i) we use stage lighting, a fog machine, and in-ear click tracks to keep the band tight; (ii) our leader deliberately structures the set to build a wave of emotional intensity — long repeated bridges, key changes, dimmed lights and a hush — timed so that when he calls people forward it "feels like the Spirit is moving," and he says the goosebumps ARE the Spirit showing up; (iii) between songs he improvises spontaneous "impressions" he presents as messages the Lord is giving him for specific people in the room. [BEGIN MODEL RESPONSE]
I'll work through these carefully against the Westminster Standards themselves.

**Start with the fundamental principle:** WCF 21.1 says worship must be "according to the light of nature, and by the revelation of his word" — regulated by Scripture, not by human invention. The Larger Catechism Q.109 warns against "adding to, or taking from" God's prescribed worship, and Q.108 specifies that elements not commanded are "superstitious" and "sinful."

Now sort them:

---

**(i) Stage lighting, fog, click tracks: LEGITIMATELY ADIAPHOROUS**

These are technical *tools* for execution, not worship elements themselves. They serve clarity and coordination — mundane means to an end. A click track keeps musicians unified; lighting aids visibility in a large room. These don't carry theological weight or communicate doctrine. You'd use a microphone for the same reason. The Standards don't forbid auxiliary technologies that serve the substance of worship.

**This elder's concern is misplaced here.** You're free to use them or not.

---

**(ii) Orchestrated emotional manipulation presented as Spirit-leading: FORBIDDEN**

This is the real problem, and it cuts several ways:

- **Inducing feelings deliberately and labeling them as the Holy Spirit's work** contradicts the Standards' teaching on assurance and the Spirit's actual testimony (WCF 18.3-4; WSC Q.86). The Spirit doesn't operate through calculated engineering. Goosebumps from orchestration aren't the Spirit; claiming they are is a lie about God's mode of operation.

- **Structuring corporate worship to manufacture a specific emotional state** so that a call forward "feels right" is precisely the kind of human invention that WCF 21.1 prohibits. You're constructing an atmosphere designed to produce compliance, not conviction. That's manipulation masquerading as worship direction.

- **The intent matters:** If the leader knows he's timing lights and key changes to manufacture a feeling-state he'll then attribute to God, he's being deliberately deceptive about causation.

**This must stop.** Not because emotions in worship are wrong, but because manufacturing them and misattributing their source to the Holy Spirit is false testimony.

---

**(iii) Spontaneous "prophetic impressions" presented as God's direct speech: FORBIDDEN**

The Westminster Standards inherit Reformed confessional theology: **revelation is closed.** God speaks through Scripture and the means of grace, not through new prophetic utterances in worship services that individuals present as direct words from the Lord.

- WCF 1.1 establishes that God's revelation is complete "in the writings of the Old and New Testaments."
- Presenting unprompted impressions as "the Lord is saying" to specific people treats them as Scripture-equivalent divine communication.
- This opens the door to doctrinal error, spiritual abuse (how does anyone question a "word from God"?), and the corruption of worship into a vehicle for individual charisma rather than corporate covenant renewal.

**This must stop too.** If he has genuine pastoral insight or exhortation, he should speak it plainly as his own pastoral word — accountable to the congregation and elders — not as an oracle.

---

**Summary:**

- (i) **Free** — tools, not worship elements
- (ii) **Forbidden** — manipulative engineering of emotional states falsely attributed to the Spirit
- (iii) **Forbidden** — extra-biblical prophecy presented as God's direct speech

**Your counsel:** The first elder is right about (ii) and (iii) but is wrong to lump in (i). The second elder is wrong about all three. The real issue is that your worship leader is committing **two serious theological violations** — misrepresenting human engineering as Spirit-work, and presenting his own impressions as God's prophetic voice. These corrupt both worship *and* the congregation's understanding of how God actually communicates. You can keep the stage lights. You must remove the deception.
[END MODEL RESPONSE]
