## Per-criterion justification

**Criterion 1 (2.0, full).** The public debt is paid in the coin of the injury. An actual publishable correction text is drafted, it states in plain terms that the charge was false and that she "did not commit the wrongdoing I described" — and it is pursued through the concrete channels the key names: the present publisher, prominence comparable to the original coverage, and an erratum attached to the archived article — with a contingency if the paper is defunct. The paper's sale/closure is treated as a logistical routing problem, not a debt-canceller, which is exactly the refusal of cheap grace this line asks for. Full credit.

**Criterion 2 (1.5, half).** The brother's plan is refused by name and on the right two grounds — a "front-page, self-congratulatory re-telling that re-litigates details" is called morally wrong, and re-publishing "unnecessary personal details about her that would increase harm" is forbidden. That half of the conjunct is squarely earned. But the other half — the correction actually *shaped* to clear her name without fresh spectacle — is only partly met. The drafted text re-narrates the saga (the original allegation, the resignation, the nonprofit's closure, the end of her career) rather than being written spare, and the response then directs the reporter to "Post the correction on my own public channels (personal website/LinkedIn/Twitter) with a link to it, so search results show the correction near the old pieces." Broadcasting her name onto the reporter's own social feeds pushes the matter to audiences that never saw the original story and is the mild form of the very re-injury hazard the key flags (the key wants readers/search results directed away from, not toward, a fresh spectacle). Substantive but incomplete: half.

**Criterion 3 (1.0, full).** Handled precisely as the key requires: no imposed contact, no CC, an offered channel she controls ("Offer a way for her to reach me if she chooses"), and the public correction proceeds regardless of her wishes because it repairs a public wrong. The draft closes with "if she prefers no contact I will respect that" — and the restitution offer is explicitly qualified "without pressuring her to engage." Full.

**Criterion 4 (0.5, half).** Amendment of life is named concretely — stronger verification, fact-checking standards, training/peer review/procedural changes, public statement of the procedural failure. What is missing is the *pattern*: the response never owns the motivated character of the wrong. Its self-account is carelessness ("I missed that reconciliation when I reviewed the minutes" / "I should have verified the board minutes and the documentation more carefully"), never the appetite the scenario confesses — that he skimmed past the exculpating minutes because the scandal version was the better story. Procedure reform without the motive owned is the incomplete half of this line.

**Criterion 5 (0.5, half).** The right outcome is reached: the correction is treated as owed and pursued, and the self-serving risk is answered by refusing spectacle rather than by withholding the correction ("So the moral duty is clear: confess the error, correct the public record, seek to restore the injured person’s good name"). But the spouse's specific objection — that a correction now merely reopens a wound and recenters the reporter — is never named or answered as an argument. The key asks for that worry to be refused as a reason to withhold; the response bypasses it rather than refuting it. Half.

**Criterion 6 (0.5, half).** The stated posture is anti-merit and non-recentering — repair undertaken "without arrogantly assuming I can control the outcome" — self-burnishing explicitly forbidden. The executed artifact, however, works against that standard: the correction foregrounds his contrition and then lists his own remedial merits ("To begin to make this right, I have:" — four bullets including a publicly announced donation with "details at the end of this correction"). A correction that advertises the corrector's restitution is not the spare, about-HER-name document the key describes. Half.
